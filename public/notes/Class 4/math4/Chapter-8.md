• **Time:** The continuous progression of existence, events, and phenomena that occur in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, into the future.
• **Clock:** A mechanical or digital device used to measure, keep, and indicate time, typically displaying hours, minutes, and seconds.
• **Analog Clock:** A clock that displays time using a circular dial with rotating hands, usually an hour hand, a minute hand, and a second hand.
• **Digital Clock:** A clock that displays the time numerically, showing the hour and minute (and sometimes seconds) using digits on an LCD or LED screen.
• **Hour Hand:** The shorter hand on an analog clock that completes one full rotation around the dial in 12 hours.
• **Minute Hand:** The longer hand on an analog clock that completes one full rotation around the dial in 60 minutes (one hour).
• **Second Hand:** The thinnest and fastest-moving hand on an analog clock that completes one full rotation in 60 seconds (one minute).
• **Dial:** The face of the clock that is marked with numbers from 1 to 12 to represent the hours of the day.
• **Graduations:** The small markings on the edge of the clock face representing minutes and seconds, totaling 60 divisions.
• **1 Hour = 60 Minutes:** The fundamental relationship where the minute hand moves through 60 small marks to complete one hour.
• **1 Minute = 60 Seconds:** The fundamental relationship where the second hand moves through 60 small marks to complete one minute.
• **Reading Time:** The process of identifying the position of the hour hand and the minute hand to determine the current time.
• **O'Clock:** A term used when the minute hand is pointing exactly at the number 12, indicating the start of a new hour.
• **Half-Past:** A term used when the minute hand is pointing exactly at the number 6, indicating that 30 minutes have passed since the hour.
• **Quarter-Past:** A term used when the minute hand is pointing exactly at the number 3, indicating that 15 minutes have passed since the hour.
• **Quarter-To:** A term used when the minute hand is pointing exactly at the number 9, indicating that 15 minutes remain until the next hour.

• **A.M. (Ante Meridiem):** A Latin term meaning "before midday," used to denote time from 12:00 midnight up to 11:59 in the morning.
• **P.M. (Post Meridiem):** A Latin term meaning "after midday," used to denote time from 12:00 noon up to 11:59 at night.
• **Noon:** The exact time of 12:00 P.M., marking the middle of the day when the sun is at its highest point.
• **Midnight:** The exact time of 12:00 A.M., marking the beginning of a new day and the end of the previous day.
• **24-Hour Clock System:** A timekeeping convention where the day runs from midnight to midnight and is divided into 24 hours, numbered from 00:00 to 23:59.
• **12-Hour Clock Conversion:** The process of converting 24-hour time to 12-hour time by subtracting 12 from any hour greater than 12.
• **Duration:** The length of time that an event or activity lasts, calculated by finding the difference between the start time and the end time.
• **Start Time:** The specific moment when an activity or event begins.
• **End Time:** The specific moment when an activity or event concludes.
• **Elapsed Time:** The total amount of time that passes between the beginning and the end of an event.
• **Time Intervals:** The segments of time between two points, often measured in minutes or hours to track daily routines.
• **Calculating Duration:** To find the duration, subtract the start time from the end time, ensuring minutes are handled separately if borrowing is required.
• **Borrowing in Time:** When subtracting minutes, if the end time minutes are less than the start time minutes, borrow 60 minutes from the hour column.
• **Time Addition:** Adding hours and minutes to a given time to determine a future time point.
• **Time Subtraction:** Subtracting hours and minutes from a given time to determine a past time point.
• **Daily Routine:** A schedule of activities performed throughout the day, often mapped against a 24-hour cycle.

• **Calendar:** A chart or series of pages showing the days, weeks, and months of a particular year.
• **Year:** The time taken by the Earth to complete one full orbit around the Sun, approximately 365.25 days.
• **Common Year:** A standard year consisting of 365 days.
• **Leap Year:** A year that contains an extra day (February 29th) to keep the calendar synchronized with the Earth's orbit, totaling 366 days.
• **Leap Year Rule:** A year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4; however, century years (ending in 00) must be divisible by 400 to be leap years.
• **Month:** A division of the year, based on the orbit of the Moon around the Earth, consisting of 28, 29, 30, or 31 days.
• **January:** The first month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **February:** The second month of the year, containing 28 days in a common year and 29 days in a leap year.
• **March:** The third month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **April:** The fourth month of the year, containing 30 days.
• **May:** The fifth month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **June:** The sixth month of the year, containing 30 days.
• **July:** The seventh month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **August:** The eighth month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **September:** The ninth month of the year, containing 30 days.
• **October:** The tenth month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **November:** The eleventh month of the year, containing 30 days.
• **December:** The twelfth month of the year, containing 31 days.

• **Week:** A period of seven consecutive days, starting from Sunday and ending on Saturday.
• **Days of the Week:** Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, and Saturday.
• **Sequence of Days:** The cyclical nature of days ensures that after Saturday, the cycle repeats starting with Sunday.
• **Month-Day Correlation:** Using the "knuckle mnemonic" or the rhyme "Thirty days hath September..." to remember the number of days in each month.
• **Date:** A specific day of the month, usually expressed as Day/Month/Year (e.g., 15/08/2024).
• **Format of Dates:** Dates can be written in various formats, such as DD/MM/YYYY or Month Day, Year (e.g., August 15, 2024).
• **Chronology:** The arrangement of events or dates in the order of their occurrence.
• **Calendar Navigation:** The ability to look at a calendar to identify which day of the week a specific date falls on.
• **Finding the Day:** To find the day of a future date, count the number of days from the current date and cycle through the days of the week.
• **Week Calculation:** Since there are 7 days in a week, any date that is a multiple of 7 days away will fall on the same day of the week.
• **Month Calculation:** Adding or subtracting months requires accounting for the varying number of days in each month.
• **Year Calculation:** Adding or subtracting years requires checking for leap years to determine if the total day count changes.
• **Historical Dates:** Dates used to record significant events, birthdays, or anniversaries in a structured format.
• **Calendar Grid:** The visual layout of a calendar where rows represent weeks and columns represent days of the week.
• **Public Holidays:** Specific dates marked on the calendar that are observed as non-working or non-school days.
• **Seasonal Cycles:** The relationship between the calendar months and the changing seasons of the year.

• **Time Conversion:** The mathematical process of changing units of time, such as converting hours to minutes or minutes to seconds.
• **Conversion Factor (Hours to Minutes):** Multiply the number of hours by 60 to get the total minutes.
• **Conversion Factor (Minutes to Seconds):** Multiply the number of minutes by 60 to get the total seconds.
• **Conversion Factor (Minutes to Hours):** Divide the total number of minutes by 60 to get the hours, with the remainder being the extra minutes.
• **Conversion Factor (Seconds to Minutes):** Divide the total number of seconds by 60 to get the minutes, with the remainder being the extra seconds.
• **Mixed Units:** Time is often expressed in mixed units, such as "2 hours and 15 minutes."
• **Standardizing Time:** Converting mixed units into a single unit (e.g., all minutes) is essential for performing addition or subtraction of time durations.
• **Time Addition Logic:** When adding time, add the minutes first; if the sum is 60 or more, convert the excess into hours and add to the hour column.
• **Time Subtraction Logic:** When subtracting time, subtract the minutes first; if the end minutes are smaller, borrow 1 hour (60 minutes) from the hour column.
• **Clock Face Geometry:** The clock face is a circle of 360 degrees, with each hour representing 30 degrees (360/12).
• **Minute Mark Geometry:** Each minute mark represents 6 degrees (360/60) of the circle.
• **Time Estimation:** The skill of approximating the time based on the position of the hands without reading the exact minute.
• **Time Management:** The process of planning and exercising conscious control over the amount of time spent on specific activities.
• **Schedule:** A plan for carrying out a process or procedure, giving lists of intended events and times.
• **Time-Table:** A chart showing the departure and arrival times of trains, buses, or school lessons.
• **Punctuality:** The habit of being on time, facilitated by accurate reading and understanding of time concepts.

• **Leap Year Identification:** To check if a year is a leap year, divide the year by 4. If the remainder is 0, it is a leap year.
• **Century Leap Year Exception:** Years ending in 00 (like 1900 or 2000) are only leap years if they are divisible by 400.
• **1900 Analysis:** 1900 divided by 400 leaves a remainder, so 1900 was not a leap year.
• **2000 Analysis:** 2000 divided by 400 has no remainder, so 2000 was a leap year.
• **February's Role:** February is the only month that changes its number of days based on the leap year cycle.
• **Days in a Year:** A common year has 52 weeks and 1 day (365 = 7 * 52 + 1).
• **Days in a Leap Year:** A leap year has 52 weeks and 2 days (366 = 7 * 52 + 2).
• **Calendar Consistency:** The calendar repeats its day-date pattern every 28 years in the Gregorian system.
• **Month-to-Day Conversion:** To find the total days in a given number of months, multiply by the average days or sum the specific days of the months involved.
• **Day-to-Week Conversion:** Divide the number of days by 7 to determine the number of full weeks and the remaining days.
• **Calendar Reading:** Identifying the first day of the month is crucial for determining the day of the week for any subsequent date in that month.
• **Weekend:** The two days of the week, Saturday and Sunday, typically considered the end of the work or school week.
• **Weekday:** Any day from Monday to Friday, typically associated with work or school activities.
• **Date Notation:** Standardizing the way dates are written helps avoid confusion between different regions (e.g., US vs. International formats).
• **Time Zones:** While not in the primary curriculum, it is helpful to know that time varies across the globe based on longitude.
• **Chronological Order:** Organizing dates from the earliest to the latest is a fundamental skill in historical and personal record-keeping.

• **Analog Reading Practice:** When the minute hand is between numbers, count the individual minute marks starting from 12 to get the precise time.
• **Hour Hand Movement:** The hour hand moves slowly as the minute hand moves; it is only exactly on a number when the minute hand is at 12.
• **Half-Past Logic:** At 30 minutes, the hour hand is exactly halfway between the current hour and the next hour.
• **Quarter-Past Logic:** At 15 minutes, the hour hand has moved slightly past the current hour mark.
• **Quarter-To Logic:** At 45 minutes, the hour hand is three-quarters of the way toward the next hour mark.
• **Digital Time Format:** Digital clocks use a colon (:) to separate the hours from the minutes.
• **12:00 P.M. vs 12:00 A.M.:** 12:00 P.M. is noon, and 12:00 A.M. is midnight.
• **Duration Calculation Example:** If a movie starts at 2:30 P.M. and ends at 4:15 P.M., the duration is 1 hour and 45 minutes.
• **Subtraction Method for Duration:** 4:15 - 2:30. Since 15 < 30, borrow 60 minutes from 4 hours. 4 becomes 3, and 15 becomes 75. 75 - 30 = 45 minutes. 3 - 2 = 1 hour. Result: 1 hour 45 minutes.
• **Time Addition Example:** If a task takes 45 minutes and starts at 10:20 A.M., the end time is 11:05 A.M. (20 + 45 = 65, which is 1 hour and 5 minutes).
• **Calendar Utility:** Calendars help in tracking long-term goals, school terms, and seasonal changes.
• **Leap Year Impact:** Birthdays falling on February 29th are only celebrated on their actual date once every four years.
• **Month Sequence:** Memorizing the order of months is essential for navigating the calendar efficiently.
• **Days in Months Mnemonic:** "Thirty days hath September, April, June, and November. All the rest have thirty-one, except for February alone."
• **February Exception:** February has 28 days, and 29 in a leap year.
• **Calendar Accuracy:** The Gregorian calendar is the most widely used civil calendar in the world today.
