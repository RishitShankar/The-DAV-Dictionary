• **Internet**: The global network of interconnected computers that allows for the exchange of information across the world.
• **World Wide Web (WWW)**: A massive collection of interlinked documents and resources accessed via the Internet.
• **Information Pool**: The Internet acts as a vast repository of knowledge, functioning like a digital library accessible from anywhere.
• **Connectivity**: The ability of computers to link together using cables, satellites, or wireless signals to form the Internet.
• **Web Browser**: A software application used to access and view websites, such as Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Microsoft Edge.
• **Website**: A collection of related web pages identified by a common domain name and published on at least one web server.
• **Web Page**: A single document on the World Wide Web, typically written in HTML, that can contain text, images, and links.
• **Home Page**: The main or introductory page of a website, which serves as the starting point for navigation.
• **Uniform Resource Locator (URL)**: The unique address of a web page on the Internet, such as www.google.com.
• **Hyperlink**: A clickable element (text or image) on a web page that directs the user to another page or section.
• **Search Engine**: A specialized website designed to help users find information on the Internet by searching for keywords.
• **Keyword**: A specific word or phrase entered into a search engine to find relevant information.
• **Server**: A powerful computer that stores website files and delivers them to users' computers upon request.
• **Client**: The computer or device used by the user to request and view information from a server.
• **ISP (Internet Service Provider)**: A company that provides individuals and organizations access to the Internet.
• **Data Transfer**: The process of moving information from one computer to another over the Internet.
• **Digital Literacy**: The ability to find, evaluate, and communicate information through various digital platforms.

• **Search Engine Mechanism**: Search engines use automated programs called 'spiders' or 'crawlers' to scan the web for information.
• **Indexing**: The process by which search engines organize the data they collect so it can be retrieved quickly.
• **Query**: The specific request or question a user types into the search bar of a search engine.
• **Search Results Page**: The page displayed by a search engine after a user submits a query, listing relevant websites.
• **Google**: The most widely used search engine, known for its fast and accurate retrieval of information.
• **Bing**: A search engine developed by Microsoft that provides search results with visual previews.
• **Educational Websites**: Online platforms dedicated to learning, such as Wikipedia, Khan Academy, or Britannica Kids.
• **Online Encyclopedias**: Digital resources that provide comprehensive information on a wide variety of topics.
• **Reliability of Information**: The importance of checking if the information found on the Internet is accurate and from a trusted source.
• **Fact-Checking**: The practice of verifying information by comparing it with multiple reputable sources.
• **Authoritative Sources**: Websites managed by educational institutions, government bodies, or well-known organizations.
• **Domain Extensions**: Suffixes at the end of a URL that indicate the type of website, such as .edu for education or .gov for government.
• **Educational Value**: The Internet provides students with instant access to historical facts, scientific discoveries, and cultural information.
• **Multimedia Content**: The Internet offers information in various formats, including text, images, audio, and video.
• **Interactive Learning**: Websites that allow students to engage with content through quizzes, simulations, and games.
• **Global Reach**: The Internet removes geographical barriers, allowing students to access information from any country.
• **Constant Updates**: Unlike printed books, online information can be updated in real-time to reflect the latest discoveries.

• **Web Browser Interface**: The layout of a browser includes the address bar, back/forward buttons, refresh button, and home button.
• **Address Bar**: The specific area at the top of the browser where the URL of a website is typed.
• **Navigation Buttons**: Tools that allow users to move between previously visited pages (Back and Forward).
• **Refresh/Reload Button**: A tool used to update the content of the current web page to show the most recent version.
• **Stop Button**: A feature that halts the loading process of a web page if it is taking too long or is no longer needed.
• **Bookmarks/Favorites**: A feature that allows users to save the addresses of frequently visited websites for quick access.
• **History**: A log maintained by the browser that records all the websites visited by the user.
• **Tabs**: A feature that allows users to open multiple web pages within a single browser window.
• **Downloads**: The process of saving a file, image, or document from the Internet onto a local computer.
• **Uploads**: The process of sending a file or document from a local computer to a server or website.
• **Browser Settings**: Options that allow users to customize their browsing experience, such as privacy and security settings.
• **Incognito/Private Mode**: A browsing mode that does not save the user's history, cookies, or site data.
• **Cache**: Temporary storage on a computer that saves parts of websites to make them load faster on subsequent visits.
• **Cookies**: Small text files stored on a user's computer by websites to remember preferences and login status.
• **Pop-ups**: Small windows that appear automatically on a screen, often used for advertisements or notifications.
• **Ad-blockers**: Tools or extensions that prevent advertisements from appearing on web pages.
• **Browser Extensions**: Small software programs that add extra functionality to a web browser.

• **Safety on the Internet**: The practice of protecting oneself from online threats, scams, and inappropriate content.
• **Cyberbullying**: The use of digital communication to harass, threaten, or intimidate others.
• **Personal Information Protection**: Never sharing sensitive details like home addresses, phone numbers, or passwords online.
• **Strong Passwords**: Using a combination of letters, numbers, and symbols to secure online accounts.
• **Privacy Settings**: Configuring social media or website settings to control who can see personal information.
• **Stranger Danger**: The rule of not interacting with or accepting requests from unknown people online.
• **Reporting Inappropriate Content**: Using the 'Report' or 'Block' features on websites to handle harmful behavior.
• **Antivirus Software**: Programs designed to detect and remove malicious software (malware) from a computer.
• **Phishing**: A fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive information by disguising as a trustworthy entity in an email or message.
• **Spam**: Unsolicited and often irrelevant messages sent to a large number of users.
• **Secure Websites (HTTPS)**: Websites that use encryption to protect data; identified by a padlock icon in the address bar.
• **Parental Supervision**: The importance of using the Internet under the guidance of parents or teachers.
• **Digital Footprint**: The trail of data left behind by a user's activities on the Internet.
• **Copyright**: The legal right to control the use of original creative work, such as images, text, or music found online.
• **Plagiarism**: The act of using someone else's work or ideas without giving them proper credit.
• **Citing Sources**: The practice of acknowledging the author or website from which information was gathered.
• **Netiquette**: The code of polite and respectful behavior while communicating online.

• **Educational Databases**: Collections of scholarly articles and research papers accessible through the Internet.
• **Virtual Tours**: Online experiences that allow users to explore museums, historical sites, and zoos from home.
• **Online Libraries**: Digital archives that provide access to books, journals, and historical documents.
• **E-books**: Electronic versions of books that can be read on computers, tablets, or e-readers.
• **Educational Games**: Interactive websites that make learning subjects like Math and Science fun and engaging.
• **Language Learning Sites**: Platforms that help students learn new languages through interactive lessons.

• **Email (Electronic Mail)**: A method of exchanging digital messages between people using electronic devices.
• **Video Conferencing**: Technology that allows people in different locations to see and hear each other in real-time.

• **Hardware Requirements**: To access the Internet, one needs a computer, a modem, and a network connection.
• **Modem**: A device that connects a computer to the Internet by converting digital signals to analog and vice versa.
• **Router**: A device that directs data traffic between different networks, often used to create Wi-Fi zones.
• **Wi-Fi**: A wireless networking technology that allows devices to connect to the Internet without cables.
• **Bandwidth**: The maximum amount of data that can be transmitted over an Internet connection in a given time.
• **Broadband**: A high-speed Internet connection that is always on and faster than traditional dial-up.
• **Fiber Optics**: A technology that uses light to transmit data at very high speeds over long distances.
• **Satellite Internet**: A method of accessing the Internet via satellites orbiting the Earth, useful in remote areas.
• **Mobile Data**: Internet access provided through cellular networks, allowing for connectivity on smartphones and tablets.
• **Network**: A group of two or more computer systems linked together.
• **LAN (Local Area Network)**: A network that connects computers within a limited area like a home or school.
• **WAN (Wide Area Network)**: A network that covers a broad area, such as a city, country, or the entire world (the Internet is a WAN).
• **IP Address**: A unique string of numbers assigned to every device connected to the Internet, acting as its digital address.
• **DNS (Domain Name System)**: A system that translates human-readable domain names (like google.com) into IP addresses.
• **Protocol**: A set of rules that govern how data is transmitted over the Internet (e.g., HTTP, TCP/IP).
• **HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol)**: The foundation of data communication for the World Wide Web.
• **HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure)**: The secure version of HTTP that encrypts data for safety.

• **History of the Internet**: The Internet began as a project called ARPANET in the 1960s, funded by the US Department of Defense.
• **Tim Berners-Lee**: The scientist who invented the World Wide Web in 1989, making the Internet accessible to everyone.
• **Evolution of Browsers**: Early browsers like Mosaic and Netscape paved the way for modern browsers like Chrome and Safari.
• **Search Engine Evolution**: Early search engines like AltaVista and Yahoo! were replaced by the more efficient Google algorithm.
• **The Growth of the Web**: The transition from Web 1.0 (read-only) to Web 2.0 (interactive and social) changed how we use the Internet.
• **Internet of Things (IoT)**: The network of physical objects embedded with sensors and software to exchange data.
• **Future of the Internet**: Trends like Artificial Intelligence (AI) and faster connectivity (5G/6G) are shaping the future of information access.
• **Artificial Intelligence in Search**: AI helps search engines understand the intent behind a user's query for better results.
• **Virtual Reality (VR)**: Using the Internet to create immersive, 3D educational environments.
• **Augmented Reality (AR)**: Overlaying digital information onto the real world through devices like smartphones.
• **Big Data**: The massive volume of information generated by Internet users, which is analyzed to improve services.
• **Global Connectivity**: Efforts to provide Internet access to every corner of the globe to bridge the digital divide.
• **Sustainability**: The impact of the Internet and data centers on energy consumption and the environment.
• **Cybersecurity**: The ongoing effort to protect networks, devices, and data from unauthorized access or criminal activity.
• **Encryption**: The process of encoding information so that only authorized parties can access it.
• **Digital Infrastructure**: The physical and logical components (cables, servers, protocols) that support the Internet.
• **Information Accessibility**: The goal of making the Internet usable for people with disabilities through assistive technologies.
