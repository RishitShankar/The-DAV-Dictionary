• **Radio Broadcasting:** A method of transmitting information, such as news or music, over long distances using electromagnetic waves.
• **Akashvani (All India Radio):** The national public radio broadcaster of India, which played a pivotal role in the country's post-independence era.
• **Transistor Radio:** A small, portable radio receiver that uses transistor-based circuitry, which revolutionized the accessibility of radio in the 1960s.
• **Vacuum Tubes:** The older, larger components used in early radios before the invention of the transistor, known for their warm, glowing light.
• **Antenna:** A metallic device used to intercept radio waves from the atmosphere and convert them into electrical signals for the speaker.
• **Tuning Dial:** A circular or linear control used to select specific frequencies or stations by adjusting the internal circuitry of the radio.
• **Signal Strength:** The power of the radio wave received; stronger signals result in clearer audio, while weak signals lead to static.
• **Frequency:** The rate at which radio waves oscillate; different stations operate on different frequencies to avoid overlapping.
• **Broadcasting Station:** A facility that transmits radio signals to a wide audience; these stations are the source of the content heard on the radio.
• **Radio Jockey (RJ):** A person who hosts a radio program, providing commentary, playing music, and interacting with the audience.
• **Live Broadcast:** Content that is transmitted in real-time, allowing listeners to feel connected to the events as they happen.
• **News Bulletins:** Scheduled segments of the day dedicated to providing updates on national and international events.
• **Radio Drama:** A theatrical performance that relies solely on sound effects, voice acting, and music to tell a story without visual elements.
• **Sound Effects (Foley):** The reproduction of everyday sound effects that are added to films, videos, and radio plays to enhance audio quality.
• **Nostalgia:** A sentimental longing for the past, which is the primary emotional driver of the grandmother’s character in the chapter.
• **Analog Technology:** A system that uses continuous signals to represent physical measurements, characteristic of traditional radio sets.

• **Radio Waves:** A type of electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths in the electromagnetic spectrum longer than infrared light.
• **Electromagnetic Spectrum:** The range of frequencies of electromagnetic radiation and their respective wavelengths and photon energies.
• **Modulation:** The process of varying one or more properties of a periodic waveform, called the carrier signal, with a modulating signal that contains information.
• **Amplitude Modulation (AM):** A modulation technique used in electronic communication, most commonly for transmitting information via a radio carrier wave.
• **Frequency Modulation (FM):** A form of modulation that conveys information over a carrier wave by varying its instantaneous frequency.
• **Receiver:** An electronic device that receives radio waves and converts the information carried by them to a usable form.
• **Transmitter:** An electronic device which, with the aid of an antenna, propagates an electromagnetic signal such as radio, television, or other telecommunications.
• **Speaker:** An electroacoustic transducer that converts an electrical audio signal into a corresponding sound.
• **Volume Control:** A potentiometer or variable resistor used to adjust the loudness of the sound output from the radio.
• **Band Selection:** A switch on the radio that allows the user to choose between different frequency bands, such as Shortwave (SW) or Medium Wave (MW).
• **Interference:** The disturbance of a signal by other signals or noise, which can cause static or distortion in the radio output.
• **Signal Propagation:** The way radio waves travel from the transmitter to the receiver, which can be affected by terrain and weather.
• **Ionosphere:** A layer of the Earth's atmosphere that reflects radio waves, enabling long-distance communication.
• **Digital Radio:** A modern method of transmitting and receiving radio programs using digital signals rather than analog ones.
• **Streaming:** The modern equivalent of radio, where audio content is delivered over the internet, bypassing traditional radio waves.
• **Technological Shift:** The transition from analog to digital represents a shift from physical, localized hardware to virtual, global software.

• **Frequency (Definition):** The number of occurrences of a repeating event per unit of time; measured in Hertz (Hz).
• **Amplitude (Definition):** The maximum extent of a vibration or oscillation, measured from the position of equilibrium.
• **Signal (Definition):** A physical quantity that varies with time, space, or any other independent variable and conveys information.
• **Noise (Definition):** Unwanted fluctuations in an electrical signal, which can obscure the desired information.
• **Antenna (Definition):** A transducer designed to transmit or receive electromagnetic waves.
• **Tuning (Definition):** The process of adjusting a radio receiver to select a specific frequency.
• **Modulation (Definition):** The process of encoding information from a message source into a carrier wave.
• **Demodulation (Definition):** The process of extracting the original information-bearing signal from a modulated carrier wave.
• **Broadcast Station (Definition):** A station that transmits radio or television signals.
• **Receiver (Definition):** An electronic device that receives radio waves and converts them into sound.
• **Transmitter (Definition):** An electronic device that generates and transmits radio waves.
• **Media (Definition):** The main means of mass communication (broadcasting, publishing, and the internet).
