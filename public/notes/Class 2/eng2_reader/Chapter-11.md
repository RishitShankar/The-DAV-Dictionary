• **Chapter Overview**: "Where is the Pink Whale?" is a narrative-based lesson designed for Class 2 students to develop reading comprehension, vocabulary, and spatial awareness.
• **Core Theme**: The story revolves around a search mission for a unique creature, the Pink Whale, emphasizing curiosity and exploration.
• **Setting the Scene**: The narrative begins in an underwater environment, introducing the vastness and mystery of the ocean.
• **The Protagonist**: The Pink Whale is the central character, serving as the object of the search and the focal point of the plot.
• **Concept of Color**: The story highlights the color "Pink," which is unusual for a whale, sparking interest and engagement among young readers.
• **Spatial Prepositions**: The chapter serves as a pedagogical tool to teach prepositions of place such as "in," "on," "under," "behind," and "near."
• **Vocabulary Building**: Key words introduced include 'ocean,' 'deep,' 'search,' 'hidden,' 'waves,' and 'creature.'
• **Narrative Flow**: The story follows a sequential search pattern, moving from one location to another to locate the whale.
• **Character Interaction**: Various sea creatures are introduced as guides or obstacles, helping the reader understand the diversity of marine life.
• **Descriptive Language**: The text uses adjectives to describe the whale's appearance, such as 'bright,' 'big,' and 'gentle.'
• **Educational Objective**: The primary goal is to improve the student's ability to locate information within a text.
• **Visual Literacy**: The chapter encourages students to correlate textual descriptions with visual illustrations provided in the textbook.
• **Phonetic Focus**: The chapter reinforces specific vowel sounds and consonant blends common in early primary English.
• **Sentence Structure**: The text utilizes simple, declarative sentences to help students grasp basic grammatical structures.
• **Tone of the Story**: The tone is lighthearted and adventurous, suitable for the developmental stage of Class 2 students.

• **Sentence Pattern 3**: "Is the Pink Whale under the seaweed?" - Yes/No question structure.
• **Sentence Pattern 4**: "No, it is not there." - Negative response structure.
• **Sentence Pattern 5**: "Yes, I found it!" - Affirmative response structure.
• **Sentence Pattern 6**: "Look at the big blue ocean." - Imperative sentence structure.
• **Sentence Pattern 7**: "The whale swims fast." - Subject + Verb + Adverb.
• **Sentence Pattern 8**: "It is a beautiful creature." - Subject + Linking Verb + Complement.
• **Sentence Pattern 9**: "The fish are swimming near the coral." - Plural subject and verb agreement.
• **Sentence Pattern 10**: "Where can we find the whale?" - Modal verb usage.
• **Sentence Pattern 11**: "Let us look over there." - Suggestive sentence structure.
• **Sentence Pattern 12**: "The whale is hiding." - Present continuous tense.
• **Sentence Pattern 13**: "It likes to play." - Simple present tense.
• **Sentence Pattern 14**: "We are happy to see it." - Expressing emotion.
• **Sentence Pattern 15**: "The search is over." - Concluding statement.
