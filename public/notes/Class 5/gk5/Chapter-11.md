• **Birsa Munda**: A legendary Indian tribal freedom fighter, folk hero, and a prominent figure in the history of the Indian independence movement.
• **Birth Details**: Born on November 15, 1875, in Ulihatu, a village in the present-day Khunti district of Jharkhand.
• **Tribal Identity**: He belonged to the **Munda tribe**, an indigenous Adivasi community residing in the Chota Nagpur Plateau region.
• **Early Life**: His childhood was spent in poverty, often moving between villages like Chalkad and Murhu due to the displacement caused by colonial land policies.
• **Education**: He received his early education at a German Mission school in Chaibasa, where he was exposed to Western education and Christianity.
• **Conversion**: During his youth, he converted to Christianity and was renamed 'Birsa David', but he later reverted to his traditional tribal beliefs.
• **The Great Uprising**: Birsa Munda is best remembered for leading the **Ulgulan** (The Great Tumult), a massive tribal movement against British colonial rule.
• **Land Rights**: The core of his struggle was the **Khuntkatti system**, a traditional tribal land-tenure system that was being destroyed by British landlords and zamindars.
• **Exploitation**: He witnessed the systematic exploitation of his people by the 'Dikus' (outsiders), which included British officials, moneylenders, and landlords.
• **Spiritual Leadership**: He emerged as a prophet-like figure among the Mundas, claiming to have received divine visions to liberate his people.
• **Birsait Sect**: He founded a new faith known as **Birsait**, which emphasized monotheism, social reform, and the rejection of superstition.
• **Social Reforms**: He urged his followers to stop animal sacrifice, abstain from alcohol, and wear the sacred thread to reclaim their dignity.
• **Cultural Pride**: He advocated for the revival of tribal culture and identity, encouraging the Munda people to take pride in their heritage.
• **Resistance Strategy**: His movement was not just political but also a socio-religious awakening aimed at restoring the 'Golden Age' of the Mundas.
• **Guerrilla Warfare**: Birsa Munda organized a small army of tribal warriors who utilized guerrilla tactics to attack British police stations and government offices.

• **The Ulgulan Movement**: This was a militant movement that challenged the British administration's authority in the Chota Nagpur region.
• **Targeting the Dikus**: The movement specifically targeted the 'Dikus' who were responsible for the alienation of tribal land and the imposition of heavy taxes.
• **British Response**: The British colonial government perceived Birsa Munda as a major threat to their administrative control and launched a massive manhunt.
• **The Battle of Sail Rakab**: A significant confrontation where Birsa Munda’s forces fought against the British army on the hills of Sail Rakab.
• **Arrest and Imprisonment**: Birsa Munda was arrested multiple times by the British authorities due to his growing influence and leadership in the rebellion.
• **First Imprisonment**: He was first arrested in 1895 and sentenced to two years of imprisonment in Hazaribagh jail for his revolutionary activities.
• **Release and Reorganization**: After his release in 1897, he returned to his people and reorganized his followers to continue the struggle for 'Abua Raj' (Our Rule).
• **Abua Raj**: This was the political vision of Birsa Munda, which aimed at establishing an independent tribal state free from British interference.
• **The Concept of Sovereignty**: He declared that the land belonged to the Munda people and that they should not pay taxes to the British government.
• **Public Support**: His charisma and message of liberation attracted thousands of tribal people from the Munda and Oraon communities.
• **Intelligence Network**: He maintained a highly efficient network of messengers and scouts who kept him informed about British troop movements.
• **British Military Might**: The British deployed the military to suppress the rebellion, leading to the burning of many Munda villages and the displacement of thousands.
• **The Final Uprising**: In 1899, he launched a final, large-scale armed rebellion against the British, which was met with brutal force.
• **Betrayal**: The movement suffered a setback when some local informants betrayed his location to the British authorities for a bounty.
• **Final Arrest**: He was captured by the British in the Jamkopai forest in Chakradharpur on February 3, 1900.

• **Imprisonment in Ranchi**: Following his final arrest, he was taken to the Ranchi jail, where he was kept in solitary confinement.
• **Health Decline**: During his imprisonment, his health deteriorated rapidly due to the harsh conditions of the jail and potential poisoning.
• **Martyrdom**: Birsa Munda passed away in the Ranchi jail on June 9, 1900, at the young age of 25.
• **Cause of Death**: While the official British records stated cholera as the cause of death, many historians believe he was poisoned by the colonial authorities.
• **Legacy of Sacrifice**: His death did not end the movement; instead, it turned him into a martyr and a symbol of resistance for generations to come.
• **Impact on British Policy**: The rebellion forced the British to pass the **Chota Nagpur Tenancy Act (1908)**, which restricted the transfer of tribal land to non-tribals.
• **Historical Significance**: He is one of the few tribal leaders whose portrait hangs in the Central Hall of the Indian Parliament.
• **Dharti Aaba**: He is affectionately known as 'Dharti Aaba' (Father of the Earth) by the tribal communities of Jharkhand.
• **Symbol of Adivasi Pride**: His life is celebrated annually on his birth anniversary, which is now observed as **Janjatiya Gaurav Divas** in India.
• **Literary Inspiration**: Numerous books, plays, and songs have been written about his life, highlighting his bravery and vision for a free society.
• **Statues and Memorials**: Several statues of Birsa Munda have been erected across India, including a prominent one at the Parliament House complex.
• **Birsa Munda Airport**: The international airport in Ranchi, Jharkhand, is named after him to honor his contribution to the nation.
• **Educational Institutions**: Many schools and colleges in Jharkhand and neighboring states are named after him to inspire the youth.
• **Folk Hero Status**: He remains a central figure in the oral traditions and folklore of the Munda and other tribal communities.
• **Inspiration for Future Movements**: His struggle paved the way for future tribal movements in India, emphasizing the importance of land rights and self-governance.
