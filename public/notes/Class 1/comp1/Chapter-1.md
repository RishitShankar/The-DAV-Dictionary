•   A computer is made up of several different parts that all work together.
•   Each part of the computer has a special and important job to do.
•   The four main parts of a computer are the **Monitor, CPU, Keyboard, and Mouse**.
•   These main parts are connected to each other, usually by wires, to function as one system.
•   Without all the main parts working together, a computer cannot perform its tasks properly.
•   **Hardware:** Refers to all the physical parts of a computer that we can see and touch.
•   The monitor, CPU, keyboard, and mouse are all examples of computer hardware.
•   These parts are like the body of the computer, allowing us to interact with it.
•   The parts help us to give instructions to the computer, which is called **input**.
•   They also help the computer to show us the results of its work, which is called **output**.
•   The CPU is often considered the "brain" of the computer because it processes information.
•   All the main parts are essential for the computer to start up and operate.
•   They are usually placed on a desk or table for easy access and use.
•   Learning about each part helps us understand how the computer functions as a whole.
•   Some parts are for putting information in, some for processing it, and some for getting information out.
•   These parts are designed to be user-friendly, even for young children.
•   The way these parts connect and communicate allows the computer to perform complex tasks.
•   It's important to know the name and function of each main part.
•   These components form the basic setup of a personal computer.
•   Understanding the main parts is the first step to learning how to use a computer effectively.

•   The **Monitor** is a very important part of the computer that looks like a television screen.
•   It is an **output device**, meaning it shows us the results of the computer's work.
•   Everything we do on the computer, like typing, drawing, or playing games, is displayed on the monitor.
•   We can see pictures, videos, text, and all the computer's activities on its screen.
•   When you type something on the keyboard, you see the letters appear on the monitor.
•   When you move the mouse, you see the pointer moving on the monitor.
•   The monitor helps us to visually interact with the computer.
•   It has a screen where images and information are projected.
•   Monitors come in different sizes, and some are flat, known as LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) or LED (Light Emitting Diode) monitors.
•   It is important to sit at a proper distance from the monitor to protect your eyes.
•   The monitor is also sometimes called a **Visual Display Unit (VDU)**.
•   Without a monitor, we would not be able to see what the computer is doing or show us.
•   It is connected to the CPU, which sends the information to be displayed.
•   The quality of the monitor affects how clear and colorful the images appear.
•   It is one of the primary ways the computer communicates information back to the user.
•   Always keep the monitor screen clean with a soft cloth to ensure clear viewing.
•   Do not touch the monitor screen with your fingers or sharp objects, as it can damage it.
•   The monitor allows us to see the graphical user interface (GUI) of the computer.
•   It is essential for both work and entertainment purposes on a computer.
•   Modern monitors can display millions of colors, making images look very realistic.

•   The **CPU** stands for **Central Processing Unit**, and it is often called the "brain" of the computer.
•   It is usually a box-like structure that sits on or under the desk.
•   The CPU is responsible for doing all the thinking, calculations, and processing inside the computer.
•   It takes all the instructions we give to the computer and processes them very quickly.
•   All other parts of the computer, like the monitor, keyboard, and mouse, are connected to the CPU.
•   The CPU helps the computer to remember things and store information temporarily.
•   It controls all the operations and functions of the other computer parts.
•   Without the CPU, the computer cannot work at all; it is the most vital component.
•   The CPU cabinet (the box) contains the actual CPU chip along with other important internal components.
•   It makes the computer smart and allows it to perform complex tasks.
•   When you click on an icon or type a command, the CPU processes that action.
•   The speed of a computer largely depends on how fast its CPU can process information.
•   It is where all the "thinking" and "decision-making" of the computer happen.
•   The CPU receives input, processes it according to instructions, and then sends out the results.
•   It is like the control center for the entire computer system.
•   The CPU helps in running all the software programs and applications.
•   It manages the flow of data between different parts of the computer.
•   The CPU is a complex microchip, but for Class 1, it's understood as the "brain box."
•   It ensures that all the computer's tasks are carried out in the correct order.
•   Proper cooling is important for the CPU to prevent overheating and ensure smooth operation.

•   The **Keyboard** is an important **input device** used for typing letters, numbers, and symbols into the computer.
•   It has many small buttons called **keys**, arranged in a specific order.
•   When we press a key, the corresponding character appears on the monitor screen.
•   We use the keyboard to give instructions and information to the computer.
•   **Alphabet keys (A-Z):** Used for typing words, sentences, and names.
•   **Number keys (0-9):** Used for typing numbers, like phone numbers or ages.
•   **Spacebar key:** The longest key on the keyboard, used to put a space between words.
•   **Enter key:** Used to move the cursor to the next line or to confirm a command.
•   **Arrow keys:** Used to move the cursor up, down, left, or right on the screen.
•   There are also special keys like Shift, Ctrl, Alt, and Delete, which perform specific functions.
•   The keyboard helps us to write stories, poems, emails, and do our homework on the computer.
•   It is essential for any task that involves typing text or entering numerical data.
•   Learning to type correctly on the keyboard is called **touch typing**.
•   Always press the keys gently; do not hit them hard.
•   Keep the keyboard clean by gently wiping it with a soft cloth to remove dust.
•   Do not spill any liquids on the keyboard, as it can damage the internal circuits.
•   The layout of the alphabet keys is commonly known as **QWERTY**.
•   It allows for efficient and precise input of textual data.
•   The keyboard is a fundamental tool for interacting with the computer's software.
•   It is connected to the CPU, sending signals for every key press.

---PAGE-BREAK---

•   The **Mouse** is a small pointing device that helps us control the computer.
•   It is an **input device** because it sends instructions to the computer.
•   It often looks like a real mouse with a "tail" (wire) connected to the computer.
•   A mouse usually has two or three buttons and a small wheel in the middle called a **scroll wheel**.
•   We move the mouse on a flat surface, usually a **mouse pad**, to make it work smoothly.
•   As we move the mouse, a small arrow or pointer moves on the monitor screen.
•   We use the mouse to select items, open programs, and draw pictures on the computer.
•   **Clicking:** Pressing the left mouse button once quickly to select an item.
•   **Double-clicking:** Pressing the left mouse button twice quickly to open a program or file.
•   **Right-clicking:** Pressing the right mouse button once to see a list of options.
•   **Dragging:** Holding down the left mouse button and moving the mouse to move an item on the screen.
•   The scroll wheel helps us to move pages up and down on the screen without using arrow keys.
•   It makes playing games and interacting with graphical elements on the computer much easier.
•   There are different types of mice, including wired mice and wireless mice.
•   Always hold the mouse gently and move it smoothly on the mouse pad.
•   The mouse is very useful for drawing shapes and coloring pictures in drawing software.
•   It provides a quick and intuitive way to navigate the computer's interface.
•   The mouse pointer changes shape depending on what it is pointing at or doing.
•   It is an essential tool for modern graphical operating systems.
•   Using a mouse helps improve hand-eye coordination.

•   Besides the four main parts, there are several other useful parts that can be connected to a computer.
•   These additional parts are called **peripheral devices** and make the computer even more versatile.
•   **Printer:** A device used to print text and pictures from the computer onto paper.
•   It produces a **hard copy** (a physical printout) of what we see on the monitor.
•   **Scanner:** A device that helps us put pictures or documents from paper into the computer's memory.
•   It is like a digital photocopier, but it creates a digital image inside the computer.
•   **Speakers:** Devices used to listen to music, sounds, and voices that come from the computer.
•   **Headphones:** Similar to speakers, but they are worn on the head, allowing only one person to listen privately.
•   **Microphone:** An input device used to record our voice or other sounds into the computer.
•   **Webcam:** A small camera connected to the computer, used to take pictures or make videos of ourselves.
•   Webcams are often used for video calls, allowing us to see and talk to people far away.
•   **Joystick:** A special input device primarily used for playing computer games, especially racing or flying games.
•   **Pen Drive (USB Flash Drive):** A small device used to store and carry data (files, photos, videos) from one computer to another.
•   These peripheral devices are connected to the CPU, usually through USB ports.
•   They extend the capabilities of the computer, allowing it to perform more specialized tasks.
•   Each peripheral device serves a unique purpose, enhancing the computer's functionality.
•   Understanding these parts helps us use the computer for a wider range of activities.
•   They are not essential for the computer to function, but they add great value.
•   Many modern computers, especially laptops, have some of these peripherals built-in (like webcam, microphone, speakers).
•   These devices make the computer a more complete and powerful tool for various needs.
•   They allow for diverse forms of input and output beyond the basic monitor and keyboard.

•   We use **input devices** like the keyboard and mouse to provide this information.
