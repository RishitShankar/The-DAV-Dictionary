• **Primary Grammar Topic**: The central grammatical focus of this chapter is **क्रिया (Verb)** and its various sub-types (**भेद**).
• **Academic Objective**: To enable Class 7 students to identify, classify, and use verbs correctly in sentences according to DAV board standards.
• **Definition of Verb**: **क्रिया (Kriya)** is defined as any word that denotes an action, the doing or happening of something, or a state of being.
• **Significance in Sentences**: Without a verb, no sentence can be complete, as it forms the predicate and drives the action of the subject (**कर्ता**).
• **Root Form (धातु)**: The fundamental building block of any verb in Hindi is called **धातु (Dhatu)**, such as 'पढ़', 'लिख', 'चल', and 'हँस'.
• **Formation of Verbs**: Verbs are formed by adding the suffix 'ना' to the root dhatu (e.g., धातु 'पढ़' + 'ना' = क्रिया 'पढ़ना').
• **Role of Subject**: The form of the verb often changes based on the gender, number, and person of the subject or object in the sentence.
• **Classification Base**: Verbs in Hindi are broadly classified into primary categories based on the presence and requirement of an object (**कर्म**).
• **Introduction to Transitive Verbs**: **सकर्मक क्रिया (Transitive Verb)** is a verb that requires an object to complete its meaning and action.
• **Introduction to Intransitive Verbs**: **अकर्मक क्रिया (Intransitive Verb)** is a verb that does not require an object; the action is confined to the subject itself.
• **Story Connection**: In "दादी का रेडियो", actions performed by the grandmother, such as tuning the radio or listening, provide practical examples of verbs.
• **Grammatical Identification**: Students learn to test whether a verb is transitive or intransitive by asking the questions 'क्या' (what) or किसे (whom).

• **Detailed Definition of Kriya**: A **क्रिया** is a lexical category representing actions, occurrences, or states, forming the grammatical core of a sentence.
• **Universal Necessity**: Every meaningful sentence in Hindi must possess at least one finite verb to express a complete thought or action.
• **Concept of Dhatu**: **धातु** represents the primal, uninflected root form of a verb from which all conjugated verb forms are derived in Hindi grammar.
• **Examples of Dhatus**: Common root forms include 'खा' (eat), 'पी' (drink), 'जा' (go), 'आ' (come), 'सो' (sleep), and 'देख' (see).
• **Dhatu to Kriya Conversion**: By appending the infinitive marker suffix '**ना**' to the dhatu, standard dictionary verbs are created (e.g., खा + ना = खाना).
• **Inflection Based on Gender**: Verbs change their endings according to the gender of the subject in masculine and feminine forms (e.g., लड़का जाता है vs. लड़की जाती है).
• **Inflection Based on Number**: Verbs change based on whether the subject is singular (**एकवचन**) or plural (**बहुवचन**), such as 'बच्चা खेलता है' vs. 'बच्चे खेलते हैं'.
• **Inflection Based on Person**: Verbs adapt to the grammatical person—first person (**उत्तम पुरुष**), second person (**मध्यम पुरुष**), and third person (**अन्य पुरुष**).
• **Action vs. State**: While many verbs show physical action (दौड़ना, लिखना), others indicate a state of existence, feeling, or happening (होना, रहना, दिखना).
• **Primary Classification**: Based on **कर्म (Object)**, verbs are fundamentally divided into two major types: **सकर्मक क्रिया** and **अकर्मक क्रिया**.
• **Understanding Object (Karm)**: The **कर्म** is the recipient of the action performed by the subject (**कर्ता**), answering the question of what is being acted upon.
• **Role in Abhyas Sagar**: Chapter 5 uses exercises based on the chapter's storyline to reinforce these theoretical definitions with practical sentence analysis.
• **Sentence Anatomy**: A typical Hindi sentence structure follows the **कर्ता + कर्म + क्रिया** (Subject + Object + Verb) word order pattern.
• **Exception to Word Order**: In poetic or stylistic variations, this order may shift, but the grammatical dependency of the verb on the subject and object remains constant.
• **Foundation for Advanced Grammar**: Mastery of basic verb forms in Class 7 is essential for learning complex concepts like tense (**काल**), voice (**वाच्य**), and mood later.

• **Sankarmak Kriya (Transitive Verb)**: **सकर्मक क्रिया** translates to a verb with an object, where the action passes from the doer to the object.
• **Etymology of Sakarmak**: The word 'सकर्मक' is formed from 'स' (with) + 'कर्म' (object), literally meaning 'along with an object'.
• **Primary Characteristic**: In a sentence with a transitive verb, the action has a direct physical or abstract effect on a recipient object.
• **Question Test ('क्या')**: To identify a transitive verb, one asks the question '**क्या**' (What) to the verb; if a logical answer emerges, it is transitive.
• **Question Test ('किसे/को')**: Alternatively, asking '**किसे**' (Whom) or 'किसको' (To whom) helps identify the object of a transitive verb.
• **Example 1 - Reading**: In the sentence "राम पुस्तक पढ़ रहा है" (Ram is reading a book), 'पढ़ रहा है' is transitive because 'पुस्तक' is the object.
• **Example 2 - Eating**: In "अमित सेब खा रहा है" (Amit is eating an apple), 'खा रहा है' is transitive as 'सेब' receives the action of eating.
• **Contextual Chapter Example**: Grandmother operating the radio in the story provides transitive action contexts, such as "दादी ने रेडियो चलाया" (Grandmother turned on the radio).
• **Presence of Object**: Even if the object is implied rather than explicitly stated, the potential to take an object makes the verb transitive in context.
• **Single vs. Double Objects**: Transitive verbs can sometimes take two objects: a direct object (**मुख्य कर्म**) and an indirect object (**गौण कर्म**).
• **Example of Double Object**: In "अध्यापक ने छात्र को पुस्तक दी", 'छात्र को' and 'पुस्तक' are two distinct objects attached to the verb 'दी'.
• **Grammatical Transformation**: Intransitive verbs can occasionally be converted into transitive verbs by modifying their root structure or adding causative elements.
• **Clarity in Practice**: DAV Abhyas Sagar exercises provide numerous sentences where students must underline the verb and circle the object to prove transitivity.
• **Common Misconceptions**: Students must remember that the presence of a noun after a verb does not automatically make it an object unless it answers 'what' or 'whom'.
• **Summary of Transitive Nature**: Sakarmak kriya always demands completion through an external recipient of the action for full semantic satisfaction.

• **Akarmak Kriya (Intransitive Verb)**: **अकर्मक क्रिया** refers to verbs that do not require an object to complete their meaning and action.
• **Etymology of Akarmak**: Derived from 'अ' (without) + 'कर्म' (object), meaning 'without an object' or lacking a direct receiver of the action.
• **Action Confinement**: In intransitive verbs, the result or effect of the action remains confined entirely to the subject (**कर्ता**) itself.
• **Absence of Object**: Sentences featuring intransitive verbs contain only a subject and a verb (and sometimes adverbs), but never a direct object.
• **The 'Kya' Test Failure**: When questioning an intransitive verb with '**क्या**' (What), no logical answer is produced (e.g., bird flies what? - No answer).
• **Example 1 - Sleeping**: "बच्चा सो रहा है" (The child is sleeping) is intransitive because sleeping is an internal state with no external object.
• **Example 2 - Crying**: "लड़की रो रही है" (The girl is crying) is intransitive; the action of crying affects only the girl herself.
• **Example 3 - Walking/Running**: Verbs like 'दौड़ना' (to run), 'हँसना' (to laugh), and 'चलना' (to walk) are typically intransitive in standard usage.
• **Story Context Example**: Sentences from "दादी का रेडियो" describing passive states or simple movements of characters often feature intransitive verbs.
• **Subject Dependency**: The verb's conjugation in intransitive sentences agrees exclusively with the gender and number of the subject.
• **Distinction Clarity**: The core difference between sakarmak and akarmak lies entirely in the presence or absence of a grammatical object.
• **Identical Verbs, Different Contexts**: Some verbs can act as both transitive or intransitive depending on how they are used in a specific sentence.
• **Example of Dual Role (Udna)**: "पक्षी उड़ते हैं" (Birds fly - Akarmak) vs. "पक्षी पतंग उड़ा रहे हैं" (People are flying kites - Sakarmak).
• **Example of Dual Role (Khelna)**: "बच्चा खेल रहा है" (Child is playing - often treated as intransitive if object is omitted) vs. "बच्चा क्रिकेट खेल रहा है" (Sakarmak).
• **Methodical Analysis**: DAV curriculum emphasizes testing verbs within specific sentence boundaries rather than viewing verbs in isolation.

• **Advanced Classification of Verbs**: Beyond transitive and intransitive, Hindi grammar categorizes verbs based on structure, composition, and function.
• **Prekarthak Kriya (Causative Verbs)**: **प्रेरणार्थक क्रिया** are verbs where the subject does not perform the action directly, but inspires or causes another person to do it.
• **Structure of Causative Verbs**: These verbs typically feature two actors: the instigator (**प्रेरक कर्ता**) and the actual doer (**प्रेरित कर्ता**).
• **First and Second Causatives**: Hindi forms two levels of causative verbs from a single root (e.g., पढ़ना -> पढ़ाना [1st] and पढ़वाना [2nd]).
• **Examples of Causatives**: Root 'लिख' leads to 'लिखाना' (to cause to write) and 'लिखवाना' (to have written by someone else).
• **Samyukt Kriya (Compound Verbs)**: **संयुक्त क्रिया** is formed by the combination of two or more different main and auxiliary verbs working together to express a single complete action.
• **Nature of Compound Verbs**: In a compound verb, the first verb carries the primary lexical meaning, while the second adds nuance, intensity, or completion.
• **Examples of Compound Verbs**: Phrases like 'चल पड़ा' (started walking), 'खा लिया' (finished eating), and 'गिर पड़ा' (fell down) are compound verbs.
• **Sahayak Kriya (Auxiliary Verbs)**: **सहायक क्रिया** are helper verbs placed at the end of a sentence to indicate tense, mood, and grammatical number (e.g., है, था, गा, गे).
• **Naam Dhatuk Kriya (Denominative Verbs)**: **नामधातु क्रिया** are verbs derived from nouns, adjectives, or pronouns (e.g., हाथ from हाथियाना, बात from बतियाना).
• **Kridant Kriya (Non-Finite Verbs / Participles)**: **कृदंत क्रिया** are formed by adding suffixes to verb roots, functioning as adjectives or nouns (e.g., चलता हुआ, पढ़ा हुआ).

• **Review of Basic Identification**: To successfully solve exercises in Chapter 5, students must rigorously apply the verb-recognition checklist.
• **Step 1 - Locate the Verb**: Read the sentence carefully and identify the word that expresses an action, state, or event, usually positioned at the end.
• **Step 2 - Identify the Subject**: Ask '**कौन**' (Who) is performing the action to pinpoint the subject of the sentence.
• **Step 3 - Apply the Object Test**: Ask '**क्या**' (What) or '**किसे**' (Whom) immediately after the verb to check for an active object.
• **Step 4 - Classify**: If a valid object answers the question, label it **सकर्मक क्रिया**; if no object exists and none is implied, label it **अकर्मक क्रिया**.
• **Common Pitfall 1 - Location Adverbs**: Words indicating places (e.g., घर, बाहर, मैदान में) often confuse students into thinking they are objects; they are actually locative adverbs (**स्थानवाचक क्रियाविशेषण**).
• **Correction of Pitfall 1**: In "बच्चा मैदान में दौड़ रहा है", 'मैदान में' is a place, not an object. Running is an **अकर्मक क्रिया**.
• **Common Pitfall 2 - Time Adverbs**: Expressions of time (कल, सुबह, रात) do not serve as grammatical objects for verbs.
• **Correction of Pitfall 2**: In "वह कल सोया था", 'कल' indicates time, making 'सोया था' strictly an **अकर्मक क्रिया**.

• **Summary of Key Definitions**: **क्रिया** is an action/state word; **धातु** is the root form; **सकर्मक** requires an object; **अकर्मक** does not require an object.
• **Quick Revision Rule 1**: If an action's result transfers to an external object, the verb is unconditionally **सकर्मक**.
• **Quick Revision Rule 2**: If an action terminates within the doer (**कर्ता**) without affecting any external entity, the verb is strictly **अकर्मक**.
• **Quick Revision Rule 3**: The addition of the suffix '**ना**' transforms an abstract root dhatu into a complete, usable infinitive verb.
• **Quick Revision Rule 4**: The presence of time or place markers in a sentence does not convert an intransitive verb into a transitive one.
