• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to the DAV Dictionary academic study notes for Class 5 My Living World, specifically focusing on Chapter 5: "Food and Health".
• **Core Biological Concept**: Food is a fundamental requirement for all living organisms, acting as the primary biological fuel that sustains life, growth, and bodily maintenance.
• **Primary Functions of Food**: Food provides the essential energy needed to perform daily physical activities, run internal metabolic processes, and maintain basal body temperature.
• **Building Blocks of the Body**: Proper nutrition supplies the raw materials necessary for the continuous construction, repair, and regeneration of worn-out tissues and cells.
• **Immunity and Defense**: Consuming the right kinds of food strengthens the body's immune system, protecting it against various pathogens, infections, and diseases.
• **Definition of Nutrients**: Nutrients are specific chemical substances present in food items that are systematically broken down and utilized by the body to function efficiently.
• **The Five Main Nutrients**: Human dietary requirements are categorized into five major nutrient groups: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals.
• **Role of Non-Nutritive Components**: In addition to the five main nutrients, water and dietary fiber (roughage) are vital components of a healthy and balanced diet.
• **Nutritional Diversity**: No single food item contains all the essential nutrients in the required proportions, which necessitates eating a diverse variety of foods daily.
• **Concept of a Balanced Diet**: A **Balanced Diet** is officially defined as a diet that contains the right amount of all essential nutrients, roughage, and water required for proper body functioning.
• **Age and Diet Dependency**: The specific nutritional requirements of an individual vary significantly depending on their age, gender, and the nature of their daily physical labor.
• **Growing Children Needs**: Growing children, such as Class 5 students, require a higher proportion of body-building proteins and energy-rich foods to support rapid skeletal and muscular development.
• **Energy-Giving Foods**: Nutrients that primarily supply the body with instant and sustained energy to perform physical and mental tasks are classified as energy-giving foods.
• **Carbohydrates as Fuel**: Carbohydrates are the primary and most easily accessible source of energy for the human body, fueling both muscles and the brain.
• **Simple vs. Complex Carbohydrates**: Carbohydrates exist in simple forms like sugars (found in fruits and honey) and complex forms like starches (found in grains and potatoes).

• **Primary Sources of Carbohydrates**: Common dietary sources rich in carbohydrates include wheat, rice, maize, potatoes, sugar cane, sweet potatoes, and various millets.
• **Starch Detection Test**: The presence of starch in a food item can be easily verified using an iodine solution, which turns a blue-black color upon reacting with starch.
• **Fats as Concentrated Energy**: Fats are highly concentrated sources of energy that provide more than twice the amount of energy yielded by an equal amount of carbohydrates.
• **Subcutaneous Fat Layer**: Excess fat consumed by the body is stored underneath the skin and around vital internal organs as adipose tissue, insulating the body against cold.
• **Plant-Based Fat Sources**: Major plant sources of edible fats include vegetable oils (such as groundnut oil, mustard oil, soybean oil, and sunflower oil), nuts, and seeds.
• **Animal-Based Fat Sources**: Animal sources of fats include butter, ghee, cream, whole milk, cheese, egg yolks, and fatty cuts of meat and fish.
• **Excess Fat Dangers**: Consuming excessive amounts of fats can lead to severe health complications, most notably obesity, cardiovascular diseases, and high blood pressure.
• **Body-Building Foods**: Proteins are popularly known as body-building foods because they are indispensable for physical growth, cellular repair, and muscle development.
• **Amino Acids as Building Blocks**: Proteins are chemically composed of smaller, linked units called amino acids, which string together to form complex structural molecules in the body.
• **Cellular Repair Mechanism**: Proteins are constantly utilized by the body to replace dead, damaged, or injured cells across skin, muscles, organs, and bones.
• **Plant Protein Sources (Pulses)**: Rich plant sources of proteins include various pulses (dals) such as moong, masoor, arhar, gram, chana, and soybeans.
• **Plant Protein Sources (Legumes)**: Legumes and nuts, including peanuts and almonds, are also exceptional vegetarian sources of high-quality dietary proteins.
• **Animal Protein Sources**: High biological value animal proteins are abundantly found in milk, curd, paneer, eggs, lean meat, chicken, and fish.
• **Protein Requirements in Youth**: Children need a high intake of dietary proteins because their muscles, bones, and organs are undergoing rapid developmental expansion.
• **Protective Foods**: Vitamins and minerals are collectively categorized as protective foods because they shield the body from diseases and regulate bodily processes.

• **Vitamins and Their Role**: Vitamins are organic compounds required by the body in very small quantities to maintain normal metabolic functions, vision, and immune responses.
• **Water-Soluble vs. Fat-Soluble Vitamins**: Vitamins are broadly classified into two categories: water-soluble vitamins (such as Vitamin B-complex and Vitamin C) and fat-soluble vitamins (such as Vitamins A, D, E, and K).
• **Vitamin A Functions**: Vitamin A is essential for maintaining healthy eyesight, clear cornea, glowing skin, and robust mucosal linings in the respiratory and digestive tracts.
• **Vitamin A Sources**: Rich dietary sources of Vitamin A include carrots, papaya, mangoes, green leafy vegetables, milk, butter, and fish liver oil.
• **Vitamin B Complex Functions**: Vitamin B-complex group is vital for proper nerve function, red blood cell formation, carbohydrate metabolism, and maintaining good muscle tone.
• **Vitamin B Sources**: Excellent sources of Vitamin B include whole grains, unpolished rice, sprouted grains, milk, eggs, green peas, and lean meats.
• **Vitamin C Functions**: Vitamin C is a powerful antioxidant that strengthens the immune system, promotes healing of wounds, and keeps gums and teeth healthy.
• **Vitamin C Sources (Citrus Fruits)**: Vitamin C is abundantly present in citrus fruits like oranges, lemons, limes, amla (Indian gooseberry), tomatoes, and guava.
• **Vitamin D Functions**: Vitamin D is crucial for the absorption of calcium and phosphorus, leading to the formation of strong, dense bones and teeth.
• **The Sunshine Vitamin**: Vitamin D is uniquely synthesized by the human skin when exposed to direct, early morning sunlight, earning it the moniker "the sunshine vitamin".
• **Vitamin D Sources**: Dietary sources of Vitamin D include fortified milk, butter, egg yolks, cod liver oil, and fatty fish like salmon and mackerel.
• **Minerals as Inorganic Nutrients**: Minerals are inorganic elements required by the body in small amounts to perform vital structural and regulatory physiological functions.
• **Calcium for Bones**: Calcium is a major mineral required for building hard, strong bones and teeth, as well as enabling proper blood clotting and muscle contraction.
• **Calcium Sources**: Excellent sources of calcium include milk, cheese, curd, paneer, ragi (finger millet), and green leafy vegetables like spinach.
• **Iron for Blood Formation**: Iron is an essential mineral required for the synthesis of hemoglobin, the red-colored pigment in red blood cells that transports oxygen.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Iron Deficiency Consequences**: A lack of iron in the diet leads to a condition called anemia, where the blood cannot carry sufficient oxygen, causing fatigue and weakness.
• **Iron Sources**: Rich dietary sources of iron include apples, spinach, pomegranate, green leafy vegetables, jaggery (gud), whole pulses, and beetroot.
• **Iodine Importance**: Iodine is a trace mineral necessary for the proper functioning of the thyroid gland, which regulates overall physical and mental development.
• **Iodine Sources**: Primary dietary sources of iodine include iodized table salt, seafood, sea fish, and vegetables grown in iodine-rich coastal soils.
• **Roughage (Dietary Fiber)**: Roughage refers to the fibrous, indigestible plant material present in our food that adds bulk to the diet and aids smooth digestion.
• **Primary Role of Roughage**: Although roughage provides zero nutritional value or energy, it is critical because it helps the digestive tract push food smoothly, preventing constipation.
• **Roughage Sources**: Outstanding sources of dietary fiber include raw salads, whole grains, unpeeled fruits, fresh vegetables, beans, oats, and whole-wheat flour.
• **Water as a Universal Solvent**: Water constitutes about 70 percent of the human body's total weight and acts as a universal medium for all metabolic and biochemical reactions.
• **Functions of Water in the Body**: Water regulates body temperature through sweating, helps transport dissolved nutrients to cells, and flushes out metabolic wastes through urine and sweat.
• **Daily Water Intake**: Health experts recommend that human beings drink at least 8 to 10 glasses of clean, potable water every day to maintain fluid balance and health.
• **Dehydration Definition**: Dehydration is a dangerous medical condition caused by excessive loss of body water, often resulting from vomiting, diarrhea, or insufficient fluid intake.
• **Need for Clean Water**: Drinking contaminated water can transmit waterborne diseases such as cholera, typhoid, jaundice, and dysentery, which can be fatal if untreated.
• **Preservation of Nutrients**: Careful food preparation is necessary to ensure that essential vitamins and minerals are not destroyed or washed away during cooking.
• **Washing Before Cutting**: Fruits and vegetables should always be thoroughly washed before peeling and cutting to prevent the loss of water-soluble vitamins.

• **Overcooking Dangers**: Overcooking food items or cooking them at excessively high temperatures destroys delicate vitamins, particularly Vitamin C and various B vitamins.
• **Excess Water in Cooking**: Boiling vegetables in too much water and throwing away that water strips the meal of vital mineral salts and vitamins leached into the liquid.
• **Deficiency Diseases**: A deficiency disease is a medical condition that arises in the human body due to the prolonged lack of one or more essential nutrients in the diet.
• **Malnutrition Definition**: Malnutrition occurs when a person's diet does not provide the correct amount of nutrients, either due to undernutrition or overnutrition.
• **Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)**: Severe deficiency of both proteins and total calories in young children leads to serious wasting diseases known as Kwashiorkor and Marasmus.
• **Kwashiorkor Characteristics**: Symptoms of Kwashiorkor include a swollen belly, stunted growth, dry and discolored skin, and extreme swelling (edema) of the hands and feet.
• **Marasmus Characteristics**: Symptoms of Marasmus include extreme emaciation, ribs becoming visible through tight skin, severe muscle wasting, and extreme physical weakness.
• **Night Blindness (Vitamin A Deficiency)**: A deficiency of Vitamin A causes night blindness, a condition where an affected individual cannot see clearly in dim light or darkness.
• **Beriberi (Vitamin B Deficiency)**: A deficiency of Vitamin B1 (thiamine) leads to Beriberi, characterized by extreme muscle fatigue, nerve damage, and heart complications.
• **Scurvy (Vitamin C Deficiency)**: A deficiency of Vitamin C causes Scurvy, marked by swollen, bleeding gums, loose teeth, joint pains, and delayed wound healing.
• **Rickets (Vitamin D Deficiency)**: A deficiency of Vitamin D causes Rickets in children, leading to soft, weak, and deformed bones, particularly bowed legs.
• **Osteomalacia Equivalent**: In adults, a similar softening of bones due to Vitamin D and calcium deficiency is known as osteomalacia.
• **Goiter (Iodine Deficiency)**: A deficiency of iodine in the diet causes Goiter, a condition characterized by the abnormal swelling of the thyroid gland in the neck.
• **Anemia (Iron Deficiency)**: Iron deficiency leads to anemia, causing pale skin, constant tiredness, dizziness, shortness of breath, and reduced concentration.

• **Communicable vs. Non-Communicable Diseases**: Diseases are broadly categorized into communicable (infectious) diseases that spread from person to person, and non-communicable diseases caused by deficiencies, genetics, or lifestyle.
• **Spread of Communicable Diseases**: Communicable diseases are caused by microscopic pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and protozoa invading the human body.
• **Modes of Transmission (Air)**: Air-borne diseases spread when an infected person coughs or sneezes, releasing droplets containing pathogens into the air, which others inhale.
• **Air-Borne Disease Examples**: Common air-borne infectious diseases include the common cold, influenza (flu), chickenpox, measles, and tuberculosis (TB).
• **Modes of Transmission (Water and Food)**: Diseases like cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, and dysentery spread through water or food contaminated by harmful germs.
• **Modes of Transmission (Insects)**: Certain insects, known as vectors, transmit diseases; for example, female Anopheles mosquitoes transmit malaria, and Aedes mosquitoes transmit dengue.
• **Vector Control**: Preventing the breeding of mosquitoes by avoiding stagnant water in coolers, pots, and tires is essential to control vector-borne diseases.
• **Personal Hygiene Practices**: Maintaining high standards of personal hygiene, such as washing hands with soap before eating and after using the restroom, prevents disease spread.
• **Sanitation and Cleanliness**: Proper disposal of household garbage, covering food items to keep flies away, and maintaining clean surroundings protect public health.
• **Vaccination Concept**: Vaccination is an artificial method of inducing immunity against specific infectious diseases by introducing a weakened or dead pathogen into the body.
• **How Vaccines Work**: Vaccines stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies that remember the pathogen, protecting the person from getting infected in the future.
• **National Immunization Programs**: Governments conduct immunization drives, such as pulse polio campaigns, to eradicate deadly infectious diseases from the population.
• **Role of Rest and Sleep**: Adequate rest and deep sleep are as important as nutrition, allowing the body's tissues to repair, recharge, and maintain a robust immune system.
• **Physical Exercise Benefits**: Regular physical exercise, outdoor games, and yoga improve blood circulation, strengthen muscles and lungs, and prevent lifestyle diseases like obesity.

• **Lifestyle Diseases**: Modern sedentary lifestyles characterized by lack of physical activity and junk food consumption lead to non-communicable lifestyle diseases.
• **Junk Food Hazards**: Fast food and junk food are typically rich in refined sugars, unhealthful trans fats, and excess salt while lacking essential vitamins and dietary fiber.
• **Harmful Effects of Carbonated Drinks**: Sugary carbonated beverages can damage tooth enamel, contribute to childhood obesity, and deplete bone calcium over time.
• **Balanced Lifestyle Integration**: True health is defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease.
• **Role of Mental Health**: Mental well-being, stress management, positive thinking, and emotional balance are crucial components of overall human health.
• **Food Preservation Definition**: Food preservation is the process of treating and handling food to stop or greatly slow down spoilage caused by microorganisms, thereby maintaining its nutritional value.
• **Causes of Food Spoilage**: Food spoilage is primarily caused by the rapid growth of bacteria, molds, and yeasts, or by the natural chemical action of enzymes within the food.
• **Refrigeration as a Method**: Keeping perishable items like milk, fruits, and cooked food inside a refrigerator slows down bacterial growth due to low temperatures.
• **Freezing Method**: Deep freezing preserves foods like meat, peas, and fish for long periods by completely halting the activity of spoilage-causing microbes.
• **Dehydration (Drying)**: Removing moisture from food items like chillies, grapes (raisins), and fenugreek leaves (methi) prevents microbial growth because germs need water to survive.
• **Salting and Sugaring**: Adding high amounts of salt or sugar draws out water from food cells through osmosis, making it impossible for bacteria to survive; used in pickles and jams.
• **Boiling Technique**: Boiling liquids like milk kills harmful germs present in them, extending their safe shelf life for consumption.
• **Canning and Bottling**: Sealing processed foods airtight in sterilized cans or bottles prevents external air and microbes from entering and spoiling the contents.
• **Chemical Preservatives**: Adding safe chemical substances like sodium benzoate helps preserve packaged foods, jams, and squashes for extended periods.

• **Nutritional Chart Formulation**: A well-planned diet chart must balance all five nutrient groups based on age, occupation, and health status to ensure optimum vitality.
• **Infant Nutrition Requirements**: Infants rely exclusively on mother's milk, which is nature's complete food, providing antibodies, proteins, and easy-to-digest nutrients.
• **Nutritional Needs of Laborers**: People engaged in heavy physical labor, such as construction workers or athletes, require a diet high in carbohydrates and fats to meet high energy demands.
• **Sedentary Worker Needs**: Desk workers and sedentary individuals require fewer carbohydrate-rich foods to prevent excess fat accumulation and weight gain.
• **Role of Roughage in Preventing Constipation**: Dietary fiber creates bulk that stimulates rhythmic muscular movements (peristalsis) in the alimentary canal, facilitating smooth bowel evacuation.
• **Significance of Hydration in Digestion**: Water lubricates the digestive tract, allowing food to pass smoothly and assisting enzymes in breaking down complex food molecules.
• **Role of Iodine in Mental Growth**: Adequate iodine intake prevents mental retardation, sluggishness, and thyroid enlargement in growing children.
• **Role of Calcium in Nerve Impulse**: Beyond bone formation, calcium ions play a vital role in transmitting nerve impulses and enabling smooth heart muscle contractions.
• **Vitamin K and Blood Clotting**: Vitamin K is an essential fat-soluble vitamin that enables the liver to synthesize proteins required for blood clotting, preventing excessive bleeding from wounds.
• **Significance of Sprouting**: Sprouting pulses and grains significantly increases their vitamin content, making them exceptionally nutritious and easier to digest.
• **Importance of Balanced Meals**: Every meal should ideally include items from energy-giving, body-building, and protective food groups to ensure comprehensive nourishment.
• **Danger of Deficiency Over Time**: Nutritional deficiencies do not appear overnight; they develop gradually over weeks or months of continuous poor dietary habits.
• **Preventative Health Measures**: Practicing good hygiene, eating balanced diets, drinking safe water, and exercising daily are the four pillars of disease prevention.
• **Food Safety and Hygiene**: Food must be kept covered at all times in homes and markets to protect it from dust, flies, and other insect vectors that carry disease-causing germs.
