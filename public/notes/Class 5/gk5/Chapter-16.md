• **Chapter Overview:** "चाँद का कुर्ता" (The Moon’s Kurta) is a classic Hindi poem written by the renowned poet Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar'.
• **Core Theme:** The poem explores the innocence of a child’s imagination regarding the changing phases of the moon.
• **Literary Genre:** It is a children’s poem (Bal Kavita) that uses personification to describe celestial bodies.
• **Poet Profile:** Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar' was a prominent Indian Hindi poet, essayist, and academic, often referred to as 'Rashtrakavi'.
• **Child’s Perspective:** The poem is narrated from the viewpoint of a small child who observes the moon from Earth.
• **The Central Conflict:** The child notices that the moon changes its size every night, which leads to the problem of the moon not having a fitting garment.
• **The Request:** The child asks his mother to stitch a 'Kurta' (a long shirt) for the moon to protect it from the cold.
• **Celestial Observation:** The child observes the moon’s cycle, noting how it grows from a thin crescent to a full orb.
• **Personification:** The moon is treated as a living entity that feels cold, necessitating the need for clothing.
• **The Concept of 'Sardi':** The child expresses concern that the moon might be shivering in the cold night air.
• **The Mother’s Role:** The mother is portrayed as the listener who hears the child’s innocent request with patience.
• **The Child’s Logic:** The child believes that if the moon wears a Kurta, it will be protected from the harsh weather.
• **Phases of the Moon:** The poem subtly introduces the concept of lunar phases (Waxing and Waning).
• **Emotional Connection:** The poem highlights the bond between a child and their mother, where the child shares their deepest observations.
• **Tone of the Poem:** The tone is whimsical, playful, and deeply empathetic.

• **The 'Amavasya' (New Moon):** The child observes the moon when it is invisible or very thin, leading to the concern about its 'disappearance'.
