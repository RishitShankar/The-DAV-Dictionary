• **Picture Talk Definition**: Picture Talk is a foundational literacy exercise designed to develop observational skills, vocabulary expansion, and oral expression by interpreting visual stimuli.
• **Visual Literacy**: This is the ability to interpret, negotiate, and make meaning from information presented in the form of an image.
• **Observation Skills**: The primary goal is to train the student to look at a picture systematically, starting from the main subject to the background details.
• **Vocabulary Building**: Students learn to identify and name objects, actions, colors, and spatial relationships found within the provided illustration.
• **Phonics Integration**: Picture Talk serves as a bridge to phonics by associating the visual representation of an object with its written word and phonetic sound.
• **Descriptive Language**: Students are encouraged to use adjectives to describe the size, shape, and color of the objects identified in the picture.
• **Action Identification**: A key component involves identifying verbs or "doing words" depicted in the scene, such as running, playing, eating, or sleeping.
• **Spatial Awareness**: Students practice using prepositions like 'on', 'in', 'under', 'beside', and 'behind' to describe where objects are located in the picture.
• **Narrative Construction**: Picture Talk encourages students to create a short story or sequence of events based on the visual cues provided.
• **Critical Thinking**: By asking "why" or "what happens next," students develop the ability to infer meaning beyond what is explicitly shown.
• **Phonemic Awareness**: This involves recognizing the initial, medial, and final sounds of the words identified during the Picture Talk session.
• **Consonant Blends**: During the analysis of objects, teachers highlight consonant blends (like 'st', 'bl', 'tr') found in the names of items in the picture.
• **Vowel Sounds**: Identifying objects allows for the reinforcement of short and long vowel sounds within the context of the visual scene.
• **Sentence Formation**: Students transition from naming single objects to forming simple sentences like "The cat is sitting on the mat."
• **Confidence Building**: Regular practice in Picture Talk helps young learners overcome hesitation in speaking English by providing a structured visual aid.

• **Systematic Scanning**: A successful Picture Talk begins by scanning the picture from left to right and top to bottom to ensure no detail is missed.
• **Subject Identification**: The first step is to identify the "Main Character" or the central focus of the illustration.
• **Setting Recognition**: Identifying the environment, such as a park, a classroom, or a kitchen, provides context for the vocabulary used.
• **Color Identification**: Students practice naming colors to describe objects, which enhances descriptive vocabulary (e.g., "a red apple," "a blue sky").
• **Counting Skills**: Integrating numeracy, students count the number of specific items (e.g., "There are three birds in the tree").
• **Emotional Cues**: Students learn to identify facial expressions and body language to describe the mood of the characters in the picture.
• **Action Verbs**: Focus on identifying what the characters are doing, which helps in understanding present continuous tense (e.g., "The boy is jumping").
• **Phonetic Mapping**: Connecting the visual image to the phonetic spelling helps in long-term memory retention of new vocabulary.
• **Initial Sound Focus**: Teachers emphasize the starting sound of objects (e.g., 'B' for 'Ball', 'C' for 'Cat') found in the picture.
• **Ending Sound Focus**: Students are encouraged to listen for the final sound of words, such as the 't' in 'cat' or the 'n' in 'sun'.
• **Rhyming Words**: Identifying objects in the picture that rhyme with other known words (e.g., 'cat' and 'hat') reinforces phonetic patterns.
• **Contextual Clues**: Using the picture to guess the meaning of unknown words based on their visual representation.
• **Categorization**: Grouping items found in the picture into categories like 'animals', 'furniture', 'toys', or 'food'.
• **Comparative Analysis**: Comparing two different pictures to find similarities and differences, which builds analytical thinking.
• **Oral Fluency**: The ultimate goal is to speak clearly and fluently about the picture using complete sentences.

• **Phonics Foundation**: Phonics is the method of teaching reading by correlating sounds with letters or groups of letters in an alphabetic writing system.
• **Letter-Sound Correspondence**: Every letter of the alphabet has a specific sound, and Picture Talk helps students map these sounds to real-world objects.
• **The 'A' Sound**: Identifying objects like 'apple', 'ant', or 'axe' in a picture to reinforce the short 'a' sound.
• **The 'B' Sound**: Recognizing 'ball', 'bat', or 'boy' to practice the plosive 'b' sound.
• **The 'C' Sound**: Distinguishing the hard 'c' sound in 'cat', 'cup', or 'cap' during the analysis of a picture.
• **The 'D' Sound**: Identifying 'dog', 'duck', or 'drum' to practice the 'd' sound.
• **The 'E' Sound**: Recognizing 'egg', 'elephant', or 'envelope' to reinforce the short 'e' sound.
• **The 'F' Sound**: Identifying 'fish', 'fan', or 'frog' to practice the fricative 'f' sound.
• **The 'G' Sound**: Recognizing 'goat', 'gate', or 'girl' to practice the hard 'g' sound.
• **The 'H' Sound**: Identifying 'hat', 'hen', or 'house' to practice the aspirate 'h' sound.
• **The 'I' Sound**: Recognizing 'ink', 'igloo', or 'insect' to reinforce the short 'i' sound.
• **The 'J' Sound**: Identifying 'jug', 'jam', or 'jet' to practice the 'j' sound.
• **The 'K' Sound**: Recognizing 'kite', 'key', or 'king' to practice the 'k' sound.
• **The 'L' Sound**: Identifying 'lamp', 'lion', or 'leaf' to practice the lateral 'l' sound.
• **The 'M' Sound**: Recognizing 'mat', 'moon', or 'monkey' to practice the nasal 'm' sound.

• **The 'N' Sound**: Identifying 'net', 'nest', or 'nose' to practice the nasal 'n' sound.
• **The 'O' Sound**: Recognizing 'orange', 'octopus', or 'ox' to reinforce the short 'o' sound.
• **The 'P' Sound**: Identifying 'pot', 'pen', or 'pig' to practice the plosive 'p' sound.
• **The 'Q' Sound**: Recognizing 'queen', 'quilt', or 'quack' to practice the 'qu' blend.
• **The 'R' Sound**: Identifying 'rat', 'rabbit', or 'rose' to practice the 'r' sound.
• **The 'S' Sound**: Recognizing 'sun', 'snake', or 'star' to practice the sibilant 's' sound.
• **The 'T' Sound**: Identifying 'tap', 'top', or 'tent' to practice the 't' sound.
• **The 'U' Sound**: Recognizing 'umbrella', 'urn', or 'up' to reinforce the short 'u' sound.
• **The 'V' Sound**: Identifying 'van', 'vase', or 'violin' to practice the 'v' sound.
• **The 'W' Sound**: Recognizing 'watch', 'well', or 'web' to practice the 'w' sound.
• **The 'X' Sound**: Identifying 'x-ray' or 'box' (final sound) to practice the 'x' sound.
• **The 'Y' Sound**: Recognizing 'yoyo', 'yak', or 'yellow' to practice the 'y' sound.
• **The 'Z' Sound**: Identifying 'zebra', 'zip', or 'zoo' to practice the voiced 'z' sound.
• **Phonic Blending**: The process of joining individual sounds together to form a word, which is practiced by naming objects in the picture.
• **Word Families**: Grouping words found in the picture that belong to the same family (e.g., -at, -in, -ot).

• **Descriptive Adjectives**: Using words like 'big', 'small', 'tall', 'short', 'fat', and 'thin' to describe objects in the picture.
• **Color Adjectives**: Using 'red', 'blue', 'green', 'yellow', 'black', and 'white' to specify the appearance of items.
• **Quantity Adjectives**: Using 'one', 'two', 'many', or 'few' to describe the number of items observed.
• **Prepositional Phrases**: Using phrases like "on the table," "under the chair," or "in the box" to describe location.
• **Action Verbs (Present Continuous)**: Using 'is' or 'are' + verb-ing to describe ongoing actions in the picture (e.g., "The girl is running").
• **Subject-Verb Agreement**: Ensuring that singular subjects take singular verbs (e.g., "The bird is flying") and plural subjects take plural verbs (e.g., "The birds are flying").
• **Pronoun Usage**: Using 'he', 'she', 'it', or 'they' to refer to characters and objects in the picture.
• **Articles**: Correctly using 'a' or 'an' before nouns based on the initial sound (e.g., 'a cat', 'an apple').
• **Question Formation**: Learning to ask "What is this?", "Where is the cat?", and "Who is playing?" based on the picture.
• **Inference**: Drawing conclusions from the picture, such as "It is sunny because the sun is shining."
• **Sequencing**: Describing events in a logical order if the picture depicts a story or a process.
• **Empathy**: Discussing how characters in the picture might be feeling, which builds emotional intelligence.
• **Creative Thinking**: Imagining what happened before or what will happen after the scene depicted in the picture.
• **Vocabulary Expansion**: Introducing new words that are not immediately obvious but are relevant to the scene.
• **Clarity of Speech**: Practicing clear pronunciation of words identified during the Picture Talk.

• **Classroom Environment**: Picture Talk is most effective in a collaborative setting where students share their observations.
• **Teacher-Led Discussion**: The teacher guides the discussion by asking open-ended questions to prompt detailed responses.
• **Student-Led Observation**: Students are encouraged to take the lead in identifying objects and describing the scene.
• **Peer Interaction**: Students discuss the picture with their partners, fostering communication and social skills.
• **Visual Aids**: High-quality, colorful, and detailed pictures are essential for effective Picture Talk.
• **Focus on Detail**: Encouraging students to look for "hidden" items or small details in the background.
• **Active Listening**: Students must listen to their peers' observations to avoid repetition and build upon others' ideas.
• **Confidence in Speaking**: Providing a safe space where students feel comfortable expressing their thoughts about the picture.
• **Vocabulary Reinforcement**: Repeatedly using the new words learned during the session in different contexts.
• **Integration with Writing**: Transitioning from oral Picture Talk to writing simple sentences about the picture.
• **Phonics Drills**: Using the picture as a starting point for daily phonics drills.
• **Interactive Whiteboards**: Using digital tools to zoom in on specific parts of the picture for closer analysis.
• **Flashcards**: Using flashcards alongside the picture to reinforce the spelling and sound of the objects.
• **Role Play**: Acting out the actions seen in the picture to make the learning experience more kinesthetic.
• **Assessment**: Evaluating the student's ability to identify objects, use correct vocabulary, and form simple sentences.

• **Silent 'e'**: Briefly introducing the concept of silent 'e' if relevant to the words identified (e.g., 'cake', 'kite').

• **Consistency**: Regular practice of phonics through Picture Talk leads to better reading and spelling outcomes.

• **Punctuation Awareness**: Understanding that sentences start with a capital letter and end with a full stop.
• **Oral Expression**: Encouraging students to speak in full, grammatically correct sentences.
• **Listening Comprehension**: Understanding the descriptions provided by the teacher or other students.
• **Vocabulary Retention**: Using visual cues to help students remember new words long after the lesson.
