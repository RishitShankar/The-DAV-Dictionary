• **Chapter Overview**: The chapter "Hanuman" introduces students to the legendary figure of Lord Hanuman, a central character in the Indian epic, the Ramayana.
• **Historical Context**: Hanuman is depicted as a powerful devotee of Lord Rama, embodying strength, devotion, and selfless service.
• **Character Profile**: Hanuman is known as the 'Vanara' (monkey) god, possessing extraordinary physical prowess and supernatural abilities.
• **Divine Origin**: According to mythology, Hanuman is considered an incarnation of Lord Shiva, born to Anjana and Kesari.
• **Symbolism**: He represents the perfect balance of physical power (Bala) and intellectual wisdom (Buddhi).
• **Core Theme**: The chapter emphasizes the values of loyalty, courage, and unwavering faith in one’s duty.
• **Key Attribute**: Hanuman is often referred to as 'Bajrangbali', signifying a body as strong as lightning or a diamond.
• **Role in Ramayana**: He plays a pivotal role in the search for Sita, Rama’s wife, who was abducted by the demon king Ravana.
• **Supernatural Ability**: Hanuman possesses the power of 'Anima' (becoming infinitely small) and 'Mahima' (becoming infinitely large).
• **Flight Capability**: He is famous for his ability to fly across vast oceans, a feat he performed to reach the island of Lanka.
• **Devotion (Bhakti)**: His life serves as a lesson on how to serve a leader or a cause with complete dedication and humility.
• **Physical Appearance**: He is typically depicted with a monkey face, a long tail, and a muscular physique.
• **Cultural Significance**: Hanuman is worshipped across India as a protector against evil and a source of inner strength.
• **Moral Lesson**: The chapter teaches young students that true strength is best used for helping others and upholding righteousness.
• **Literary Style**: The text uses simple, narrative language suitable for Class 2 students to understand the greatness of Hanuman.

• **The Birth of Hanuman**: Hanuman was born in a cave to his mother, Anjana, who was a celestial being (Apsara).
• **Childhood Feat**: As a child, Hanuman mistook the sun for a ripe fruit and attempted to leap into the sky to eat it.
• **Divine Intervention**: Lord Indra, the king of gods, struck Hanuman with his thunderbolt (Vajra) to stop him from consuming the sun.
• **The Resulting Injury**: The thunderbolt hit Hanuman’s jaw, leaving a permanent mark, which is why he is named 'Hanuman' (meaning 'one with a disfigured jaw').
• **Blessings Received**: Following the incident, various gods bestowed blessings upon Hanuman, granting him immortality and invincibility.
• **The Vayu Connection**: Hanuman is also known as 'Pavanputra', the son of Vayu (the Wind God), which explains his speed and agility.
• **Educational Value**: The story of his childhood teaches children about curiosity and the boundless energy of youth.
• **Meeting Lord Rama**: Hanuman first met Lord Rama and his brother Lakshmana while they were searching for Sita in the forest.
• **Recognition**: Hanuman immediately recognized Rama as the divine incarnation and pledged his life to his service.
• **The Bond of Friendship**: The meeting marked the beginning of a lifelong alliance between the Vanara army and Lord Rama.
• **Language and Communication**: Hanuman is described as a master of the Vedas and a highly eloquent speaker, despite his animal form.
• **The Search Mission**: Rama entrusted Hanuman with the crucial task of finding Sita’s location in the kingdom of Lanka.
• **The Leap across the Ocean**: Hanuman jumped across the vast sea to reach Lanka, demonstrating his immense power and determination.
• **Overcoming Obstacles**: During his flight, he encountered various challenges, including sea monsters, which he overcame with intelligence.
• **Strategic Thinking**: Hanuman did not just use force; he used his wit to navigate the dangers of the enemy territory.

• **Arrival in Lanka**: Upon reaching Lanka, Hanuman shrunk his size to remain undetected by the demon guards.
• **Exploration**: He searched the entire city of Lanka, including the palace of Ravana, to locate Sita.
• **The Ashoka Vatika**: Hanuman eventually found Sita sitting under an Ashoka tree, grieving for her husband.
• **The Token of Love**: To prove his identity to Sita, Hanuman presented her with Lord Rama’s ring.
• **Reassurance**: He comforted Sita, telling her that Rama would soon arrive to rescue her from captivity.
• **The Destruction of the Garden**: Before leaving, Hanuman destroyed the beautiful Ashoka Vatika to show the power of Rama’s messenger.
• **Confrontation**: Hanuman was captured by Ravana’s soldiers and brought before the demon king.
• **The Tail Incident**: Ravana ordered his soldiers to set Hanuman’s tail on fire as a punishment for his actions.
• **The Burning of Lanka**: Hanuman used his burning tail to set the entire city of Lanka on fire, showcasing his defiance.
• **Return to Rama**: After completing his mission, Hanuman returned to Rama and gave him the news of Sita’s safety.
• **The War of Righteousness**: Hanuman fought bravely in the great battle between Rama’s army and Ravana’s forces.
• **Saving Lakshmana**: When Lakshmana was critically wounded, Hanuman flew to the Himalayas to fetch the Sanjeevani herb.
• **The Mountain Lift**: Unable to identify the specific herb, Hanuman lifted the entire mountain and brought it to the battlefield.
• **The Value of Service**: This act highlights Hanuman’s willingness to go to any lengths to save those he loves.
• **Victory of Good over Evil**: Hanuman’s efforts were instrumental in Rama’s victory over Ravana, restoring peace to the world.
