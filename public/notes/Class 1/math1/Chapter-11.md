• **Concept of Subtraction:** Subtraction is the mathematical process of taking away, removing, or finding the difference between two quantities.
• **The Minus Sign:** The symbol ‘–’ is known as the minus sign, which represents the operation of subtraction in a mathematical expression.
• **The Equals Sign:** The symbol ‘=’ is known as the equals sign, which indicates the result or the final value after the subtraction process.
• **Difference:** The result obtained after subtracting one number from another is technically referred to as the difference.
• **Decreasing Order:** Subtraction always results in a number that is smaller than the original starting number (the minuend).
• **Minuend:** The first number in a subtraction sentence, which represents the total amount before any items are taken away.
• **Subtrahend:** The second number in a subtraction sentence, which represents the specific quantity being removed or subtracted.
• **Counting Backwards:** A fundamental mental math strategy where one starts at the minuend and counts backward by the value of the subtrahend to find the difference.
• **Number Line Utility:** A number line is a visual tool where jumping backward from a starting point helps students visualize the subtraction process.
• **Concrete Objects:** Using physical manipulatives like beads, blocks, or fingers is the primary method for Class 1 students to understand the removal of items.
• **Subtraction as Comparison:** Subtraction is used to compare two groups to determine how many more or how many fewer items one group has compared to the other.
• **The Concept of 'Zero':** Subtracting zero from any number leaves the number unchanged because nothing is being removed from the original set.
• **Subtracting a Number from Itself:** When a number is subtracted from itself, the result is always zero, as the entire set is removed.
• **Horizontal Subtraction:** Writing subtraction problems in a line, such as 8 – 3 = 5, is known as horizontal notation.
• **Vertical Subtraction:** Writing subtraction problems in a column, with the minuend on top and the subtrahend below, is known as vertical notation.

• **The 'Left' Concept:** The term "left" signifies the remaining quantity after a portion of the original group has been removed.
• **Number Bonds:** Subtraction is the inverse of addition; knowing that 3 + 2 = 5 helps students understand that 5 – 2 = 3.
• **Systematic Approach:** Always identify the total (minuend) first before attempting to remove the subtrahend.
• **One-to-One Correspondence:** When subtracting, each item removed must be counted individually to ensure the accuracy of the final difference.

• **Accuracy over Speed:** In the early stages of learning, it is more important to count carefully than to rush the subtraction process.

• **Mathematical Language:** Using the phrase "5 minus 2 equals 3" helps reinforce the relationship between the numbers and the operation.

• **Subtraction Stories:** Creating short narratives helps students translate real-world situations into mathematical subtraction expressions.

• **The Number Line as a Tool:** A number line is a straight line with numbers placed at equal intervals, used to perform subtraction by moving left.
• **Leftward Movement:** On a number line, moving to the left represents a decrease in value, which is the essence of subtraction.
• **Starting Point:** Always place your pencil on the minuend (the larger number) before beginning the subtraction process on the number line.
• **Counting Jumps:** The number of jumps to the left corresponds to the subtrahend (the number being taken away).
• **Landing Point:** The number where you land after completing the jumps is the difference.
• **Visualizing Distance:** The number line helps students see that subtraction is essentially finding the distance between two numbers.
• **Precision:** Ensure that each jump on the number line lands exactly on the next number to maintain accuracy.
• **Number Line Limitations:** While useful for small numbers, the number line is a foundational tool that transitions into mental math.
• **Interactive Learning:** Using a physical number line on the floor allows students to jump and kinesthetically learn subtraction.
• **Number Line vs. Objects:** Objects are better for understanding "taking away," while the number line is better for understanding "difference."
• **Consistent Practice:** Regular use of the number line helps students internalize the sequence of numbers in reverse.
• **Number Line Orientation:** Number lines are typically drawn from left to right, with numbers increasing in value.
• **Subtraction Direction:** Because subtraction decreases value, the direction of movement on the number line is always toward the smaller numbers.
• **Zero on the Number Line:** The number line includes zero, which is the starting point for many subtraction problems.
• **Mastery:** Once a student can use the number line effectively, they are ready to move toward mental subtraction.

• **Common Mistakes:** A frequent error is confusing the minuend and the subtrahend, leading to an impossible subtraction.
• **Reversing Numbers:** Students sometimes subtract the larger number from the smaller number, which is not possible in the set of whole numbers for Class 1.
• **Miscounting:** Skipping a number while counting backward is a common cause of errors in subtraction.
• **Forgetting to Subtract:** Sometimes students accidentally add instead of subtract, especially when the numbers are close.
• **Importance of Checking:** Always re-read the problem to ensure the correct numbers were used in the correct order.
• **Visual Verification:** If a result seems wrong, use objects to verify the subtraction process.
• **Teacher Guidance:** Teachers play a vital role in identifying and correcting these common misconceptions early.
• **Peer Review:** Working in pairs allows students to check each other's work and identify errors in logic.
• **Systematic Checking:** Developing a habit of checking work prevents the repetition of simple mistakes.
• **Focus on Process:** Emphasize the process of subtraction rather than just the final answer to ensure long-term learning.
• **Patience:** Learning subtraction takes time, and mistakes are a natural part of the learning process.
• **Positive Reinforcement:** Encouraging students when they identify and fix their own mistakes builds a growth mindset.
• **Error Analysis:** Discussing why a mistake occurred helps students avoid the same error in the future.
• **Clear Presentation:** Writing numbers clearly and neatly helps prevent errors caused by messy handwriting.
• **Structured Practice:** Using worksheets with clear instructions helps students maintain focus and accuracy.
