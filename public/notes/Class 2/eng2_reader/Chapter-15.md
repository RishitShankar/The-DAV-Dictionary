• **Chapter Overview:** The poem "When I was a Baby" explores the theme of growth, development, and the transition from infancy to childhood.
• **Core Concept:** The speaker reflects on their past state as an infant, highlighting the physical and behavioral differences between being a baby and being a school-going child.
• **Theme of Maturation:** The poem emphasizes the natural progression of human life, focusing on how abilities change over time.
• **Perspective:** The poem is written from the first-person perspective, allowing the reader to empathize with the speaker's personal journey.
• **Tone:** The tone is nostalgic, innocent, and observant, suitable for the developmental stage of a Class 2 student.
• **Key Vocabulary - Baby:** A very young child who is not yet able to walk or talk fluently.
• **Key Vocabulary - Growth:** The process of increasing in physical size and developing new skills.
• **Key Vocabulary - Change:** The act of becoming different over a period of time.
• **Developmental Milestone - Mobility:** Babies are characterized by their inability to walk, often crawling or being carried.
• **Developmental Milestone - Communication:** Babies communicate primarily through crying or making simple sounds rather than complex sentences.
• **Developmental Milestone - Dependency:** Infants rely entirely on caregivers for feeding, cleaning, and movement.
• **Contrast - Physical Size:** The speaker notes that they were much smaller in size when they were a baby compared to their current stature.
• **Contrast - Strength:** Babies possess limited physical strength compared to a growing child who can run and play.
• **Contrast - Diet:** Infants typically consume milk or soft foods, whereas children eat a wider variety of solid foods.
• **Contrast - Clothing:** Babies wear diapers and simple garments, while children wear school uniforms and play clothes.
• **Educational Value:** The chapter teaches students to appreciate their own growth and the milestones they have achieved.
