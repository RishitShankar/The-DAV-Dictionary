• **Concept of Time:** Time is a continuous measurement of the sequence of events that happen from the past, through the present, and into the future.
• **Daily Routine:** A daily routine is the sequence of activities performed by a person throughout the day, starting from waking up to going to sleep.
• **Morning:** The time of day when the sun rises and the day begins; typically associated with waking up, brushing teeth, and eating breakfast.
• **Afternoon:** The period of the day between noon and evening, usually when the sun is at its highest point in the sky.
• **Evening:** The time of day when the sun begins to set and the sky starts to darken; often associated with playtime or returning home.
• **Night:** The period of darkness when the sun is not visible; it is the time for sleeping and resting.
• **Sequence of Events:** Understanding that events happen in a specific order, such as "before" and "after."
• **Before:** A term used to describe an event that happened earlier in a sequence of time.
• **After:** A term used to describe an event that happens later in a sequence of time.
• **Chronology:** The arrangement of events or dates in the order of their occurrence.
• **Sunlight:** A natural indicator of time; bright light signifies daytime, while darkness signifies nighttime.
• **Routine Order:** The logical flow of a day: Waking up, brushing, bathing, eating breakfast, going to school, studying, playing, and sleeping.
• **Duration:** The length of time an activity takes to complete, such as a short time for brushing teeth versus a long time for sleeping.
• **Past:** Events that have already occurred and are finished.
• **Present:** Events that are happening right now at this very moment.
• **Future:** Events that are yet to happen or are expected to occur later.
• **Time Awareness:** Developing the ability to distinguish between different parts of the day based on environmental changes.
• **Daily Cycle:** The repeating pattern of day and night caused by the rotation of the Earth.

• **The Clock:** A mechanical or digital device used to measure, keep, and indicate time.
• **Clock Face:** The front part of an analog clock, also known as the dial, which displays the time.
• **Clock Hands:** The moving pointers on an analog clock that indicate the hours and minutes.
• **Hour Hand:** The shorter hand on the clock that moves slowly to point toward the current hour.
• **Minute Hand:** The longer hand on the clock that moves faster to indicate the minutes passed in an hour.
• **Numbers on a Clock:** A standard analog clock face features numbers from 1 to 12 arranged in a circle.
• **Full Circle:** The hour hand completes one full rotation around the clock face in 12 hours.
• **Twelve-Hour Cycle:** A clock face represents 12 hours, and the hour hand goes around twice in a single 24-hour day.
• **Reading the Hour:** When the minute hand points to 12, the hour hand points directly to a number, indicating an "o'clock" time.
• **O'Clock:** A term used to indicate the exact hour without any additional minutes.
• **Analog Clock:** A clock that uses hands to show the time on a circular face.
• **Digital Clock:** A clock that displays the time using numbers (digits) rather than hands.
• **Clock Mechanism:** The internal gears or electronic components that drive the movement of the hands or the change of digits.
• **Positioning:** The hour hand is always shorter and thicker than the minute hand to help distinguish between the two.
• **Timekeeping:** The process of recording or tracking the passage of time using standardized units.
• **Clock Maintenance:** Ensuring the clock has a power source, such as a battery, to keep the hands moving accurately.
• **Standardization:** All clocks follow the same numerical layout to ensure time is read consistently everywhere.
• **Visualizing Time:** Using the clock face as a tool to map out the progression of the day.

• **The Hour:** A unit of time equal to 60 minutes or 1/24th of a day.
• **One O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 1 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Two O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 2 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Three O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 3 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Four O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 4 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Five O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 5 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Six O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 6 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Seven O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 7 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Eight O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 8 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Nine O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 9 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Ten O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 10 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Eleven O'Clock:** The time when the hour hand points to 11 and the minute hand points to 12.
• **Twelve O'Clock:** The time when both the hour hand and the minute hand point to 12.
• **Noon:** The specific time of 12:00 in the daytime, often associated with the sun being at its highest point.
• **Midnight:** The specific time of 12:00 at night, marking the start of a new day.
• **Reading Accuracy:** Always check the position of the short hand first to identify the hour.
• **Clock Direction:** The hands of a clock move in a "clockwise" direction, from 1 towards 12.
• **Consistency:** Each hour on the clock face represents an equal interval of time.

• **Days of the Week:** A cycle of seven days that repeats continuously.
• **Monday:** The first day of the school week for many students.
• **Tuesday:** The second day of the week.
• **Wednesday:** The third day of the week, often considered the middle of the work/school week.
• **Thursday:** The fourth day of the week.
• **Friday:** The fifth day of the week, often the last day of the school week.
• **Saturday:** The sixth day of the week, usually part of the weekend.
• **Sunday:** The seventh day of the week, usually a holiday or day of rest.
• **Weekend:** The combination of Saturday and Sunday, typically free from school or work.
• **Weekly Cycle:** The sequence of seven days that repeats every week.
• **Yesterday:** The day that came immediately before today.
• **Today:** The current day.
• **Tomorrow:** The day that will come immediately after today.
• **Calendar:** A chart or device used to track days, weeks, and months.
• **Day Sequence:** Understanding that after Sunday comes Monday, and the cycle continues.
• **Planning:** Using the days of the week to schedule activities, such as sports on Saturday or school on Monday.
• **Time Management:** Organizing tasks based on the days of the week to maintain a balanced lifestyle.
• **Weekly Routine:** Identifying recurring events that happen on specific days, like a weekly assembly or a library visit.

• **Months of the Year:** A division of the year into 12 distinct periods.
• **January:** The first month of the year, typically cold in many regions.
• **February:** The second month of the year, known for having the fewest days.
• **March:** The third month of the year, often marking the beginning of spring.
• **April:** The fourth month of the year.
• **May:** The fifth month of the year.
• **June:** The sixth month of the year, often associated with the start of summer.
• **July:** The seventh month of the year.
• **August:** The eighth month of the year.
• **September:** The ninth month of the year, often marking the start of a new academic term.
• **October:** The tenth month of the year.
• **November:** The eleventh month of the year.
• **December:** The twelfth and final month of the year.
• **Annual Cycle:** The 12 months repeat every year in the same order.
• **Year:** A period of 12 months, representing one full orbit of the Earth around the Sun.
• **Month Duration:** Months have varying lengths, with some having 30 days, some 31, and February having 28 or 29.
• **Seasonality:** Months are often grouped into seasons based on weather patterns.
• **Calendar Usage:** Months help us track birthdays, festivals, and holidays throughout the year.

• **Seasons:** The natural divisions of the year based on changes in weather, ecology, and daylight hours.
• **Summer:** A season characterized by hot weather and long days.
• **Winter:** A season characterized by cold weather and short days.
• **Spring:** A season of transition where plants begin to grow and temperatures become mild.
• **Autumn (Fall):** A season where leaves change color and fall from trees as the weather cools.
• **Monsoon (Rainy Season):** A season characterized by heavy rainfall, common in many tropical regions.
• **Seasonal Impact:** Seasons influence what we wear, what we eat, and the activities we participate in.
• **Weather Patterns:** The atmospheric conditions that define each season.
• **Clothing:** We adapt our clothing to the season, such as sweaters in winter and light cotton clothes in summer.
• **Nature's Clock:** Plants and animals often follow seasonal cues for growth, migration, and hibernation.
• **Cyclical Nature:** Seasons follow a predictable, repeating order year after year.
• **Temperature Variations:** The primary factor that distinguishes one season from another.
• **Daylight Changes:** The amount of sunlight varies significantly between summer and winter.
• **Seasonal Festivals:** Many cultures celebrate specific festivals that are tied to the timing of seasons.
• **Agricultural Cycle:** Farmers plant and harvest crops based on the seasonal cycle.
• **Environmental Awareness:** Observing how the environment changes helps children understand the passage of time.
• **Adaptation:** Humans and animals adapt their behaviors to survive and thrive in different seasons.
• **Yearly Progression:** Seasons are a way to measure the long-term progression of time within a year.

• **Measuring Time:** The act of quantifying the duration of events using standard units.
• **Seconds:** The smallest standard unit of time commonly used in daily life.
• **Minutes:** A unit of time equal to 60 seconds.
• **Hours:** A unit of time equal to 60 minutes.
• **Days:** A unit of time equal to 24 hours.
• **Weeks:** A unit of time equal to 7 days.
• **Months:** A unit of time ranging from 28 to 31 days.
• **Years:** A unit of time equal to 12 months or 365/366 days.
• **Stopwatch:** A handheld device used to measure the exact time elapsed for a specific activity.
• **Timer:** A device that counts down from a set time to zero, used for tasks like cooking or games.
• **Punctuality:** The habit of being on time for appointments and responsibilities.
• **Time Estimation:** The ability to guess how long an activity will take without looking at a clock.
• **Speed of Events:** Some events happen quickly (like a blink), while others take a long time (like growing up).
• **Time Comparison:** Comparing the duration of two activities to see which one takes longer.
• **Standard Units:** Using universal units ensures that everyone understands the duration of time in the same way.
• **Timekeeping Tools:** Clocks, watches, calendars, and sundials are all tools for tracking time.
• **Historical Timekeeping:** Ancient civilizations used the sun, moon, and stars to track time before mechanical clocks were invented.
• **Importance of Time:** Time is a limited resource that must be managed effectively to complete daily tasks.

• **Later:** An event that will happen after another event.

• **Slowly:** Taking a long time to complete an action.

• **Schedule:** A plan for carrying out a process or procedure, giving lists of intended events and times.
• **Pace:** The speed at which an activity is performed.
• **Moment:** A very brief period of time.
• **Era:** A long and distinct period of history.
• **Time-Related Phrases:** Common expressions like "in a minute," "an hour ago," or "next week."
• **Communication:** Using time vocabulary helps in coordinating plans with others.
