• **Division: Definition**: Division is the mathematical process of distributing a total number of items into equal groups or parts.
• **Concept of Equal Sharing**: Division is essentially the act of sharing a collection of objects so that every group receives the exact same amount.
• **Concept of Equal Grouping**: Division involves forming groups of a specific size from a larger total to see how many such groups can be created.
• **Repeated Subtraction**: Division is defined as repeated subtraction, where the same number is subtracted from the total until zero is reached.
• **The Division Symbol**: The symbol '÷' is used to represent the operation of division in mathematical expressions.
• **Dividend**: The **Dividend** is the total number of items that are being divided or shared among groups.
• **Divisor**: The **Divisor** is the number by which the dividend is being divided, representing the size of each group or the number of groups.
• **Quotient**: The **Quotient** is the result obtained after performing the division operation, representing the final answer.
• **Division Fact**: A division fact is a mathematical statement that shows the relationship between the dividend, divisor, and quotient.
• **Relationship with Multiplication**: Division is the inverse operation of multiplication, meaning they are opposite processes.
• **Multiplication Table Dependency**: Knowing multiplication tables is essential for solving division problems quickly and accurately.
• **Zero Property of Division**: When zero is divided by any non-zero number, the quotient is always zero (e.g., 0 ÷ 5 = 0).
• **Division by One**: Any number divided by one results in the number itself as the quotient (e.g., 8 ÷ 1 = 8).
• **Division by Itself**: Any non-zero number divided by itself results in a quotient of one (e.g., 9 ÷ 9 = 1).
• **Undefined Division**: Division by zero is not possible in mathematics because it is undefined.

• **Long Division Method**: A systematic way to solve division problems by breaking the dividend into smaller, manageable parts.
• **The Division Bracket**: The symbol '⟌' is used in long division to separate the divisor from the dividend.
• **Step-by-Step Process**: Long division follows a repeating cycle of four main steps: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, and Bring Down.
• **Divide Step**: Determine how many times the divisor fits into the first digit (or digits) of the dividend.
• **Multiply Step**: Multiply the quotient digit by the divisor to find the value to be subtracted from the dividend.
• **Subtract Step**: Subtract the product obtained from the relevant part of the dividend to find the remainder.
• **Bring Down Step**: Bring down the next digit from the dividend to continue the division process until all digits are used.
• **Remainder**: The **Remainder** is the amount left over after division when the dividend is not perfectly divisible by the divisor.
• **Remainder Constraint**: The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor; if it is larger, the division is incomplete.
• **Perfect Division**: When the remainder is zero, the dividend is said to be perfectly divisible by the divisor.
• **Place Value Importance**: Understanding place value is crucial because division starts from the highest place value (leftmost digit) of the dividend.
• **Mental Math**: Simple division problems can often be solved mentally by recalling multiplication facts.
• **Grouping Strategy**: Visualizing objects in sets helps in understanding the logic behind division for smaller numbers.
• **Array Representation**: An array is a visual arrangement of objects in rows and columns that helps illustrate division facts.
• **Verification**: Division can be verified by multiplying the quotient by the divisor and adding the remainder, which should equal the dividend.

• **Division of 2-Digit Numbers**: Dividing a two-digit number by a single-digit number involves working through the tens place first, then the ones place.
• **Tens Place Division**: Start by dividing the digit in the tens place of the dividend by the divisor.
• **Carrying Over**: If the tens digit is smaller than the divisor, combine it with the ones digit to form a larger number for division.
• **Ones Place Division**: After processing the tens, bring down the ones digit and perform division again.
• **No Remainder Scenarios**: In many Class 3 problems, the division results in a zero remainder, indicating exact distribution.
• **With Remainder Scenarios**: Some problems result in a non-zero remainder, which is recorded as the final leftover amount.
• **Standard Form**: Writing division in the form 'Dividend ÷ Divisor = Quotient' is the standard way to present the operation.
• **Horizontal Division**: Writing the equation in a single line is useful for simple mental calculations.
• **Vertical Division**: Writing the equation using the long division bracket is the standard format for multi-step calculations.
• **Checking Work**: To check if 12 ÷ 3 = 4 is correct, multiply 4 (quotient) by 3 (divisor) to get 12 (dividend).
• **Division as Sharing**: If 15 candies are shared among 3 children, each child gets 5 candies; this is 15 ÷ 3 = 5.
• **Division as Grouping**: If you have 20 pencils and put them in packs of 4, you get 5 packs; this is 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
• **Vocabulary**: Terms like 'each', 'every', 'equally', and 'per' are indicators that a division operation is required.
• **Problem Solving**: Identifying the total and the number of groups is the first step in solving word problems involving division.
• **Logical Reasoning**: Division helps in determining how many items are needed to fulfill a specific requirement for a group.

• **Division with Remainders**: A situation where the dividend is not a multiple of the divisor.
• **The Concept of 'Leftover'**: The remainder represents the items that cannot be placed into a full, equal group.
• **Notation**: A remainder is often written as 'R' followed by the number (e.g., 17 ÷ 5 = 3 R 2).
• **Division Algorithm**: The formula Dividend = (Divisor × Quotient) + Remainder is the fundamental rule of division.
• **Applying the Algorithm**: This formula is used to check the correctness of any division problem, including those with remainders.
• **Example of Checking**: For 17 ÷ 5 = 3 R 2, check: (5 × 3) + 2 = 15 + 2 = 17.
• **Remainder Property**: The remainder must always be less than the divisor (Remainder < Divisor).
• **Zero Remainder**: A remainder of zero indicates that the division is exact or "even."
• **Non-Zero Remainder**: A remainder greater than zero indicates that the division is "uneven."
• **Visualizing Remainders**: If you have 7 cookies and share them among 2 people, each gets 3, and 1 is left over.
• **Handling Remainders in Real Life**: In real-life situations, the remainder might be discarded, or it might require an extra group.
• **Division Logic**: Understanding that division is about creating the largest possible equal groups.
• **Practice Importance**: Repeated practice with remainders helps in mastering the division algorithm.
• **Error Prevention**: Always double-check the subtraction step in long division to ensure the remainder is correct.
• **Mathematical Precision**: Precision in division is vital for building a strong foundation for higher-level mathematics.
