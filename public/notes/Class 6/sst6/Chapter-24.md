• **Introduction to Chapter 24**: Welcome to Chapter 24 of Class 6 Social Science (We and Our World: Social Science), titled "Our Urban Government," published by the DAV College Managing Committee for Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board.
• **Concept of Urban Areas**: **Urban areas** refer to cities and large towns characterized by high population density, dense residential and commercial settlements, and advanced infrastructural facilities compared to rural areas.
• **Shift from Rural to Urban**: While rural local government is managed by Panchayati Raj institutions (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zilla Parishad), urban local governance requires a completely different administrative framework to handle urban-specific challenges.
• **Nature of Urban Problems**: Urban areas face distinct civic challenges including severe traffic congestion, massive garbage generation, housing shortages, slum management, air and water pollution, and high demand for clean drinking water.
• **Need for Local Bodies**: **Urban Local Government** refers to self-governing bodies established by state laws to manage civic affairs, public services, and infrastructure development in cities and towns.
• **Historical Roots in India**: Local self-government in urban areas has deep historical roots, evolving from ancient municipal administrations (such as those during the Mauryan and Chola periods) to modern institutions established during the British Raj.
• **Lord Ripon’s Contribution**: The foundation of modern municipal governance in India was significantly shaped by Lord Ripon's famous Resolution of 1882, often hailed as the "Magna Carta" of local self-government in India.
• **Constitutional Recognition**: Urban local bodies gained constitutional status, institutional stability, and uniform structure across India through the **74th Constitutional Amendment Act of 1992**.
• **Introduction of Schedule XII**: The 74th Amendment added the **Twelfth Schedule** to the Constitution of India, which explicitly outlines 18 functional items entrusted to urban local bodies for effective local planning and development.
• **Diversity in Urban Bodies**: The structure of urban government in India is not uniform; it varies depending on the size of the population, geographical area, economic importance, and historical background of the specific urban settlement.
• **Classification of Urban Bodies**: Urban local bodies in India are broadly classified into three main types: Nagar Panchayats (for transitional areas), Municipal Councils (for smaller cities), and Municipal Corporations (for large metropolitan cities).
• **Role of State Governments**: State legislatures enact specific Municipal Acts that empower these urban bodies, define their territorial jurisdictions, determine financial powers, and regulate their operational mechanisms.
• **Democratic Foundation**: Urban local governments operate on democratic principles, where citizens elect their representatives through direct, periodic, and secret-ballot elections based on universal adult suffrage.
• **Civic Participation**: These institutions foster grassroots democracy by encouraging active citizen participation, public accountability, and community involvement in urban planning and resource allocation.
• **Focus of the Chapter**: Throughout this comprehensive chapter, students will explore the structural hierarchy, administrative functions, financial resources, and operational challenges of India’s vibrant urban local governance system.

• **The Three Tiers of Urban Government**: India’s urban local governance is structured hierarchically into three distinct categories based on population size, ensuring appropriate administrative attention for every scale of settlement.
• **Nagar Panchayat (Notified Area Committee / City Board)**: A **Nagar Panchayat** is established in areas in transition from a rural area to an urban area, typically having a population ranging from small towns to semi-urban pockets.
• **Characteristics of Nagar Panchayats**: These bodies bridge the gap between rural panchayats and full-fledged municipalities, providing basic civic amenities like street lighting, minor sanitation, and drinking water supply to rapidly growing semi-urban settlements.
• **Municipal Council (Municipal Board / Nagar Palika)**: A **Municipal Council** (often called Nagar Palika) is established for smaller cities and towns where the population generally ranges from 20,000 to several lakhs, depending on state-specific legislations.
• **Functions of Municipal Councils**: Municipal Councils handle broader civic responsibilities than Nagar Panchayats, including road maintenance, public health centers, primary education, local drainage systems, and firefighting services.
• **Municipal Corporation (Nagar Nigam)**: A **Municipal Corporation** is the highest tier of urban local government, established in large, densely populated metropolitan cities and major urban hubs like Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, and Bengaluru.
• **Scale of Municipal Corporations**: Because of their massive administrative scale, high revenue generation, and complex infrastructure requirements, Municipal Corporations enjoy greater financial and executive autonomy compared to smaller municipalities.
• **Territorial Division (Wards)**: Every urban local body jurisdiction—whether a Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, or Municipal Corporation—is geographically divided into smaller administrative units known as **wards**.
• **Ward Representation**: Each ward elects one representative to the municipal body, ensuring direct, localized representation for every neighborhood and community within the city.
• **Title of Ward Representatives**: Elected members representing individual wards in a Municipal Corporation or Municipal Council are officially designated as **Councillors** or Corporators (referred to as *Parshad* in Hindi).
• **Term of Office**: Elected representatives in urban local bodies hold office for a fixed term of **five years**, subject to earlier dissolution or disqualification under state municipal laws.
• **Electoral Qualifications**: To contest elections for an urban local body, a candidate must be an Indian citizen, must have attained the prescribed minimum age (usually 21 years), and must be a registered voter within the municipal ward.
• **Reservation of Seats**: To ensure social justice and inclusive governance, the Constitution mandates the reservation of seats in urban local bodies for Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), and women.
• **Reservation for Women**: At least **one-third (33%)** of total elected seats and leadership positions in urban local bodies are constitutionally reserved exclusively for women, empowering them in local decision-making.
• **Delimitation of Wards**: The process of fixing the boundaries of territorial wards based on population changes and census data is known as **delimitation**, usually managed by the State Election Commission.

• **The Political Executive Head (Mayor)**: In a Municipal Corporation, the ceremonial and political head of the city is known as the **Mayor** (referred to as *Mahayor*), who is elected by the municipal councillors from among themselves or directly by citizens in certain states.
• **Role of the Mayor**: The Mayor presides over meetings of the general body of the Corporation, represents the city at official functions, guides policy formulation, and serves as the primary public face of the municipal administration.
• **Deputy Mayor**: To assist the Mayor and perform mayoral duties in their absence, the corporation elects a **Deputy Mayor** from among the councillors.
• **President of Municipal Council**: In a Municipal Council (Nagar Palika), the political head is designated as the **President** or Chairperson, assisted by a Vice-President.
• **The General Body**: The **General Body** comprises all elected councillors along with nominated members (if any) and forms the supreme legislative and policy-making assembly of the urban local body.
• **Committees System**: Due to the vast volume of specialized civic work, municipal bodies establish various statutory and standing committees (such as Water Supply Committee, Sanitation Committee, Finance Committee, and Works Committee) for efficient functioning.
• **Role of Standing Committees**: These committees examine specific administrative issues in detail, prepare policy recommendations, and submit reports to the general body for final approval and implementation.
• **The Administrative Head (Municipal Commissioner)**: While the Mayor represents the political executive, the actual day-to-day administration and execution of policies are headed by a senior bureaucrat known as the **Municipal Commissioner**.
• **Appointment of Municipal Commissioner**: The Municipal Commissioner is an **IAS (Indian Administrative Service)** officer or a senior state civil service officer appointed directly by the state government, not elected by the public.
• **Executive Authority**: The Municipal Commissioner controls the administrative machinery, prepares the annual municipal budget, implements decisions passed by the general body, and supervises all municipal employees and departments.
• **Separation of Powers**: This dual structure ensures a healthy balance between democratic representation (councillors and Mayor) and professional administrative expertise (Municipal Commissioner and civil servants).
• **Coordination and Accountability**: The Municipal Commissioner is directly accountable to the General Body and the State Government, ensuring that policy decisions made by elected representatives are translated into effective ground execution.
• **Municipal Secretariat**: Assisting the Commissioner is a vast bureaucratic network comprising engineers, sanitation officers, health inspectors, accountants, and clerical staff organized into specialized municipal departments.
• **Departmental Organization**: Key departments within a Municipal Corporation typically include Engineering, Public Health, Education, Revenue, Fire Services, Town Planning, and Information Technology.
• **Administrative Efficiency**: The clear demarcation of duties between elected policy-makers and appointed administrators prevents political interference in routine bureaucratic tasks while maintaining democratic oversight.

• **Categorization of Municipal Functions**: The duties and responsibilities discharged by urban local bodies are broadly classified into two major categories: **Obligatory (Mandatory) Functions** and **Discretionary Functions**.
• **Definition of Obligatory Functions**: **Obligatory functions** are those essential civic services that an urban local body is legally bound to provide to its residents without exception, funded primarily through mandatory revenues.
• **Water Supply**: Ensuring an uninterrupted, safe, and treated supply of drinking water for domestic and commercial consumption across all city wards is a primary obligatory duty of municipal bodies.
• **Sanitation and Cleanliness**: Maintaining public hygiene through regular sweeping of streets, collection, transportation, and scientific disposal of solid waste and garbage is an indispensable obligatory function.
• **Drainage and Sewerage**: Constructing, maintaining, and desilting underground drainage networks, storm-water drains, and sewage treatment plants to prevent waterlogging and waterborne diseases is strictly mandatory.
• **Public Health and Hospitals**: Establishing and running municipal dispensaries, maternity homes, vaccination centers, and implementing vector-borne disease control programs (like anti-malaria and anti-dengue drives) are obligatory duties.
• **Street Lighting**: Installing, repairing, and maintaining electric street lights and public illumination across all roads, lanes, and public squares to ensure nighttime safety and security.
• **Registration of Births and Deaths**: Maintaining official, legally recognized civil registration records of all births, stillbirths, and deaths occurring within the municipal limits is a mandatory statutory obligation.
• **Road Infrastructure**: Constructing, widening, carpeting, and repairing local city roads, bridges, culverts, subways, and footpaths, along with removing illegal encroachments from public pathways.
• **Firefighting Services**: Maintaining modern fire stations, specialized vehicles, equipment, and trained personnel to tackle fire emergencies, industrial accidents, and rescue operations within the city.
• **Definition of Discretionary Functions**: **Discretionary functions** are optional or welfare-oriented services that urban local bodies undertake depending on the availability of surplus financial resources and local community priorities.
• **Public Parks and Gardens**: Developing, landscaping, and maintaining green spaces, public parks, children's play areas, botanical gardens, and roadside plantation drives to enhance the urban environment.
• **Libraries and Cultural Centers**: Establishing and running public libraries, reading rooms, museums, art galleries, community halls, and cultural auditoriums to promote education and community engagement.
• **Sports and Recreation**: Constructing and maintaining municipal sports complexes, swimming pools, stadiums, gymnasiums, and organizing community sports tournaments.
• **Public Housing and Slum Improvement**: Undertaking affordable housing projects for economically weaker sections (EWS), slum clearance, and upgrading urban slum infrastructure with basic amenities.
• **Transport and Welfare**: Operating city bus services (public transport), managing animal shelters, establishing night shelters for the homeless, and running cremation grounds and burial grounds.

• **Financial Requirements of Urban Bodies**: To discharge their extensive obligatory and discretionary functions effectively, urban local bodies require substantial, consistent, and sustainable financial resources.
• **Sources of Municipal Revenue**: The income of urban local bodies is derived from multiple streams, broadly categorized into **tax revenues**, **non-tax revenues**, and **government grants/loans**.
• **Tax Revenues as Primary Income**: Taxes levied and collected directly by municipal bodies constitute a major, recurring source of financial independence for local self-governments.
• **Property Tax (House Tax)**: **Property Tax** is levied annually on residential, commercial, and industrial land and buildings within the municipal limits, calculated based on the area, location, and rental value of the property.
• **Professional Tax**: A tax levied on individuals earning an income from professions, trades, callings, or employment within the municipal jurisdiction.
• **Octroi (Historical Context)**: **Octroi** was a historical tax collected on goods entering a town or city for consumption, use, or sale; however, it has been largely abolished and replaced by modern indirect tax frameworks like GST.
• **Toll Tax**: Collected on vehicles using specific municipal infrastructure such as bridges, flyovers, highways, or heavy commercial roads maintained by the local body.
• **Entertainment Tax**: Levied on tickets for movies, theatrical performances, amusement parks, exhibitions, and sporting events held within city boundaries.
• **Non-Tax Revenues**: Income generated from fees, fines, user charges, and commercial assets owned by the municipal corporation rather than through direct taxation.
• **User Charges and Fees**: Fees collected for specific municipal services, such as water connection fees, solid waste collection charges, parking fees in municipal lots, and building plan approval fees.
• **Licensing Fees**: Fees charged for issuing and renewing trade licenses, eating house permits, factory licenses, pet registrations, and hoarding/advertising permissions.
• **Fines and Penalties**: Revenue collected through penalizing citizens for civic violations, such as illegal dumping of garbage, littering, spitting, traffic rule violations within municipal limits, and unauthorized constructions.
• **Income from Municipal Property**: Rent and lease revenue earned from municipal-owned commercial complexes, shops, markets, community marriage halls, and parking spaces.
• **Government Grants and Financial Devolution**: Substantial financial assistance, grants-in-aid, and revenue shares allocated to urban local bodies by the State Government and the Central Government based on recommendations of Finance Commissions.
• **State Finance Commission**: A constitutional body established in every state that reviews the financial position of panchayats and urban local bodies and recommends principles for sharing tax revenues between the state and local governments.

• **The Metropolitan Exception (Mega Cities)**: Major mega-cities like Delhi, Mumbai, and Kolkata possess special governance models due to their enormous population, complex economies, and overlapping administrative jurisdictions.
• **Delhi’s Unique Governance Model**: The National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi features a unique administrative structure shared between the Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD), the Government of NCT of Delhi, and the Central Government.
• **Cantonment Boards**: Areas housing military installations and defense personnel are excluded from municipal jurisdiction and are administered directly by autonomous bodies known as **Cantonment Boards** under the Cantonments Act.
• **Composition of Cantonment Boards**: Cantonment boards comprise elected military and civilian members, nominated military officers, and a Chief Executive Officer (CEO) appointed by the Central Ministry of Defense.
• **Town Improvement Trusts / Development Authorities**: In many growing cities, specialized urban development bodies (such as Delhi Development Authority - DDA, or Lucknow Development Authority - LDA) handle long-term master planning, land acquisition, and mega-infrastructure construction.
• **Coordination Challenges**: Managing large urban centers often requires seamless coordination between municipal corporations, state police departments, electricity boards, water boards, and state transport authorities.
• **Smart Cities Mission**: Launched by the Government of India, the **Smart Cities Mission** aims to drive economic growth and improve quality of life by enabling local area development and harnessing technology, especially Area-Based Development solutions.
• **Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT)**: AMRUT focuses on establishing infrastructure that ensures adequate robust sewage networks, water supply, and urban transport enhancements in hundreds of Indian cities.
• **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (Urban)**: A monumental nationwide cleanliness campaign that revolutionized urban local governance by introducing door-to-door waste collection, segregation at source, mechanized street sweeping, and open-defecation-free (ODF) city ratings.
• **Digital Governance (e-Governance)**: Modern urban local bodies are rapidly adopting digital platforms for citizen services, enabling online property tax payments, birth/death certificate downloads, grievance redressal portals, and building plan sanctions.
• **Public-Private Partnerships (PPP)**: To overcome financial and operational constraints, urban bodies frequently engage in **Public-Private Partnerships** with private enterprises for solid waste management, parking management, and bus transit operations.
• **Role of Civil Society and NGOs**: Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs), Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), and citizen collectives play a vital collaborative role in monitoring civic services, promoting recycling, and voicing neighborhood grievances.
• **Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs)**: RWAs serve as active grassroots bodies connecting individual households to municipal authorities, facilitating community-level waste management, security, and park maintenance.
• **Urbanization Pressures**: Rapid and unplanned rural-to-urban migration places immense stress on municipal finances, housing stock, clean water availability, and traffic management systems across Indian cities.
• **Need for Sustainable Urban Planning**: Future urban governance requires visionary master planning, environmental sustainability, green building norms, public transport prioritization, and climate-resilient infrastructure.

• **Comparative Analysis (Rural vs. Urban Government)**: While both rural and urban local governments form the bedrock of India’s three-tier democratic structure, they exhibit stark differences in scale, structure, and administrative focus.
• **Structural Difference**: Rural governance follows the Panchayati Raj system (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zilla Parishad), whereas urban governance follows the municipal system (Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation).
• **Administrative Head Contrast**: Rural bodies are headed by elected political leaders (Sarpanch, Block Pramukh, Zilla Chairperson) supported by government-appointed secretaries (Gram Panchayat Secretary), whereas large urban corporations feature professional IAS Municipal Commissioners.
• **Revenue Base Disparity**: Urban local bodies generally generate significantly higher revenues through property taxes, user fees, and commercial rents compared to rural bodies, which depend heavily on state and central grants.
• **Infrastructure Complexity**: Urban governance deals with multi-storied buildings, heavy industrial zones, massive vehicular traffic, complex sewage grids, and dense commercial hubs, requiring specialized engineering departments.
• **Demographic Context**: Rural governance addresses agrarian communities, cottage industries, land-use patterns tied to agriculture, and rural livelihood schemes (like MGNREGA), unlike urban governance.
• **Significance of Grassroots Democracy**: Despite structural differences, both systems share the common philosophy of decentralization, empowering local communities to solve their unique problems without excessive dependence on higher governments.
• **Constitutional Safeguards**: Both rural (73rd Amendment) and urban (74th Amendment) local bodies derive their ultimate legal authority, regular election mandates, and reservation policies directly from the Constitution of India.
• **State Election Commission**: Independent constitutional bodies known as **State Election Commissions** are established in every state to conduct free, fair, and timely elections to all local bodies, both rural and urban.
• **State Finance Commissions**: Similarly, State Finance Commissions ensure equitable distribution of financial resources between the state government and all tiers of local self-government.
• **Social Inclusion Mechanisms**: Mandatory reservations for women, Scheduled Castes, and Scheduled Tribes across both rural and urban local bodies ensure that marginalized voices participate actively in governance.
• **Transparency and Public Audits**: Modern local governance emphasizes social audits, Right to Information (RTI) compliance, and public disclosure of budgets to eliminate corruption and enhance accountability.
• **Community Policing and Traffic Control**: While police forces in Indian cities are generally controlled directly by the State Home Department or Police Commissionerate, municipal bodies work closely with them on urban safety and encroachment removals.
• **Disaster Management in Cities**: Urban local bodies play a pivotal role during urban floods, building collapses, fires, and epidemics by coordinating disaster response teams, relief camps, and emergency health services.
• **Future Outlook**: As India’s urban population is projected to grow exponentially in the coming decades, strengthening the financial and administrative capacity of urban local governments is critical for national development.

• **Key Term Definitions (Review)**: **Urban Local Government** represents the third tier of government operating in cities and towns, responsible for delivering civic amenities and managing local infrastructure.
• **Key Term Definitions (Ward)**: A **Ward** is a specific territorial constituency into which an urban local body is divided for the purpose of conducting elections and ensuring localized administration.
• **Key Term Definitions (Councillor)**: A **Councillor** (or Corporator) is the democratically elected representative of a specific municipal ward in a Municipal Corporation or Municipal Council.
• **Key Term Definitions (Mayor)**: The **Mayor** is the elected political head of a Municipal Corporation who presides over general body meetings and represents the city at official events.
• **Key Term Definitions (Municipal Commissioner)**: The **Municipal Commissioner** is the chief executive officer appointed by the state government to oversee the administrative machinery of a Municipal Corporation.
• **Key Term Definitions (Nagar Panchayat)**: A **Nagar Panchayat** is an urban local body governing transitional areas that are shifting from rural to urban characteristics.
• **Key Term Definitions (Municipal Council)**: A **Municipal Council** (Nagar Palika) is a self-governing urban body established for smaller cities and towns.
• **Key Term Definitions (Municipal Corporation)**: A **Municipal Corporation** (Nagar Nigam) is the highest tier of urban local government established for large metropolitan cities.
• **Key Term Definitions (Property Tax)**: **Property Tax** is a mandatory annual tax levied by urban local bodies on the owners of residential, commercial, or industrial buildings and land.
• **Key Term Definitions (Octroi)**: **Octroi** was a historical levy collected on goods brought into a municipal area for consumption, which has since been replaced by modern tax systems.
• **Key Term Definitions (Discretionary Functions)**: **Discretionary functions** are optional civic services, such as maintaining public parks, museums, and libraries, undertaken based on available municipal funds.
• **Key Term Definitions (Obligatory Functions)**: **Obligatory functions** are mandatory civic duties—such as water supply, sanitation, and street lighting—that urban bodies must legally provide.
• **Key Term Definitions (74th Amendment Act)**: The **74th Constitutional Amendment Act (1992)** is the landmark legislation that granted constitutional status and a uniform structure to urban local bodies in India.
• **Key Term Definitions (Twelfth Schedule)**: The **Twelfth Schedule** of the Indian Constitution lists the 18 functional domains entrusted to urban local bodies.
• **Key Term Definitions (Cantonment Board)**: A **Cantonment Board** is a specialized municipal body administering areas housing military cantonments under the control of the Ministry of Defense.
