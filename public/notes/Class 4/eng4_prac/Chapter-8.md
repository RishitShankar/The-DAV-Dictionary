• **Definition of Adverbs:** An adverb is a word that adds more information about a verb, an adjective, or another adverb in a sentence.
• **Function of Adverbs:** They tell us how, when, where, or to what extent an action is performed.
• **The 'How' Factor:** Many adverbs describe the manner in which an action takes place, answering the question "How?".
• **Formation Rule:** Most adverbs of manner are formed by adding the suffix "-ly" to an adjective.
• **Example of Formation:** The adjective "quick" becomes the adverb "quickly" when describing an action.
• **Adjective vs. Adverb:** An adjective modifies a noun (e.g., "a slow turtle"), while an adverb modifies a verb (e.g., "the turtle walks slowly").
• **The 'When' Factor:** Adverbs of time indicate the specific moment or frequency of an action, answering the question "When?".
• **Examples of Time:** Words like "today," "yesterday," "soon," "now," and "later" function as adverbs of time.
• **The 'Where' Factor:** Adverbs of place specify the location of an action, answering the question "Where?".
• **Examples of Place:** Words like "here," "there," "everywhere," "inside," and "outside" are common adverbs of place.
• **The 'How Often' Factor:** Adverbs of frequency tell us how many times an action occurs, such as "always," "never," "often," or "seldom."
• **Sentence Placement:** Adverbs of manner usually come after the main verb or the object of the sentence.
• **Placement Flexibility:** Adverbs of time and place can often be placed at the beginning or the end of a sentence for emphasis.
• **Modifying Adjectives:** Adverbs can also modify adjectives to show intensity, such as "very," "extremely," or "quite."
• **Example of Intensity:** In the phrase "very tall," the word "very" is an adverb modifying the adjective "tall."

• **Adverbs of Manner:** These describe the quality or style of an action, providing clarity on how a subject performs a task.
• **Spelling Rule (Consonant + y):** When an adjective ends in a consonant followed by 'y', change the 'y' to 'i' before adding '-ly' (e.g., "happy" becomes "happily").
• **Spelling Rule (le ending):** When an adjective ends in '-le', drop the 'e' and add 'y' (e.g., "gentle" becomes "gently").
• **Irregular Adverbs:** Some adverbs do not follow the "-ly" rule and change their form entirely, such as "good" becoming "well."
• **The "Well" Distinction:** "Well" is the adverbial form of "good," used to describe how someone performs an action.
• **Adverbs of Frequency:** These are essential for establishing the regularity of habits or recurring events.
• **Placement of Frequency:** Adverbs of frequency like "always" or "often" typically appear before the main verb.
• **Exceptions to Placement:** When using the verb "to be" (am, is, are, was, were), the adverb of frequency comes after the verb.
• **Example (Frequency):** "She is always punctual" shows the adverb after the verb "is."

• **Common Adverbs of Degree:** Words like "too," "quite," "rather," "almost," and "hardly" help define the scope of an action.

• **Negative Adverbs:** Words like "never" or "hardly" act as adverbs but carry a negative connotation regarding frequency or possibility.
• **Identifying Adverbs:** To identify an adverb, look for words that provide extra context to the action verb in the sentence.
• **Contextual Clues:** If a word can be removed from a sentence without destroying the basic grammar, it is likely an adverb providing extra detail.
