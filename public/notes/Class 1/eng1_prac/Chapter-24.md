• **Chapter Overview**: The chapter "Picture Story (Practice & Phonics)" serves as a foundational bridge between visual literacy and phonetic decoding for Class 1 students.
• **Visual Literacy**: This concept involves the ability to interpret, negotiate, and make meaning from information presented in the form of an image.
• **Phonetic Foundation**: The chapter emphasizes the relationship between graphemes (written letters) and phonemes (sounds) within the context of narrative storytelling.
• **Narrative Sequencing**: Students learn to identify the beginning, middle, and end of a story by observing the progression of visual cues in a picture series.
• **Vocabulary Expansion**: The chapter introduces descriptive words that correlate with objects, actions, and emotions depicted in the provided illustrations.
• **Contextual Clues**: Students are taught to use visual details—such as facial expressions, weather, and setting—to predict what might happen next in a story.
• **Phonic Blending**: The practice component focuses on blending individual letter sounds to form CVC (Consonant-Vowel-Consonant) words relevant to the picture story.
• **Sight Word Integration**: High-frequency words are embedded within the picture descriptions to reinforce recognition and fluency.
• **Sentence Construction**: The chapter guides students in forming simple, grammatically correct sentences based on the actions observed in the pictures.
• **Oral Expression**: A primary objective is to encourage students to narrate the story in their own words, fostering confidence and linguistic development.
• **Phonemic Awareness**: The chapter highlights the importance of hearing and identifying specific vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u) within the context of the story's characters and objects.
• **Character Identification**: Students learn to name characters and describe their attributes based on visual evidence provided in the illustrations.
• **Setting Recognition**: The chapter teaches students to identify the 'where' of a story, such as a park, a classroom, or a home, through visual background details.
• **Action Verbs**: The chapter emphasizes identifying and naming actions (e.g., running, jumping, eating) to build a dynamic vocabulary.
• **Logical Flow**: The practice of arranging pictures in a sequence helps students understand the chronological order of events in a narrative.
• **Critical Thinking**: Students are encouraged to infer emotions and motivations of characters by analyzing their posture and expressions in the pictures.

• **Phonics Focus**: The chapter specifically targets the reinforcement of consonant blends (e.g., bl, st, tr) found in the names of objects within the pictures.
• **Vowel Sound Mastery**: Short vowel sounds are isolated and practiced to ensure students can decode simple words found in the story captions.

• **Medial Sounds**: The chapter provides exercises in identifying the middle vowel sound in words like 'cat', 'hen', 'pig', 'dog', and 'sun'.

• **Word Families**: The chapter introduces word families (e.g., -at, -en, -it, -ot, -un) to help students categorize words with similar phonetic patterns.
• **Visual-Auditory Link**: The core mechanism of the chapter is the constant mapping of a visual image to its corresponding spoken and written sound.
• **Descriptive Adjectives**: Students are introduced to basic adjectives (e.g., big, small, happy, sad) to describe the characters and objects in the story.
• **Prepositional Awareness**: The chapter uses pictures to teach spatial relationships, such as 'on', 'in', 'under', and 'beside', as they relate to the story's objects.
• **Punctuation Awareness**: The chapter introduces the concept of a full stop (.) at the end of a sentence through the written captions accompanying the pictures.
• **Capitalization Rules**: Students observe that every sentence in the story begins with a capital letter, reinforcing basic writing conventions.
• **Story Retelling**: The chapter promotes the skill of summarizing a sequence of events, which is essential for reading comprehension.
• **Predictive Reading**: Students are taught to look at a picture and guess the story's theme before reading the accompanying text.
• **Phonic Decoding**: The process of breaking down complex words into smaller, manageable phonetic units is practiced using the story's vocabulary.
• **Fluency Development**: Repeated reading of the picture story captions helps students improve their reading speed and expression.
• **Engagement Strategy**: The use of vibrant illustrations is designed to maintain student interest and facilitate deeper cognitive connection to the text.
