• **Chapter Overview**: The poem "अगर पेड़ भी चलते होते" (If Trees Could Walk) is a creative and imaginative piece of literature designed for Class 3 students.
• **Core Theme**: The poem explores the concept of personification, attributing human-like mobility and actions to trees.
• **Central Idea**: The poet expresses a whimsical desire to see trees move, which would change the nature of our interaction with nature.
• **Poetic Device**: The poem uses 'Imagination' (कल्पना) as its primary tool to engage young readers.
• **Nature Appreciation**: It encourages students to observe trees closely and understand their stationary nature.
• **Structure**: The poem is written in simple, rhythmic Hindi, making it easy for children to memorize and recite.
• **Tone**: The tone is playful, curious, and lighthearted, reflecting a child's perspective on the world.
• **Key Concept: Mobility**: The central premise is the hypothetical scenario where trees possess the ability to walk.
• **Key Concept: Interaction**: The poem highlights how our daily lives would change if trees were not fixed in one place.
• **Educational Goal**: To foster a love for nature and environmental awareness through creative thinking.
• **Vocabulary Focus**: Introduction of simple yet descriptive Hindi vocabulary related to nature and movement.
• **Visual Imagery**: The poem creates vivid mental pictures of trees wandering through fields and streets.
• **Emotional Connection**: It builds an emotional bond between the child and the natural environment.
• **Linguistic Style**: The use of 'Tukbandi' (rhyming words) enhances the musical quality of the text.
• **Moral Lesson**: While trees cannot walk, they provide us with shade and life-sustaining oxygen, which is their true 'service' to humanity.

• **Stanza Analysis**: The poem begins by posing a hypothetical question: "If trees could walk, what would happen?"
• **Concept: 'Agar' (अगर)**: This word signifies a conditional state, inviting the reader to think beyond reality.
• **Concept: 'Ped' (पेड़)**: The subject of the poem, representing the stationary giants of the forest.
• **Movement Imagery**: The poet imagines trees picking up their roots and moving like humans.
• **The 'Rassi' (Rope) Concept**: The poem mentions tying trees with a rope, suggesting a playful way to keep them nearby.
• **Control Mechanism**: The idea of tying a tree to a post reflects a child’s desire to 'own' or 'keep' a friend close.
• **Playfulness**: The poem suggests that if trees were mobile, we could play with them just like we play with friends.
• **Companionship**: Trees are portrayed as potential playmates rather than just silent observers.
• **The 'Khat' (Cot) Connection**: The poem mentions taking the tree along wherever we go, even to sleep.
• **Imagination vs. Reality**: The poem teaches the difference between what we wish for and the biological reality of plants.
• **Root System**: The poem implicitly teaches that trees are fixed because of their roots, which anchor them to the soil.
• **Environmental Role**: Even in imagination, the tree remains a source of comfort and shade.
• **Childlike Wonder**: The poem captures the innocence of a child who sees no boundaries between humans and nature.
• **Rhythmic Flow**: The poem’s structure mimics the swaying movement of trees in the wind.
• **Linguistic Simplicity**: The language is kept accessible to ensure Class 3 students can grasp the metaphorical meaning.
