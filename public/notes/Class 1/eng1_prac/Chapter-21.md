• **Chapter Overview:** Chapter 21, "Let's Compose (Practice & Phonics)," focuses on the integration of creative writing skills and phonetic awareness for Class 1 students.
• **Objective:** The primary goal is to enable students to construct meaningful sentences using visual cues and phonetic patterns.
• **Composition Basics:** Composition involves the systematic arrangement of words to express a complete thought or idea.
• **Visual Literacy:** The chapter emphasizes the importance of observing pictures carefully before attempting to write sentences about them.
• **Sentence Structure:** A basic sentence requires a Subject (who or what the sentence is about) and a Predicate (what the subject is doing or is).
• **Phonetic Foundation:** Phonics is the method of teaching reading by correlating sounds with letters or groups of letters.
• **Consonant Blends:** The chapter reinforces the understanding of two or more consonants appearing together, such as 'bl', 'st', or 'tr'.
• **Vowel Sounds:** Mastery of short and long vowel sounds is essential for accurate spelling during composition.
• **Capitalization Rules:** Every sentence must begin with a capital letter to signify the start of a new thought.
• **Punctuation Basics:** The use of a full stop (.) at the end of a statement is a mandatory grammatical rule introduced in this chapter.
• **Vocabulary Expansion:** Students are encouraged to use descriptive words (adjectives) to make their compositions more vivid.
• **Word Order:** The logical sequence of words (Subject + Verb + Object) is the backbone of clear communication.
• **Picture Description:** Students learn to identify key elements in an image: people, objects, actions, and the setting.
• **Action Words (Verbs):** Identifying what is happening in a picture helps students select the correct verb for their sentences.
• **Phonetic Spelling:** Students are taught to "sound out" words to write them independently during the composition process.

• **The Writing Process:** This involves three stages: Planning (looking at the picture), Drafting (writing the sentences), and Editing (checking for errors).
• **Planning Phase:** Students should list words related to the picture before writing full sentences.
• **Drafting Phase:** This is where the student writes their initial thoughts down on paper.
• **Editing Phase:** Checking for capital letters, full stops, and correct spelling is the final step.
• **Phonetic Spelling Strategy:** If a student doesn't know a word, they should say it slowly and write down the sounds they hear.
• **Word Families:** Grouping words like 'cat', 'bat', 'mat' helps in memorizing spelling patterns.
• **Rhyming Words:** Identifying rhyming words helps students understand phonetic similarities.
• **Sentence Construction:** A simple sentence structure is: [Article] + [Adjective] + [Noun] + [Verb].
• **Example Structure:** "The red ball rolls." (Article: The, Adjective: red, Noun: ball, Verb: rolls).
• **Visual Prompts:** Pictures of parks, classrooms, or homes are common themes for composition.
• **Descriptive Language:** Using words like 'happy', 'sunny', or 'bright' adds emotion to a composition.
• **Logical Flow:** Sentences should follow a sequence that tells a story or describes a scene logically.
• **The Importance of Practice:** Frequent writing exercises build muscle memory and phonetic confidence.
• **Confidence Building:** Celebrating small successes in writing encourages students to write more frequently.
• **Integration:** The chapter successfully bridges the gap between reading phonics and writing compositions.

• **Phonetic Rules:** These are the guidelines that govern how sounds are represented by letters in English.
• **Vowel Teams:** Pairs of vowels like 'ea' (as in 'eat') or 'ou' (as in 'out') create specific sounds.
• **Double Consonants:** Words like 'ball', 'bell', and 'kiss' use double consonants to maintain the short vowel sound.
• **The 'ck' Rule:** The 'ck' sound is typically used after a short vowel (e.g., 'duck', 'back').
• **Sentence Logic:** Every sentence must make sense; it should not be just a random collection of words.
• **Subject Identification:** The subject is the 'doer' of the action.
• **Predicate Identification:** The predicate is the 'action' or 'state' of the subject.
• **Punctuation Rules:** A full stop is never used in the middle of a sentence; it only marks the end.
• **Capitalization Rules:** The first letter of every sentence must be capitalized, regardless of the word.
• **Proper Noun Capitalization:** Names of people (e.g., Ram, Sita) always start with a capital letter.
• **Sentence Expansion Technique:** Start with "The cat sits." Then add detail: "The fat cat sits on the mat."
• **Vocabulary Building:** Keeping a personal dictionary of new words helps students in their composition tasks.
• **Phonetic Fluency:** The ability to write words quickly without over-thinking the spelling.
• **Creative Thinking:** Encouraging students to think about what happens *before* or *after* the scene in the picture.
• **Writing for an Audience:** Writing with the intention that someone else will read and understand the work.
