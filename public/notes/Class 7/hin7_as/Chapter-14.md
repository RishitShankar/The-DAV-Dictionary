• **Term: अमर (Immortal)** - 'जिसकी कभी मृत्यु न हो' (That which never dies); closely related conceptually to things that transcend time and physical aging.
• **Term: चिरंजीवी (Long-lived)** - One who is blessed with a long life span, often used as a term of blessing by elders for younger generations.
• **Term: युवा (Youth)** - The transitional stage between adolescence and middle age, representing vigor, energy, and physical prime.
• **Term: बाल्यावस्था (Childhood)** - The earliest stage of human life, characterized by innocence, rapid learning, and complete dependence on caregivers.
• **Term: प्रौढ़ (Middle-aged)** - A person who has crossed youth and is in the mature, established phase of life before entering old age.
• **Term: अनुभव (Experience)** - The accumulation of knowledge and practical wisdom that comes with the progression of age, especially highlighted in old age.
• **Term: मार्गदर्शन (Guidance)** - The act of elderly or experienced individuals showing the right path to the younger generation.
• **Term: संरक्षक (Guardian / Protector)** - One who takes care of the young, the old, or the vulnerable members of society.
• **Term: परंपरा (Tradition)** - Customs and beliefs passed down from older generations to younger ones, preserving cultural continuity.
• **Term: विरासत (Inheritance / Legacy)** - The wealth, knowledge, or cultural values handed down by aged predecessors to their descendants.

• **Structural Mechanics of Word Formation in Hindi Grammar** - Understanding how one-word substitutions are built from phrases reveals the inner logic of Hindi vocabulary.
• **Role of Descriptive Clauses (विस्तृत कथन)** - Every substitute originates from a descriptive clause acting as a relative or adjectival phrase (e.g., 'जो कभी न मरे' becomes 'अमर').
• **Sanskrit Roots (तत्सम रूप)** - A significant portion of DAV Class 7 vocabulary relies on pure Sanskrit etymologies, preserving ancient linguistic roots.
• **Prefixal Modification** - Prefixes like 'अ-' (a-) or 'अन-' (an-) are frequently used to negate meanings, transforming positive traits into their exact opposites (e.g., 'दृश्य' into 'अदृश्य').
• **Suffixal Application** - Suffixes such as '-शाली', '-वान', and '-मती' help convert nouns into descriptive adjectives or substitute titles.
• **Compound Words (समास प्रभाव)** - Many one-word substitutions are essentially compacted compound words where two or more independent words merge into a single lexical entity.
• **Semantic Precision** - The shift from phrase to single word tightens logical connections, eliminating ambiguity in descriptive writing.
• **Phonetic Harmony** - DAV grammar rules dictate that one-word substitutes must flow naturally in spoken Hindi without creating jarring phonetic clashes.
• **Contextual Adaptation** - Students must learn that a single word can have different substitutes depending on whether it refers to a person, an object, or a time period.
• **Historical Evolution** - Many traditional substitutes have evolved over centuries, blending regional dialects (तद्भव) with classical Sanskrit (तत्सम) forms.
• **Grammatical Agreement (लिंग और वचन)** - One-word substitutes must match the gender (लिंग) and number (वचन) of the noun they modify in a sentence.
• **Minimizing Redundancy** - The primary structural goal of this chapter is to train students to spot and eliminate verbal clutter in their creative essays.
• **Stylistic Variety** - Using diverse substitutes prevents repetitive sentence openings and keeps the reader engaged throughout long compositions.
• **Analytical Skill Building** - Breaking down a long phrase into its constituent parts exercises logical deduction and analytical thinking among middle-school learners.
• **Standardization by DAV Board** - The vocabulary lists provided in 'Abhyas Sagar' represent standardized academic requirements for Class 7 students across all affiliated institutions.

• **Common Errors and Pitfalls in Exam Writing** - Students frequently commit specific mistakes when attempting one-word substitution questions in DAV examinations.
• **Spelling Inaccuracies (वर्तनी की अशुद्धियाँ)** - Minor errors in matras (vowel signs) or conjunct consonants (संयुक्त अक्षर) completely invalidate an otherwise correct substitute.
• **Confusing Opposites** - Mistaking 'अजर' (ageless) for 'अमर' (immortal) or 'कृतज्ञ' (grateful) for 'कृतघ्न' (ungrateful) is a frequent error stemming from superficial reading.
• **Over-reliance on Rote Memorization** - Memorizing words without understanding their underlying descriptive phrases leads to misapplication in sentence-formation tasks.
• **Ignoring Gender and Pluralization Rules** - Failing to adjust the form of the substitute word when the sentence structure changes its grammatical requirements.
• **Using Informal Slang** - Substituting traditional literary words with colloquial or regional slang words that lack formal grammatical approval.
• **Confusing Synonyms with Substitutes** - Treating a simple synonym as a replacement for an entire descriptive clause, leading to grammatical incoherence.
• **Misinterpreting Negative Prefixes** - Misplacing or omitting 'अ-' or 'अन-' prefixes, thereby completely reversing the intended meaning of the substitute.
• **Neglecting Contextual Clues** - Failing to read the surrounding sentence in the exam paper, resulting in a logically mismatched word choice.
• **Punctuation and Capitalization Errors** - Incorrectly formatting answers in tabular or matching exercises within the answer sheet.
• **Incomplete Answers** - Writing only part of a compound substitute word instead of the complete, recognized DAV-approved term.
• **Phonetic Misspellings** - Writing words phonetically based on regional pronunciations rather than standard Hindi orthography (मानक हिंदी वर्तनी).
• **Failure to Revise** - Skipping post-writing review, which allows careless slip-ups in complex Sanskrit-derived words to go unnoticed.
• **Mismatching Categories** - Applying a time-related substitute to a human behavioral description due to lack of conceptual clarity.
• **Overcomplicating Simple Meanings** - Using excessively obscure vocabulary where a simpler, standard one-word substitute is required by the syllabus.
