• **Chapter Overview:** The chapter "ऐसे थे लाल बहादुर शास्त्री" (Such was Lal Bahadur Shastri) is a biographical sketch of India’s second Prime Minister, focusing on his humble beginnings, integrity, and simplicity.
• **Subject:** Hindi (Bhasha Madhuri) - Class 3.
• **Core Theme:** The chapter highlights the moral values, honesty, and dedication of Lal Bahadur Shastri towards the nation.
• **Early Life:** Lal Bahadur Shastri was born on October 2, 1904, in Mughalsarai, Uttar Pradesh.
• **Family Background:** He was born into a very poor family, which shaped his understanding of the struggles of common people.
• **Childhood Struggle:** Despite extreme poverty, he never lost his focus on education and personal character.
• **Character Trait - Simplicity:** Shastri Ji is remembered for his extremely simple lifestyle, which remained unchanged even after becoming the Prime Minister.
• **Character Trait - Honesty:** He was a man of high integrity who believed that public office should be used for service, not personal gain.
• **The Incident of the River:** The chapter narrates a famous incident where he swam across the Ganges to reach school because he could not afford the boat fare.
• **Determination:** This incident showcases his immense willpower and his commitment to education despite physical and financial hardships.
• **National Service:** He joined the freedom struggle at a young age, inspired by Mahatma Gandhi.
• **Political Journey:** He served in various capacities, including Minister of Police and Transport, before becoming the Prime Minister.
• **Leadership Style:** He was known for his calm demeanor and his ability to make firm decisions during crises.
• **The Slogan:** He gave the famous slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan" (Hail the Soldier, Hail the Farmer) during the 1965 war.
• **Significance of the Slogan:** This slogan boosted the morale of the Indian army and the farmers, who were the backbone of the country.
• **Legacy:** He remains an inspiration for students to lead a life of honesty, hard work, and patriotism.

• **Freedom Struggle Involvement:** Shastri Ji joined the Indian National Congress and participated in various movements led by Gandhi Ji.
• **Non-Violence:** He was a staunch follower of 'Ahimsa' (non-violence), a core principle of the Indian freedom struggle.

• **Public Perception:** He was widely respected across all political parties for his honesty and clean image.

• **The 1965 War:** This was a defining moment in his career where he demonstrated exceptional leadership.
• **Military Support:** He gave full freedom to the Indian Army to defend the country’s borders, which boosted their confidence.
• **Agricultural Focus:** He realized that a strong nation needs food security, leading to his emphasis on agriculture.
• **Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan:** This slogan became the rallying cry for the entire nation, uniting soldiers and farmers.
• **Meaning of 'Jawan':** The soldier who protects the borders of the country.
• **Meaning of 'Kisan':** The farmer who works hard in the fields to feed the nation.
• **Unity:** The slogan emphasized that the strength of the nation lies in the hands of those who defend it and those who feed it.
• **Food Shortage:** During his time, India faced a food crisis, and he encouraged people to skip one meal a week to save food.
• **Personal Sacrifice:** He himself started the practice of fasting to lead by example during the food crisis.
• **Tashkent Agreement:** He traveled to Tashkent, Uzbekistan, to sign a peace treaty with Pakistan to end the war.
• **Diplomacy:** He believed in solving international disputes through dialogue and peaceful negotiations.
• **Sudden Demise:** He passed away in Tashkent on January 11, 1966, shortly after signing the agreement.
• **National Mourning:** His death was a massive shock to the nation, and he was mourned by millions.
• **Bharat Ratna:** He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honor, in 1966.
• **Legacy of Honesty:** He is often cited as the gold standard for political integrity in India.
