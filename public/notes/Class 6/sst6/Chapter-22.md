• **Introduction to Chapter:** Welcome to DAV Academic Notes for Class 6 Social Science (We and Our World), focusing on Chapter 22: "Democracy and Government."
• **Definition of Society and Order:** Human beings are social animals who naturally live in groups, communities, and societies, requiring a system of rules to maintain peace and order.
• **Need for Regulation:** To prevent chaos, conflict, and lawlessness within a society, a supreme governing authority is essential to make laws, enforce them, and maintain justice.
• **Definition of Government:** **Government** is defined as the institutional system or administrative machinery through which a state, nation, or community is ruled, managed, and organized.
• **Primary Role of Government:** The fundamental responsibility of any government is to formulate laws, execute policies, maintain public order, and protect the rights of its citizens.
• **Provision of Public Services:** Governments are responsible for building and maintaining critical public infrastructure such as roads, railways, hospitals, schools, and sanitation systems.
• **Internal Security Maintenance:** Governments maintain internal law and order through specialized security forces, including police departments and judicial systems, to punish lawbreakers.
• **External Defense:** Governments protect the territorial integrity and sovereignty of the nation from external aggressions by maintaining a well-equipped national defense force (military).
• **Economic Management:** Governments regulate trade, collect taxes, print currency, control inflation, and implement fiscal policies to ensure steady economic growth and financial stability.
• **Welfare Initiatives:** Modern governments actively run welfare programs aimed at poverty alleviation, subsidized food distribution, healthcare access, and educational support for marginalized groups.
• **Disaster Management:** During natural calamities like floods, earthquakes, or droughts, the government coordinates rescue operations, relief distribution, and rehabilitation efforts.
• **Evolution of Governments:** Over the course of human history, the structure, form, and scope of governments have undergone massive transformations, moving from primitive tribal councils to modern states.
• **Historical Monarchy Systems:** In ancient times, absolute monarchies were common where power rested entirely in the hands of a hereditary king or queen without public accountability.
• **Dictatorial Rule:** **Dictatorship** is an authoritarian form of government where absolute political power is concentrated in the hands of a single individual or a small ruling junta.
• **Introduction to Democracy:** **Democracy** is universally recognized as the most progressive, inclusive, and people-centric form of government, famously described as rule "of the people, by the people, and for the people."
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Etymological Roots of Democracy:** The term 'democracy' originates from two Greek words: 'demos' (meaning people) and 'kratos' (meaning power or rule), literally translating to "rule by the people."
• **Abraham Lincoln's Definition:** Former US President Abraham Lincoln provided the classic definition of democracy as "government of the people, by the people, and for the people."
• **Core Principle of Popular Sovereignty:** In a democracy, **Popular Sovereignty** dictates that the ultimate source of all political power and authority resides within the general populace.
• **Equality as a Pillar:** Democracy stands on the foundational pillar of socio-political, economic, and legal equality, ensuring that all citizens are treated equally before the law.
• **Universal Adult Franchise:** **Universal Adult Franchise** is the bedrock of democratic systems, granting the right to vote to all adult citizens regardless of caste, creed, gender, or wealth.
• **One Person, One Vote, One Value:** A core democratic tenet ensures that every eligible citizen's vote carries equal weight, value, and significance in an election.
• **Types of Democracy:** Broadly classified into two primary forms: Direct Democracy and Indirect (or Representative) Democracy, based on how citizens exercise their political power.
• **Direct Democracy Explained:** **Direct Democracy** is a system where citizens directly participate in law-making, policy decisions, and governance without electing intermediaries (historically seen in ancient Greek city-states).
• **Indirect Democracy Explained:** **Indirect (Representative) Democracy** is a modern system where citizens elect representatives through periodic elections to make laws and govern on their behalf.
• **Need for Representative Democracy:** Due to massive populations and vast geographical territories of modern nation-states, direct democracy is impractical, making representative democracy essential.
• **Pillars of Parliamentary Democracy:** India operates under a representative parliamentary democracy where the elected legislature holds supreme law-making authority and executive accountability.
• **Role of Elections:** Periodic, free, and fair **elections** serve as the vital mechanism through which citizens express their political choice and hold leaders accountable.
• **Multi-Party System:** Democratic nations typically feature a multi-party political framework, offering voters a diverse array of ideological choices and political platforms.
• **Role of Opposition Parties:** A constructive political opposition acts as a crucial check on the ruling government, pointing out policy flaws and preventing authoritarian tendencies.
• **Rule of Law:** **Rule of Law** means that no individual—regardless of how wealthy, powerful, or high-ranking—is above the law of the land; everyone is subject to the same legal code.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Fundamental Rights in Democracy:** Democratic constitutions guarantee a set of basic civil liberties and **Fundamental Rights** (such as freedom of speech and expression) to safeguard individual dignity.
• **Independence of Judiciary:** A fair, impartial, and **Independent Judiciary** is vital in a democracy to interpret laws, protect citizen rights, and resolve disputes without executive interference.
• **Freedom of the Press:** A free and independent media functions as the "fourth pillar" of democracy, informing the public objectively and acting as a watchdog against corruption.
• **Civic Participation:** Democracy requires active, informed, and continuous participation of citizens beyond merely casting votes, including debates, public forums, and peaceful protests.
• **Tolerance and Pluralism:** Healthy democratic societies celebrate diversity, pluralism, cultural differences, and mutual tolerance, respecting varied viewpoints and minority rights.
• **Accountability and Transparency:** Democratic governments are perpetually accountable to the electorate and must maintain transparency in administrative decision-making processes.
• **Evolution of Indian Democracy:** The Indian democratic journey began with independence in 1947, culminating in the adoption of the Constitution on January 26, 1950, establishing a Sovereign Democratic Republic.
• **Vision of Indian Constitution:** The Constitution of India enshrines justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity as the foundational ideals for all citizens of the nation.
• **Levels of Government in India:** To ensure efficient administration and grassroots governance, the Indian political system operates on three distinct levels: Central, State, and Local.
• **Central Government (Union Government):** The **Central Government** exercises supreme authority over matters of national importance, such as defense, foreign affairs, railways, and currency.
• **State Government:** The **State Government** manages regional and local matters within individual states, including law and order, public health, state roadways, and agriculture.
• **Local Self-Government:** **Local Self-Government** (Panchayati Raj in rural areas and Municipalities in urban areas) empowers local communities to solve their immediate civic issues directly.
• **Introduction to Panchayati Raj:** The **Panchayati Raj System** represents the three-tier structure of rural local self-government in India, bringing democracy down to the village level.
• **Gram Panchayat:** The **Gram Panchayat** is the lowest tier of the Panchayati Raj system, operating at the village level and consisting of elected panchs and a head called the Sarpanch.
• **Gram Sabha:** The **Gram Sabha** is the general body comprising all adult residents registered in the electoral rolls of a village panchayat area, serving as a direct democratic assembly.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Panchayat Samiti:** The **Panchayat Samiti** forms the middle tier of the Panchayati Raj system, functioning at the block level (Tehsil or Mandal) to coordinate between villages and districts.
• **Zilla Parishad:** The **Zilla Parishad** is the apex tier of the rural local government, operating at the district level to oversee development plans and fund allocations across blocks.
• **Urban Local Government:** For cities and towns, urban local governance structures are established, categorized into Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils, and Nagar Panchayats.
• **Nagar Panchayat:** A **Nagar Panchayat** is constituted for transitional areas—regions moving rapidly from rural settlements to urban centers.
• **Municipal Council (Municipal Board):** A **Municipal Council** is established for smaller cities and towns with medium-sized urban populations.
• **Municipal Corporation:** A **Municipal Corporation** (**Nagar Nigam**) is established for large metropolitan cities with massive populations, headed politically by a Mayor.
• **Functions of Local Bodies:** Local government bodies manage civic amenities such as street lighting, potable water supply, garbage collection, sewage management, and primary education.
• **Sources of Local Revenue:** Local bodies generate funds through property taxes, water charges, market fees, entertainment taxes, and financial grants received from state governments.
• **Significance of Local Self-Government:** Local self-governance trains citizens in leadership, ensures decentralized planning, and resolves hyper-local grievances swiftly and efficiently.
• **Role of Women in Panchayati Raj:** Constitutional amendments introduced reservation of seats for women in local bodies, significantly empowering rural women politically and socially.
• **Decision-Making Process in a Democracy:** Unlike dictatorships where a single leader dictates laws, democratic laws are debated, drafted, voted upon, and passed by elected representatives in parliaments.
• **Public Opinion Formation:** Public opinion is shaped through newspaper editorials, television debates, public discussions, social media campaigns, and peaceful demonstrations.
• **Resolution of Conflicts:** In pluralistic societies like India, conflicts may arise over language, religion, resources, or regional boundaries; democracy provides peaceful mechanisms for dispute resolution.
• **Judicial Resolution:** Legal disputes are peacefully resolved by courts of law, ensuring that justice is delivered based on constitutional principles rather than mob violence or coercion.
• **Role of Compromise and Negotiation:** Democratic governance relies heavily on dialogue, compromise, and consensus-building among diverse political and social factions.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Comparison: Democracy vs. Monarchy:** While monarchy concentrates power in a single hereditary ruler with no public recourse, democracy distributes power to citizens through periodic elections.
• **Comparison: Democracy vs. Dictatorship:** Dictatorships suppress dissent, ban opposition parties, and restrict personal freedoms, whereas democracy encourages freedom of speech, pluralism, and political opposition.
• **Advantages of Democracy:** Promotes equality, protects human rights, prevents the abuse of power, ensures peaceful transitions of government, and fosters national unity amidst diversity.
• **Challenges to Democracy:** Issues such as illiteracy, poverty, corruption, casteism, communalism, and criminalization of politics pose serious threats to the smooth functioning of democracy.
• **Need for Informed Electorate:** A successful democracy requires an educated, aware, and vigilant electorate that evaluates political manifestos critically before casting votes.
• **Eradication of Social Evils:** Democratic ideals encourage the legal abolition of regressive social practices such as untouchability, child labor, gender discrimination, and bonded labor.
• **Promotion of Social Justice:** Modern democratic governments implement affirmative action policies, such as reservations, to uplift historically disadvantaged and marginalized communities.
• **Economic Equality Challenge:** While political equality is guaranteed by universal adult franchise, achieving economic equality remains a persistent challenge in many democratic nations.
• **Role of Youth in Democracy:** Young citizens represent the future of democracy; by understanding civic duties, participating in school parliaments, and obeying laws, they strengthen society.
• **Concept of Civic Sense:** **Civic sense** involves respecting public property, obeying traffic rules, maintaining cleanliness, and showing consideration for fellow citizens in shared spaces.
• **Rights and Duties Pairing:** In a democracy, every fundamental right enjoyed by a citizen is inextricably linked to a corresponding fundamental duty toward the nation.
• **Fundamental Duties in India:** The Indian Constitution outlines specific civic duties for citizens, emphasizing patriotism, preservation of heritage, environmental protection, and scientific temper.
• **Environmental Protection Responsibility:** Modern democracies increasingly focus on sustainable development, urging citizens and governments to combat climate change and protect natural resources.
• **Global Democracy Trends:** The spread of democratic governance worldwide reflects a universal human desire for freedom, self-determination, dignity, and human rights protection.
• **Conclusion of Chapter Theme:** Democracy is not merely a form of government but a way of life that demands continuous participation, tolerance, and respect for human dignity.

• **Glossary Term - Government:** The system or group of people governing an organized community, often a state, responsible for making and enforcing laws.
• **Glossary Term - Democracy:** A system of government by the whole population or all the eligible members of a state, typically through elected representatives.
• **Glossary Term - Monarchy:** A form of government with a monarch (king or queen) at the head, where power is typically inherited through royal lineage.
• **Glossary Term - Dictatorship:** Government by a dictator, characterized by absolute, unchecked political power and the suppression of opposition.
• **Glossary Term - Universal Adult Franchise:** The principle that every adult citizen has the right to vote in elections, without discrimination based on wealth, gender, or status.
• **Glossary Term - Constitution:** The supreme foundational legal document and set of fundamental principles according to which a state is acknowledged to be governed.
• **Glossary Term - Legislature:** The branch of government responsible for debating, formulating, drafting, and passing laws.
• **Glossary Term - Executive:** The branch of government responsible for implementing, administering, and enforcing laws passed by the legislature.
• **Glossary Term - Judiciary:** The system of courts that interprets and applies the law in the name of the state, ensuring justice and legal order.
• **Glossary Term - Gram Sabha:** The primary assembly of all adult villagers in a village panchayat area, acting as the foundation of grassroots democracy.
• **Glossary Term - Sarpanch:** The democratically elected head or chairperson of a village Gram Panchayat in India.
• **Glossary Term - Municipal Corporation:** The highest tier of urban local self-government responsible for administering large metropolitan cities.
• **Glossary Term - Opposition:** Political parties that are not in power, tasked with keeping the ruling government accountable through scrutiny and debate.
• **Glossary Term - Rule of Law:** The legal principle that law applies to all members of society equally, including lawmakers, law enforcement, and rulers.
• **Glossary Term - Transparency:** The condition of being open to public scrutiny, allowing citizens to see how governmental decisions and financial allocations are made.

• **Key Concept Summary - Evolution of State Systems:** Political structures evolved from tribal heads and absolute monarchs to constitutional republics and representative democracies over millennia.
• **Key Concept Summary - Power of the Ballot:** The ballot box represents the peaceful weapon of the common citizen, allowing bloodless revolutions through democratic elections.
• **Key Concept Summary - Decentralization of Power:** Decentralization distributes administrative power from the central authority down to state and local levels, ensuring efficient grassroots governance.
• **Key Concept Summary - Panchayati Raj Impact:** Panchayati Raj transforms rural citizens from passive subjects into active architects of their own local development and welfare.
• **Key Concept Summary - Urban Governance Challenges:** Urban local bodies face immense challenges due to rapid urbanization, population migration, pollution, and the demand for better infrastructure.
• **Key Concept Summary - Duties Over Rights:** True democratic citizenship requires balancing personal entitlements (rights) with societal responsibilities (duties).
• **Key Concept Summary - Media's Role:** Media must remain unbiased, factual, and fearless to ensure that citizens receive accurate information necessary for forming sound political judgments.
• **Key Concept Summary - Social Harmony:** Democracy provides a peaceful framework for heterogeneous communities to coexist, negotiate differences, and build a cohesive national identity.
• **Key Concept Summary - Dignity of the Individual:** The core moral value of democracy is the acknowledgment of the intrinsic dignity and worth of every individual human being.
• **Key Textbook Fact - India's Status:** India is universally recognized as the largest functioning democracy in the entire world by population size and electorate scale.
• **Key Textbook Fact - Adoption Date:** The Constitution of India was officially adopted on November 26, 1949, and came into full force and effect on January 26, 1950.
• **Key Textbook Fact - Three Tiers:** The Panchayati Raj system in India operates strictly across three administrative tiers: Village (Gram Panchayat), Block (Panchayat Samiti), and District (Zilla Parishad).
• **Key Textbook Fact - Universal Voting Age:** Under the Indian Constitution, the voting age for citizens was lowered from 21 years to 18 years via the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act of 1988.
• **Key Textbook Fact - Urban Heads:** The political head of a Municipal Corporation in major Indian cities is designated as the Mayor, while administrative execution is handled by a Municipal Commissioner.

• **In-Depth Analysis - Why Democracy Prevails:** Democracy has emerged as the preferred global political system because it minimizes civil unrest by giving every citizen a peaceful voice.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Checks and Balances:** The separation of powers among the legislature, executive, and judiciary prevents any single branch of government from becoming tyrannical.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Role of Political Parties:** Political parties act as vital bridges between the general public and the state apparatus, articulating public grievances into concrete policy agendas.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Impact of Poverty on Voting:** Economic deprivation and illiteracy can sometimes make voters vulnerable to corruption, vote-buying, and populist manipulation, challenging democratic integrity.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Gender Representation:** Empowering women through political reservations at local levels has catalyzed a silent social revolution across rural India.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Rule of Majority vs. Minority Rights:** While democracy functions on majority rule, a true democracy vigorously protects the cultural, religious, and linguistic rights of minorities.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Public Property Protection:** Destroying public property during protests contradicts democratic principles, as public assets belong to all citizens collectively and are built using taxpayers' money.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Freedom of Speech Limits:** Freedom of speech is a fundamental right in a democracy, but it comes with reasonable restrictions to prevent hate speech, defamation, and national security threats.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Grassroots Democracy Success:** Local self-government ensures that drinking water, sanitation, and street lighting issues are addressed locally without waiting for state-level approvals.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Secret Ballot Mechanism:** The introduction of electronic voting machines (EVMs) and secret ballot voting ensures that citizens can vote freely without fear of intimidation.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Civic Education Necessity:** Teaching civics in schools like DAV ensures that young students grow into responsible, politically aware, and empathetic national citizens.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Secularism in Democracy:** Indian democracy practices positive secularism ("Sarva Dharma Sambhava"), treating all religions equally without establishing any official state religion.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Accountability Mechanisms:** Question hour in parliament, Right to Information (RTI) acts, and public audits serve as potent tools to hold governments accountable.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Social Mobility:** Democracy dismantles rigid hierarchical social structures, offering every individual equal opportunity for upward economic and social mobility.
• **In-Depth Analysis - Ultimate Power of Citizens:** In a democracy, politicians are public servants rather than masters; the ultimate political power rests permanently in the hands of the voting citizens.
