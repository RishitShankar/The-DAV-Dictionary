• **Chapter Title:** तिरंगा (Tiranga) – The National Flag of India.
• **Subject Context:** This chapter introduces young learners to the significance, colors, and symbolism of the Indian National Flag.
• **Core Concept:** The term **तिरंगा (Tiranga)** literally means 'three-colored' (Tri-color).
• **Symbolism:** The flag is the most important symbol of our national pride and sovereignty.
• **Visual Structure:** The flag consists of three horizontal bands of equal width.
• **Top Band:** The top band is **केसरिया (Saffron)** in color.
• **Saffron Meaning:** It represents courage, sacrifice, and the spirit of renunciation.
• **Middle Band:** The middle band is **सफेद (White)** in color.
• **White Meaning:** It symbolizes peace, purity, and truth.
• **Bottom Band:** The bottom band is **हरा (Green)** in color.
• **Green Meaning:** It represents prosperity, fertility, and the greenery of the land.
• **Central Emblem:** In the center of the white band, there is a blue wheel.
• **Wheel Name:** This wheel is known as the **अशोक चक्र (Ashoka Chakra)**.
• **Chakra Color:** The color of the Ashoka Chakra is **गहरा नीला (Navy Blue)**.
• **Chakra Origin:** It is taken from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath.
• **Chakra Symbolism:** It represents the wheel of the law of dharma (righteousness) and progress.
• **Motion:** The wheel signifies constant movement and the forward progress of the nation.
• **Spokes Count:** The Ashoka Chakra contains exactly 24 spokes.
• **Educational Goal:** To instill a sense of patriotism and respect for national symbols in Class 2 students.

• **Respecting the Flag:** We must always show deep respect to our national flag.
• **Flag Hoisting:** The flag is hoisted on important national occasions like Independence Day and Republic Day.
• **Independence Day:** Celebrated on **15th August** every year to mark India's freedom.
• **Republic Day:** Celebrated on **26th January** every year to mark the adoption of the Constitution.
• **Physical Handling:** The flag should never be allowed to touch the ground.
• **Dignity:** It should always be kept clean and in good condition.
• **Placement:** When displayed, the saffron band must always be at the top.
• **Salutation:** We salute the flag to show our loyalty and love for the country.
• **National Identity:** The Tiranga unites all Indians regardless of caste, creed, or religion.
• **Historical Context:** The current design of the flag was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947.
• **Designer:** The flag was designed by **Pingali Venkayya**.
• **Material:** Traditionally, the flag was made of Khadi, a hand-spun cotton fabric.
• **Khadi Significance:** Khadi represents the self-reliance movement led by Mahatma Gandhi.
• **Public Display:** Citizens are encouraged to display the flag on their homes and offices during national festivals.
• **Behavioral Code:** There is a strict 'Flag Code of India' that governs the display and handling of the national flag.
• **Unity:** The three colors represent the diverse yet united nature of India.
• **Patriotism:** Learning about the flag is the first step in understanding one's duty toward the nation.
• **Visual Recognition:** Students must be able to identify the correct order of colors: Saffron, White, Green.
• **Emotional Connection:** The flag evokes feelings of belonging and pride in every Indian heart.

• **Ashoka Chakra (अशोक चक्र):** The navy blue wheel at the center of the white band.
• **Dharma Chakra:** It is also known as the 'Wheel of Duty' or 'Wheel of Law'.
• **Navy Blue:** The color represents the vastness of the sky and the depth of the ocean.
• **24 Spokes:** These spokes represent the 24 hours of the day.
• **Time Significance:** It reminds us that time is precious and we should use it for the welfare of the nation.
• **Continuous Progress:** The wheel signifies that the nation must always keep moving forward.
• **Non-Stagnation:** Just as a wheel moves, the country must progress without stopping.
• **Ancient Roots:** The design is derived from the Sarnath Lion Capital of Emperor Ashoka.
• **Historical Legacy:** It connects modern India to its glorious ancient history.
• **Moral Values:** The wheel represents the moral laws that guide our society.
• **Geometric Precision:** The chakra is placed exactly in the center of the white band.
• **Symmetry:** Its placement ensures the flag remains symmetrical and balanced.
• **Symbol of Change:** It represents the dynamic nature of Indian society.
• **Inspiration:** It inspires citizens to work hard and contribute to the nation's growth.
• **Educational Importance:** Understanding the chakra helps students appreciate the depth of our national symbols.
• **Visual Focus:** The blue color of the chakra stands out clearly against the white background.
• **Identity:** The Ashoka Chakra is a unique identifier of the Indian National Flag.
• **National Emblem:** It is also a part of the official State Emblem of India.
• **Respect:** We must treat the Ashoka Chakra with the same reverence as the rest of the flag.

• **Independence Day (स्वतंत्रता दिवस):** Celebrated on 15th August.
• **Historical Significance:** On this day in 1947, India gained independence from British rule.
• **Flag Hoisting Ceremony:** The Prime Minister hoists the national flag at the Red Fort.
• **Republic Day (गणतंत्र दिवस):** Celebrated on 26th January.
• **Constitutional Significance:** On this day in 1950, the Constitution of India came into effect.
• **Parade:** A grand parade is held in New Delhi, showcasing India's military and cultural strength.

• **National Anthem:** 'Jana Gana Mana' is played during flag hoisting ceremonies.
• **Protocol:** Everyone must stand in attention while the flag is being hoisted.
• **Respectful Posture:** Standing straight shows our respect for the nation.
• **Celebration:** Schools, offices, and homes are decorated with the Tiranga.

• **Flag Code (ध्वज संहिता):** A set of rules for the proper display and use of the flag.
• **No Damage:** The flag should never be torn, dirty, or damaged.
• **No Defacement:** Nothing should be written or drawn on the flag.
• **Correct Orientation:** The saffron band must always be at the top.
• **No Other Flags:** No other flag or emblem should be placed higher than the Tiranga.
• **Ground Contact:** The flag should never touch the ground or the floor.
• **Water Contact:** It should not be allowed to trail in water.
• **Clothing:** The flag should not be used as a costume or clothing.
• **Disposal:** If a flag is damaged, it should be disposed of in a dignified manner, usually by burning or burying in private.
• **Night Display:** Traditionally, the flag was only flown from sunrise to sunset, but rules have been updated to allow for night display under proper lighting.
• **Respectful Handling:** Always fold the flag neatly and store it carefully.
• **Public Conduct:** When the flag is being hoisted or lowered, one should face the flag and stand at attention.
• **Vehicle Usage:** The flag can be flown on the cars of high-ranking officials like the President and Prime Minister.
• **Private Display:** Citizens are encouraged to display the flag at their homes with pride and respect.
• **Awareness:** Teaching children these rules helps them grow into responsible citizens.
• **Discipline:** Following these rules is a sign of a disciplined and patriotic society.
• **National Honor:** Respecting the flag is equivalent to respecting the nation itself.
• **Legal Protection:** Misuse of the national flag is a punishable offense under the law.
• **Duty:** It is the duty of every Indian to uphold the dignity of the Tiranga.

• **Visualizing the Flag:** The flag is a rectangle with a length-to-width ratio of 3:2.
• **Saffron Band:** Occupies the top one-third of the flag.
• **White Band:** Occupies the middle one-third of the flag.
• **Green Band:** Occupies the bottom one-third of the flag.
• **Ashoka Chakra Placement:** Centered exactly in the middle of the white band.
• **Color Shades:** The saffron is a deep, warm shade; the green is a vibrant, natural shade.
• **Blue Chakra:** The navy blue color must be distinct and clear.
• **Flag Pole:** The flag is usually hoisted on a tall pole.
• **Rope Mechanism:** A pulley and rope system is used to hoist the flag.
• **Unfurling:** The flag is unfurled at the top of the pole during the ceremony.
• **Flower Petals:** Often, flower petals are placed inside the folded flag to shower down when it is unfurled.
• **Respectful Unfurling:** The flag should be unfurled gently and with grace.
• **Weather Conditions:** The flag should be lowered if the weather is extremely stormy.
• **Night Lighting:** If flown at night, the flag must be properly illuminated.
• **Storage:** When not in use, the flag should be folded properly and stored in a clean place.
• **Maintenance:** Keep the flag free from dust and stains.
• **Display Standards:** Ensure the flag is not faded or torn.
• **Public Awareness:** Educate others about the correct way to handle the flag.
• **Symbolic Power:** The flag is not just a piece of cloth; it is the soul of the nation.
