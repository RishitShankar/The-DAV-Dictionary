• **Course Context**: Welcome to the DAV Dictionary academic study notes for Class 6 English Practice Book, Chapter 10: "Passive Voice."
• **Introduction to Voice**: Voice is that form of a transitive verb which shows whether the subject of a sentence does the action or has the action done to it.
• **Primary Classification**: English grammar divides voices into two main types: the Active Voice and the Passive Voice.
• **Active Voice Definition**: A sentence is in the Active Voice when the subject performs the action denoted by the verb.
• **Passive Voice Definition**: A sentence is in the Passive Voice when the subject receives the action done by the verb, rather than performing it.
• **Focus Shift**: The fundamental difference between the two voices lies in the focus of the sentence: the doer in active voice versus the receiver/object in passive voice.
• **Transitive Verbs Requirement**: Only transitive verbs (verbs that take a direct object) can be transformed from the active voice into the passive voice.
• **Intransitive Verbs Limitation**: Intransitive verbs cannot be changed into the passive voice because they do not have a direct object to receive the action.
• **Subject-Object Interchange**: The basic structural transformation involves interchanging the positions of the subject and the object of the active sentence.
• **Object Becomes Subject**: The direct object of the active verb becomes the new subject of the passive sentence.
• **Subject Becomes Agent**: The original subject of the active sentence becomes the agent in the passive sentence, usually preceded by the preposition "by."
• **Verb Form Modification**: The main verb in the active voice is always converted into its past participle form (Third Form of the Verb, or V3) in the passive voice.
• **Auxiliary Verb Addition**: A suitable form of the auxiliary verb "to be" (is, am, are, was, were, be, being, been) must be added before the main verb according to the tense.
• **DAV Curriculum Objective**: This chapter in the DAV English Practice Book aims to build conceptual clarity around sentence restructuring and structural variety.
• **Utility in Writing**: Mastering the passive voice helps Class 6 students enhance their formal writing, report writing, and objective descriptions.

• **Core Components of Active Voice**: Every active voice sentence minimally requires a subject, a finite verb, and a direct object (Subject + Verb + Object).
• **Example of Active Structure**: In the sentence "The cat chases the mouse," "The cat" is the subject, "chases" is the active verb, and "the mouse" is the object.
• **Core Components of Passive Voice**: Every passive voice sentence requires a new subject (former object), a suitable auxiliary verb, the V3 form of the main verb, and the optional agent phrase (New Subject + Auxiliary Verb + V3 + by + Agent).
• **Example of Passive Structure**: Converting the previous sentence yields "The mouse is chased by the cat," where "The mouse" is the subject receiving the action.
• **The Role of the Preposition 'By'**: The preposition "by" is conventionally used to introduce the doer of the action (the agent) in a passive sentence.
• **Omission of the Agent**: The agent phrase (by + agent) can be omitted in the passive voice if the doer is unknown, obvious, irrelevant, or general.
• **Example of Omission**: "English is spoken all over the world" does not require "by people" because the agent is universally understood.
• **Pronoun Transformation**: Pronouns change their case when moving from the subject position in active voice to the object/agent position in passive voice.
• **Nominative to Objective**: Personal pronouns transform as follows: 'I' changes to 'me', 'we' changes to 'us', 'he' changes to 'him', and 'she' changes to 'her'.
• **Pronoun 'They' and 'It'**: The pronoun 'they' changes to 'them' in the passive agent phrase, while 'it' remains 'it' without any change in form.
• **Pronoun 'You'**: The pronoun 'you' also remains unchanged in form when shifting from the subject to the object position (e.g., "You help him" becomes "He is helped by you").
• **Tense Preservation Rule**: The tense of the original active sentence must always be maintained during the conversion into the passive voice.
• **Auxiliary Selection Mechanism**: The specific auxiliary verb chosen for the passive construction depends entirely on the tense of the active verb and the number of the new subject.
• **Singular and Plural Agreement**: The new subject must agree in number (singular or plural) with the auxiliary verb in the passive voice.
• **Foundation for Advanced Syntax**: Understanding these basic elements sets the necessary foundation for mastering complex grammatical transformations in higher classes.

• **Simple Present Tense Overview**: The Simple Present tense expresses habitual actions, universal truths, or general facts in the active voice.
• **Active Structure in Simple Present**: Subject + First Form of the Verb (V1 / V1+s/es) + Object (e.g., "The gardener waters the plants").
• **Passive Structure Formula**: New Subject + is / am / are + Third Form of the Verb (V3) + by + Agent.
• **Auxiliary Selection for Simple Present**: Use "is" for singular third-person subjects, "am" strictly for the pronoun "I," and "are" for plural subjects.
• **Passive Conversion Example 1**: Active: "The teacher praises the student." -> Passive: "The student is praised by the teacher."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2**: Active: "Farmers grow wheat in this region." -> Passive: "Wheat is grown by farmers in this region."
• **Passive Conversion Example 3 (Plural Object)**: Active: "She writes poems." -> Passive: "Poems are written by her."
• **Passive Conversion Example 4 (First Person)**: Active: "You invite me." -> Passive: "I am invited by you."
• **Negative Sentences in Simple Present**: Active: "He does not like apples." -> Passive: "Apples are not liked by him."
• **Interrogative Sentences (Yes/No Questions)**: Active: "Does she sing a song?" -> Passive: "Is a song sung by her?"
• **Interrogative Sentences (Wh- Questions)**: Active: "Why do you clean this room?" -> Passive: "Why is this room cleaned by you?"
• **Habitual Action Transformation**: Active: "My mother makes delicious cakes." -> Passive: "Delicious cakes are made by my mother."
• **Universal Truth Transformation**: Active: "The sun gives us light and heat." -> Passive: "Light and heat are given to us by the sun."
• **Key Pitfall to Avoid**: Students often incorrectly use "is" with plural subjects when converting simple present sentences into the passive voice.
• **Practice Mastery**: Mastery of Simple Present passive voice requires regular practice with both countable and uncountable nouns as subjects.

• **Present Continuous Tense Overview**: The Present Continuous tense describes an action that is ongoing or happening at the exact moment of speaking.
• **Active Structure in Present Continuous**: Subject + is / am / are + Verb-ing + Object (e.g., "The mechanic is repairing the car").
• **Passive Structure Formula**: New Subject + is / am / are + being + Third Form of the Verb (V3) + by + Agent.
• **The Crucial Role of 'Being'**: The word "being" is the mandatory marker used in all continuous tense passive transformations to indicate ongoing action.
• **Auxiliary Selection**: The primary auxiliary verbs remain "is," "am," and "are," governed by the new subject, followed immediately by "being."
• **Passive Conversion Example 1**: Active: "The boy is flying a kite." -> Passive: "A kite is being flown by the boy."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2**: Active: "They are building a new bridge." -> Passive: "A new bridge is being built by them."
• **Passive Conversion Example 3 (Plural Object)**: Active: "She is reading interesting books." -> Passive: "Interesting books are being read by her."
• **Passive Conversion Example 4 (First Person)**: Active: "You are helping me." -> Passive: "I am being helped by you."
• **Negative Sentences in Present Continuous**: Active: "The teacher is not punishing the students." -> Passive: "The students are not being punished by the teacher."
• **Interrogative Sentences (Yes/No)**: Active: "Is he painting a portrait?" -> Passive: "Is a portrait being painted by him?"
• **Interrogative Sentences (Wh-)**: Active: "What are you cooking in the kitchen?" -> Passive: "What is being cooked by you in the kitchen?"
• **Common Student Error**: Forgetting to include the word "being" is the most frequent mistake made by Class 6 students in continuous tense conversions.
• **Visualizing Continuous Action**: The combination of "is/am/are" + "being" + "V3" perfectly captures the passive equivalent of an ongoing event.
• **Consolidation**: Continuous tense passive voice emphasizes the process of the action being done to the object in real-time.

• **Simple Past Tense Overview**: The Simple Past tense is used to describe actions that were completed at a specific time in the past.
• **Active Structure in Simple Past**: Subject + Second Form of the Verb (V2) + Object (e.g., "Columbus discovered America").
• **Passive Structure Formula**: New Subject + was / were + Third Form of the Verb (V3) + by + Agent.
• **Auxiliary Selection for Simple Past**: Use "was" for singular subjects and the pronoun "I," and use "were" for all plural subjects.
• **Passive Conversion Example 1**: Active: "The hunter killed a tiger." -> Passive: "A tiger was killed by the hunter."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2**: Active: "Shakespeare wrote many plays." -> Passive: "Many plays were written by Shakespeare."
• **Passive Conversion Example 3 (Pronoun Shift)**: Active: "He scolded me yesterday." -> Passive: "I was scolded by him yesterday."
• **Passive Conversion Example 4 (Plural Subject)**: Active: "The police caught the thieves." -> Passive: "The thieves were caught by the police."
• **Negative Sentences in Simple Past**: Active: "She did not submit her homework." -> Passive: "Her homework was not submitted by her."
• **Interrogative Sentences (Yes/No)**: Active: "Did the principal announce the results?" -> Passive: "Were the results announced by the principal?"
• **Interrogative Sentences (Wh-)**: Active: "Who broke this expensive vase?" -> Passive: "By whom was this expensive vase broken?"
• **Special Rule for 'Who'**: In passive interrogative sentences starting with "Who," the phrase transforms into "By whom" at the beginning of the sentence.
• **Alternative for 'Who'**: "Who broke this vase?" can also be correctly converted to "This vase was broken by whom?" though "By whom" is preferred in formal DAV grammar.
• **Avoiding Auxiliary Confusions**: Care must be taken not to use present auxiliary verbs ("is/are") when dealing with past tense active sentences.
• **Historical and Narrative Utility**: Simple past passive voice is extensively used in history textbooks to describe past events, inventions, and historical milestones.

• **Past Continuous Tense Overview**: The Past Continuous tense describes an action that was ongoing at a specific moment in the past.
• **Active Structure in Past Continuous**: Subject + was / were + Verb-ing + Object (e.g., "The children were playing football").
• **Passive Structure Formula**: New Subject + was / were + being + Third Form of the Verb (V3) + by + Agent.
• **Auxiliary Maintenance**: The past auxiliary verbs "was" and "were" are retained, accompanied by the continuous marker "being" and the past participle (V3).
• **Passive Conversion Example 1**: Active: "She was washing the clothes." -> Passive: "The clothes were being washed by her."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2**: Active: "The gardener was watering the plants." -> Passive: "The plants were being watered by the gardener."
• **Passive Conversion Example 3 (Singular Object)**: Active: "They were cleaning the room." -> Passive: "The room was being cleaned by them."
• **Passive Conversion Example 4 (First Person)**: Active: "He was scolding me." -> Passive: "I was being scolded by him."
• **Negative Sentences in Past Continuous**: Active: "The mechanic was not repairing my bicycle." -> Passive: "My bicycle was not being repaired by the mechanic."
• **Interrogative Sentences (Yes/No)**: Active: "Was she singing a melodious song?" -> Passive: "Was a melodious song being sung by she? (Correct: Was a melodious song being sung by her?)"
• **Interrogative Sentences (Wh-)**: Active: "What were you writing in your notebook?" -> Passive: "What was being written by you in your notebook?"
• **Subject-Verb Agreement Nuance**: Ensure that if the active object is singular, the passive auxiliary is "was," and if plural, it becomes "were," regardless of the original active subject.
• **Descriptive Narrative Focus**: Past continuous passive structures are ideal for describing background scenes, interruptions, or ongoing historical states.
• **Error Prevention Drill**: Always check for the sequence: [Was/Were] + [being] + [V3] to ensure structural accuracy in past continuous passive sentences.
• **Class 6 Learning Milestone**: Mastering past continuous passive represents an advanced stage of syntactic flexibility for middle-school learners.

• **Simple Future Tense Overview**: The Simple Future tense expresses actions that have not yet happened and will take place in time to come.
• **Active Structure in Simple Future**: Subject + shall / will + First Form of the Verb (V1) + Object (e.g., "The principal will announce the winner").
• **Passive Structure Formula**: New Subject + shall / will + be + Third Form of the Verb (V3) + by + Agent.
• **The Inclusion of 'Be'**: The word "be" is added directly after "shall" or "will" in the passive voice construction of the simple future tense.
• **Modal Usage Rules**: "Shall" is traditionally used with first-person pronouns (I, we), while "will" is used with second and third persons (you, he, she, it, they).
• **Modern Auxiliary Trends**: In modern English, "will" is frequently used across all persons, but DAV grammar exercises encourage maintaining traditional subject-auxiliary pairings.
• **Passive Conversion Example 1**: Active: "She will write a letter." -> Passive: "A letter will be written by her."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2**: Active: "We shall finish the project tomorrow." -> Passive: "The project will be finished by us tomorrow."
• **Passive Conversion Example 3 (Plural Object)**: Active: "The DAV school will organize a science exhibition." -> Passive: "A science exhibition will be organized by the DAV school."
• **Passive Conversion Example 4 (Pronoun Shift)**: Active: "They will help you." -> Passive: "You will be helped by them."
• **Negative Sentences in Simple Future**: Active: "He will not solve this difficult sum." -> Passive: "This difficult sum will not be solved by him."
• **Interrogative Sentences (Yes/No)**: Active: "Will the teacher test our knowledge?" -> Passive: "Will our knowledge be tested by the teacher?"
• **Interrogative Sentences (Wh-)**: Active: "When will you return my book?" -> Passive: "When will my book be returned by you?"
• **Future Continuous Exclusion**: Note that Future Continuous tense sentences do not have a standard passive form in English grammar.
• **DAV Curriculum Scope**: The DAV Class 6 syllabus restricts future tense passive study strictly to the Simple Future tense.

• **Imperative Sentences Overview**: Imperative sentences express commands, requests, advice, suggestions, or orders in the active voice.
• **Active Structure of Commands**: Typically begin directly with the base verb and lack an explicit subject (e.g., "Close the door").
• **Passive Structure Formula for Commands**: Let + New Subject + be + Third Form of the Verb (V3).
• **Passive Conversion Example 1 (Command)**: Active: "Open the window." -> Passive: "Let the window be opened."
• **Passive Conversion Example 2 (Command)**: Active: "Post this letter immediately." -> Passive: "Let this letter be posted immediately."
• **Negative Imperative Active**: Active: "Do not pluck the flowers." -> Passive: "Let the flowers not be plucked" or "Let not the flowers be plucked."
• **Passive Conversion for Advice/Suggestions**: When an imperative sentence expresses advice or moral duty, the structure "Subject + should be + V3" is preferred.
• **Example of Advice Transformation**: Active: "Help the poor and needy." -> Passive: "The poor and needy should be helped."
• **Example of Duty Transformation**: Active: "Respect your elders." -> Passive: "Elders should be respected by you."
• **Passive Conversion for Requests**: Sentences beginning with "Please" or "Kindly" are converted using the polite phrase "You are requested to..."
• **Request Transformation Example**: Active: "Please lend me your pen." -> Passive: "You are requested to lend me your pen."
• **Negative Request Transformation**: Active: "Please do not make a noise." -> Passive: "You are requested not to make a noise."
• **Imperative Verbs with Objects**: Transitive imperative verbs require their direct objects to shift to the subject position after the word "Let."
• **Grammatical Nuance**: Imperative passive sentences do not feature an explicit agent unless it is recast with "should be" for advice.
• **Clarity in Directives**: Transforming imperatives into passive voice helps maintain a formal, objective tone in instructional writing.

• **Interrogative Sentences Overview**: Interrogative sentences are questions that seek information, beginning either with auxiliary verbs (Yes/No) or question words (Wh- words).
• **Yes/No Question Transformation Strategy**: First, mentally convert the interrogative sentence into an assertive (declarative) sentence to easily identify subject, verb, and object.
• **Step-by-Step Conversion Example**: Active Question: "Does he speak English?" -> Assertive: "He speaks English." -> Passive Assertive: "English is spoken by him." -> Final Passive Question: "Is English spoken by him?"
• **Past Tense Question Conversion**: Active Question: "Did you write this letter?" -> Assertive: "You wrote this letter." -> Passive Assertive: "This letter was written by you." -> Final Passive Question: "Was this letter written by you?"
• **Continuous Tense Question Conversion**: Active Question: "Are they playing a match?" -> Assertive: "They are playing a match." -> Passive Assertive: "A match is being played by them." -> Final Passive Question: "Is a match being played by them?"
• **Wh- Word Question Strategy**: Questions beginning with "What," "Where," "When," "Why," or "How" retain their question word at the very beginning of the passive sentence.
• **Example with Wh- Word**: Active: "Why did you punish the boy?" -> Passive: "Why was the boy punished by you?"
• **Exception for 'Who'**: As noted earlier, sentences starting with "Who" change to "By whom" at the beginning of the passive sentence.
• **Example with 'Who'**: Active: "Who painted this masterpiece?" -> Passive: "By whom was this masterpiece painted?"
• **Exception for 'Whom'**: Sentences starting with "Whom" change to "Who" at the beginning of the passive sentence.
• **Example with 'Whom'**: Active: "Whom did you invite to the party?" -> Passive: "Who was invited by you to the party?"
• **Auxiliary-Subject Inversion**: Always ensure that the auxiliary verb precedes the new subject in the final passive question format.
• **Punctuation Rule**: Every interrogative passive sentence must end with a question mark (?) rather than a full stop.
• **Common Student Pitfall**: Forgetting to invert the auxiliary and subject in the final step is the primary error in interrogative voice conversion.
• **Rigorous Practice**: Mastering interrogative transformations requires patience, systematic conversion steps, and meticulous attention to tense rules.

• **Comprehensive Review of Rules**: Summary of all active-to-passive transformation rules across simple, continuous, future, imperative, and interrogative sentences.
• **The Golden Rule Revisited**: The object becomes the subject, the subject becomes the agent (with "by"), the main verb changes to V3, and an appropriate auxiliary is added.
• **Tense Mapping Summary Table**: Simple Present (is/am/are + V3), Present Continuous (is/am/are being + V3), Simple Past (was/were + V3), Past Continuous (was/were being + V3), Simple Future (shall/will be + V3).
• **Pronoun Checklist**: Always double-check pronoun transformations (I -> me, we -> us, he -> him, she -> her, they -> them) in the agent phrase.
• **When Not to Use Passive**: Active voice is preferred when the doer is more important than the action, or when writing lively, direct narratives.
• **When to Use Passive**: Passive voice is preferred in scientific writing, official reports, instructions, and headlines where the focus rests entirely on the action or recipient.
• **DAV Examination Tips**: Read the active sentence carefully, identify the tense accurately, determine the direct object, and apply the structural formula step-by-step.
• **Self-Correction Strategy**: Always verify that the passive sentence retains the exact same tense and core meaning as the original active sentence.
• **Avoid Double Auxiliaries**: Ensure students do not mix up auxiliary verbs (e.g., writing "is was done" instead of "was done").
• **Spelling and Morphology**: Pay close attention to the spelling of irregular past participle verbs (V3) such as write/written, break/broken, catch/caught.
• **Importance of Prepositions**: Never omit mandatory prepositions that accompany phrasal verbs when converting to passive voice (e.g., "laughed at" remains "laughed at by").
