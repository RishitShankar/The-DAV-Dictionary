• **Detailed Vocabulary Expansion**: A thorough understanding of Chapter 7 requires mastery over specialized terminology related to botany, geography, and moral ethics.
• **Term: 'हरियाली' (Hariyali - Greenery)**: An abstract noun derived from 'हरा' (green), representing the dense growth of plants and trees across a landscape.
• **Term: 'पर्यावरण' (Paryavaran - Environment)**: The surrounding natural world comprising air, water, soil, plants, and animals, which is the core subject of Sundar Lal's mission.
• **Term: 'संरक्षण' (Sanrakshan - Conservation/Protection)**: The careful management and preservation of natural resources to prevent exploitation, destruction, or neglect.
• **Term: 'रोपण' (Ropan - Plantation)**: The deliberate act of planting seeds, saplings, or cuttings into the soil to grow new trees and vegetation.
• **Term: 'निस्वार्थ' (Niswarth - Selfless)**: An adjective describing an action performed without any expectation of personal gain, reward, or recognition.
• **Term: 'परिश्रम' (Parishram - Hard Work / Labor)**: Physical or mental effort directed toward achieving a specific goal, vital for both gardening and academic success.
• **Term: 'संकल्प' (Sankalp - Resolve / Determination)**: A firm mental decision or solemn promise to accomplish a challenging task, exemplified by Sundar Lal's vow to green his village.
• **Term: 'सहायता' (Sahayata - Assistance / Help)**: The act of supporting others, which in this context refers to how nature supports human life and how humans must support nature.
• **Term: 'उत्सव' (Utsav - Festival / Celebration)**: Often associated in the chapter with events like *Van Mahotsav*, where communities gather to plant trees collectively.
• **Term: 'भूमि' (Bhumi - Earth / Soil)**: The foundational layer of land that provides nutrients and anchorage for plant life, requiring constant care and protection.
• **Term: 'छाया' (Chhaya - Shade / Shadow)**: The cool, dark area produced by the canopy of trees, offering relief from the harsh tropical sun.
• **Term: 'जीवनदाता' (Jeevandata - Life-giver)**: A respectful epithet used for trees, acknowledging their critical role in supplying oxygen and sustaining life on Earth.
• **Term: 'प्रकृति' (Prakriti - Nature)**: The physical material world including plants, animals, the landscape, and other features of the earth, contrasted with human creations.
• **Term: 'प्रेरणा' (Prerna - Inspiration)**: The stimulus or driving force that motivates an individual to emulate positive behaviors and noble deeds.

• **Advanced Grammar Drill: Sentence Analysis (*वाक्य विश्लेषण*)**: Building upon DAV curriculum standards, students learn to dissect sentences from the chapter into their primary structural components.
• **Subject (*उद्देश्य*) and Predicate (*विधेय*)**: Every sentence in Hindi is divided into the doer/topic (उद्देश्य) and the information about the doer (विधेय), practiced using chapter sentences.
• **Types of Sentences by Meaning (*अर्थ के आधार पर वाक्य भेद*)**: Sentences from "सुंदर लाल" are categorized into declarative (*विधानवाचक*), interrogative (*प्रश्नवाचक*), imperative (*आज्ञावाचक*), and exclamatory (*विस्मयादिबोधक*).
• **Declarative Sentences (*विधानवाचक वाक्य*)**: Statements stating a general truth or fact about the story, such as "सुंदर लाल ने पौधे लगाए" (Sundar Lal planted saplings).
• **Exclamatory Sentences (*विस्मयादिबोधक वाक्य*)**: Expressions of sudden emotion related to nature's beauty or devastation, such as "कितना सुंदर यह पेड़ है!" (How beautiful this tree is!).
• **Imperative Sentences (*आज्ञावाचक वाक्य*)**: Commands or requests encouraging environmental protection, such as "पेड़ मत काटिए, पौधे लगाइए" (Do not cut trees, plant saplings).
• **Interrogative Sentences (*प्रश्नवाचक वाक्य*)**: Questions formulated to test comprehension of the text, such as "सुंदर लाल ने गांव में क्या किया?" (What did Sundar Lal do in the village?).
• **Transformation of Sentences (*वाक्य परिवर्तन*)**: Exercises teach students how to convert a simple affirmative sentence into a negative or interrogative form without altering its core meaning.
• **Gender Rules in Hindi (*लिंग*)**: Students review masculine (*पुल्लिंग*) and feminine (*स्त्रीलिंग*) rules, paying special attention to nouns related to nature and animals found in the chapter.
• **Number Rules in Hindi (*वचन*)**: Singular (*एकवचन*) and plural (*बहुवचन*) transformations are practiced using chapter-specific vocabulary like 'पेड़' (tree/trees) and 'पत्ता' (leaf) to 'पत्ते' (leaves).
• **Case Markers (*कारक चिह्न / विभक्तियाँ*)**: The usage of postpositions like 'ने', 'को', 'से', 'पर', and 'का/की/के' is analyzed within the context of narrative sentences.
• **Conjunctions (*समुच्चयबोधक*)**: Words like 'और' (and), 'लेकिन' (but), 'क्योंकि' (because), and 'इसलिए' (therefore) are studied for their role in joining clauses and sentences smoothly.
• **Interjections (*विस्मयादिबोधक अव्यय*)**: Words expressing surprise, joy, or sorrow are identified to understand how authors inject emotional tone into prose.
• **Direct and Indirect Speech Elements**: Basic introductory concepts of reporting statements made by characters in the narrative are explored through guided exercises.
• **Practical Application in Writing**: The grammar rules learned through these drills are immediately applied by students in drafting short paragraphs about environmental conservation.
