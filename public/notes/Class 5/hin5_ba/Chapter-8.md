• **Tense Identification (काल):** Analyzing sentences from the story to determine whether an action happened in the past (भूतकाल), present (वर्तमानकाल), or future (भविष्यत्काल).

• **Gender Identification (लिंग पहचान):** Understanding the two grammatical genders in Hindi—Masculine (पुल्लिंग) and Feminine (स्त्रीलिंग)—using words from the chapter.
• **Masculine Nouns in Chapter (पुल्लिंग शब्द):** Terms such as 'बाघ' (tiger), 'बंदर' (monkey), 'वर्षा' (rain - contextually used in certain constructs, though standard rain is feminine, specific terms vary), and 'गधा' (donkey).
• **Feminine Nouns in Chapter (स्त्रीलिंग शब्द):** Terms such as 'बुढ़िया' (old woman), 'झोपड़ी' (hut), 'रात' (night), and 'बरसात' (heavy rain/monsoon).
• **Gender Transformation Rules:** Learning how masculine words transform into feminine forms by adding suffixes like '-इया' (e.g., चूहा to चुहिया, बेटा to बेटी, or specific terms like बाघ to बाघिन).
• **Feminine of Tiger (बाघिन):** The specific feminine form of the wild animal featured prominently in the chapter title and plot.
• **Gender Agreement in Sentences:** Ensuring adjectives and verbs agree in gender with the subject noun (e.g., बूढ़ी बुढ़िया, डरा हुआ बाघ).
• **Number Identification (वचन):** Mastering the concept of Singular (एकवचन) and Plural (बहुवचन) forms for all nouns appearing in the lesson.
• **Singular Nouns (एकवचन):** Words representing a single entity, such as 'बाघ' (tiger), 'पेड़' (tree), and 'बर्तन' (utensil).
• **Plural Nouns (बहुवचन):** Words representing multiple entities, such as 'बाघों' (tigers), 'पेड़ों' (trees), and 'बर्तनों' (utensils) in oblique cases.
• **Pluralization Rules (-ए):** Changing ending sounds to form plurals, such as 'झोपड़ी' to 'झोपड़ियाँ' (applying rules for feminine nouns ending in 'ई').
• **Pluralization Rules (-आ to -ए):** Transforming masculine nouns ending in 'आ' to 'ए' (e.g., बकरा to बकरे - applicable in general exercises linked to the text).
• **Unchanged Words in Plural:** Certain nouns remain identical in both singular and plural forms depending on the sentence structure and कारक (case).
• **Case Markers (कारक चिन्ह):** Examining postpositions like 'ने', 'को', 'से', 'पर', 'में' as they appear in the sentences of "टपके का डर".
• **Subjective Case (कर्ता कारक):** Identifying the doer of the action marked by 'ने' in past tense transitive verbs (e.g., बुढ़िया ने कहा).
• **Objective Case (कर्म कारक):** Identifying the receiver of the action marked by 'को' within chapter-based sentences.
