• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to the DAV Class 7 Social Science academic study notes for Chapter 4, titled "Air Around Us", from the textbook *We and Our World*.
• **Atmosphere Definition**: The Earth is surrounded by a massive blanket of air known as the **atmosphere**, which extends thousands of kilometers above the planetary surface.
• **Gravitational Retention**: The atmosphere is firmly held in place around the Earth due to the continuous and powerful pull of **gravitational attraction**.
• **Biological Necessity**: Without this protective envelope of air, life on Earth would be entirely impossible as it provides the fundamental gases required for respiration and metabolic processes.
• **Thermal Insulation**: The atmosphere acts as a vital thermal blanket that traps heat, preventing extreme temperature fluctuations between freezing nights and scorching days.
• **UV Radiation Shield**: The atmospheric layers contain specialized gases, most notably ozone, that successfully filter out harmful ultraviolet radiation emitted by the sun.
• **Primary Gas Composition**: Dry air in the lower atmosphere is predominantly composed of two major permanent gases: **Nitrogen** and **Oxygen**.
• **Nitrogen Percentage**: **Nitrogen** is the most abundant gas in the atmosphere, making up approximately **78 percent** of total air volume by percentage.
• **Nitrogen Function**: Although humans and animals cannot use nitrogen directly from the air, plants require it for growth through biological nitrogen fixation processes.
• **Oxygen Percentage**: **Oxygen** is the second most abundant gas, accounting for roughly **21 percent** of the total volume of atmospheric air.
• **Respiration Role**: Oxygen is essential for the survival of most living organisms, as humans and animals inhale it to metabolize food and generate biological energy.
• **Combustion Support**: Oxygen acts as a crucial supporter of combustion, meaning no fire or burning process can take place in its complete absence.
• **Argon Presence**: **Argon** is the third most abundant gas in the atmosphere, representing roughly **0.93 percent** of the air volume.
• **Trace Gases**: The remaining **0.04 percent** of the atmosphere consists of carbon dioxide, helium, neon, methane, hydrogen, and variable amounts of water vapor.
• **Carbon Dioxide Proportion**: **Carbon Dioxide (CO2)** constitutes only about **0.03 percent** of the atmosphere, yet it plays a disproportionately massive role in regulating planetary climate.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Greenhouse Effect Basics**: Carbon dioxide is a primary greenhouse gas that absorbs infrared radiation emitted from the Earth's surface, keeping the planet warm and habitable.
• **Photosynthesis Cycle**: Green plants utilize atmospheric carbon dioxide during daytime hours to synthesize their food through the vital biochemical process of **photosynthesis**.
• **Balance Maintenance**: A delicate natural balance of gases is maintained in the atmosphere through the interconnected cycles of respiration, combustion, and photosynthesis.
• **Atmospheric Stratification**: The atmosphere is not uniform; instead, it is divided vertically into **five distinct layers** based on temperature profiles, density, and altitude.
• **Troposphere Definition**: The **troposphere** is the lowest and densest layer of the atmosphere, extending from the Earth's surface up to an average height of 13 kilometers.
• **Weather Phenomenon Hub**: Virtually all weather phenomena—including clouds, rain, snow, fog, thunderstorms, and windstorms—occur exclusively within the troposphere.
• **Temperature Decrease with Height**: Within the troposphere, temperature decreases steadily with an increase in altitude, dropping at an average rate of 6.5°C per kilometer.
• **Tropopause Boundary**: The upper boundary of the troposphere is known as the **tropopause**, which acts as a thermal transition zone separating it from the layer above.
• **Stratosphere Location**: The **stratosphere** lies directly above the troposphere, extending from the tropopause up to an altitude of approximately 50 kilometers.
• **Calm Layer Conditions**: The stratosphere is characterized by extremely calm, clear weather conditions devoid of clouds, making it the ideal cruising altitude for commercial jet aircraft.
• **Temperature Increase in Stratosphere**: Unlike the troposphere, the temperature in the lower stratosphere remains constant and then rises with altitude due to the absorption of solar energy.
• **Ozone Layer Presence**: A high concentration of ozone molecules forms the **ozone layer**, which is located entirely within the stratosphere between 15 and 35 kilometers high.
• **Ozone Protection Mechanism**: The ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun, protecting biological life forms from severe skin cancers and immune disorders.
• **Mesosphere Position**: The **mesosphere** extends above the stratosphere from an altitude of 50 kilometers up to roughly 85 kilometers from the Earth's surface.
• **Coldest Atmospheric Layer**: The mesosphere is recognized as the coldest layer of the atmosphere, with temperatures plummeting as low as minus 90 degrees Celsius near its top.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Meteor Destruction**: Incoming space rocks, or meteoroids, typically burn up upon entering the dense frictional gases of the mesosphere, preventing them from striking Earth's surface.
• **Thermosphere Characteristics**: The **thermosphere** extends upward from 85 kilometers to about 600 kilometers, where extreme solar radiation causes molecules to become electrically charged.
• **Ionization and Radio Waves**: Due to high-energy radiation, the lower part of the thermosphere contains ions, forming a sub-layer called the **ionosphere** that reflects radio waves back to Earth.
• **Temperature Rise in Thermosphere**: Temperatures in the thermosphere rise dramatically with increasing altitude, sometimes reaching thousands of degrees due to direct bombardment by solar photons.
• **Exosphere Boundary**: The **exosphere** is the uppermost, extremely rarefied outermost layer of the atmosphere, gradually merging seamlessly into the vacuum of interplanetary outer space.
• **Exosphere Composition**: The exosphere consists primarily of extremely light gases such as **hydrogen** and **helium**, which float off into space due to weak gravitational pull at high altitudes.
• **Weather Definition**: **Weather** refers to the day-to-day, short-term state of the atmosphere at a specific location, encompassing variables like temperature, humidity, wind, and precipitation.
• **Climate Definition**: **Climate** represents the long-term, generalized weather pattern of a large geographical region averaged over a span of 30 years or more.
• **Insolation Concept**: **Insolation** (Incoming Solar Radiation) is the total amount of solar energy received by the Earth's surface from the sun.
• **Equator to Pole Gradient**: Insolation is highest at the equator because the sun's rays fall vertically, and it decreases progressively toward the poles where rays slant diagonally.
• **Temperature Measurement**: Atmospheric temperature is measured using a **thermometer**, typically calibrated in degrees Celsius (°C) or Fahrenheit (°F).
• **Greenhouse Gas Warming**: Industrial activities and the burning of fossil fuels have increased atmospheric carbon dioxide levels, enhancing the greenhouse effect and driving **global warming**.
• **Global Warming Consequences**: Unchecked global warming leads to severe environmental consequences, including rising global sea levels, melting polar ice caps, and unpredictable climate disruptions.
• **Air Pressure Definition**: **Air pressure** is defined as the continuous physical force exerted by the weight of a column of air against the Earth's surface and all objects upon it.
• **Pressure Variation Factors**: Air pressure is never uniform; it varies significantly across different locations and at different times due to altitude, temperature, and moisture content.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Altitude Effect on Pressure**: Air pressure is highest at sea level because the entire weight of the atmospheric column presses down, and it decreases rapidly as altitude increases.
• **Low Pressure at Heights**: Mountaineers and high-altitude aviators experience lower air pressure and thinner oxygen levels, which can lead to altitude sickness if they ascend too quickly.
• **Temperature and Pressure Link**: Warm air expands, becomes lighter, and ascends, creating a zone of **low atmospheric pressure** near the surface of the earth.
• **Cold Air and High Pressure**: Cold air is dense, heavy, and sinks toward the ground, creating a distinct zone of **high atmospheric pressure** in that specific geographical region.
• **Barometer Instrument**: Atmospheric pressure is measured precisely using an instrument known as a **barometer**, commonly available in mercury or aneroid types.
• **Wind Definition**: **Wind** is defined as the horizontal movement of air flowing naturally from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure across the Earth's surface.
• **Wind Vane Usage**: The direction from which the wind blows is determined using a **wind vane** (or weather vane), while wind speed is measured using an **anemometer**.
• **Planetary Winds**: **Planetary winds** (also known as permanent or prevailing winds) blow constantly throughout the year in a particular direction across vast latitudinal belts.
• **Trade Winds**: Trade winds are steady permanent easterly winds that blow from the subtropical high-pressure belts toward the equatorial low-pressure trough.
• **Westerlies**: Westerlies are prevailing winds that blow from the subtropical high-pressure regions toward the subpolar low-pressure belts in the middle latitudes.
• **Polar Easterlies**: Polar easterlies are cold, dry prevailing winds that blow continuously from the polar high-pressure regions toward the subpolar low-pressure zones.
• **Seasonal Winds (Monsoons)**: **Seasonal winds** (or **monsoons**) completely reverse their direction periodically with the changing seasons, most famously exemplified by the Indian subcontinent weather system.
• **Summer Monsoon Characteristics**: During summer, landmasses heat up faster than oceans, creating a low-pressure center that draws moisture-laden winds inward from the sea, bringing heavy rain.
• **Winter Monsoon Characteristics**: During winter, the land cools rapidly, creating a high-pressure zone that pushes dry winds outward from the continent toward the ocean.
• **Local Winds**: **Local winds** blow only over small localized areas for specific periods of the day or year, driven by regional temperature and pressure differences.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Land Breeze Mechanism**: A **land breeze** is a local wind that blows from the land toward the sea during the night as the land cools down faster than the adjacent water body.
• **Sea Breeze Mechanism**: A **sea breeze** is a daytime local wind that blows from the cool sea toward the warm land as rising air over the land creates a localized low-pressure pocket.
• **Loo Wind Example**: The **Loo** is a notorious, scorching, dry, and gusty local wind that blows across the northern and western plains of India during peak summer afternoons.
• **Cyclone Definition**: A **cyclone** (often called a hurricane or typhoon regionally) is a violent, spinning atmospheric storm characterized by a central low-pressure core surrounded by spiraling winds.
• **Moisture in Air (Humidity)**: **Humidity** refers to the actual amount of invisible water vapor present in a given volume of atmospheric air at any specific time and place.
• **Absolute vs Relative Humidity**: Absolute humidity measures total water vapor per unit volume, whereas relative humidity expresses water vapor content as a percentage of the maximum capacity at that temperature.
• **Saturated Air**: Air is said to be **saturated** when it holds the absolute maximum amount of water vapor possible at its current temperature.
• **Evaporation Process**: **Evaporation** is the physical process by which liquid water absorbs thermal energy and transforms into invisible water vapor gas, rising into the atmosphere.
• **Condensation Process**: **Condensation** occurs when warm, moisture-laden air cools down, causing water vapor to change back into microscopic liquid water droplets or ice crystals.
• **Dew Point Definition**: The **dew point** is the critical temperature at which air becomes fully saturated with water vapor and condensation begins to form on cool surfaces.
• **Dew Formation**: On clear, calm, and cold winter nights, moisture in the air condenses directly onto cold ground objects like grass and leaves, forming tiny liquid droplets known as **dew**.
• **Frost Formation**: If the local dew point temperature drops below the freezing point of water (0°C), the condensing moisture forms delicate ice crystals known as **frost**.
• **Fog Definition**: **Fog** is essentially a low-lying cloud formed when condensation of water vapor occurs around microscopic dust particles suspended near the Earth's surface, reducing visibility.
• **Cloud Definition**: **Clouds** are massive visible aggregates of countless microscopic water droplets or ice crystals floating together high up in the troposphere.
• **Precipitation Definition**: **Precipitation** is any product of the condensation of atmospheric water vapor that falls under gravity from clouds down to the Earth's surface.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Rainfall Definition**: **Rainfall** is the most common form of precipitation, consisting of liquid water droplets that have grown heavy enough to fall from clouds.
• **Convectional Rainfall**: Convectional rainfall occurs when intensely heated surface air rises rapidly, cools adiabatically, forms cumulus clouds, and dumps heavy afternoon showers with thunder.
• **Relief (Orographic) Rainfall**: Orographic rainfall happens when moisture-laden winds are forced to ascend a mountain barrier, cooling and precipitating on the windward slopes.
• **Rain Shadow Area**: The leeward side of a mountain receives very little precipitation and is designated as a **rain shadow area** due to the descending dry air currents.
• **Cyclonic (Frontal) Rainfall**: Cyclonic rainfall is produced when warm and cold air masses meet along a front, causing the lighter warm air to rise over the cold air and condense.
• **Acid Rain Phenomenon**: Industrial pollutants like sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides mix with atmospheric water vapor to form **acid rain**, which damages forests, aquatic life, and ancient monuments.
• **Ozone Depletion Crisis**: Anthropogenic chemicals such as **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** released from refrigerants and aerosols drift into the stratosphere and destroy protective ozone molecules.
• **Montreal Protocol Impact**: International environmental agreements like the **Montreal Protocol** successfully phased out ozone-depleting substances, initiating the gradual healing of the ozone layer.
• **Air Pollution Sources**: Air pollution is caused by the release of harmful particulate matter, toxic gases, carbon monoxide, and lead into the atmosphere primarily through vehicular exhausts and factories.
• **Health Impacts of Pollution**: Prolonged exposure to polluted air causes severe respiratory illnesses, chronic asthma, cardiovascular diseases, and systemic health complications in humans.
• **Carbon Footprint Concept**: A **carbon footprint** represents the total set of greenhouse gas emissions generated directly and indirectly by an individual, organization, or product.
• **Afforestation Importance**: Large-scale **afforestation** and tree-planting drives are crucial environmental strategies because trees absorb carbon dioxide and release fresh oxygen.
• **Renewable Energy Shift**: Transitioning from polluting fossil fuels to clean, **renewable energy sources** like solar, wind, and hydroelectric power helps mitigate climate change.
• **Atmospheric Monitoring**: Global meteorological agencies continuously monitor atmospheric parameters using advanced satellites, radar systems, and weather balloons to forecast weather accurately.
• **Student Takeaway**: Understanding the dynamic nature of the "Air Around Us" empowers students to appreciate the delicate ecological balance sustaining all life on planet Earth.

• **Comprehensive Review of Chapter 4**: We now enter the review module of the DAV Class 7 curriculum for "Air Around Us", focusing on key interactions within the Earth-atmosphere system.
• **Role of Dust Particles**: The atmosphere contains microscopic solid particles such as dust, pollen, salt crystals, and smoke, which act as **hygroscopic nuclei** essential for cloud formation.
• **Scattering of Light**: Atmospheric gases and particles scatter incoming sunlight in all directions through **Rayleigh scattering**, which is the primary reason why the daytime sky appears blue.
• **Red Sunrise and Sunset**: During sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through a much thicker layer of atmosphere, scattering shorter blue wavelengths and allowing longer red wavelengths to reach our eyes.
• **Greenhouse Gas Trap Mechanism**: Earth's surface absorbs shortwave solar radiation and re-emits it as longwave infrared radiation, which is trapped by greenhouse gases to maintain a life-friendly warmth.
• **Natural Greenhouse Effect vs Enhanced**: While the *natural greenhouse effect* keeps Earth's average temperature at a livable 15°C, the *enhanced greenhouse effect* causes unnatural global heating.
• **Barometric Pressure Belts**: The Earth's surface is marked by permanent pressure belts: the Equatorial Low, Subtropical Highs, Subpolar Lows, and Polar Highs.
• **Coriolis Effect Introduction**: The **Coriolis effect** is an apparent deflection of moving air (winds and ocean currents) caused by the continuous rotation of the Earth on its axis.
• **Direction of Deflection**: Due to the Coriolis effect, moving winds are deflected to the **right** in the Northern Hemisphere and to the **left** in the Southern Hemisphere.
• **Geostrophic Winds**: High above the surface, pressure gradient forces and the Coriolis effect balance each other out perfectly, creating **geostrophic winds** that flow parallel to isobars.
• **Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)**: The **ITCZ** is a low-pressure belt circling Earth near the equator where trade winds from the Northern and Southern Hemispheres converge.
• **Monsoon Mechanism in India**: The seasonal reversal of winds in the Indian subcontinent is driven by the northward shift of the ITCZ and differential heating of the Asian landmass.
• **Cloud Classifications**: Clouds are scientifically classified based on their altitude and appearance into major categories: **Cirrus** (high, wispy), **Cumulus** (fluffy, piled), and **Stratus** (layered).
• **Nimbus Clouds**: **Nimbus** clouds are dark, heavy, thick layers of clouds associated directly with continuous rain, thunderstorms, and heavy precipitation events.
• **Hail Formation**: Hailstones form when strong updrafts within towering cumulonimbus clouds carry water droplets high into freezing zones, where layers of ice accumulate before falling.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Sleet Definition**: **Sleet** consists of frozen raindrops or refelted snowflakes that form when snowflakes pass through a warm layer of air and then refreeze in a sub-freezing layer near the ground.
• **Atmospheric Moisture Sources**: Water vapor enters the atmosphere predominantly through the evaporation of oceans, seas, lakes, and rivers, supplemented by plant transpiration.
• **Transpiration Process**: **Transpiration** is the biological process by which plants release absorbed soil water in the form of water vapor through microscopic pores called stomata.
• **Evapotranspiration**: The combined total loss of water to the atmosphere via direct soil evaporation and plant transpiration is collectively referred to as **evapotranspiration**.
• **Air Masses**: An **air mass** is a massive body of air covering thousands of kilometers that acquires uniform temperature and moisture characteristics from its underlying geographic region.
• **Frontal Boundaries**: A **front** is the narrow transition zone or boundary separating two distinct air masses with contrasting temperatures, densities, and humidity levels.
• **Warm Front Action**: A **warm front** occurs when a warm air mass slides gently up and over a retreating wedge of colder, denser air, producing steady, prolonged drizzle.
• **Cold Front Action**: A **cold front** forms when a dense, cold air mass plows aggressively under a warm air mass, forcing the warm air upward rapidly to trigger violent thunderstorms.
• **Barometric Tendency**: A sudden, sharp drop in barometric pressure is a reliable meteorological indicator that a severe storm, cyclone, or severe weather front is fast approaching.
• **Aneroid Barometer Mechanics**: An aneroid barometer utilizes a flexible, sealed metal chamber (aneroid cell) that expands and contracts with shifting air pressure without using liquid mercury.
• **Wind Speed Calibration**: The **Beaufort scale** is an empirical measure that relates wind speed directly to observed sea conditions, land effects, and structural damage intensities.
• **Anemometer Operation**: A standard cup anemometer uses rotating hollow cups driven by moving air, with the rotational speed electronically calibrated to display exact wind velocity.
• **Air Quality Index (AQI)**: The **Air Quality Index (AQI)** is a standardized numerical scale used by governments to communicate to the public how polluted the air currently is or is forecasted to become.
• **Smog Formation**: **Smog** is a hazardous mixture of smoke, particulate matter, and chemical fog that blankets urban and industrial centers, severely impairing visibility and respiratory health.
• **Photochemical Smog**: Photochemical smog forms when sunlight reacts chemically with nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds emitted by automobiles and industrial plants.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Indoor Air Pollution**: Indoor air pollution is often overlooked, caused by burning solid fuels (wood, dung, coal) in poorly ventilated traditional stoves, releasing toxic carbon monoxide indoors.
• **Ozone Hole Phenomenon**: The term **ozone hole** refers to a seasonal thinning of the ozone layer primarily observed over the Antarctic continent during the Southern Hemisphere's spring.
• **UV-B Radiation Hazards**: Unfiltered ultraviolet-B rays reaching the Earth due to ozone depletion can cause cataracts, suppress human immune systems, and disrupt marine phytoplankton populations.
• **Kyoto Protocol Overview**: The **Kyoto Protocol** was an international treaty committing industrialized nations to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to combat global climate change effectively.
• **Paris Agreement Targets**: The **Paris Agreement** is a legally binding international treaty on climate change adopted to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.
• **Carbon Sequestration**: **Carbon sequestration** is the long-term removal and storage of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through natural sinks (forests, oceans) or artificial geological technologies.
• **Role of Oceans as Carbon Sinks**: The world's oceans absorb roughly a quarter of all human-generated carbon dioxide emissions, though this causes harmful ocean acidification that threatens coral reefs.
• **Wind Energy Utilization**: Wind turbines harness the kinetic energy of moving air masses to generate clean, renewable electricity without emitting greenhouse gases or pollutants.
• **Solar Power Integration**: Integrating solar panels on rooftops captures abundant insolation to produce sustainable electrical energy, reducing reliance on coal-fired power plants.
• **Meteorological Stations**: National meteorological departments operate vast networks of automated weather stations on land, at sea, and in space to continuously track atmospheric conditions.
• **Radiosondes Importance**: **Radiosondes** are instrumented weather packages attached to helium balloons that ascend high into the stratosphere to transmit real-time data on pressure, temperature, and humidity.
• **Satellite Meteorology**: Weather satellites orbit the Earth to capture high-resolution imagery of cloud patterns, track cyclones in real-time, and monitor global atmospheric circulation loops.
• **Climatic Zones of Earth**: Based on insolation and latitude, the Earth is divided into three major climatic zones: the **Torrid Zone**, the **Temperate Zone**, and the **Frigid Zone**.
• **Torrid Zone Characteristics**: The **Torrid Zone** lies between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn, experiencing high insolation and hot, humid tropical climates year-round.
• **Frigid Zone Characteristics**: The **Frigid Zone** surrounds both polar regions inside the Arctic and Antarctic Circles, receiving slanted sun rays and enduring extreme, freezing conditions.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Temperate Zone Characteristics**: The **Temperate Zone** is situated between the tropical and polar regions in both hemispheres, featuring moderate temperatures and four distinct seasonal shifts.
• **Altitude and Climate**: Even within the Torrid Zone, high-altitude locations like mountainous regions (e.g., Quito in the Andes) experience cool or cold climates due to the lapse rate.
• **Maritime vs Continental Climate**: Areas situated close to the sea experience a **maritime (oceanic) climate** with moderate temperature ranges, while interior continental landmasses experience extreme ranges.
• **Monsoon Impact on Agriculture**: Indian agriculture is heavily dependent on the timely arrival and spatial distribution of the **south-west monsoon winds** for successful crop irrigation and harvest yields.
• **Rainfed Farming Vulnerability**: Regions lacking robust irrigation infrastructure depend entirely on monsoon rainfall, making agricultural output vulnerable to droughts and delayed seasonal winds.
