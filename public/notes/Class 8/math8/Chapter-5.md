### [BLUE] PAGE 2: Fundamental Principles & Theory Deep-Dive

*   💼 **Economic Reality of Commercial Transactions**:
    *   Every commercial transaction revolves around an exchange of goods or services for monetary value between a **Manufacturer/Wholesaler**, a **Retailer**, and a **Consumer**.
*   🏭 **The Anatomy of Cost Price (CP)**:
    *   **Definition**: The absolute financial expenditure incurred by a buyer to acquire an article.
    *   **Direct Cost**: The primary acquisition price paid to the direct vendor.
    *   **Overhead Expenses**: Additional unforeseen or mandatory expenditures incurred *after* purchase but *before* resale (e.g., cartage, freight, repair, octroi, wages, GST/taxes).
    *   **Principle of Effective CP**: Total/Effective $\text{CP} = \text{Direct Purchase Price} + \text{Overhead Expenses}$. *All percentage calculations for profit or loss must always be anchored to this Net Effective CP.*
*   🏷️ **The Mechanics of Selling Price (SP)**:
    *   **Definition**: The actual realized monetary value for which an article is transferred from the seller to the consumer.
    *   **Determinants**: Market demand, competitor pricing, manufacturing cost, and desired profit margins.
*   ⚖️ **The Profit-Loss Dichotomy**:
    *   **Profit (Gain)**: Occurs strictly when $\text{SP} > \text{CP}$. It represents net economic surplus.
    *   **Loss**: Occurs strictly when $\text{CP} > \text{SP}$. It represents net economic deficit.
    *   **Break-Even**: Occurs when $\text{SP} = \text{CP}$. Neither economic gain nor loss is realized.
*   🏷️ **Marked Price (MP) & Discount Theory**:
    *   Retailers rarely sell goods at manufacturing cost. They inflate the price tag to create a buffer for bargaining and promotional discounts.
    *   **Marked Price (MP)**: The printed, catalogued, or tagged price of an article. Also known as **List Price**.
    *   **Discount**: A voluntary monetary concession or percentage reduction offered by the seller on the Marked Price to incentivize consumer purchase.

### [BLUE] PAGE 3: Scientific Laws, Formulas, Equations & Principles

*   📐 **Core Profit and Loss Equations**:
    *   $\text{Profit} (P) = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \quad [\text{Valid only when } \text{SP} > \text{CP}]$
    *   $\text{Loss} (L) = \text{CP} - \text{SP} \quad [\text{Valid only when } \text{CP} > \text{SP}]$
*   📊 **Percentage Formula Matrix**:
    *   $\text{Profit } \% = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100$
    *   $\text{Loss } \% = \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100$
*   🔄 **Direct SP and CP Derivation Formulas**:
    *   $\text{SP} = \left( \frac{100 + P\%}{100} \right) \times \text{CP}$
    *   $\text{SP} = \left( \frac{100 - L\%}{100} \right) \times \text{CP}$
    *   $\text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 + P\%} \right) \times \text{SP}$
    *   $\text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 - L\%} \right) \times \text{SP}$
*   🏷️ **Marked Price and Discount Equations**:
    *   $\text{Discount } (D) = \text{MP} - \text{SP}$
    *   $\text{Discount } \% = \left( \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}} \right) \times 100$
    *   $\text{SP} = \text{MP} - \text{Discount}$
    *   $\text{SP} = \left( \frac{100 - D\%}{100} \right) \times \text{MP}$
    *   $\text{MP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 - D\%} \right) \times \text{SP}$
*   ⚡ **Advanced Multi-Step Formulas**:
    *   If an article is sold at a profit of $x\%$, and had it been bought for $y\%$ less and sold for ₹$z$ less, the new CP/SP can be solved via linear algebraic modeling.
    *   **Successive Discounts Principle**: If two successive discounts of $a\%$ and $b\%$ are offered on an MP, the **Effective Single Equivalent Discount** is given by:
        $$\text{Net Discount } \% = \left( a + b - \frac{a \times b}{100} \right)\%$$

### [BLUE] PAGE 4: Exhaustive Technical Glossary & Precision Definitions

*   📖 **Cost Price (CP)**: The total financial outlay required to procure an item and make it ready for sale, inclusive of all hidden or direct logistics charges.
*   📖 **Selling Price (SP)**: The final price realized by the vendor upon the disposal of the merchandise.
*   📖 **Overhead Charges**: Auxiliary expenses such as transportation, repairs, warehousing, and taxes that do not alter the physical nature of the goods but artificially elevate their baseline CP.
*   📖 **Net Profit**: The absolute monetary gain realized when revenue from sales exceeds the total cumulative cost of acquisition and handling.
*   📖 **Net Loss**: The absolute monetary deficit incurred when sales revenue falls short of the total cumulative cost.
*   📖 **Marked Price (MP) / List Price**: The baseline price inscribed on the product tag, catalogue, or packaging, serving as the ceiling price before any promotional reductions.
*   📖 **Discount**: The absolute financial abatement deducted from the Marked Price by the retailer.
*   📖 **Selling Price via Discount**: The ultimate transaction price calculated by subtracting the absolute discount from the Marked Price ($\text{SP} = \text{MP} - D$).
*   📖 **Successive Discounts**: A chain of multiple percentage discounts applied sequentially (e.g., a discount on the marked price, followed by a festive discount on the discounted price).
*   📖 **Commercial Margin**: The ratio of gross profit to the selling price or cost price, used by businesses to measure operational efficiency.

### [BLUE] PAGE 5: Detailed Page-by-Page Textbook Highlights & Core Factoids

*   📌 **Textbook Highlight 1: The Golden Rule of Denominators**:
    *   Profit percentage and loss percentage are *always* calculated with respect to the **Cost Price** (specifically Net CP), never the Selling Price, unless explicitly specified by a specialized problem statement.
*   📌 **Textbook Highlight 2: The Marked Price Anchor**:
    *   Discount percentage is *always* calculated with respect to the **Marked Price (MP)**, never the Cost Price or Selling Price. Confusing MP with CP is the #1 source of student error in DAV Board examinations.
*   📌 **Textbook Highlight 3: Handling Overhead Expenses**:
    *   *Factoid*: If a secondhand bicycle is bought for ₹2000 and ₹300 is spent on its immediate repairs, the student must *never* use ₹2000 as CP. The true baseline for all calculations is $\text{CP} = 2000 + 300 = \text{₹}2300$.
*   📌 **Textbook Highlight 4: The SP Bridge**:
    *   The Selling Price (SP) acts as the fundamental bridge linking the procurement side ($\text{CP} \leftrightarrow \text{Profit/Loss}$) with the consumer side ($\text{MP} \leftrightarrow \text{Discount}$). Mastery of problems bridging these two domains is essential for high scores.
*   📌 **Textbook Highlight 5: Zero-Profit Condition**:
    *   When $\text{SP} = \text{CP}$, the profit is zero and the loss is zero. The transaction is termed a "No-Gain, No-Loss" scenario.

### [BLUE] PAGE 6: Conceptual Breakdown, Practical Examples & Applications

*   💡 **Concept Application 1: Factoring Overheads**
    *   *Scenario*: A merchant purchases 50 chairs at ₹400 each. He pays ₹1000 for transport. He sells all of them at ₹500 each. Find his total profit percentage.
    *   *Step-by-Step Resolution*:
        1. Direct Cost of chairs = $50 \times 400 = \text{₹}20,000$.
        2. Overhead Expenses = $\text{₹}1000$.
        3. Total Effective $\text{CP} = 20,000 + 1000 = \text{₹}21,000$.
        4. Total $\text{SP} = 50 \times 500 = \text{₹}25,000$.
        5. $\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} = 25,000 - 21,000 = \text{₹}4,000$.
        6. $\text{Profit } \% = \left( \frac{4000}{21000} \right) \times 100 = \frac{400}{21} \% = 19.05\%$.
*   💡 **Concept Application 2: Marked Price and Discount Interplay**
    *   *Scenario*: An article is marked at ₹1200. A shopkeeper allows a 15% discount and still makes a 20% profit. Find the Cost Price of the article.
    *   *Step-by-Step Resolution*:
        1. Calculate $\text{SP}$ from $\text{MP}$ and $D\%$:
           $\text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left( \frac{100 - D\%}{100} \right) = 1200 \times \left( \frac{85}{100} \right) = 12 \times 85 = \text{₹}1020$.
        2. Calculate $\text{CP}$ from $\text{SP}$ and $P\%$:
           $\text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + P\%} \right) = 1020 \times \left( \frac{100}{120} \right) = \frac{102000}{120} = \text{₹}850$.
        3. Conclusion: The Cost Price is ₹850.

### [BLUE] PAGE 7: DAV Board Exam High-Yield Factoids & Golden Highlights

*   🌟 **DAV Board Examination Golden Highlights**:
    *   [RED] **Critical Warning**: Questions involving "selling two articles at the same price, one at $x\%$ profit and the other at $x\%$ loss" *always* result in an overall **LOSS**. The net loss percentage is universally given by:
        $$\text{Net Loss } \% = \left( \frac{x}{10} \right)^2 \% = \frac{x^2}{100} \%$$
    *   [GREEN] **High-Yield Tip**: In word problems where the actual numerical value of CP is not given, always assume $\text{CP} = \text{₹}100$ (or $100x$). This converts complex algebraic fractions into simple, linear arithmetic percentages.
    *   [GOLD] **Marking Scheme Insight**: Board examiners strictly look for the explicit mention of formulas before substitution. Writing $\text{SP} = \text{CP} + \text{Profit}$ or $\text{SP} = \frac{100+P}{100} \times \text{CP}$ carries dedicated step-marks.
    *   [BLUE] **Unit Consistency**: Always ensure that all monetary values (CP, SP, MP, Overhead) are in the *same currency unit* before applying operational equations.

### [BLUE] PAGE 9: Crucial Distinctions, Conceptual Comparisons & Pitfalls

*   ⚠️ **Common Pitfalls & Misconceptions**:
    *   *Pitfall 1*: Calculating Profit Percentage on Selling Price instead of Cost Price.
        *   *Correction*: Unless the problem explicitly states "profit calculated on SP", *always* divide the profit amount by the Cost Price.
    *   *Pitfall 2*: Treating Overhead Expenses as optional or ignoring them when calculating percentage returns.
        *   *Correction*: Repairs, freight, and taxes are compulsory additions to the purchase price to determine true capital investment.
    *   *Pitfall 3*: Adding successive discounts directly (e.g., treating a 20% and 10% discount as a flat 30% discount).
        *   *Correction*: Successive discounts apply to a diminishing balance. A 20% discount on ₹100 leaves ₹80; a subsequent 10% discount applies to ₹80 (yielding ₹8), resulting in a net price of ₹72 (a true total discount of 28%, *not* 30%).
    *   *Pitfall 4*: Confusing Marked Price with Cost Price.
        *   *Correction*: Marked Price is the retail tag (higher than CP); Cost Price is the acquisition cost (lower than MP in profitable retail trade).
