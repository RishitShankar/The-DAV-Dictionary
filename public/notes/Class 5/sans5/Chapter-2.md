• **Chapter Overview**: The second chapter of the Class 5 Sanskrit textbook 'सुरभि' is titled **"मम परिवारः"**, which translates to "My Family" in English.
• **Core Objective**: The primary goal of this chapter is to introduce students to the Sanskrit terminology for various family members and to teach basic sentence structures using pronouns and verbs.
• **Term: मम (Mama)**: This is a possessive pronoun in Sanskrit meaning "My." It is derived from the 'अस्मद्' (Asmad) shabda-roop in the Genitive case (षष्ठी विभक्ति).
• **Term: परिवारः (Parivāraḥ)**: This noun means "Family." It is a masculine noun (अकारान्त पुल्लिंग) ending in the 'ah' sound.
• **Concept of Introduction**: The chapter uses a pictorial method to help students identify family figures and associate them with their specific Sanskrit names.
• **Term: एषः (Eṣaḥ)**: This is a demonstrative pronoun meaning "This." It is specifically used for masculine nouns (पुल्लिंग) that are near the speaker.
• **Term: एषा (Eṣā)**: This is the feminine form of the demonstrative pronoun "This." It is used for feminine nouns (स्त्रीलिंग) in the singular number.
• **Grammatical Gender Agreement**: The chapter emphasizes that the pronoun must match the gender of the family member being described (e.g., **एषः पिता** vs **एषा माता**).
• **Term: अस्ति (Asti)**: This is a verb (क्रिया) meaning "is." It is the Lat-Lakara (Present Tense), Prathama Purusha (Third Person), Ekavachanam (Singular) form of the root 'अस्' (to be).
• **Sentence Structure**: A typical sentence in this chapter follows the pattern: [Demonstrative Pronoun] + [Possessive Pronoun] + [Noun] + [Verb]. Example: **एषः मम पिता अस्ति** (This is my father).
• **Identification of Father**: The term **पिता (Pitā)** is used for father. In formal Sanskrit grammar, the root word is 'पितृ' (Pitṛ), but in the nominative singular, it becomes 'पिता'.
• **Identification of Mother**: The term **माता (Mātā)** is used for mother. The root word is 'मातृ' (Mātṛ), which becomes 'माता' in the nominative singular.
• **Identification of Grandfather**: The term **पितामहः (Pitāmahaḥ)** refers to the paternal grandfather. It is a masculine noun.
• **Identification of Grandmother**: The term **पितामही (Pitāmahī)** refers to the paternal grandmother. It is a feminine noun ending in 'ī' (ईकारान्त स्त्रीलिंग).
• **Identification of Brother**: The term **भ्राता (Bhrātā)** is used for brother. The root word is 'भ्रातृ' (Bhrātṛ).
• **Identification of Sister**: The term **भगिनी (Bhaginī)** is used for sister. It is an 'ī-kārānta' feminine noun.
• **Term: अहम् (Aham)**: This pronoun means "I." It is used by the narrator to introduce themselves within the family context.
• **Visual Learning**: The chapter relies on the "Look and Say" method, where students look at a family tree or picture and name the relations in Sanskrit.
• **Cultural Context**: The chapter reflects the traditional Indian family structure, emphasizing respect and recognition of each member's role.

• **Masculine Pronoun Usage**: **एषः (Eṣaḥ)** is used exclusively for male family members such as the father, grandfather, and brother.
• **Feminine Pronoun Usage**: **एषा (Eṣā)** is used exclusively for female family members such as the mother, grandmother, and sister.
• **Term: पितामहः (Pitāmahaḥ)**: Detailed breakdown—'Pita' (Father) + 'Maha' (Great/Senior), signifying the father of the father.
• **Term: पितामही (Pitāmahī)**: Detailed breakdown—The feminine counterpart of Pitāmahaḥ, signifying the mother of the father.
• **Term: जनकः (Janakaḥ)**: Although the chapter primarily uses 'पिता', 'जनकः' is a common synonym introduced in DAV Sanskrit to denote the "progenitor" or father.
• **Term: जननी (Jananī)**: A synonym for 'माता' (Mother), often used in supplementary exercises to expand the student's vocabulary.
• **Term: अनुजः (Anujaḥ)**: While 'भ्राता' is general, 'अनुजः' specifically refers to a younger brother (born after).
• **Term: अग्रजः (Agrajaḥ)**: This term refers to an elder brother (born before).
• **Term: अनुजा (Anujā)**: This term refers to a younger sister.
• **Term: अग्रजा (Agrajā)**: This term refers to an elder sister.
• **The Role of 'च' (Ca)**: The word 'च' means "and." In Sanskrit, it is placed after the last word of the list it connects (e.g., माता पिता च - Mother and Father).
• **Subject-Verb Agreement**: Since all family members are referred to in the singular (Ekavachanam), the verb **अस्ति** (is) remains constant throughout the introductory sentences.
• **Term: भ्रातृ (Bhrātṛ) vs भ्राता (Bhrātā)**: Students are taught that 'भ्राता' is the form used in sentences, while 'भ्रातृ' is the base word (Prātipadika).
• **Term: मातृ (Mātṛ) vs माता (Mātā)**: Similarly, 'माता' is the functional word used to address or describe the mother in the first case (Prathamā Vibhakti).
• **Vowel Endings**: The chapter introduces students to different vowel endings: 'ah' (Masculine), 'ā' (Feminine), and 'ī' (Feminine).
• **Phonetic Practice**: Pronouncing the 'Visarga' ( : ) in words like **एषः** and **पितामहः** is a key learning point for Class 5 students.
• **Possessive Consistency**: The word **मम** (My) does not change its form based on the gender of the noun it qualifies in this introductory stage.
• **Sentence Logic**: The chapter teaches that to identify someone, we use the sequence: Pronoun + Relation.

• **Action Verbs (Kriyā-padāni)**: Beyond identification, the chapter introduces what family members do using simple present tense verbs.
• **Term: पठति (Paṭhati)**: This verb means "reads" or "is reading." It is used for members engaged in study, like a brother or sister.
• **Term: लिखति (Likhati)**: This verb means "writes" or "is writing." It often describes a father or a student.
• **Term: खादति (Khādati)**: This verb means "eats" or "is eating."
• **Term: गच्छति (Gacchati)**: This verb means "goes" or "is going." It can be used for a father going to the office.
• **Term: क्रीडति (Krīḍati)**: This verb means "plays" or "is playing." It is typically associated with the narrator (अहम्) or siblings.
• **Term: धावति (Dhāvati)**: This verb means "runs" or "is running."
• **Term: हसति (Hasati)**: This verb means "laughs" or "is laughing."
• **Term: गायति (Gāyati)**: This verb means "sings" or "is singing," often used for a mother or sister.
• **Verb Structure**: All these verbs are in the **लट् लकार** (Present Tense), **प्रथम पुरुष** (Third Person), and **एकवचन** (Singular).
• **The Suffix 'ति' (-ti)**: Students learn that 'ति' at the end of a verb root (Dhatu) indicates a singular person performing an action in the present.
• **Root: पठ् (Paṭh)**: The root for reading.
• **Root: लिख् (Likh)**: The root for writing.
• **Root: क्रीड् (Krīḍ)**: The root for playing.
• **Root: गम् (Gam)**: The root for going (which changes to 'Gacch' in the present tense).
• **Root: हस् (Has)**: The root for laughing.
• **Root: खाद् (Khād)**: The root for eating.
• **Root: गै (Gai)**: The root for singing (which becomes 'Gāy' in the present tense).
• **Sentence Expansion**: The chapter moves from "This is my father" to "My father reads" (**मम पिता पठति**).
• **Implicit Subject**: In Sanskrit, the verb often carries the weight of the subject, but in this chapter, the subject is explicitly stated for clarity.

• **Sentence Example**: **एषा मम पितामही अस्ति** (This is my grandmother).

• **Term: वदति (Vadati)**: To speak or say.

• **Sentence Example**: **एषः मम पिता अस्ति** (This is my father).

• **Term: नृत्यति (Nṛtyati)**: To dance.

• **Self-Introduction**: The narrator identifies themselves using the word **अहम्** (I).
• **Sentence Example**: **अहम् [Name] अस्मि** (I am [Name]).
• **Term: अस्मि (Asmi)**: This is the first-person singular form of 'अस्' (to be). It must always be used with 'अहम्'.
• **Distinction between अस्ति and अस्मि**: 'अस्ति' is for "He/She/It is," while 'अस्मि' is for "I am."
• **Narrator's Action**: **अहम् पठामि** (I read) or **अहम् क्रीडामि** (I play).
• **The Suffix 'आमि' (-āmi)**: Students learn that when 'अहम्' is the subject, the verb ends in 'āmi' instead of 'ti'.
• **Verb Transformation**: पठति (He reads) becomes पठामि (I read).
• **Verb Transformation**: लिखति (He writes) becomes लिखामि (I write).
• **Verb Transformation**: गच्छति (He goes) becomes गच्छामि (I go).
• **Verb Transformation**: हसति (He laughs) becomes हसामि (I laugh).
• **Verb Transformation**: खादति (He eats) becomes खादामि (I eat).
• **Grammar Rule**: The change from 'ti' to 'āmi' is the student's first introduction to **Purusha** (Person) in Sanskrit grammar.
• **Concept of 'मम' with 'अहम्'**: The narrator says, "This is my family" (**एषः मम परिवारः**) and then introduces themselves as a part of it.
• **Family Unity**: The chapter concludes that all these members together constitute the 'Parivāraḥ'.
• **Term: मिलित्वा (Militvā)**: This means "together" or "having met." It is used to describe family members living or playing together.
• **Term: वसन्ति (Vasanti)**: This verb means "they live." It is the plural (Bahuvachanam) form, used when referring to the whole family.
• **Term: स्नेहः (Snehaḥ)**: Meaning "love" or "affection." The chapter implies that a family is bound by 'Sneha'.
• **Term: सुखदः (Sukhadaḥ)**: Meaning "pleasant" or "happy." A family is often described as **सुखदः परिवारः** (A happy family).

• **Gender Classification**: Sanskrit has three genders, but this chapter focuses on **Pullinga** (Masculine) and **Strīlinga** (Feminine).
• **Masculine Nouns (पुल्लिंग)**: पितामहः, पिता, भ्राता, परिवारः, जनकः.
• **Feminine Nouns (स्त्रीलिंग)**: पितामही, माता, भगिनी, जननी, अनुजा.
• **Identifying Masculine Endings**: Most masculine words in this chapter end with the Visarga ( : ) or are 'ṛ-kārānta' (like Pitṛ).
• **Identifying Feminine Endings**: Most feminine words in this chapter end with 'ā' (आ) or 'ī' (ई).
• **Pronoun Agreement**: The chapter reinforces that **एषः** cannot be used with **माता**, and **एषा** cannot be used with **पिता**.
• **Number (Vachanam)**: The chapter primarily uses **Ekavachanam** (Singular) to keep the introduction simple for Class 5.
• **Case (Vibhakti)**: The nouns are used in **Prathamā Vibhakti** (Nominative Case), which is used for the subject of the sentence.
• **Possessive Case**: **मम** is in the **Ṣaṣṭhī Vibhakti** (Genitive Case), indicating relationship or ownership.
• **Sentence Syntax**: Sanskrit word order is flexible, but the textbook follows: [Subject] + [Verb] or [Pronoun] + [Subject] + [Verb].
• **The 'Visarga' Sound**: Students are taught that the ':' sound is like a soft 'h' (e.g., Rama-ha).
• **The 'Anusvara' Sound**: The dot above a letter (like in अहम् or कार्यम्) represents an 'm' sound.
• **Vowel Length**: Distinguishing between short 'i' and long 'ī' (e.g., in भगिनी) is crucial for correct spelling.
• **Vowel Length**: Distinguishing between short 'a' and long 'ā' (e.g., in माता) is essential.
• **Dhatu (Root)**: The concept that every verb comes from a root word is introduced (e.g., Paṭh is the root of Paṭhati).
• **Usage of 'अस्ति'**: It acts as a copula, linking the subject to its identity.
• **Negative Sentences (Implicit)**: While not the focus, the placement of 'न' (not) before the verb is a logical next step.

• **Adjectives for Family**: **मम परिवारः विशालः अस्ति** (My family is large).

• **Reading Comprehension Skills**: Students are expected to read short paragraphs about a character's family and answer who is who.
• **Example Paragraph**: "एषः मधुरः अस्ति। एषः मधुरस्य पिता अस्ति। एषा मधुरस्य माता अस्ति।"
• **Genitive Case (-sya)**: The suffix **-स्य (-sya)** is introduced to mean "of" (e.g., मधुरस्य = of Madhur).
• **Application of -sya**: **भ्रातुः** (of the brother) or **पितुः** (of the father) are more complex forms, so the textbook often sticks to name-based genitives like **रामस्य** or **बालकस्य**.
• **Identifying Relationships**: If the text says "एषः मम पितामहः," the student must understand the speaker is the grandchild.
• **Verb Identification**: Identifying the action in a sentence like "माता भोजनं पचति" (Mother cooks food).
• **Term: भोजनम् (Bhojanam)**: Food or meal.
• **Term: करोति (Karoti)**: Does. Used in **कार्यं करोति** (does work).
• **Term: शृणोति (Śṛṇoti)**: Listens. Often used with **कथाम्** (story).
• **Term: कथाम् (Kathām)**: Story. **पितामही कथाम् वदति** (Grandmother tells a story).
• **Term: चित्रम् (Citram)**: Picture. The chapter is often based on a **चित्र-वर्णनम्** (Picture description).
• **Term: पश्यति (Paśyati)**: Sees/Looks at. **बालकः चित्रं पश्यति** (The boy looks at the picture).
• **Connecting Words**: The use of **अपि (api)** meaning "also." Example: **भ्राता पठति, भगिनी अपि पठति** (Brother reads, sister also reads).
• **Sentence Flow**: Learning how to transition from introducing a person to describing their action.
• **Logical Sequence**: Introduction -> Name -> Relation -> Action.
• **Vocabulary Retention**: Repeating the names of family members through chanting or writing helps in memorization.
