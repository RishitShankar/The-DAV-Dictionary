• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to the DAV Class 7 Academic Notes for *We and Our World: Social Science*, Chapter 2: "The Earth and the Changes on It", curated specifically for advanced student mastery.
• **Dynamic Nature of Earth**: The Earth is not a static or unchanging sphere; rather, it is a dynamic, evolving planet constantly undergoing transformation across geological and historical time scales.
• **Role of Forces**: The continuous changes occurring on the Earth's surface are primarily driven by various physical forces categorized broadly into internal and external geological agents.
• **Lithospheric Plates**: The outermost shell of the Earth, known as the lithosphere, is broken into several large and small tectonic plates that float independently on the semi-fluid asthenosphere.
• **Internal vs. External Forces**: Geological forces are bifurcated into **Endogenic Forces** (originating from within the interior of the Earth) and **Exogenic Forces** (acting upon the surface of the Earth).
• **Endogenic Forces Definition**: Endogenic forces are sudden or slow movements originating deep inside the Earth that give rise to major structural features like mountains, plateaus, rifts, and faults.
• **Exogenic Forces Definition**: Exogenic forces are external agents of change—such as running water, moving ice, wind, and waves—that continuously wear down and reshape the landforms created by endogenic forces.
• **Catastrophic Endogenic Events**: Certain endogenic forces operate suddenly, causing massive, catastrophic changes over short periods, prominent examples being earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.
• **Slow Endogenic Events**: Other endogenic forces operate extremely slowly over millions of years, leading to mountain building (orogeny) and continental drift.
• **Volcanoes Definition**: A **Volcano** is a vent, rupture, or opening in the Earth's crust through which molten rock, hot ash, and gases escape from the magma chamber below.
• **Magma vs. Lava**: **Magma** is molten rock located beneath the surface of the Earth, whereas **Lava** is the exact same molten rock once it has successfully erupted onto the Earth's surface.
• **Crater and Vent**: The vertical pipe through which magma travels is called the **vent**, and the funnel-shaped depression at the top of the volcanic cone is termed the **crater**.
• **Active Volcanoes**: Active volcanoes are those that have erupted recently and are expected to erupt again in the near future, such as Mount Etna or Stromboli.
• **Dormant Volcanoes**: Dormant volcanoes are those that have not erupted for a very long time but show signs of potential future activity, often referred to metaphorically as sleeping volcanoes.
• **Extinct Volcanoes**: Extinct volcanoes have completely ceased all volcanic activity and have no remaining supply of magma, rendering future eruptions scientifically impossible.

• **Volcanic Landforms**: Volcanic activity creates unique landforms on the Earth's surface, including volcanic mountains, plateaus (like the Deccan Plateau), and volcanic cones built from accumulated ash and lava.
• **Earthquakes Definition**: An **Earthquake** is a sudden, violent shaking or trembling of the ground caused by the sudden release of energy in the Earth's crust along fault lines.
• **Tectonic Plate Movement**: The relentless collision, divergence, and lateral sliding of tectonic plates build up immense stress within the rock strata of the lithosphere.
• **Faulting**: When accumulated stress exceeds the internal strength of the rocks, they rupture and fracture along zones of weakness known as **faults**.
• **Focus (Hypocenter)**: The **Focus** (or hypocenter) is the exact subterranean point deep inside the Earth where an earthquake rupture and initial energy release actually originate.
• **Epicenter**: The **Epicenter** is the geographical point on the Earth's surface directly situated right above the focus, where seismic tremors are first felt and are usually most destructive.
• **Seismic Waves**: The energy released at the focus travels outward in all directions through the Earth's interior and across its surface as shockwaves called **seismic waves**.
• **Types of Seismic Waves**: Seismic waves are categorized primarily into three types: Primary waves (P-waves), Secondary waves (S-waves), and Surface waves (L-waves), each with different propagation characteristics.
• **Seismograph**: A **Seismograph** (or seismometer) is a sensitive scientific instrument used to detect, record, and measure the intensity, duration, and pattern of seismic waves.
• **Richter Scale**: The **Richter Scale** is a logarithmic numerical scale ranging from 0 to 10 used to quantify the total magnitude and energy released by an earthquake.
• **Mercalli Scale**: The **Mercalli Scale** measures earthquake intensity based on the actual observed destructive effects and impacts felt by human populations and structures.
• **Tsunami Phenomenon**: When a powerful earthquake occurs beneath the ocean floor, it can displace massive volumes of water, generating gigantic, destructive ocean waves called **tsunamis**.
• **Preparedness and Safety**: Earthquake preparedness involves constructing earthquake-resistant buildings, securing heavy furniture, conducting safety drills, and knowing safe "drop, cover, and hold on" protocols.
• **Weathering Definition**: **Weathering** is the slow, continuous breaking down, disintegration, and chemical decomposition of rocks in situ on the Earth's surface by atmospheric agents.
• **Mechanical (Physical) Weathering**: **Mechanical Weathering** involves the physical fragmentation of rocks into smaller pieces without altering their chemical composition, often caused by thermal expansion, frost wedging, or root pressure.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Chemical Weathering**: **Chemical Weathering** alters the internal mineral composition of rocks through chemical reactions like oxidation, carbonation, hydration, and solution in the presence of water and atmospheric gases.
• **Biological Weathering**: **Biological Weathering** is the breakdown of rocks caused by the vital activities of living organisms, including plant roots wedging into cracks and burrowing animals turning over soil.
• **Erosion Definition**: **Erosion** is the dynamic process of wearing away and transporting weathered rock particles, soil, and minerals from one location to another by active natural agents.
• **Agents of Erosion**: The principal geomorphic agents responsible for driving the erosion cycle across the landscape are running water (rivers), moving ice (glaciers), wind, and sea waves.
• **Deposition Definition**: **Deposition** is the geological process where eroded materials, sediments, and debris are finally dropped or laid down in new locations when the transporting agent loses its kinetic energy.
• **Interplay of Processes**: The continuous trio of weathering, erosion, and deposition acts together to constantly sculpt, level, and reshape the surface of the Earth.
• **Work of a River**: A river is one of the most powerful and active exogenic agents of erosion, transportation, and deposition, shaping landscapes from its mountainous source to its oceanic mouth.
• **River Courses**: A river's journey is typically divided into three distinct courses: the upper course (youth), the middle course (mature), and the lower course (old age).
• **Upper Course Features**: In its steep upper course, the river possesses immense erosive energy, carving deep, narrow **V-shaped valleys**, steep gorges, and dramatic waterfalls.
• **Waterfalls**: A **Waterfall** forms when a river flows over an alternate band of hard and soft rock layers, eroding the softer rock faster and creating a sheer vertical drop.
• **Middle Course Features**: In its gently sloping middle course, the river slows down, widens its valley, and begins to meander across the landscape while transporting large loads of sediment.
• **Meanders**: **Meanders** are large, sweeping bends, loops, or curves formed as a river flows across a relatively flat valley plain due to lateral (sideways) erosion and deposition.
• **Oxbow Lakes**: An **Oxbow Lake** is formed when a sharp river meander is eventually cut off from the main channel during a flood, creating a crescent-shaped stagnant lake.
• **Lower Course Features**: In its flat lower course near the sea, the river slows down significantly, resulting in extensive deposition and the formation of low-gradient floodplains.
• **Floodplains**: A **Floodplain** is a flat, fertile tract of land flanking a river, formed by the continuous deposition of rich alluvial silt during periodic seasonal floods.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Levees**: **Levees** are natural elevated ridges or embankments of sediment deposited along the immediate banks of a river channel during repeated flooding events.
• **Distributaries**: As a river approaches its mouth and its bed becomes choked with sediments, it breaks up into a network of smaller branching channels known as **distributaries**.
• **Delta Formation**: A **Delta** is a triangular-shaped deposit of silt, sand, and clay formed at the mouth of a river where it empties into a standing body of water like a sea or ocean.
• **Famous Deltas**: Classic examples of major global deltas include the vast Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta (the Sundarbans) and the Nile River Delta.
• **Glaciers Definition**: A **Glacier** is a massive, slow-moving river of compacted ice and snow that forms in high mountain regions or polar latitudes under freezing conditions.
• **Types of Glaciers**: Glaciers are primarily classified into **Continental Glaciers** (ice sheets covering vast polar landmasses like Antarctica and Greenland) and **Valley Glaciers** (alpine glaciers flowing down mountain valleys).
• **Glacial Erosion**: Glaciers act as massive, slow-moving abrasive bulldozers, eroding underlying bedrock through plucking (tearing away rocks) and abrasion (scraping with rock fragments).
• **U-Shaped Valleys**: Unlike rivers that carve V-shaped valleys, slow-moving glaciers carve broad, steep-sided, flat-bottomed valleys known as **U-shaped glacial valleys** (or glacial troughs).
• **Cirques (Corries)**: A **Cirque** is a deep, arm-chair shaped, steep-walled hollow carved out by the head of a glacier high up on a mountain slope.
• **Moraines**: **Moraines** are heaps, ridges, or accumulations of unsorted glacial debris, rocks, and boulders deposited by melting glaciers at their sides (lateral), middle (medial), or terminus (terminal).
• **Hanging Valleys**: A **Hanging Valley** occurs when a smaller tributary glacier joins a main valley glacier, and upon melting, its valley is left suspended high above the main valley floor, often creating waterfalls.
• **Eskers and Drumlins**: Other notable glacial depositional landforms include winding ridges of sand called **eskers** and elongated, teardrop-shaped hills of till called **drumlins**.
• **Wind Erosion Work**: Wind is an exceptionally active agent of erosion and deposition primarily operating in arid, dry environments such as deserts where vegetation cover is sparse or absent.
• **Deflation**: **Deflation** is the wind erosion process where loose, dry dust, sand, and fine soil particles are lifted up and blown away by turbulent wind currents.
• **Abrasion by Wind**: Wind-driven sand grains act like natural sandpaper, abrading, polishing, and carving exposed rock surfaces near the ground in a process called **wind abrasion**.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Mushroom Rocks**: A **Mushroom Rock** is a distinctive rock formation shaped like a mushroom, created because wind-blown sand erodes the softer lower section of a rock faster than its harder top.
• **Sand Dunes**: **Sand Dunes** are massive mounds, hills, or ridges of wind-blown sand deposited in deserts when wind encounters an obstacle that reduces its velocity.
• **Barchans**: **Barchans** are crescent-shaped sand dunes formed in deserts with steady, unidirectional winds, having horns pointing downwind.
• **Loess**: **Loess** refers to extensive, fertile blankets of fine-grained, yellowish silt and dust that have been transported and deposited by winds far away from desert source regions.
• **Loess Deposits Example**: The famous Loess Plateau in northern China contains massive wind-blown dust deposits derived from the Gobi Desert over thousands of years.
• **Work of Sea Waves**: Coastal regions are dynamic zones continuously shaped by the powerful, relentless erosive and depositional action of sea waves, tides, and coastal currents.
• **Coastal Erosion Mechanics**: Sea waves pound against rocky coastlines with immense hydrostatic pressure, compressing air into rock cracks and fracturing them through hydraulic action and abrasion.
• **Sea Cliffs**: A **Sea Cliff** is a steep, vertical rock face formed along a shoreline where continuous wave action undercuts the coastal landmass, causing upper layers to collapse.
• **Sea Caves**: **Sea Caves** are hollow tunnels or caverns excavated by relentless wave action into zones of weakness or softer rock along the base of a sea cliff.
• **Sea Arches**: When waves eventually break completely through a headland from both sides of a sea cave, they create a natural bridge-like structure known as a **sea arch**.
• **Sea Stacks**: When the roof of a sea arch eventually collapses due to continued weathering and wave erosion, the isolated vertical pillar of rock left standing in the sea is called a **sea stack**.
• **Stumps**: Further wave erosion wearing down a sea stack until it is submerged or barely visible at high tide produces a coastal feature called a **stump**.
• **Beaches**: A **Beach** is a broad, gently sloping deposit of sand, shingle, pebbles, or shells laid down by constructive waves along the shoreline of an ocean, sea, or lake.
• **Spits and Bars**: A **Spit** is a ridge of sand connected to the land at one end and terminating in open water, while a **Bar** is a ridge of sediment that completely or partially blocks the entrance of a bay.
• **Lagoons**: A **Lagoon** is a shallow body of brackish or saltwater separated from the open sea by a barrier beach, sandbar, or coral reef.

• **Summary of Exogenic Agents**: Exogenic agents—running water, glaciers, wind, and sea waves—work in harmony as a continuous geological conveyor belt across all terrestrial biomes.
• **The Rock Cycle Connection**: The dynamic changes on Earth directly link to the **Rock Cycle**, where rocks are continuously transformed between igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic states through tectonic and surface processes.
• **Soil Formation (Pedogenesis)**: Soil is formed as a direct result of the weathering of parent rocks combined with the decomposition of organic matter by living organisms over long periods.
• **Soil Profile Horizons**: A mature soil profile consists of distinct layers known as horizons: O-horizon (organic), A-horizon (topsoil), B-horizon (subsoil), C-horizon (weathered parent rock), and R-bedrock.
• **Importance of Soil Conservation**: Preventing soil erosion caused by wind and water is critical for maintaining agricultural productivity, global food security, and terrestrial ecosystem stability.
• **Deforestation Impacts**: Human activities like large-scale deforestation remove natural root binding, accelerating soil erosion rates and altering local hydrological cycles.
• **Afforestation and Reforestation**: Planting trees (afforestation) and restoring depleted forests (reforestation) serve as vital bio-engineering strategies to stabilize soil and mitigate exogenic erosion.
• **Terrace Farming**: Practiced in mountainous terrain, **Terrace Farming** involves cutting step-like flat fields into steep slopes to slow down water runoff and prevent catastrophic soil loss.
• **Contour Ploughing**: Plowing fields along natural elevation contours rather than up and down slopes creates water-trapping ridges that significantly reduce soil erosion by rainwater runoff.
• **Shelterbelts (Windbreaks)**: Planting rows of dense trees and shrubs along the edges of agricultural fields forms **shelterbelts** that drastically reduce wind velocity and prevent wind erosion in arid zones.
• **Intercropping**: Growing two or more different crop species simultaneously in alternating strips or rows helps maintain soil cover and prevents nutrient depletion.
• **Check Dams**: Constructing small barriers across seasonal streams and gullies (check dams) reduces water velocity, traps moving sediment, and aids in groundwater recharge.
• **Human Impact on Geomorphology**: Human engineering projects—such as constructing large dams, building coastal breakwaters, mining, and urban paving—significantly alter natural exogenic and endogenic balances.
• **Case Study: River Damming**: Constructing dams on rivers traps downstream sediment delivery, altering delta ecosystems and causing coastal erosion downstream.
• **Sustainable Land Management**: Integrating geological understanding with sustainable ecological practices is essential for balancing human developmental needs with Earth's natural systems.

• **Detailed Vocabulary Review**: Mastering technical geological vocabulary is crucial for academic success in DAV Social Science geography assessments.
• **Endogenic Review**: Terms like *Endogenic* derive from Greek roots (*endo* meaning inside, *genesis* meaning origin), describing forces originating from the planetary interior.
• **Exogenic Review**: Terms like *Exogenic* derive from *exo* meaning outside, denoting forces acting upon the external geomorphological boundary of the Earth.
• **Seismology Terminology**: *Seismology* is the dedicated scientific study of earthquakes and the propagation of elastic seismic waves through the interior of the Earth.
• **Magnitude vs. Intensity**: Always distinguish clearly between **Magnitude** (total energy released at the earthquake source, measured via Richter scale) and **Intensity** (local structural damage observed, measured via Mercalli scale).
• **Fluvial Geomorphology**: The scientific study of running water and the landforms it creates is known in physical geography as **Fluvial Geomorphology**.
• **Glacial Geomorphology**: The study of glacial dynamics, ice movement, and glacial erosion/deposition landforms is termed **Glacial Geomorphology**.
• **Aeolian Geomorphology**: The study of wind action as a geomorphic agent and the resulting desert landforms (dunes, loess) is called **Aeolian Geomorphology**.
• **Coastal Geomorphology**: The study of shoreline processes, wave mechanics, cliffs, beaches, and marine deposition is classified as **Coastal Geomorphology**.
• **Weathering vs. Erosion Distinction**: Remember that *weathering* is static (breaking rocks in place), whereas *erosion* is dynamic (transporting weathered material away).
• **Alluvium Definition**: **Alluvium** is rich, fertile soil, silt, sand, and gravel deposited by flowing river water across floodplains, riverbeds, and deltas.
• **Till Definition**: **Till** is unstratified, unsorted glacial sediment deposited directly by melting ice sheets and valley glaciers without sorting by water.
• **Deflation Hollows**: Wind erosion in dry deserts can scoop out large, shallow depressions known as **deflation hollows** or blowouts.
• **Oasis Formation**: When wind erosion in a desert deepens a deflation hollow down to the regional water table, it creates a fertile water-bearing **oasis**.
• **Geological Time Scale**: Earth's surface changes must be understood within the vast framework of the geological time scale, where millions of years feel like seconds.

• **Concept Map: Forces on Earth**: 
  - Earth Forces -> Endogenic (Internal) -> Sudden (Earthquakes, Volcanoes) & Slow (Mountain building, Folding, Faulting).
  - Earth Forces -> Exogenic (External) -> Weathering, Erosion, Deposition (Rivers, Glaciers, Wind, Waves).
• **Concept Map: River Landforms**:
  - Upper Course -> V-shaped valleys, Gorges, Waterfalls.
  - Middle Course -> Meanders, Oxbow lakes, Floodplains.
  - Lower Course -> Distributaries, Deltas, Levees.
• **Concept Map: Glacial Landforms**:
  - Erosional -> U-shaped valleys, Cirques, Hanging valleys.
  - Depositional -> Moraines, Eskers, Drumlins.
• **Concept Map: Wind (Aeolian) Landforms**:
  - Erosional -> Mushroom rocks, Deflation hollows, Yardangs.
  - Depositional -> Sand dunes, Barchans, Loess deposits.
• **Concept Map: Coastal Landforms**:
  - Erosional -> Sea cliffs, Sea caves, Sea arches, Sea stacks.
  - Depositional -> Beaches, Spits, Bars, Lagoons.
• **Key Scientific Instruments**: 
  - Seismograph (records earthquake waves).
  - Anemometer (measures wind speed, related to aeolian studies).
  - Clinometer (measures slopes, relevant to landform analysis).
• **Significance of Exogenic Cycles**: Exogenic cycles prevent the Earth's surface from becoming completely rugged by wearing down high mountain ranges and filling low basins with sediment.
• **Interconnectedness of Spheres**: Changes on Earth demonstrate the constant interaction between the **Lithosphere** (land), **Hydrosphere** (water), **Atmosphere** (air), and **Biosphere** (life).
• **Role of Vegetation**: Plant roots stabilize soil, reduce runoff velocity, and provide critical protection against wind and water erosion across all biomes.
• **Geological Hazards Mitigation**: Understanding endogenic and exogenic forces allows human societies to implement effective disaster management, zoning laws, and resilient civil engineering.
• **Tectonic Plate Boundaries**: Most major earthquakes and active volcanoes occur along tectonic plate boundaries, specifically convergent, divergent, and transform fault boundaries.
• **Ring of Fire**: The **Pacific Ring of Fire** is a major horseshoe-shaped area in the basin of the Pacific Ocean characterized by an exceptionally high concentration of active volcanoes and major earthquakes.
• **Himalayan Mountain Building**: The majestic Himalayan mountain range continues to rise slowly as a result of the ongoing continental collision between the Indian Tectonic Plate and the Eurasian Tectonic Plate.
• **Role of Water in Weathering**: Water acts as a universal solvent and physical wedge, playing a central role in both chemical and mechanical weathering processes.
