• **Weight: The Fundamental Concept**: Weight is a measure of how heavy or light an object is.
• **Heavier vs. Lighter**: Comparing two objects involves determining which one has more mass (heavier) and which has less mass (lighter).
• **Standard Units of Measurement**: In mathematics, we use standard units to measure weight accurately so that results are consistent everywhere.
• **The Gram (g)**: The gram is the standard unit used for measuring the weight of light objects.
• **The Kilogram (kg)**: The kilogram is the standard unit used for measuring the weight of heavy objects.
• **Relationship between Units**: 1 kilogram is equal to 1000 grams (1 kg = 1000 g).
• **Measuring Instruments**: A weighing balance or a weighing scale is the primary tool used to determine the weight of an object.
• **Common Weighing Scales**: Different types of scales include the beam balance, digital weighing machine, and spring balance.
• **The Beam Balance**: This instrument works by comparing the weight of an object on one pan against standard weights on the other pan.
• **Equilibrium**: When both sides of a beam balance are at the same level, the weight of the object is equal to the sum of the standard weights.
• **Standard Weights**: These are metal blocks of fixed mass (e.g., 50g, 100g, 200g, 500g, 1kg, 2kg, 5kg) used to measure items.
• **Estimation**: Before measuring, we often estimate weight by holding objects in our hands to guess which is heavier.
• **Direct Comparison**: Placing two objects on a balance allows for a direct visual comparison of their weights.
• **Non-standard Units**: Historically, people used stones or bags of grain to measure weight, but these are not accurate for modern mathematics.
• **Importance of Accuracy**: Using standard units prevents errors in trade, cooking, and scientific measurements.

• **Addition of Weights**: When combining two or more objects, we add their weights together using standard addition rules.
• **Addition Rule**: Always add grams to grams and kilograms to kilograms separately.
• **Carrying Over**: If the sum of grams reaches 1000 or more, it must be converted into kilograms (since 1000g = 1kg).
• **Subtraction of Weights**: To find the difference in weight between two objects, we subtract the smaller weight from the larger weight.
• **Borrowing in Subtraction**: If the grams in the top number are smaller than the grams in the bottom number, we borrow 1 kg and convert it to 1000g.
• **Word Problems**: Real-life scenarios often require us to calculate the total weight of a shopping basket.
• **Total Weight Calculation**: If a bag of apples weighs 2 kg and a bag of oranges weighs 3 kg, the total is 2 + 3 = 5 kg.
• **Difference Calculation**: If a packet of rice is 5 kg and a packet of sugar is 2 kg, the difference is 5 - 2 = 3 kg.

• **Gross Weight**: The total weight of an object including its container or packaging.
• **Net Weight**: The weight of the actual product inside the packaging, excluding the container.
• **Tare Weight**: The weight of the empty container or packaging material.
• **Formula for Net Weight**: Net Weight = Gross Weight - Tare Weight.
• **Packaging Importance**: Packaging protects goods but adds to the total weight, which is why net weight is important for consumers.

• **Mass vs. Weight**: While often used interchangeably in primary school, mass is the amount of matter, and weight is the force of gravity on that matter.
• **Consistency of Mass**: An object's mass remains the same regardless of location, but its weight can change if gravity changes.
