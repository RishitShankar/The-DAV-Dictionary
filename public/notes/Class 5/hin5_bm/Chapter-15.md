• **Chapter Introduction:** Welcome to the DAV Dictionary academic study notes for Class 5 Hindi (भाषा माधुरी), Chapter 15 titled **"अँधेर नगरी"** (Andher Nagri).
• **Literary Genre:** This chapter is a famous **nukkad natak / prahas** (street play / farce) written by the legendary Hindi pioneer **Bharatendu Harishchandra**.
• **Author Significance:** **Bharatendu Harishchandra** is revered as the father of modern Hindi literature and modern Hindi theatre, known for his social satires.
• **Core Theme:** The central theme of **"अँधेर नगरी"** is a sharp political and social satire exposing corrupt governance, foolish administration, and arbitrary justice.
• **Proverbial Origin:** The title stems from the famous Hindi proverb *"अँधेर नगरी, चौपट राजा, टके शेर भाजी, टके शेर खाजा"* meaning a chaotic city with an idiotic king.
• **Setting the Scene:** The play opens on the outskirts of a strange city where **Mahant** (a wise ascetic/sage) and his two disciples arrive in search of alms.
• **Character Introduction - Mahant Ji:** **Mahant (महंत)** is a wise, experienced, and deeply observant spiritual leader who senses danger in the city immediately.
• **Character Introduction - Govardhandas:** **Govardhandas (गोवर्द्धनदास)** is one of the disciples of the Mahant, who is extremely greedy, foodie, and lacks foresight.
• **Character Introduction - Narayanadas:** **Narayanadas (नारायणदास)** is the second disciple, who is sensible and decides to heed his guru's warning to leave the city.
• **The Strange Market:** Govardhandas visits the city's market and is astonished to find that every single item—whether vegetables or sweets—costs the exact same price: **one taka (टके शेर)**.
• **Uniform Pricing Anomaly:** In Andher Nagri, premium expensive sweets like *khaja* and ordinary vegetables like spinach (*bhaji*) are sold at the same throwaway price of one taka.
• **Guru's Warning:** **Mahant Ji** immediately recognizes the grave danger of living in a kingdom where there is no distinction between good and bad, valuable and worthless.
• **Wisdom of the Guru:** The Mahant warns his disciples that staying in such a chaotic kingdom ruled by a foolish king can lead to sudden ruin and death.
• **Rejection of Warning:** While Narayanadas listens to the guru and departs, **Govardhandas** ignores the warning out of sheer greed for cheap food.
• **Govardhandas's Stay:** Govardhandas decides to stay back in Andher Nagri alone to enjoy eating his fill of sweets and delicacies at negligible cost.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Shift in Scene:** The narrative shifts from the bustling market to the royal court of the eccentric ruler of Andher Nagri, the **Chaupat Raja**.
• **Character Introduction - Chaupat Raja:** **Chaupat Raja (चौपट राजा)** represents an utterly foolish, ignorant, arrogant, and whimsical monarch who lacks basic common sense.
• **Character Introduction - Minister (मंत्री):** The king's minister is equally incompetent, sycophantic, and terrified of the king's arbitrary moods and erratic decisions.
• **The Court Proceedings:** The royal court begins with bizarre complaints being brought before the king, showcasing the total breakdown of logical law and order.
• **Comical Injustice:** The cases brought to the king highlight the absurdities of the judicial system where real criminals go free and innocents are punished.
• **First Case - The Wall Collapse:** A citizen comes running to the court complaining that his wall collapsed and crushed his innocent goat to death.
• **Accusation Chain Reaction:** What follows is a hilarious and symbolic chain of accusations moving from the wall builder to the lime-maker, mason, and dancing girl.
• **The Dancing Girl's Defense:** The dancing girl blames the goldsmith for making her anklets jingle too loudly, which distracted her while she walked during construction.
• **The Goldsmith's Defense:** The goldsmith blames the royal merchant for delaying his gold work, forcing him to rush his craft.
• **The Merchant's Blame:** The merchant points out that the fault lies with the careless executioner who made the railing too loose.
• **Blaming the Innocent:** Through this comical chain of events, the king fails to find the root cause and instead focuses on punishing the person who is most physically available.
• **Absurd Logic of the King:** The king uses deeply flawed logic, declaring that whoever is found guilty by physical size or availability must be executed immediately.
• **The Execution Order:** Because the wall was weak, the bricklayer was thin, but the stake didn't fit him, the king orders the guards to find the fattest person in the city.
• **Targeting Govardhandas:** The royal guards scour the city streets looking for someone who is extremely fat and well-nourished to fit the oversized execution stake.
• **Consequence of Greed:** Govardhandas, who gorged himself on cheap sweets for days, has grown exceptionally fat, making him the prime target for the guards.
• **Sudden Arrest:** The guards arrest Govardhandas without any crime of his own, purely because he is the fattest man available to be hanged.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **The Arrest Scene:** Govardhandas is dragged to the execution ground by the royal guards, wailing and protesting his innocence loudly.
• **Helplessness of Govardhandas:** Govardhandas repeatedly tells the guards that he is an innocent sannyasi who has committed no crime, but the guards ignore his pleas.
• **Blind Obedience of Guards:** The guards state that they are mere servants of the Chaupat Raja and must follow royal orders blindly, regardless of justice.
• **Realization of the Guru's Words:** Tied to the execution stake, Govardhandas bitterly remembers his guru's prophetic warning about the perils of Andher Nagri.
• **Regret:** Govardhandas experiences intense remorse for ignoring his guru's wise counsel in favor of his gluttony and cheap food.
• **The Guru's Arrival:** Just as Govardhandas is about to be executed, **Mahant Ji** miraculously appears at the scene to save his foolish disciple.
• **Secret Plan (The Trick):** Seeing his disciple in peril, the wise Mahant whispers a clever secret plan into Govardhandas's ear to manipulate the foolish king.
• **The Dispute for Execution:** The Mahant and Govardhandas suddenly begin a loud, fake argument in front of the executioners over who will get hanged first.
• **Astonished Executioners:** The executioners and guards are completely baffled to see a guru and his disciple fighting to be executed on the fatal stake.
• **Intervention of the King:** Hearing the commotion, the **Chaupat Raja** and his minister rush to the execution site to investigate this strange dispute.
• **Curiosity of Chaupat Raja:** The foolish king asks the Mahant why he is so eager to die on the execution stake instead of his disciple.
• **The Auspicious Moment:** The clever Mahant replies with a fabricated, highly auspicious astrological claim to trap the king in his own ignorance.
• **The Secret Claim:** The Mahant whispers to the king that anyone who is hanged on this specific stake at this particular moment will directly attain rebirth as a universal emperor (Chakravarti Samrat).
• **Value of the Stake:** The Mahant stresses that this particular execution stake is extraordinarily holy, divine, and auspicious, unlike any ordinary stake in the world.
• **Greed of the King:** Hearing that he can become a world emperor, the foolish Chaupat Raja falls directly into the trap set by the wise sage.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Climax of the Play:** Blinded by greed for power and glory, the Chaupat Raja orders his guards to release Govardhandas immediately.
• **The King's Command:** The king proudly proclaims that as the supreme ruler of the land, he has the absolute first right to attain martyrdom on this holy stake.
• **Minister's Warnings Ignored:** The foolish minister tries weakly to stop the king, but the king brushes aside all caution in his blind pursuit of emperorship.
• **Execution of the King:** The royal guards, bound by duty to obey the king's direct command, reluctantly execute the Chaupat Raja on the very stake meant for Govardhandas.
• **End of Tyranny:** With the execution of the Chaupat Raja, the tyrannical, chaotic, and foolish rule in Andher Nagri comes to an abrupt and dramatic end.
• **Rejoicing Citizens:** The citizens of Andher Nagri celebrate the death of the foolish king, finally relieved of his absurd laws and erratic governance.
• **Crowning the Wise:** The people of the city unanimously beg the wise **Mahant Ji** to take over the throne and become their new righteous ruler.
• **Mahant's Reluctance:** Being a detached ascetic, the Mahant initially refuses worldly power, but eventually agrees to guide the kingdom toward peace and justice.
• **Restoration of Order:** Under the guidance of Mahant Ji, righteousness (*dharma*), logical governance, fair pricing, and true justice are restored to the kingdom.
• **Moral of the Play:** The ultimate moral of **"अँधेर नगरी"** is that foolish leadership, blind justice, and unchecked greed ultimately lead to total self-destruction.
• **Satirical Target:** Bharatendu Harishchandra used this play to severely criticize the British colonial administration and native princely states of 19th-century India.
• **Literary Excellence:** The play remains a masterpiece of Hindi literature due to its witty dialogues, fast-paced action, and deep socio-political relevance.
• **Thematic Relevance:** Even today, the phrase *"Andher Nagri"* is used in everyday Hindi conversation to describe any office, institution, or government lacking rules and order.
• **Character Dynamics:** The sharp contrast between the wise guru, the greedy disciple, and the foolish king drives the entire dramatic tension of the plot.
• **Concluding Note:** Thus, Bharatendu Harishchandra's classic play successfully blends entertainment with profound moral and political instruction for readers of all ages.

• **Detailed Vocabulary - अँधेर नगरी (Andher Nagri):** A metaphorical term used to describe a lawless place where utter chaos, confusion, and injustice prevail.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - चौपट राजा (Chaupat Raja):** An expression denoting a foolish, incompetent, and destructive king who ruins his own state.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - टके शेर (Take Ser):** An ancient unit of currency and weight denoting extremely cheap, uniform pricing without regard to quality or value.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - महंत (Mahant):** A Hindu ascetic, priest, or spiritual head of a monastery who possesses deep wisdom and detachment from material greed.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - चेला (Chela):** A disciple, follower, or student who learns under the direct guidance of a spiritual guru.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - भिक्षा (Bhiksha):** Alms or charity collected by ascetics and sages for their basic sustenance.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - खाजा (Khaja):** A traditional, sweet, layered pastry delicacy sold in the market of Andher Nagri.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - भाजी (Bhaji):** Leafy green vegetables sold alongside expensive sweets at the same uniform price in the satirical market.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - फरियाद (Fariyaad):** A formal complaint, plea for justice, or cry for help brought before a ruler or authority.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - कोतवाल (Kotwal):** A police chief, magistrate, or high-ranking law enforcement officer in traditional Indian governance.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - कारीगर (Karigar):** A skilled artisan, craftsman, builder, or worker such as a mason, bricklayer, or goldsmith.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - फाँसी (Faansi):** Capital punishment by hanging, administered via a wooden execution stake (*फाँसी का फंदा / सूली*).
• **Detailed Vocabulary - मूर्खता (Moorkhata):** Extreme foolishness, ignorance, or lack of basic common sense demonstrated by the king and Govardhandas.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - लालच (Lalach):** Greed, avarice, or excessive desire for food and material wealth, exemplified by Govardhandas.
• **Detailed Vocabulary - न्याय (Nyay):** Justice, fairness, and truth, which are mocked and distorted throughout the court proceedings of Andher Nagri.

• **Important Literary Device - Satire (व्यंग्य):** Bharatendu employs biting satire to expose the flaws of contemporary governance, bureaucracy, and societal apathy.
• **Important Literary Device - Dramatic Irony:** The audience knows the lethal danger of Andher Nagri long before Govardhandas realizes it, creating suspense and humor.
• **Important Literary Device - Folk Idiom:** The integration of traditional folk sayings and proverbs makes the play relatable and deeply rooted in Indian culture.
• **Character Study - Mahant Ji:** Represents intellect, foresight, spiritual detachment, and the ability to outsmart corrupt systems using psychological tactics.
• **Character Study - Govardhandas:** Represents human weakness, short-sightedness, uncontrolled appetite, and the dangerous consequences of ignoring elders' advice.
• **Character Study - Chaupat Raja:** Embodies absolute power combined with zero intellect, highlighting the dangers of hereditary autocracy without accountability.
• **Socio-Political Context:** Written in 1881, the play reflects the disillusionment of Indian intellectuals with the bureaucratic indifference of the British Raj.
• **Theatrical Form:** Written as a *Natak* (play), it is designed for performance, making use of stage directions (*rangmanch nirdesh*) to guide actors' movements.
• **Dialogue Quality:** The dialogues are crisp, rhythmic, witty, and filled with humorous wordplay that keeps young readers engaged while delivering a serious message.
• **Pacing of the Plot:** The play moves swiftly through six distinct scenes, transitioning from the open road to the market, the royal court, and the execution ground.
• **Symbolism of the Market:** The uniform pricing (*Take Ser*) symbolizes a society where moral values are inverted, and merit has no special recognition.
• **Symbolism of the Wall:** The collapsing wall and the resulting chain of blame symbolize how systemic failures in governance trace back to flawed leadership at the top.
• **Symbolism of the Execution Stake:** The fatal stake represents the ultimate instrument of arbitrary state power, which ultimately destroys the tyrant himself.
• **Pedagogical Value:** Included in the DAV curriculum to impart lessons on critical thinking, financial prudence, respect for elders, and awareness of civic justice.
• **Linguistic Style:** Written in a colloquial, accessible blend of Khari Boli Hindi and regional idioms, making it easy for Class 5 students to comprehend and perform.
