• **Chapter Title:** ताँगे वाले की मूँछें (Tange Wale Ki Moonchein).
• **Core Concept:** This chapter introduces the **Chandrabindu (ँ)** matra, also known as **Anunāsik**.
• **Linguistic Definition:** The Chandrabindu is a diacritic mark placed above a letter to indicate nasalization of the vowel.
• **Phonetic Mechanism:** When pronouncing words with Chandrabindu, the sound escapes through both the mouth and the nose.
• **Visual Representation:** It is represented by a crescent moon shape with a dot inside (ँ).
• **Contextual Setting:** The chapter uses the story of a 'Tange Wala' (carriage driver) to teach vocabulary containing the Chandrabindu.
• **Primary Character:** The 'Tange Wala' is the central figure, whose physical appearance serves as the anchor for the lesson.
• **Key Feature:** The 'Moochhein' (mustache) of the Tange Wala is the focal point for introducing the nasal sound.
• **Vocabulary Focus:** Words like 'Moochhein' (मूँछें) demonstrate the placement of the Chandrabindu on the long vowel 'oo' (ऊ).
• **Educational Objective:** To enable Class 1 students to distinguish between Anusvar (dot) and Anunasik (Chandrabindu).
• **Visual Learning:** The chapter uses illustrations to help students associate the nasal sound with the physical object.
• **Grammatical Rule:** Chandrabindu is typically used on vowels that do not have a vertical line extending above the headline (Shirorekha).
• **Exceptions:** In some cases, when a vowel sign (matra) extends above the line, a simple dot is used instead of the full Chandrabindu for typographical convenience.
• **Word Structure:** The word 'Moochhein' consists of 'Ma' + 'oo' + 'Chandrabindu' + 'Chha' + 'e' + 'dot'.
• **Phonetic Practice:** Students are encouraged to practice the vibration in the nose while saying 'Moochhein'.

• **Phonetic Analysis:** The nasal sound in 'Chandrabindu' is softer and more resonant than the 'Anusvar' (dot).
• **Comparison:** 'Anusvar' (e.g., 'Rang') produces a distinct 'n' or 'm' sound, whereas 'Chandrabindu' (e.g., 'Paanv') modifies the vowel itself.
• **Articulation:** To produce the sound correctly, the tongue position remains neutral while the soft palate lowers to allow air into the nasal cavity.
• **Syllabic Breakdown:** Words like 'Ga-aon' show how the Chandrabindu sits on the primary vowel 'aa'.
• **Visual Recognition:** Students must identify the crescent-dot symbol above the letter 'Ma' in 'Moochhein'.
• **Writing Practice:** The stroke order for Chandrabindu involves drawing the crescent first, then placing the dot.
• **Placement Rule:** It is always placed above the 'Shirorekha' (the horizontal line on top of Hindi letters).
• **Word Association:** Associating 'Moochhein' with the Tange Wala helps in long-term memory retention.
• **Contextual Usage:** The story integrates these words into simple sentences to show how they function in a sentence structure.
• **Sentence Example:** "Tange wale ki moochhein lambi hain" (The carriage driver's mustache is long).
• **Grammar Integration:** 'Hain' (हैं) is used at the end of the sentence to indicate plurality or respect, also utilizing the Chandrabindu.
• **Visual Cues:** The textbook uses bold fonts for the Chandrabindu to make it stand out for the learner.
• **Reading Fluency:** Repeated reading of the chapter helps in mastering the nasalized pronunciation.
• **Auditory Discrimination:** Teachers often play audio clips to help students distinguish between 'Ma' and 'Maan'.
• **Interactive Learning:** Students are encouraged to point to their 'Aankh' (eyes) or 'Daant' (teeth) when pronouncing the words.

• **Technical Note: The 'Shirorekha':** The horizontal line is the backbone of Hindi script.
• **Technical Note: Matra Placement:** The Chandrabindu is placed above the matra if the matra is a vowel sign like 'aa' (ा).
• **Technical Note: Typographical Rules:** In modern printing, sometimes the dot is used if the Chandrabindu is too crowded, but in handwriting, the full symbol is preferred.
• **Technical Note: Nasalization:** It is a linguistic feature where the velum is lowered.
• **Technical Note: Vowel Length:** Chandrabindu often appears on long vowels, but can appear on short ones too.
• **Technical Note: Word Finality:** It often appears at the end of words to denote plurality.
• **Technical Note: Syllable Structure:** It does not change the number of syllables in a word.
• **Technical Note: Phonetic Transcription:** In IPA, it is represented by a tilde (~) over the vowel.
• **Technical Note: Orthography:** The Chandrabindu is a distinct character in the Unicode block for Devanagari.
• **Technical Note: Historical Context:** The Chandrabindu has been part of the Devanagari script for centuries.
• **Technical Note: Regional Variations:** Pronunciation of nasalized vowels can vary slightly by dialect.
• **Technical Note: Standard Hindi:** The textbook follows the standard Khariboli dialect.
• **Technical Note: Pedagogical Sequence:** The chapter is placed after students have mastered basic vowels and consonants.
• **Technical Note: Complexity Level:** It is designed for the cognitive level of a 6-7 year old.
