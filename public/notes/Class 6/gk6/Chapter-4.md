• Topic Focus: Bharat Ratna, the highest civilian award of the Republic of India, established on January 2, 1954.
• Meaning of the Name: 'Bharat Ratna' literally translates from Sanskrit and Hindi as the 'Jewel of India'.
• Eligibility Criteria: Awarded for exceptional service and highest performance without distinction of race, occupation, position, or sex.
• Original Scope: Initially limited to achievements in the arts, literature, science, and public services as per 1954 rules.
• Expansion of Scope: In December 2011, the Government of India expanded the criteria to include 'any field of human endeavor'.
• Annual Quota: A maximum of three Bharat Ratna awards can be conferred in a particular year, though exceptions exist.
• Award Symbol/Medal: The award design is in the shape of a peepal leaf, made of toned bronze, carrying the national emblem and motto.
• Inscription on Medal: The words 'Bharat Ratna' are embossed on the obverse side in Devanagari script.
• Material Composition: The leaf is crafted from platinum-toned bronze with the sun pattern and inscription in burnished platinum.
• Privileges and Status: Recipients rank seventh in the Indian order of precedence, granting them high ceremonial status.
• Absence of Monetary Grant: The award does not carry any monetary grant; it is accompanied by a Sanad (certificate) and a medallion.
• First Recipients (1954): Conferred upon three eminent figures: C. Rajagopalachari, Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, and C. V. Raman.
• First Posthumous Recipient: Lal Bahadur Shastri became the first individual to receive the Bharat Ratna posthumously in 1966.
• Youngest Recipient: Sachin Tendulkar, who received the honor in 2014 at the age of 40, becoming the first sportsperson to win it.
• Foreign Recipients: Awarded to non-citizens including Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987) and Nelson Mandela (1990).
• Significance in Curriculum: Teaching students about the pinnacle of recognition for selfless dedication to the Indian nation.

• Topic Focus: Padma Awards, the second-highest civilian awards in India after the Bharat Ratna.
• Establishment: Instituted in 1954 by the Government of India to recognize distinguished contributions in various fields.
• Three Tiers of Padma Awards: Divided into three distinct categories based on the magnitude and impact of the achievement.
• Padma Vibhushan: The highest of the three tiers, awarded for 'exceptional and distinguished service'.
• Padma Bhushan: The second tier, awarded for 'distinguished service of a high order'.
• Padma Shri: The third tier, awarded for 'distinguished service' in any field.
• Fields of Recognition: Covers art, social work, public affairs, science and engineering, trade and industry, medicine, literature and education, sports, and civil service.
• Announcement Timing: Traditionally announced every year on the occasion of Republic Day (January 26th).
• Conferment Ceremony: Presented by the President of India at ceremonial functions held at Rashtrapati Bhavan.
• Nomination Process: Recommendations are invited from all state governments, union territories, ministries, and previous award recipients.
• Committee Selection: Final selections are made by the Padma Awards Committee headed by the Cabinet Secretary.
• Exclusion of Government Servants: Employees working with Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) are generally excluded, barring doctors and scientists.
• Posthumous Restriction: The awards are not conferred posthumously, except in rare and highly deserving cases where the government deems fit.
• Nature of Award: Consists of a certificate (Sanad) signed by the President and a medallion.
• Absence of Cash Prize: Similar to Bharat Ratna, Padma awards do not carry any monetary allowance or financial stipend.
• Educational Objective: Inspiring Class 6 students to recognize excellence across diverse professional domains within Indian society.

• Topic Focus: Param Vir Chakra (PVC), India's highest military decoration for acts of valor and self-sacrifice in the presence of the enemy.
• Meaning of the Name: Translates to 'Wheel of the Ultimate Brave' in Sanskrit.
• Establishment Date: Instituted on January 26, 1950, with retroactive effect from August 15, 1947, by the President of India.
• Equivalent Honor: Equivalent to the Victoria Cross in the British honors system or the Medal of Honor in the United States.
• Eligibility: Granted to members of the Indian Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force) for most conspicuous bravery, pre-eminent valor, or self-sacrifice.
• Design of the Medal: Crafted from bronze, circular in shape, featuring four replicas of Indra's Vajra around the State Emblem of India.
• Reverse Inscription: The words 'Param Vir Chakra' are embossed in English and Hindi on the reverse, with two lotus flowers separating the text.
• Designer of the Medal: Savitri Khanolkar, a Swiss-born Indian national, designed the iconic military medal.
• Ribbon Color: Plain maroon-colored ribbon attached to the medal.
• Posthumous Conferment: A vast majority of PVC recipients have been awarded posthumously due to the extreme nature of the acts performed.
• First Recipient: Major Somnath Sharma, who made the supreme sacrifice during the Battle of Badgam in Kashmir in November 1947.
• Notable Recipient: Captain Vikram Batra, honored posthumously for his unmatched bravery during the 1999 Kargil War.
• Monetary Allowance: Recipients receive a monthly cash allowance from the central government, along with state-level pensions and honors.
• Protocol Status: Awardees hold an esteemed position in military protocol and are deeply revered nationally.
• Core Lesson: Instilling deep patriotism, courage, and respect for the armed forces defenders of the nation in young minds.

• Topic Focus: Wartime and Peacetime Gallantry Awards in the Indian Armed Forces.
• Classification System: Gallantry awards in India are divided into two primary categories: wartime gallantry and peacetime gallantry.
• Wartime Gallantry Awards: Given for acts of valor in the presence of the enemy on the battlefield.
• Maha Vir Chakra (MVC): The second-highest wartime gallantry award, recognizing acts of conspicuous gallantry in the presence of the enemy.
• Vir Chakra (VrC): The third-highest wartime gallantry award, given for acts of bravery during wartime operations.
• Peacetime Gallantry Awards: Instituted to recognize acts of valor, courage, and self-sacrifice away from the battlefield, such as during counter-insurgency.
• Ashoka Chakra: India's highest peacetime military decoration, equivalent to the Param Vir Chakra, for valor away from the enemy.
• Kirti Chakra: The second-highest peacetime gallantry award, recognizing conspicuous gallantry.
• Shaurya Chakra: The third-highest peacetime gallantry award, given for an act of valor or courageous action away from the battlefield.
• Joint Eligibility: All ranks of officers, enlisted personnel of the military, and sometimes members of reserve forces and civilians are eligible.
• Order of Precedence: Param Vir Chakra, Ashoka Chakra, Maha Vir Chakra, Kirti Chakra, Vir Chakra, and Shaurya Chakra.
• Investiture Ceremony: Conferred by the President of India during the annual Defence Investiture Ceremony at Rashtrapati Bhavan.
• Purpose of Distinction: Honoring both active combat heroism and operational preparedness in peace zones.
• Role in Character Building: Teaching students about the multifaceted sacrifices made by security personnel beyond direct war scenarios.
• Textbook Relevance: Crucial general knowledge facts tested frequently in school examinations and quiz competitions.

• Topic Focus: National Sports Awards of India, recognizing excellence in sports coaching, performance, and lifetime contribution.
• Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award: Formerly known as the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna, it is India's highest sporting honor.
• Origin of Khel Ratna: Instituted in 1991–1992 to honor stellar and most outstanding performance in sports over a period of four years.
• Renaming Significance: Named after Major Dhyan Chand, legendary Indian hockey wizard, to honor his unparalleled legacy.
• Arjuna Award: Established in 1961, it is India's oldest national sports award for consistent good performance over four years.
• Symbol of Arjuna Award: Consists of a bronze statuette of Arjuna, a scroll, and a cash prize.
• Dronacharya Award: Instituted in 1985 to honor eminent coaches who have successfully trained athletes or teams to international success.
• Name Origin: Named after Dronacharya, the legendary martial arts guru from the epic Mahabharata.
• Dhyan Chand Award: Established in 2002 for lifetime achievement in sports development and contribution to sport post-retirement.
• Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar: Given to corporate entities, sports control boards, and NGOs for playing a role in sports promotion.
• Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy: Awarded to the top-performing university in inter-university tournaments, instituted in 1956–1957.
• Presentation Authority: Presented annually by the President of India on National Sports Day (August 29th).
• National Sports Day Significance: Celebrated on the birth anniversary of Major Dhyan Chand.
• Cash Incentives: Recipients receive substantial monetary grants alongside trophies and citations to support their sporting careers.
• Educational Impact: Encouraging students to pursue physical fitness, competitive sports, and dedication to athletic excellence.

• Topic Focus: The Nobel Prize, widely regarded as the most prestigious international recognition in the world.
• Origin and Will: Established by the 1895 will of Alfred Nobel, a Swedish chemist, engineer, and inventor of dynamite.
• Funding Source: Funded by the colossal fortune left by Alfred Nobel in his will, managed by the Nobel Foundation.
• Inception Year: First awarded in 1901 after the establishment of the Nobel Foundation guidelines.
• Original Categories: Originally awarded in five fields: Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, and Peace.
• Economics Addition: A sixth category, the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences, was added in 1968 by the Bank of Sweden.
• Announcement Location: Winners are announced annually in October by distinct committees based in Sweden and Norway.
• Award Ceremony Date: Held annually in Stockholm, Sweden (and Oslo, Norway for the Peace Prize) on December 10th, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death.
• Award Components: Recipients receive a gold medal, a diploma, and a substantial monetary prize sum.
• Indian Nobel Laureates: Rabindranath Tagore (Literature, 1913) was the first Indian and Asian Nobel laureate.
• Other Indian Icons: C.V. Raman (Physics, 1930), Mother Teresa (Peace, 1979), Amartya Sen (Economics, 1998), and Kailash Satyarthi (Peace, 2014).
• Nobel Peace Prize Committee: Uniquely awarded by a committee appointed by the Parliament of Norway, unlike other prizes managed in Sweden.
• Posthumous Rule: Since 1974, Nobel Prizes cannot be awarded posthumously unless the recipient died after the announcement.
• International Prestige: Represents the absolute zenith of human intellectual, scientific, and humanitarian achievement.
• Textbook Connection: Essential general knowledge linking global science, literature, and peace efforts to school-level learning.

• Topic Focus: Literary and Artistic Awards in India, celebrating excellence in language, writing, and creative expression.
• Sahitya Akademi Award: A literary honor conferred annually by Sahitya Akademi, India's National Academy of Letters.
• Establishment of Sahitya Akademi: Established in 1954 to promote Indian literature and coordinate literary activities across various languages.
• Scope of Sahitya Akademi: Awarded to books in 24 major Indian languages recognized by the Akademi, alongside English and Rajasthani.
• Jnanpith Award: India's highest literary honor, instituted in 1961 by the Bharatiya Jnanpith trust.
• Eligibility for Jnanpith: Conferred annually upon an author for their 'outstanding contribution towards literature'.
• Language Scope for Jnanpith: Originally restricted to languages in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, later expanded to English as well.
• Award Components: Includes an 11-lakh cash prize, a citation plaque, and a bronze replica of Vagdevi (Goddess Saraswati).
• First Jnanpith Winner: G. Sankara Kurup, the renowned Malayalam poet, won the inaugural award in 1965.
• National Film Awards: Established in 1954, administered by the Directorate of Film Festivals, celebrating cinematic excellence.
• Dadasaheb Phalke Award: India's highest award in cinema, instituted in 1969 to commemorate the father of Indian cinema, Dadasaheb Phalke.
• First Dadasaheb Phalke Winner: Devika Rani was the inaugural recipient of this prestigious lifetime achievement film award.
• Academy Awards (Oscars): Internationally renowned film awards presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in the USA.
• Cultural Preservation: These awards ensure the protection, promotion, and celebration of regional and national art forms.
• Textbook Value: Cultivating appreciation for books, regional languages, cinema, and cultural heritage among young learners.

• Topic Focus: Environmental, Children's, and Bravery Awards that inspire social responsibility and moral courage.
• National Bravery Awards: Formerly given annually to children under 18 years of age for meritorious acts of exceptional courage against grave danger.
• Rebranding of Bravery Awards: Integrated under the Pradhan Mantri Rashtriya Bal Puraskar to honor young achievers.
• Pradhan Mantri Rashtriya Bal Puraskar: Conferred by the President of India in categories including bravery, innovation, social service, and sports.
• Gandhi Peace Prize: International award conferred by the Government of India for contributions towards social, economic, and political transformation through non-violence.
• Indira Gandhi Prize for Peace: Awarded annually by the Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust for international disarmament and development.
• Right Livelihood Award: Often referred to as the 'Alternative Nobel Prize', honoring those addressing global environmental and social challenges.
• Tyler Prize for Environmental Achievement: Recognized internationally as the premier award for environmental science, policy, and energy.
• UNEP Champions of the Earth: The United Nations' highest environmental honor recognizing outstanding leaders from government, civil society, and the private sector.
• Cartier Women's Initiative: Supporting women entrepreneurs addressing major global challenges through sustainable business models.
• Civilian Social Impact: Highlighting how ordinary citizens and children can drive monumental positive change in society.
• Moral Education Linkage: Teaching students values of empathy, quick thinking, fearlessness, and ecological stewardship.
• Textbook Significance: Ensuring that Class 6 general knowledge extends beyond academics into civic duties and humanitarian ideals.
