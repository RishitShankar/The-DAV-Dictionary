• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to the DAV Dictionary Academic Notes for Class 7 Kid Wiz (General Knowledge), Chapter 13, titled "साहस को सलाम" (Salute to Courage).
• **Core Theme**: This chapter is dedicated to recognizing, honoring, and understanding extraordinary acts of human bravery, resilience, and supreme sacrifice displayed by individuals in critical situations.
• **Educational Objective**: The primary goal is to inspire students of Class 7 to cultivate moral courage, physical bravery, and a strong sense of duty toward society and the nation.
• **Etymology of Sahas**: The Hindi word **'साहस' (Sahas)** translates to courage, valor, fearlessness, or grit in the face of immense danger, adversity, or uncertainty.
• **Nature of Bravery**: True courage is not the absence of fear, but the triumph over it, where individuals act altruistically despite personal peril.
• **National Recognition**: India honors its bravest citizens, particularly children and members of the armed forces, through prestigious national awards and decorations.
• **Inspirational Value**: Stories of survival, rescue, and unyielding determination serve as timeless beacons of hope and moral fortitude for the younger generation.
• **Scope of Chapter**: The chapter explores real-life incidents, historical bravery, institutional awards, and the psychological attributes that define a true hero.
• **Social Responsibility**: Bravery often manifests as standing up against injustice, social evils, and protecting the vulnerable members of society.
• **Physical vs. Moral Courage**: The chapter distinguishes between physical bravery (facing bodily harm) and moral courage (upholding ethical principles despite opposition).
• **Role of DAV Ethos**: Aligning with Dayanand Anglo-Vedic values, the chapter emphasizes selfless service (*Seva*) and fearlessness rooted in righteousness (*Dharma*).
• **Global Relevance**: While featuring Indian heroes, the principles of courage discussed apply universally to humanitarian acts across the globe.
• **Empathy as a Catalyst**: Acts of supreme courage are almost always driven by deep empathy, compassion, and love for fellow human beings.
• **Overcoming Adversity**: The narratives highlight how ordinary people rise above extraordinary circumstances through sheer mental toughness.
• **Analytical Approach**: Students are encouraged to analyze the context, decision-making process, and impact of the brave acts documented in this chapter.

• **Pradhan Mantri Rashtriya Bal Puraskar**: Formerly known as the National Bravery Award, this is India's highest civilian honor specifically bestowed upon exceptional children.
• **Ministry of Women and Child Development**: The national awards for children are coordinated and conferred annually by this nodal ministry of the Government of India.
• **Eligibility Criteria**: Children aged between 6 and 18 years who display outstanding acts of valor, meritorious service, or remarkable intelligence are considered for the Bal Puraskar.
• **Historical Origin**: The tradition of honoring brave children began organically in 1957 when a child showed exemplary courage during a public function in Delhi.
• **First Recipients**: Harish Chandra and Smt. Geeta Phul were among the first children to be recognized for their spontaneous and selfless acts of bravery.
• **Categories of Recognition**: Awards are typically divided into categories such as Bravery, Innovation, Social Service, Sports, Art and Culture, and Scholastic achievements.
• **Republic Day Participation**: Historically, recipients of the National Bravery Awards ride on decorated elephants during the Republic Day Parade on Kartavya Path.
• **National Recognition**: The awards bring national spotlight to unsung young heroes who inspire millions of peers across the country.
• **Financial Support**: Awardees often receive monetary grants, certificates, and assistance for their higher education until the completion of their studies.
• **Government Support**: The Indian Council for Child Welfare (ICCW) previously played a foundational role in screening and nurturing these young aspirants.
• **Transformative Impact**: Receiving such an honor alters the trajectory of a child's life, instilling profound confidence and a lifelong commitment to public service.
• **Spontaneous Action**: Most child bravery awardees exhibit split-second decision-making abilities without pausing to calculate personal safety or risk.
• **Examples of Acts**: Documented acts include rescuing drowning peers, fighting off wild animals, averting train accidents, and saving people from fires.
• **Anti-Crime Heroism**: Several children have shown extraordinary presence of mind in thwarting robberies, kidnapping attempts, and terrorist intrusions.
• **Media Role**: Documenting these stories in school curricula ensures that the spirit of heroism remains alive and accessible to growing minds.

• **Param Vir Chakra (PVC)**: India's highest military decoration awarded for most conspicuous bravery, self-sacrifice, and presence of mind in the presence of the enemy.
• **Design of PVC**: Designed by Savitri Khanolkar, the medal is made of bronze, features Indra's Vajra (thunderbolt), and is suspended from a purple ribbon.
• **Major Somnath Sharma**: The very first recipient of the Param Vir Chakra, who made the supreme sacrifice at the Battle of Badgam in Kashmir in 1947.
• **Motto of PVC**: The medal's design embodies supreme selflessness, inscribed with 'Param Vir Chakra' in Hindi and English.
• **Peacetime Gallantry**: While PVC is for wartime bravery, peacetime gallantry is honored with the Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra, and Shaurya Chakra.
• **Ashoka Chakra**: The highest peacetime military decoration in India, equivalent to the Param Vir Chakra, awarded for valor away from the battlefield.
• **Captain Vikram Batra**: A legendary PVC recipient from the 1999 Kargil War, famous for his inspiring wartime catchphrase "Yeh Dil Maange More!"
• **Subedar Major Bana Singh**: Honored with the PVC for capturing the strategic Quaid Post at an altitude of 21,000 feet during Operation Rajiv in the Siachen Glacier.
• **Second Lieutenant Arun Khetarpal**: The youngest PVC recipient, who displayed legendary tank combat skills during the Battle of Basantar in the 1971 war.
• **Rifleman Jaswant Singh Rawat**: Fought heroically for 72 hours single-handedly during the 1962 Indo-China War at Nuranang, capturing public imagination.
• **Institutional Discipline**: Military bravery is rooted in rigorous training, fierce regimental pride, and absolute devotion to the motherland.
• **Posthumous Honors**: A significant percentage of highest gallantry awards are given posthumously, signifying ultimate sacrifice.
• **Family Legacy**: The families of gallantry awardees are treated with immense national respect and receive lifelong state patronage and pensions.
• **Battlefield Ethics**: Indian armed forces maintain strict adherence to the Geneva Conventions while displaying unmatched tactical aggression against aggressors.
• **Educational Integration**: Studying military heroes teaches students about patriotism, duty, honor, and the heavy price of national sovereignty.

• **Civilian Rescue Heroes**: Beyond military personnel, ordinary citizens frequently transform into emergency responders during natural and man-made disasters.
• **Railway Track Rescues**: Numerous instances highlight individuals jumping onto railway tracks seconds before oncoming trains to save fallen children or elderly persons.
• **Flood Relief Operations**: Local swimmers and fishermen who deploy makeshift boats to evacuate marooned villagers during seasonal floods exemplify grassroot courage.
• **Earthquake Survivors**: Rescue workers and neighborhood volunteers who dig through rubble with bare hands following seismic disasters demonstrate monumental tenacity.
• **Firefighting Heroism**: Individuals who rush into burning residential complexes to pull trapped occupants out of infernos show exceptional selflessness.
• **Medical Bravery**: Doctors and healthcare workers who risk their lives during pandemics, outbreaks, and conflict zones embody professional courage.
• **Aviation Rescues**: Pilots and crew members who execute emergency landings or divert crashing aircraft away from populated areas save countless lives at personal peril.
• **Environmental Protection**: Grassroot activists who stand up against illegal deforestation, poaching, and industrial pollution face grave physical threats.
• **Whistleblower Protection**: Individuals who expose corruption, financial scams, and organized crime syndicates display immense moral and physical courage.
• **Animal Rescue Operations**: People who rescue distressed stray animals from deep wells, sticky tar, or barbed wires exhibit compassion-driven bravery.
• **Road Accident Good Samaritans**: Citizens who rush bleeding accident victims to hospitals without fear of legal harassment are vital pillars of civil society.
• **Women Led Valor**: Countless rural and urban women have displayed stellar courage in confronting eve-teasers, domestic abusers, and criminal elements.
• **Community Policing**: Neighborhood watch volunteers who patrol dangerous zones to maintain public safety demonstrate collective civic courage.
• **Psychological Resilience**: The mental recovery of individuals who survive tragic accidents and return to help others is a profound form of silent bravery.
• **Media Documentation**: Newspapers, documentaries, and school textbooks act as permanent repositories for these everyday tales of extraordinary human grit.

• **Kargil War Heroes**: The 1962, 1965, 1971, and 1999 wars produced a vast pantheon of bravehearts whose exploits are studied in strategic institutions.
• **Operation Vijay (1999)**: Fought in the rugged, freezing heights of Kargil, Kargil war heroes overcame vertical climbs against heavily entrenched intruders.
• **Captain Manoj Kumar Pandey**: Awarded the PVC posthumously for single-handedly clearing enemy bunkers during the capture of Khalubar ridge.
• **Grenadier Yogendra Singh Yadav**: The youngest living PVC recipient, severely injured during the capture of Tiger Hill, who continued to fight relentlessly.
• **Lieutenant Manoj Kumar Pandey's Diary**: His personal diary famously contained the line "Some goals are so worthy, it's glorious even to fail," inspiring generations.
• **Sanjay Kumar**: Rifleman Sanjay Kumar crawled through enemy fire and used an enemy machine gun against them during the capture of Area Flat Top.
• **Captain Anuj Nayyar**: Fought valiantly to clear Pimple Complex during the Kargil conflict, ensuring safe passage for his advancing battalion.
• **Major Mariappan Saravanan**: Fought bravely in hand-to-hand combat during the assault on Batalik sector before making the supreme sacrifice.
• **Squadron Leader Ajay Ahuja**: Indian Air Force pilot who displayed exemplary courage when his MiG-21 was shot down over enemy territory.
• **Kargil War Memorial**: Situated in Drass, Ladakh, this monument etches the names of every soldier who fell during the conflict, serving as a pilgrimage site.
• **Altitude Warfare**: Fighting at altitudes exceeding 18,000 feet requires psychological resilience against hypoxia, frostbite, and freezing winds.
• **Logistical Triumphs**: Military porters and local Ladakhi guides who supplied ammunition and rations up sheer cliffs displayed monumental physical bravery.
• **Media Coverage**: The Kargil War was one of the first televised conflicts in India, bringing the raw realities of battlefield bravery directly into living rooms.
• **National Integration**: Soldiers from every corner of India fought shoulder to shoulder, transcending linguistic, regional, and religious divides.
• **Lasting Legacy**: The valor displayed in Kargil cemented the Indian Armed Forces' reputation as one of the most professional and fearless military bodies.

• **Historical Figures of Courage**: Indian history is replete with legendary figures who challenged tyranny, fought oppression, and reshaped destiny through bravery.
• **Maharana Pratap**: The ruler of Mewar who fiercely resisted the Mughal Empire, living a life of hardship in the Aravalli hills rather than accepting subjugation.
• **Battle of Haldighati**: Fought in 1576, this historic battle showcased Maharana Pratap's unmatched tactical grit and his legendary loyal horse, Chetak.
• **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj**: Founder of the Maratha Empire, known for his innovative guerrilla warfare tactics (*Ganimi Kava*) against vast imperial armies.
• **Fort of Sinhagad**: The heroic capture of this fort by Tanaji Malusare, who fought to his last breath, symbolizes supreme loyalty and tactical bravery.
• **Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi**: The Queen of Jhansi who led her troops into battle during the 1857 Revolt, fighting fiercely with her infant tied to her back.
• **Tatya Tope**: A brilliant guerrilla commander during the 1857 rebellion who evaded capture for months while continuously harassing British forces.
• **Bhagat Singh**: Revolutionary freedom fighter who displayed supreme moral courage, walking to the gallows with a smile while chanting slogans for freedom.
• **Chandrashekhar Azad**: Pledged never to be caught alive by the British, fighting a fierce lone battle at Alfred Park in Allahabad before shooting himself.
• **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose**: Formed the Azad Hind Fauj (Indian National Army) and gave the clarion call "Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom!"
• **Birsa Munda**: Tribal freedom fighter who led the Ulgulan (rebellion) against British land policies and exploitative landlords at a very young age.
• **Velu Nachiyar**: Queen of Sivagangai, the first Indian queen to wage war against the East India Company, utilizing intelligence and women's battalions.
• **Kittur Chennamma**: Queen of Kittur who led an armed rebellion against the Doctrine of Lapse imposed by the British East India Company.
• **Mangal Pandey**: Sepoy whose defiant actions at Barrackpore in March 1857 served as the early spark for the First War of Indian Independence.
• **Philosophical Roots**: Indian historical bravery is deeply intertwined with the philosophical teachings of the Bhagavad Gita regarding duty (*Karma*) and righteousness.

• **Global Icons of Courage**: Worldwide historical and contemporary figures offer parallel lessons in overcoming insurmountable odds through bravery.
• **Mahatma Gandhi**: Championed *Satyagraha* (soul force and truth), demonstrating profound moral courage by dismantling an empire through non-violent resistance.
• **Nelson Mandela**: Imprisoned for 27 years on Robben Island, he emerged without bitterness to lead South Africa out of apartheid through reconciliation.
• **Abraham Lincoln**: Navigated the American Civil War, abolished slavery, and preserved the Union despite constant death threats and immense political turmoil.
• **Martin Luther King Jr.**: Spearheaded the American Civil Rights Movement, facing systemic racism, bombings, and arrests with unwavering non-violent resolve.
• **Rosa Parks**: Refused to give up her bus seat to a white passenger in Montgomery, sparking a bus boycott that energized the global civil rights movement.
• **Joan of Arc**: Teenage peasant girl who led the French army to crucial victories during the Hundred Years' War, displaying peerless battlefield leadership.
• **Malala Yousafzai**: Pakistani activist for female education who survived an assassination attempt by the Taliban and became the youngest Nobel Prize laureate.
• **Anne Frank**: Jewish diarist whose hidden life during the Holocaust documented human resilience, hope, and psychological endurance amidst absolute horror.
• **Harriet Tubman**: Escaped slavery and subsequently risked her life dozens of times to lead hundreds of enslaved people to freedom via the Underground Railroad.
• **Marie Curie**: Conducted pioneering research on radioactivity despite severe health hazards, societal sexism, and scarce laboratory resources.
• **Neil Armstrong & Buzz Aldrin**: Stepped onto the lunar surface in 1963, trusting untested technology and stepping into the ultimate unknown void of space.
• **Eugenio Zolli and Rescuers**: Countless individuals who risked their lives hiding persecuted populations during the dark eras of world history.
• **Global Relevance**: These figures transcend national boundaries, proving that the human spirit possesses an innate capacity for extraordinary courage.
• **Universal Values**: Studying global icons helps students understand that bravery is a multicultural, cross-historical human imperative.

• **Psychology of Courage**: Understanding why and how individuals act bravely involves exploring neurological, emotional, and cognitive frameworks.
• **Amygdala and Fear Response**: The brain's threat detection center triggers fight, flight, or freeze responses during critical moments of crisis.
• **Prefrontal Cortex Regulation**: Courageous individuals harness executive brain functions to override panic, assess risks logically, and formulate rescue actions.
• **Altruistic Personality**: Psychologists identify specific traits—such as empathy, internal locus of control, and social responsibility—predisposing individuals to heroism.
• **Training and Conditioning**: Emergency personnel, soldiers, and athletes undergo rigorous simulation training to convert panic into automatic, disciplined responses.
• **Role of Habit**: Repeated exposure to minor challenges builds psychological resilience, making individuals more equipped to handle major life-threatening crises.
• **Cognitive Reappraisal**: Brave people reframe terrifying situations from "I am in danger" to "I have a job to do," altering their emotional state.
• **Social Proof and Contagion**: Acts of courage are often contagious; when one person steps forward, others are emboldened to join rescue efforts.
• **Overcoming Bystander Effect**: Understanding diffusion of responsibility helps individuals break the paralysis of inaction in crowded public emergencies.
• **Post-Traumatic Growth**: Many survivors and rescuers experience positive psychological transformation, gaining deeper life appreciation after surviving ordeals.
• **Fear Management**: True courage is not the absence of fear, but the psychological mastery of physiological symptoms like rapid heartbeat and trembling.
• **Moral Identity**: Individuals who view themselves as ethical actors feel compelled to intervene when they witness wrongdoing or suffering.
• **Resilience Training in Schools**: Modern education systems incorporate resilience-building exercises to help students manage academic and personal stress.
• **Emotional Regulation**: Mindfulness, deep breathing, and focused intent assist in calming the nervous system during high-stress scenarios.
• **Lifelong Development**: Courage is treated not as a fixed genetic trait, but as a muscle that strengthens with every ethical and brave choice made.

• **Everyday Courage in Student Life**: Class 7 students encounter numerous scenarios in daily school and home environments that require quiet, steadfast bravery.
• **Standing Up to Bullying**: Refusing to join peer groups in mocking or harassing a classmate requires immense social and moral courage.
• **Admitting Mistakes**: Confessing to academic errors, broken items, or accidental damage rather than lying demonstrates integrity and moral bravery.
• **Peer Pressure Resistance**: Saying 'no' to harmful habits, substance abuse, or unethical shortcuts demands strong personal conviction.
• **Speaking the Truth**: Reporting cheating, vandalism, or unsafe behavior to teachers or parents, despite fear of being labeled a whistleblower.
• **Embracing New Challenges**: Participating in debates, sports tournaments, or stage performances despite stage fright is a form of personal bravery.
• **Supporting Vulnerable Peers**: Befriending new students who feel isolated or defending children with special needs against exclusion.
• **Academic Persistence**: Continuing to work hard and overcome learning difficulties in tough subjects rather than giving up out of frustration.
• **Standing for Justice**: Defending a classmate who is falsely accused or unfairly treated by peers or authority figures.
• **Health and Medical Bravery**: Facing vaccinations, surgeries, or chronic illnesses with a positive and brave attitude.
• **Family Support**: Helping parents manage domestic crises, financial hardships, or caring for sick family members with emotional maturity.
• **Environmental Stewardship**: Taking proactive steps to clean school grounds or stop classmates from wasting resources, despite social mockery.
• **Digital Courage**: Reporting cyberbullying, online harassment, or inappropriate content to trusted adults instead of suffering in silence.
• **Creative Risk-Taking**: Exploring unconventional hobbies, art forms, or scientific projects despite fear of failure or peer judgment.
• **Cultivating Character**: Building these everyday habits ensures that students are prepared to act courageously when faced with major life-altering events.
