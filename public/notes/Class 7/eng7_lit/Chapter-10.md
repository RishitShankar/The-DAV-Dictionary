• **Chapter Title and Origin:** "Friends and Flatterers" is a classic didactic poem authored by the renowned English Renaissance poet, playwright, and composer, William Shakespeare.
• **Curriculum Context:** This chapter is part of the DAV Class 7 English Literature syllabus, designed to impart moral values, critical life lessons, and advanced vocabulary to young learners.
• **Core Theme:** The central theme of the poem revolves around the stark contrast between true friends who stand by in adversity and false friends (flatterers) who only associate with prosperity.
• **Literary Form:** The poem is structured as a lyric or a ballad-style moral discourse, utilizing rhythm and rhyme to make its profound philosophical message memorable and impactful.
• **Tone and Mood:** The tone of the poem shifts from observational and advisory in the opening lines to cautionary, sobering, and eventually deeply philosophical as it exposes human hypocrisy.
• **Poetic Persona:** The speaker in the poem adopts the voice of a wise mentor, elder, or experienced observer imparting hard-earned truths about human nature to a younger or naive listener.
• **Title Analysis:** The title immediately establishes a dichotomous relationship between 'Friends' (those offering genuine, unconditional support) and 'Flatterers' (those offering insincere praise for personal gain).
• **Significance in DAV Framework:** Within the DAV curriculum, this poem aligns with the core Vedic value of discernment (*Viveka*), teaching students to distinguish between outward show and inner truth.
• **Historical Resonance:** Written during the Elizabethan era, the poem reflects Shakespeare’s deep psychological insight into court politics, patronage, and the fickle nature of societal loyalty.
• **Universal Relevance:** Despite being centuries old, the poem's message remains completely modern, addressing the contemporary social media phenomena of fair-weather associations and clout-chasing.
• **Vocabulary Foundation:** The text introduces students to critical abstract nouns and adjectives related to human behavior, such as 'flattery', 'adversity', 'prosperity', 'heed', and 'pennyless'.
• **Structural Progression:** The poem systematically builds its argument by first describing the behavior of fake friends during wealth, then their disappearance during poverty, and finally defining true friendship.
• **Moral Imperative:** The ultimate objective of the chapter is to teach students emotional resilience, self-reliance, and the wisdom to value quality over quantity in interpersonal relationships.
• **Reading Comprehension Focus:** Students studying this chapter are expected to master poetic devices, rhythmic patterns, metaphorical language, and underlying moral allegories.
• **Cross-Curricular Link:** The poem connects English Literature with Moral Science and Psychology, encouraging students to evaluate their own social circles and emotional dependencies.

• **Opening Stanza Premise:** The poem begins with a powerful cautionary statement about the deceptive nature of human relationships, urging the reader to pay close attention to who surrounds them.
• **Definition of Flatterers:** Flatterers are identified as individuals who use excessive, insincere praise not out of genuine affection, but to manipulate others for their own selfish, material benefits.
• **Behavior During Prosperity:** When a person is wealthy, successful, and surrounded by abundance, flatterers flock around them like bees to honey, offering endless validation and companionship.
• **Metaphor of the Season:** Flatterers are implicitly compared to migratory birds or seasonal weather, appearing only when the sun shines and the environment is favorable and comfortable.
• **The Illusion of Loyalty:** During times of wealth, false friends create an elaborate illusion of unbreakable loyalty, making grand promises and treating the affluent person like royalty.
• **Greed as a Motive:** The primary engine driving the behavior of flatterers is greed; they consume the host's resources, time, and energy while contributing nothing of substance in return.
• **The Danger of Excess Praise:** The poem warns that sweet words and constant agreement are often weapons of deception, used to blind the victim to the true motives of the speaker.
• **Superficial Affection:** The affection shown by flatterers is entirely conditional, dependent solely on the material status, social standing, and financial liquidity of the person they flatter.
• **Lack of Substance:** Just as empty vessels make the most noise, flatterers make the loudest professions of love while possessing zero genuine emotional depth or commitment.
• **Social Climbing:** Many flatterers use wealthy individuals as stepping stones to elevate their own social status, abandoning them the moment those stones are no longer useful.
• **The Flatterer's Smile:** The poem highlights the unsettling dual nature of the flatterer, whose face is perpetually adorned with a welcoming smile that conceals a calculating and opportunistic mind.
• **Encouragement of Vanity:** By feeding a person's ego with undeserved praise, flatterers encourage vanity and blindness, making it difficult for the victim to see reality clearly.
• **Exploitation of Generosity:** Wealthy individuals are often naturally generous, and flatterers exploit this generosity by constantly asking for favors, gifts, and financial assistance.
• **The Trap of Comfort:** Being surrounded by constant praise creates a comfortable bubble of delusion, which makes the eventual betrayal by flatterers all the more shocking and painful.
• **Literary Device - Contrast:** Shakespeare establishes an immediate structural contrast in these opening lines, setting up the impending fall from grace that will test these relationships.

• **The Turning Point (Adversity):** The poem introduces a dramatic shift in circumstances, moving from wealth and prosperity to poverty, hardship, and sudden misfortune.
• **Definition of Adversity:** Adversity is presented as the ultimate test of human character, stripping away superficial comforts to reveal the true nature of people around us.
• **Disappearance of the Flock:** The moment financial ruin or hardship strikes, the once-crowded room of flatterers empties out with astonishing speed and absolute silence.
• **Cowardice of False Friends:** Flatterers are exposed as absolute cowards who lack the emotional fortitude to face difficulty, pain, or sacrifice alongside their supposed friend.
• **The Metaphor of Fair-Weather Friends:** The poem solidifies the concept of 'fair-weather friends'—those who enjoy the sunny days of your life but run indoors the moment a storm arrives.
• **Broken Promises:** All the grand vows of eternal loyalty made by flatterers during good times vanish instantly, proving that their words possessed zero intrinsic value.
• **Isolation in Need:** When the individual is left 'pennyess' and vulnerable, they realize with a heavy heart that they are completely alone, abandoned by those who claimed to love them most.
• **The Shock of Betrayal:** The realization of abandonment brings profound emotional pain, teaching the speaker a harsh lesson about the superficiality of human society.
• **Silence of the Deceivers:** Not only do flatterers leave, but they often actively avoid their former benefactor out of guilt, shame, or fear of being asked for help.
• **Reversal of Fortune:** The dramatic change underscores the wheel of fortune—how quickly status can invert, turning the envied host into a neglected outcast.
• **Psychological Impact:** The experience of desertion by fake friends causes disillusionment, forcing the individual to reevaluate their judgment of character and human nature.
• **Material vs. Spiritual Worth:** The abandonment highlights a shallow world that values people exclusively for what they own rather than who they are as human beings.
• **Absence of Sympathy:** Unlike true friends who offer a shoulder to cry on, flatterers view misfortune as a contagious disease from which they must distance themselves immediately.
• **Shedding of the Mask:** The crisis strips away the polite masks worn by flatterers, revealing their true, selfish identities in the harsh light of reality.
• **Transition to Wisdom:** This painful period of isolation serves as the crucible in which the speaker gains true wisdom, learning to separate wheat from chaff in human relationships.

• **The Definition of True Friendship:** After detailing the betrayal of flatterers, the poem pivots to define what a genuine, steadfast friend actually looks and acts like in the real world.
• **Loyalty in Hard Times:** A true friend is identified primarily by their unwavering presence, support, and loyalty during times of difficulty, sorrow, and financial ruin.
• **The Metaphor of Winter:** True friends are compared to hardy evergreen trees or winter-resistant companions who remain green and steadfast when all other leaves have fallen.
• **Sharing Grief and Pain:** While flatterers only want to share in joy and celebration, a true friend willingly shares the burden of grief, sorrow, and emotional distress.
• **Absence of Selfishness:** Genuine friendship is characterized by selflessness, where the friend puts the well-being of the companion above their own comfort or convenience.
• **Action Over Words:** True friends do not rely on excessive flattery or empty praise; their love is proven through concrete actions, sacrifices, and steadfast deeds.
• **The Acid Test of Need:** Need is established as the absolute acid test for friendship—if someone stays when you have nothing to offer, they are a friend indeed.
• **Emotional Sanctuary:** A true friend provides a safe, non-judgmental emotional sanctuary where a person can drop their defenses and be completely authentic without fear of rejection.
• **Constructive Honesty:** Unlike flatterers who say what you want to hear, a true friend will courageously tell you the hard truth when you are making mistakes, for your own betterment.
• **Steadfastness Across Time:** True friendship remains constant regardless of fluctuations in wealth, social status, health, or external circumstances.
• **Relief in Distress:** When misfortune strikes, the presence of even a single true friend acts as a powerful balm, easing the sting of betrayal and restoring faith in humanity.
• **Mutual Respect:** The relationship between true friends is built on a foundation of deep mutual respect, equality, and shared values rather than utility or manipulation.
• **The Proverbial Wisdom:** The poem reinforces the ancient proverb "A friend in need is a friend indeed," translating it into emotionally resonant poetic verse.
• **Quiet Dedication:** True friends do not seek public applause, social media validation, or grand recognition for their support; their loyalty is quiet, humble, and profound.
• **Enduring Bond:** The bond shared by true friends outlasts the trials of time, surviving distance, misunderstandings, and external pressures with grace and resilience.

• **Poetic Metaphor Analysis (Birds and Seasons):** Shakespeare masterfully employs natural imagery, comparing fair-weather friends to birds that migrate away from the cold of winter.
• **The Winter Metaphor:** 'Winter' in the poem symbolizes not just the physical cold season, but metaphorically represents old age, poverty, failure, and hardship.
• **The Summer Metaphor:** 'Summer' represents youth, wealth, success, popularity, and abundance—the peak operating conditions that attract opportunistic flatterers.
• **The Concept of the 'Ears':** The poem discusses how flatterers use sweet words to tickle the ears, manipulating human vanity and the innate desire to be liked and praised.
• **The Thorn and the Rose:** False friends are often likened to thorns disguised by beautiful petals, attracting the unwary traveler only to cause pain when grasped.
• **The Mirror of Truth:** Adversity acts as a moral mirror, reflecting back the true faces of those around us once the glittering decorations of wealth are removed.
• **Alliteration and Rhyme Scheme:** The poem utilizes rhythmic rhyming couplets and cross-rhymes that give it a musical quality, aiding memorization and recitation for DAV students.
• **Diction and Vocabulary:** Words like *flatterer*, *adversity*, *depart*, *smart*, *sorrow*, and *borrow* are chosen with precision to maximize emotional resonance and phonetic impact.
• **Contrastive Imagery:** The sharp juxtaposition of light and dark, warmth and cold, abundance and scarcity, enhances the dramatic tension throughout the text.
• **Personification of Abstract Concepts:** Abstract ideas like 'Flattery', 'Friendship', and 'Adversity' are almost personified through their vivid behavioral descriptions in the poem.
• **The Rhythm of Life:** The regular meter of the poem mimics the steady, dependable heartbeat of a true friend, contrasting with the erratic, chaotic movements of flatterers.
• **Symbolism of Gold:** Wealth and gold are symbolized as magnets that draw superficial admirers, highlighting the corrupting influence of materialism on human bonding.
• **The Power of Retcelling:** DAV students are encouraged to recite the poem aloud to grasp how the cadence mirrors the shifting emotional landscape of betrayal and loyalty.
• **Metaphorical Cleansing:** The departure of flatterers, though painful, is metaphorically framed as a necessary cleansing process that purifies one's life of toxic elements.
• **Literary Heritage:** Studying these metaphors connects Class 7 students to the broader tradition of Elizabethan literature and Shakespearean moral philosophy.

• **Psychological Dynamics of Flattery:** The chapter encourages students to analyze *why* people fall for flattery, identifying human vulnerability, loneliness, and ego as key factors.
• **The Role of Vanity:** Vanity makes individuals susceptible to praise, blinding them to the reality that excessive flattery almost always serves a hidden, self-serving agenda.
• **Building Emotional Resilience:** Students learn that experiencing fake friendships, while painful, builds emotional armor and sharpens interpersonal discernment for future life stages.
• **The Danger of Isolation:** While false friends are harmful, the poem cautions against becoming entirely cynical or isolated, emphasizing the precious nature of finding genuine companionship.
• **Defining Trust:** Trust is framed as a fragile, invaluable asset that must be earned over time through consistent action rather than granted freely based on sweet words.
• **The Ethics of Association:** The text prompts young readers to evaluate their own peer groups, encouraging them to surround themselves with positive, honest, and supportive influences.
• **Empathy Development:** By exploring the pain of betrayal, students develop greater empathy for those who have experienced hardship, recognizing the importance of being reliable friends.
• **Self-Reflection Exercise:** The poem acts as an internal mirror, prompting students to ask themselves: "Am I a true friend to others, or do I exhibit traits of a fair-weather companion?"
• **The Myth of Popularity:** In the context of modern school life, the poem warns against chasing superficial popularity and large circles of acquaintances over deep, meaningful friendships.
• **Handling Disappointment:** Students are taught philosophical maturity—understanding that people changing or leaving when times get tough reveals *their* character, not the victim's worth.
• **The Value of Solitude:** Better to be alone than in bad company; the poem validates the peace that comes from shedding toxic relationships, even if it brings temporary loneliness.
• **Communication in Friendship:** True friends communicate with radical honesty and respect, whereas flatterers communicate with calculated manipulation and constant agreement.
• **Moral Courage:** It takes moral courage to reject flattery and accept constructive criticism; the poem encourages students to cultivate this inner strength.
• **Long-Term Perspective:** The chapter teaches students to look at the long-term arc of relationships rather than short-term gains, social clout, or material benefits.
• **Holistic Personality Growth:** Aligned with DAV ethos, this psychological exploration fosters emotional intelligence (*EQ*), self-awareness, and sound moral judgment.

• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 1:** The poem commences by alerting readers to the omnipresence of deceitful individuals who wear the mask of friendship while seeking only self-enrichment.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 2:** During days of financial ease and social triumph, these flatterers surround their host, offering lavish praise, constant agreement, and enthusiastic companionship.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 3:** The speaker notes that these individuals are driven entirely by greed, consuming the host's wealth like parasites while providing no emotional sustenance.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 4:** The pivotal moment arrives when fortune turns; poverty and adversity strike the individual, stripping away all material comfort and social standing.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 5:** Instantly, the flock of flatterers vanishes without a trace, proving that their loyalty was tethered solely to bank accounts and social status rather than the person.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 6:** Left alone in penury and sorrow, the speaker experiences the bitter shock of abandonment, realizing the emptiness of human praise.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 7:** The poem then introduces the antithesis of the flatterer—the true friend who appears precisely when all others have fled in cowardice.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 8:** This true friend shares in grief, offers unconditional emotional and practical support, and demonstrates loyalty through enduring, quiet action.
• **Comprehensive Poetic Summary - Part 9:** The concluding philosophical stance reinforces that true friends are rare gems discovered only through the testing fires of hardship and need.
• **Structural Flow:** The poem's progression from warning to observation, crisis to realization, and betrayal to discovery creates a complete narrative arc within a short lyrical format.
• **Thematic Resolution:** The resolution brings emotional catharsis, transforming bitterness into wisdom and teaching the reader to cherish loyalty above all superficial praise.
• **Poetic Economy:** Shakespeare achieves immense psychological depth using minimal words, proving his mastery over rhythm, metaphor, and concise human observation.
• **Classroom Pedagogy Tip:** Teachers often ask students to map the poem's narrative flow into a timeline: Prosperity -> Flatterers Present -> Adversity Strikes -> Flatterers Flee -> True Friend Arrives.
• **Examinable Facts:** DAV exams frequently test students on the specific metaphors used for flatterers versus true friends, requiring precise identification of seasonal imagery.
• **Final Takeaway:** The ultimate summary of the chapter is encapsulated in the eternal truth: "Flattery is cheap and common, but true friendship is priceless and rare."
