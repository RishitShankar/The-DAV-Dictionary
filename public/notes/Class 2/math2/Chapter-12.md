• **Length**: The measurement of how long an object is from one end to the other.
• **Dimension**: Length is a one-dimensional measurement used to determine the size of an object.
• **Distance**: The total space between two points or two locations.
• **Height**: The measurement of an object from its base to its top, often referred to as vertical length.
• **Width**: The measurement of an object from side to side, often referred to as breadth.
• **Non-Standard Units**: Tools used for measurement before the invention of standardized systems, based on body parts.
• **Handspan**: The distance measured from the tip of the thumb to the tip of the little finger when the hand is fully stretched.
• **Footspan**: The length of a person's foot, often used to measure the length of a floor or a room.
• **Cubit**: The distance measured from the tip of the middle finger to the elbow.
• **Pace**: The distance covered in one single step while walking.
• **Digit**: The width of a single finger, used for measuring very small objects.
• **Arm Span**: The distance from the tip of the middle finger of one hand to the tip of the middle finger of the other hand when arms are stretched wide.
• **Limitation of Non-Standard Units**: These units vary from person to person because every individual has different body sizes.
• **Subjectivity**: Because body parts differ in size, non-standard measurements are not accurate or reliable for scientific purposes.
• **Need for Standardization**: To ensure consistency in trade, construction, and science, universal units of measurement were developed.

• **Standard Units**: Fixed units of measurement that remain the same regardless of who is measuring.
• **Centimetre (cm)**: The standard unit used to measure the length of small objects like pencils, erasers, and notebooks.
• **Metre (m)**: The standard unit used to measure the length of larger objects like cloth, tables, or the length of a room.
• **Kilometre (km)**: The standard unit used to measure very long distances, such as the distance between two cities or schools.
• **Abbreviation**: Centimetre is written as 'cm', Metre as 'm', and Kilometre as 'km'.
• **Ruler**: A common tool used to measure short lengths in centimetres.
• **Measuring Tape**: A flexible tool used by tailors to measure body dimensions and by carpenters to measure furniture.
• **Metre Scale**: A rigid rod exactly one metre long, used in shops to measure fabric.
• **Relationship between Units**: 1 metre is equal to 100 centimetres (1 m = 100 cm).
• **Relationship between Units**: 1 kilometre is equal to 1000 metres (1 km = 1000 m).
• **Measurement Precision**: Always align the '0' mark of the ruler with the starting edge of the object being measured.
• **Reading the Scale**: Look directly down at the ruler markings to avoid parallax error when reading the length.

• **Largest Unit**: The kilometre is the largest unit of length in the primary mathematics curriculum.
