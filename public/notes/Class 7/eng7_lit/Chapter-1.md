• **Chapter Title and Context**: **"Monkey Trouble"** is the opening chapter of the Class 7 English Literature textbook prescribed by the DAV College Managing Committee (DAVCMC) for Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public Schools.
• **Author Profile**: The chapter is authored by **Ruskin Bond**, a renowned Indian author of British descent celebrated for his prolific writing in children's literature, short stories, and novellas set in the foothills of the Himalayas.
• **Core Theme**: The chapter explores the complex, often chaotic, yet affectionate relationship between humans and animals, specifically highlighting the misadventures of keeping a mischievous primate as a pet.
• **Setting**: The narrative is primarily set in a quaint, quiet hill station in northern India, reflecting Ruskin Bond's signature atmospheric descriptions of flora, fauna, and small-town life.
• **Genre**: It falls under the genre of **humorous and realistic fiction**, drawing heavily from the author’s childhood observations and interactions with nature and wildlife.
• **Narrative Perspective**: The story is written in the **first-person narrative point of view**, allowing readers to experience the chaotic events directly through the eyes of the young protagonist and his family.
• **Central Character - Tutu**: The central animal character is **Tutu**, a female baby monkey whose name translates to a cute, affectionate moniker, masking her extraordinary capacity for creating trouble.
• **Acquisition of Tutu**: Tutu enters the household when the narrator's **Grandfather** purchases her from a roadside street entertainer (madari) for a sum of ten rupees to rescue her from potential neglect and mistreatment.
• **Grandfather's Animal Affection**: **Grandfather** is portrayed as an eccentric, compassionate animal lover who frequently brings home diverse, exotic, and often troublesome creatures, much to the dismay of other family members.
• **Grandmother's Reaction**: **Grandmother** represents the voice of domestic order, pragmatism, and traditional housekeeping, consistently opposing the arrival of wild animals inside the domestic household.
• **Aunt Ruby's Character**: **Aunt Ruby** is depicted as a stern, fastidious, and easily irritable member of the household who is excessively concerned with her social standing, appearance, and maintaining strict decorum.
• **The Narrator**: The young **narrator** acts as a silent observer and occasional accomplice to Tutu's various exploits, capturing the humor and disbelief of living alongside an untamed creature.
• **Initial Impressions**: Initially, Tutu appears small, harmless, and endearing, wearing a cheerful red-checked waistcoat, which quickly proves to be a deceptive disguise for her notorious mischief.
• **First Signs of Trouble**: Tutu’s destructive tendencies begin subtly by shredding curtains, tearing up cushions, and examining household objects with alarming curiosity and destructive force.
• **Primate Intelligence**: Ruskin Bond cleverly emphasizes the high level of **primate intelligence and adaptability**, showing how Tutu learns to open locked doors, untie knots, and outsmart human attempts to contain her.
• **The Concept of Domestic Menagerie**: The household functions as an informal **private zoo**, highlighting the challenges of integrating wild fauna into human domestic architecture and social routines.

• **Escalation of Mischief**: As Tutu settles into the household, her daily routines evolve from minor nuisances into carefully orchestrated acts of household disruption and public embarrassment.
• **Aunt Ruby's Vulnerability**: Aunt Ruby becomes Tutu's primary target for harassment, as the monkey instinctively recognizes her rigidity and nervousness, exploiting them for comedic effect.
• **The Wardrobe Invasion**: Tutu frequently sneaks into Aunt Ruby’s impeccably organized bedroom, using the wardrobe as a personal playground and disturbing neatly folded garments.
• **Destruction of Personal Property**: Tutu takes a particular liking to tearing up Aunt Ruby's expensive lace handkerchiefs, costly cosmetics, and decorative items left unattended on dressing tables.
• **The Red-Checked Waistcoat**: Tutu’s signature **red-checked waistcoat**, initially meant to make her look civilized, becomes a visual symbol of her rebellious and untamable wild nature.
• **Climbing Proclivities**: True to her species, Tutu treats every piece of furniture, ceiling fan, and curtain rod as a vertical climbing track, defying gravity and human commands with ease.
• **Food-Stealing Tactics**: Tutu exhibits exceptional stealth in raiding the kitchen, snatching bananas, mangoes, and biscuits, and distributing half-eaten pieces over unsuspecting household members.
• **The Element of Surprise**: The narrative maintains a rapid comedic pace by utilizing the **element of surprise**, wherein peaceful domestic moments are abruptly shattered by Tutu's sudden appearances.
• **Grandfather's Indulgence**: Despite the mounting complaints from Grandmother and Aunt Ruby, Grandfather maintains an attitude of amused tolerance, refusing to punish Tutu severely.
• **Grandmother's Ultimatums**: Grandmother regularly issues strict ultimatums demanding that Tutu be given away to the local zoo or returned to the street entertainer, citing domestic safety concerns.
• **The Social Embarrassment**: The central conflict intensifies when Tutu's antics begin to affect the family's social interactions, particularly involving Aunt Ruby’s impending marriage preparations.
• **Introduction of the Suitor**: **Aunt Ruby's suitor, Ramesh**, is introduced as a pompous, overly formal young man who is desperate to make a favorable impression on the family.
• **The Engagement Context**: The household is undergoing hectic preparations for a formal tea party where Ramesh is formally visiting to solidify the matrimonial alliance with Aunt Ruby.
• **Aunt Ruby's Anxiety**: Aunt Ruby spends hours grooming, dressing in her finest silk sari, and instructing everyone in the house to behave with utmost decorum to impress Ramesh.
• **The Calm Before the Storm**: The narrative builds dramatic tension by contrasting Aunt Ruby's rigid obsession with perfection against the volatile, unpredictable presence of Tutu locked in the veranda.
• **Primate Curiosity vs. Human Protocol**: The upcoming tea party serves as a classic literary setup where **human social pretension** directly collides with **raw, unbridled animal instinct**.

• **The Tea Party Setup**: The formal tea party takes place in the central garden-facing veranda, complete with fine bone china tea sets, silver cutlery, and elaborate trays of expensive snacks.
• **Ramesh's Arrival**: Ramesh arrives dressed impeccably in a Western suit, carrying a bouquet of flowers, meticulously projecting an image of sophistication, refinement, and upper-class poise.
• **Tutu's Escape**: Amidst the polite conversation and formal introductions, Tutu manages to slip her bonds or pick the latch of her enclosure, entering the tea party undetected.
• **First Sight of the Feast**: Tutu spots the lavish display of food on the tea table, instantly calculating the strategic advantage of raiding the multi-tiered cake stand and fruit baskets.
• **The Silent Ambush**: Moving with acrobatic stealth, Tutu climbs up the veranda pillars and positions herself directly above the table where Ramesh and Aunt Ruby are seated.
• **The Initial Raid**: Tutu launches her attack by snatching a large, juicy mango right from the fruit bowl, startling Ramesh and causing him to spill his hot tea.
• **Aunt Ruby's Mortification**: Aunt Ruby experiences absolute horror and public humiliation as her worst nightmare—being made a laughingstock in front of her future husband—begins to unfold.
• **Escalation on the Table**: Instead of retreating, Tutu descends directly onto the tea table, scattering delicate cups, saucers, and silverware with sweeping motions of her hands and feet.
• **The Targeted Assault on Ramesh**: Tutu singles out Ramesh for special attention, finding his elaborate hairstyle and stiff posture an irresistible invitation for playful torment.
• **Pulls and Tugs**: With a swift, agile leap, Tutu lands on Ramesh's shoulder, grabbing a fistful of his meticulously gelled hair and giving it a sharp, painful yank.
• **Ramesh's Loss of Composure**: The pompous suitor instantly loses his dignified facade, shrieking in shock, flailing his arms wildly, and tumbling backward off his wicker chair.
• **Domestic Chaos**: The entire tea party descends into absolute pandemonium, with family members shouting, Grandmother scolding, and Grandfather struggling to suppress his laughter.
• **Tutu's Triumph**: Tutu revels in the chaos, snatching a sugar cube from the spilled bowl and scampering up the nearest neem tree to consume her loot in safety.
• **The Aftermath of the Party**: The formal gathering is permanently ruined, with tea spilled across expensive tablecloths, broken crockery littering the floor, and Ramesh nursing a bruised ego.
• **Aunt Ruby's Breakdown**: Aunt Ruby bursts into tears of rage and absolute despair, declaring that her life is ruined and demanding immediate, permanent banishment of the monkey.
• **The Breaking Point for Grandmother**: Grandmother officially puts her foot down, issuing an ultimatum that leaves Grandfather with no legal or domestic loophole to keep Tutu any longer.

• **Decision to Relocate**: Recognizing that Tutu's presence has become a permanent threat to domestic peace and family relationships, Grandfather reluctantly agrees to give her away.
• **The Search for a New Home**: Grandfather embarks on a mission to find a suitable, secure environment where Tutu can live without destroying human property or causing social disasters.
• **The Ticket Collector Incident**: A major turning point in Tutu's eventual departure involves an earlier, hilarious episode featuring a **railway ticket collector** and Grandfather's traveling menagerie.
• **Grandfather's Travel Habits**: Grandfather frequently travels by train across Indian railways, often taking his favorite exotic pets along with him in canvas bags or baskets.
• **The Canvas Bag Transport**: On one particular journey to Dehra Dun, Tutu is securely packed inside a large canvas traveling bag to prevent her from escaping inside the passenger compartment.
• **Curiosity Unleashed**: True to her nature, Tutu spends the journey chewing through the canvas fibers, creating a small ventilation hole through which she peeps out with bright, inquisitive eyes.
• **The Railway Station Encounter**: At a scheduled stop, the strict **ticket collector** boards the carriage to inspect tickets, engaging in a formal, authoritarian dialogue with Grandfather.
• **Tutu's Unexpected Appearance**: Just as Grandfather produces his ticket, Tutu pokes her head completely out of the canvas bag, flashing a wide, mischievous grin at the startled ticket collector.
• **The Ticket Collector's Shock**: The railway official, accustomed to dealing with human passengers, is utterly horrified to find an unauthorized primate traveling without a valid passenger ticket.
• **Bureaucratic Confrontation**: A comical debate ensues regarding whether a monkey constitutes a "dog" or a "pet" under railway regulations, and what fare category should apply to her.
• **Classification Dilemma**: The ticket collector insists that Tutu must be classified as a **dog** for ticketing purposes, forcing Grandfather into an amusing argument over taxonomic definitions.
• **Grandfather's Defense**: Grandfather stubbornly argues that Tutu is neither a dog nor a quadraped in the traditional sense, refusing to pay the extra three rupees demanded by the official.
• **Escalation of the Railway Fee**: To spite Grandfather, the ticket collector aggressively pulls out his rulebook and classifies Tutu as a **tortoise** or alternative small animal, levying a fine.
• **Retaliation by Tutu**: Enraged by the ticket collector's stern tone, Tutu reaches out of the bag and snatches the official’s official cap, tossing it onto the railway tracks below.
• **Public Spectacle**: The platform erupts in laughter as the flustered ticket collector scrambles to retrieve his cap, while the train blows its whistle and begins to pull out of the station.
• **Resolution of the Railway Episode**: Grandfather ultimately pays a nominal fine to preserve peace, but the incident cements Tutu's reputation as a financial and logistical liability.
----PAGE-BREAK---
• **The Return to the Street Entertainer**: With the tea party disaster acting as the final straw, Grandfather decides to return Tutu to her original owner, the **street entertainer (madari)**.
• **Locating the Madari**: Grandfather tracks down the traveling entertainer near the local bazaar where he originally purchased Tutu for the meager sum of ten rupees.
• **The Reunion Scene**: When the street entertainer spots Tutu, his face lights up with genuine recognition, proving that despite her mischievous nature, she holds a familiar place in his heart.
• **Tutu's Reaction to the Entertainer**: Surprisingly, Tutu does not show fear; instead, she instantly recognizes the familiar beat of the entertainer's small drum and chatters excitedly.
• **Financial Transaction in Reverse**: Grandfather happily returns Tutu to the madari, refusing to take back the original ten rupees, effectively treating it as a donation for her upkeep.
• **Farewell to Tutu**: The narrator and Grandfather bid a bittersweet farewell to the little monkey, acknowledging that while she brought chaos, she also filled the house with unstoppable energy.
• **Restoration of Domestic Order**: Following Tutu's departure, the household returns to its quiet, predictable routine, much to the immense relief of Grandmother and Aunt Ruby.
• **Aunt Ruby's Recovery**: Aunt Ruby manages to salvage her relationship with Ramesh, apologizing for the "wild animal attack" and successfully proceeding with her wedding plans.
• **Grandmother's Triumph**: Grandmother enjoys uninterrupted peace in her clean, undamaged drawing room, free from the constant anxiety of flying cushions and shredded curtains.
• **Grandfather's Philosophy**: Grandfather reflects philosophically on the nature of wild animals, concluding that while humans domesticate pets for companionship, wild creatures ultimately belong in their natural habitats.
• **Literary Significance of the Ending**: The resolution reinforces a core moral of DAV literature chapters: **respecting wildlife boundaries** and understanding that wild animals do not make ideal domestic pets.
• **Ruskin Bond's Style in the Chapter**: The story exemplifies Bond's trademark ability to blend gentle humor, realistic human reactions, and affectionate descriptions of nature into an engaging narrative.
• **Character Arc of Tutu**: Tutu’s character arc remains dynamic yet consistent—she is an agent of chaos who resists all forms of human civilization, clothing, and domestic rules.
• **Character Arc of Grandfather**: Grandfather's unwavering affection for animals highlights his gentle, eccentric personality, serving as a foil to Grandmother's practical domesticity.
• **Thematic Exploration of Freedom**: The chapter subtly touches upon the theme of **freedom**, demonstrating how restraining a wild animal in human garments and indoor spaces inevitably leads to rebellion.
• **Enduring Appeal**: "Monkey Trouble" remains a timeless favorite in the DAV curriculum because it bridges moral education with lighthearted, relatable childhood comedy.

• **Detailed Character Analysis - Grandfather**: Grandfather is portrayed as a retired government official whose pension is entirely dedicated to supporting his eccentric passion for collecting animals.
• **Grandfather's Menagerie**: His collection extends beyond Tutu to include tortoises, a pet squirrel, rabbits, a tame python in the bath, and occasional stray dogs, creating a lively home ecosystem.
• **Grandfather's Empathy**: His motivation for buying Tutu from the street entertainer stems from genuine compassion, as he notices the animal shivering in the cold wearing an ill-fitting waistcoat.
• **Detailed Character Analysis - Grandmother**: Grandmother serves as the practical anchor of the household, constantly fighting a losing battle against dirt, destruction, and botanical or zoological intrusions.
• **Grandmother's Pragmatism**: Unlike Grandfather, who sees emotional value in every creature, Grandmother evaluates pets based on their cleanliness, utility, and potential damage to household assets.
• **Detailed Character Analysis - Aunt Ruby**: Aunt Ruby represents the rising middle-class aspiration for social respectability, fashion consciousness, and strict adherence to societal norms in colonial or post-colonial India.
• **Aunt Ruby's Vulnerability to Comedy**: Her excessive pride and fear of embarrassment make her the perfect comedic victim for Tutu's instinctive, uninhibited assaults.
• **Detailed Character Analysis - The Narrator**: The young narrator functions as a sympathetic witness, capturing the humor of the conflicts without taking sides, thereby guiding the reader's emotional response.
• **Setting Analysis - The Hill Station**: The unnamed hill station provides a picturesque backdrop, with colonial-style bungalows, veranda tea settings, neem trees, and bustling local bazaars.
• **Atmospheric Details**: Ruskin Bond uses sensory details—the scent of pine trees, the rustle of dry leaves, the sound of train whistles, and the chatter of monkeys—to ground the story in authentic reality.
• **The Motif of Clothing**: The **red-checked waistcoat** acts as a brilliant literary motif, symbolizing the artificial attempt by humans to civilize nature, which the animal inevitably rips apart.
• **The Motif of Food**: Food items—mangoes, bananas, wedding cakes, and tea biscuits—serve as catalysts for conflict, triggering Tutu's predatory instincts and disrupting human social rituals.
• **Humor Through Exaggeration**: Bond employs hyperbole and slapstick comedy, such as Ramesh tumbling off his chair and the ticket collector losing his cap, to heighten the entertaining value of the tale.
• **Irony in the Narrative**: The central irony lies in the fact that Tutu is purchased to be "rescued" from a street entertainer, only for her to wreak far more calculated havoc inside a respectable middle-class home.
• **Pacing and Structure**: The plot moves briskly through distinct episodes—acquisition, household destruction, the train journey, the tea party disaster, and final repatriation—ensuring sustained reader interest.
• **Educational Value for Class 7 Students**: The chapter encourages young learners to observe animal behavior with empathy while understanding the practical limitations and responsibilities of pet ownership.

• **Vocabulary Word - Menagerie**: **Menagerie (Noun)**: A collection of wild animals kept in captivity for exhibition or, in Grandfather's case, rescued and housed domestically as pets.
• **Vocabulary Word - Primate**: **Primate (Noun)**: A mammalian order that includes monkeys, apes, and humans, characterized by high intelligence, flexible hands, and complex social behaviors.
• **Vocabulary Word - Pandemonium**: **Pandemonium (Noun)**: Wild and noisy disorder or confusion; a situation that perfectly describes the state of the household during Tutu's tea party raid.
• **Vocabulary Word - Pompous**: **Pompous (Adjective)**: Affected and irritatingly grand, solemn, or self-important—a trait embodied by Aunt Ruby's unfortunate suitor, Ramesh.
• **Vocabulary Word - Repatriation**: **Repatriation (Noun)**: The act of returning someone or something to their country or place of origin, mirrored in Tutu's return to her original street entertainer.
• **Vocabulary Word - Fastidious**: **Fastidious (Adjective)**: Very attentive to and concerned about accuracy, cleanliness, and detail, perfectly describing Aunt Ruby’s housekeeping standards.
• **Vocabulary Word - Acrobatic**: **Acronym/Adjective**: Characterized by gymnastic ability and agility, explaining Tutu's effortless climbing across veranda pillars and neem trees.
• **Vocabulary Word - Ulterior**: **Ulterior (Adjective)**: Existing beyond what is obvious or admitted; though Tutu's motives seem simple mischief, her planning often feels calculated.
• **Vocabulary Word - Ultimatum**: **Ultimatum (Noun)**: A final demand or statement of terms, rejected or accepted under threat of penalty, as issued by Grandmother to Grandfather regarding Tutu.
• **Vocabulary Word - Comedic Relief**: **Comedic Relief (Literary Term)**: The inclusion of a humorous character or scene to relieve emotional tension, beautifully executed through Tutu's antics amid tense family preparations.
• **Grammatical Concept - First-Person Narration**: The chapter provides an excellent practical example for students to study **first-person pronouns** (*I, me, my, we*) and subjective storytelling perspectives.
• **Grammatical Concept - Descriptive Adjectives**: Ruskin Bond’s rich usage of adjectives (*fastidious, pompous, acrobatic, mischievous*) teaches students how to enhance creative writing descriptions.
• **Grammatical Concept - Action Verbs**: The text is packed with dynamic action verbs (*shredded, snatched, tumbled, scampered, yanked*) that give the narrative its fast, energetic rhythm.
• **Comprehension Skill - Cause and Effect**: Students learn to trace direct cause-and-effect chains, such as Grandfather buying Tutu (cause) leading to Grandmother's anger and eventual household chaos (effect).
• **Critical Thinking Concept - Anthropomorphism**: The story subtly critiques **anthropomorphism**—the attribution of human traits to animals—showing that wild animals operate on instinct rather than human social etiquette.
• **DAV Curriculum Objective**: Fulfills the DAV requirement of exposing students to classic Indian English literature that promotes environmental awareness, animal empathy, and moral introspection.

• **Thematic Deep Dive - Human vs. Nature Conflict**: "Monkey Trouble" allegorically represents the broader ecological conflict between human civilization (represented by the brick bungalow, formal tea sets, and rigid rules) and wild nature (represented by Tutu).
• **The Illusion of Control**: Grandfather and Aunt Ruby operate under the false illusion that nature can be dressed up in a red-checked waistcoat and taught polite manners, which Tutu repeatedly shatters.
• **Generational Dynamics**: The family dynamic displays a generational divide, where Grandfather represents indulgent, progressive affection, Grandmother represents conservative traditional order, and the narrator represents neutral observation.
• **The Role of Animals in Bond's Universe**: In Ruskin Bond’s literary universe, animals are never mere props; they possess distinct personalities, emotional capacities, and an inherent right to freedom that humans frequently violate.
• **Satire on Social Pretension**: Bond uses Ramesh and Aunt Ruby to gently satirize middle-class social anxiety, showing how adults become overly concerned with superficial appearances during marriage proposals.
• **The Comic Climax Analysis**: The climax at the tea party is structurally designed as a **farce**, featuring rapid physical comedy, shattered social norms, and public embarrassment without causing permanent physical harm.
• **Symbolism of the Neem Tree**: The neem tree in the courtyard acts as Tutu’s safe haven and a boundary line between the domesticated human world and the wild canopy of nature.
• **Comparative Analysis with Other Pets**: While Grandfather’s other pets (like tortoises and rabbits) remain quiet and unobtrusive, Tutu’s high-energy primate nature makes her uniquely unsuited for indoor apartment-style living.
• **Psychological Impact on Tutu**: Though Bond depicts Tutu as mischievous, readers must infer the underlying stress a wild baby monkey experiences when separated from its troop and confined indoors.
• **The Ethics of Animal Trade**: By showing how Tutu is bought from a street entertainer and later returned, the chapter subtly prompts young readers to question the ethics of capturing wild animals for entertainment.
• **Narrative Tone Analysis**: The tone throughout the chapter remains light, affectionate, humorous, and devoid of bitterness, making heavy themes palatable for young adolescent readers.
• **The Art of Short Story Writing**: The chapter serves as a textbook example of how to structure a short story with a clear exposition, rising action, comic climax, and satisfying resolution.
• **Linguistic Simplicity**: Bond’s prose is characterized by **deceptive simplicity**, using simple sentence structures to convey complex emotional and comedic dynamics.
• **Regional Setting Realism**: The details of Indian hill station life—railway journeys, bazaars, tea parties, and family structures—offer students a culturally authentic literary experience.
• **Reinforcement of DAV Values**: Aligns with DAV's core educational philosophy of fostering compassion for all living beings (*Jiva Daya*) while maintaining practical wisdom and social harmony.
