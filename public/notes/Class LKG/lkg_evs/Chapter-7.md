• **Chapter Introduction:** Welcome to Chapter 7 of Class LKG Environmental Studies (EVS) for The DAV Dictionary, focusing on "Seasons, Sun & Rainy Days".
• **Core Subject Focus:** This chapter introduces young learners to fundamental meteorological concepts, natural phenomena, and environmental changes experienced in daily life.
• **Target Audience Adaptation:** Concepts are simplified for LKG students using relatable sensory experiences, visual descriptions, and foundational vocabulary words.
• **DAV Curriculum Alignment:** Designed in strict compliance with the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board pedagogical standards for early childhood education.
• **Holistic Environmental Awareness:** Encourages children to observe their immediate surroundings, weather patterns, and the celestial body that drives Earth's climate.
• **Sensory Learning Approach:** Emphasizes how children feel warmth, see bright light, experience wetness, and perceive temperature variations in nature.
• **Primary Natural Elements:** Explores three core pillars of the LKG weather curriculum: seasonal changes, the Sun as a primary heat source, and rainy days.
• **Observational Skills Development:** Trains young minds to look out windows, notice sky conditions, and describe whether it is sunny, cloudy, or raining.
• **Vocabulary Building:** Introduces foundational terms like **Sun**, **Rain**, **Hot**, **Cold**, **Sky**, **Clouds**, **Umbrella**, and **Season**.
• **Foundation for Science:** Builds the primary building blocks for future scientific inquiry into Earth sciences, geography, and physical science.
• **Daily Life Connection:** Connects classroom learning directly to children's daily routines, including clothing choices, outdoor play, and food habits.
• **Visual Literacy:** Encourages recognition of pictorial representations of weather symbols commonly used in early childhood educational materials.
• **Interactive Engagement Concept:** Promotes guided discussions between teachers and students regarding what they wear and eat during different weather states.
• **Safety and Care:** Introduces basic concepts of protecting oneself from extreme weather, such as staying out of harsh midday sun or keeping dry in the rain.
• **Appreciation of Nature:** Fosters an early sense of wonder and appreciation for the natural world and the changing cycles of the environment.

• **Definition of Weather:** **Weather** is defined as the daily condition of the air and sky around us, changing from morning to afternoon and day to day.
• **The Concept of Change:** LKG learners discover that the sky does not always look the same; it can be bright and clear, covered in gray clouds, or pouring rain.
• **Introduction to Seasons:** A **Season** is a longer period of the year characterized by a specific pattern of weather conditions, temperature, and environment.
• **The Four Major Seasons Overview:** DAV curriculum introduces young children to the primary seasonal cycle: Summer, Monsoon (Rainy), Autumn, and Winter.
• **Summer Season Characteristics:** **Summer** is identified as the hot season where the Sun shines brightly for long hours, making everyone feel warm.
• **Winter Season Characteristics:** **Winter** is introduced as the cold season where chilly winds blow, requiring warm woolly clothes to stay cozy.
• **Monsoon Season Characteristics:** The **Monsoon** or rainy season brings dark clouds, heavy downpours, and refreshing wetness to the thirsty earth.
• **Spring and Autumn Transition:** Brief mentions of transitional seasons where leaves change color or flowers bloom, refreshing the natural landscape.
• **Personal Experience Mapping:** Students learn to connect their personal physical comfort—feeling hot, cold, or wet—to the prevailing seasonal weather condition.
• **Clothing Adaptation by Season:** Introduction to how clothing changes across seasons, wearing light cottons in summer and heavy woolens in winter.
• **Food and Drink Association:** Linking seasons to food choices, such as enjoying cold water and ice cream in summer versus hot soups in winter.
• **Nature's Wardrobe:** Describing trees and plants shedding leaves in autumn and blooming with vibrant flowers during the spring season.
• **Animal Behavior in Seasons:** Simple observations of how some animals seek shade in summer or thick coats in winter to survive environmental shifts.
• **Outdoor Activity Adjustments:** Understanding that outdoor play spaces and playground equipment feel different depending on whether the sun is blazing or rain has fallen.
• **Language Development:** Equipping children with descriptive adjectives like *hot*, *cold*, *wet*, *dry*, *bright*, and *dark* to express weather states accurately.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **The Sun as a Celestial Body:** The **Sun** is introduced as a giant, glowing ball of fire located far away in the sky that provides light and heat to Earth.
• **Daytime Illumination:** The Sun is responsible for turning night into day, illuminating our world so we can see, work, and play safely.
• **Primary Source of Heat:** The Sun acts as the ultimate natural heater of our planet, warming up the land, oceans, and air around us.
• **Sunrise Phenomenon:** **Sunrise** marks the beginning of the day when the Sun slowly appears on the eastern horizon, bringing morning light.
• **Sunset Phenomenon:** **Sunset** marks the end of the daytime when the Sun dips below the western horizon, leading into evening and nighttime.
• **Shadow Formation:** The Sun helps create **shadows** when an opaque object blocks its light rays, creating dark silhouettes on the ground.
• **Plant Growth Dependency:** LKG students learn that green plants need sunlight to make their food, grow tall, and produce flowers and fruits.
• **Vitamin D and Health:** The gentle morning sunlight provides essential warmth and vitality, helping humans and animals maintain health and energy.
• **Visualizing the Sun:** Characterized in early learning materials as a smiling, bright yellow circle with radiating lines representing warm beams of light.
• **Sun Safety Rules:** Young learners are taught never to stare directly at the bright Sun with naked eyes to protect their delicate vision.
• **Sunscreen and Protection:** Introduction to using hats, sunglasses, and light protective clothing when playing outdoors under strong afternoon sunbeams.
• **Daytime vs. Nighttime Sky:** Contrasting the bright, sunny daytime sky with the dark, moon-and-star-filled nighttime sky for fundamental spatial awareness.
• **Energy and Mood:** Observing how sunny days make people feel energetic, happy, and eager to participate in outdoor games and activities.
• **Evaporation Concept (Simplified):** The Sun's heat dries up puddles on the street after a rainstorm, introducing the basic concept of water drying up.
• **Celestial Neighbor Recognition:** Understanding that the Sun is the closest star to our planet Earth, giving us both day and warmth.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Introduction to Rainy Days:** A **Rainy Day** is characterized by overcast skies, falling water droplets, damp air, and a refreshing coolness in the atmosphere.
• **Formation of Rain (Simplified):** Water goes up into the sky, collects inside fluffy clouds, and when they become too heavy, water falls down as rain drops.
• **Cloud Observation:** **Clouds** are described as soft, fluffy, white or gray masses floating high in the sky that hold moisture and signal weather changes.
• **Rainy Sky Characteristics:** On rainy days, the bright Sun hides behind thick, dark gray storm clouds, making the sky look dim and moody.
• **Sound of Rain:** Introduction to auditory sensory awareness through the rhythmic pitter-patter sound of raindrops hitting roofs, windows, and leaves.
• **Puddle Formation:** Rainwater collects in low spots on the ground, forming delightful **puddles** of water that children love to watch or splash in.
• **Smell of Wet Earth:** The unique, earthy fragrance released when dry soil gets touched by fresh rainwater, often called *Petrichor* in simplified terms.
• **Plant and Tree Rejoicing:** Rain provides essential water for plants, trees, and grass, washing away dust and helping them grow green and lush.
• **Agricultural Importance:** Helping young learners understand that farmers depend on rain to water their crops so we can have fruits, vegetables, and grains.
• **River and Pond Replenishment:** Rain fills up rivers, lakes, ponds, and wells, ensuring that animals and humans have adequate drinking water supplies.
• **Indoor Activities on Rainy Days:** Exploring how children stay indoors during heavy downpours, engaging in quiet play, drawing, reading, or puzzle-solving.
• **Nature's Bath:** Observing how rain acts as a natural cleaner, washing cars, streets, trees, and rooftops clean of dust and debris.
• **Rainbow Sighting:** The magical appearance of a colorful **rainbow** across the sky when sunlight shines through lingering raindrops after a storm.
• **Temperature Drop:** Noticing how the temperature drops during and after a rain shower, bringing a pleasant, cool breeze after a hot day.
• **Sensory Experience Integration:** Encouraging children to describe the chill in the air, the sound of thunder, and the sight of falling water drops.

• **Rain Gear Introduction:** Special clothing and accessories designed specifically to keep us dry and comfortable during wet weather conditions.
• **The Umbrella:** An **Umbrella** is a portable canopy mounted on a folding metal pole, opened overhead to shield a person from falling raindrops.
• **Raincoat Utility:** A **Raincoat** is a waterproof outer garment worn over regular clothes to completely cover the torso and arms, keeping the body dry.
• **Gumboots (Rain Boots):** Special waterproof rubber boots worn on feet to step safely into muddy patches and water puddles without getting socks wet.
• **Waterproof Materials:** Introduction to shiny, plastic, or rubberized fabrics that do not let water pass through, protecting skin and clothing.
• **Bright Colors of Rain Gear:** Rain gear is often manufactured in vibrant, cheerful colors like yellow, red, and blue to ensure high visibility on dark rainy days.
• **Handling an Umbrella:** Teaching LKG students basic motor skills on how to hold an umbrella handle correctly and keep it tilted against the wind.
• **Drying Off Procedures:** Learning good hygiene habits of wiping wet feet, changing out of damp clothes, and using a towel after coming inside from the rain.
• **Paper Boats:** A classic traditional monsoon activity where children fold paper into little floating boats and set them sailing in rainwater streams.
• **Splash Safety:** Understanding the fun of splashing in puddles while also learning to be considerate not to splash muddy water onto others.
• **Keeping Books Safe:** Recognizing the importance of keeping school bags and notebooks safe inside waterproof covers or school bag rain shields.
• **Umbrella Etiquette:** Learning how to close and shake off an umbrella outside before entering a room to avoid making indoor floors wet and slippery.
• **Animal Rain Defenses:** Observing how birds tuck their heads under wings and stray animals seek shelter under roofs or trees when rain begins.
• **Hot Snacks Tradition:** Associating rainy days with cozy family moments enjoying warm snacks like roasted corn or hot milk indoors.
• **Wind and Rain Combinations:** Understanding that sometimes heavy winds accompany rain, making umbrellas hard to hold upright without adult help.

• **Summer Season Deep Dive:** Expanding on the summer season, noting how the Sun beats down fiercely and days are much longer than nights.
• **Summer Clothing:** Wearing loose, lightweight **cotton clothes** that absorb sweat and allow air to circulate around the body to keep cool.
• **Summer Hydration:** Emphasizing the importance of drinking plenty of clean water, fresh juices, and coconut water to prevent dehydration in the heat.
• **Summer Fruits:** Delicious seasonal treats like juicy watermelons, mangoes, and musk melons that help keep the body cool and refreshed.
• **Cooling Appliances:** Introduction to household devices like electric fans, coolers, and air conditioners that provide relief from indoor heat.
• **Sun Protection Gear:** Using sunglasses to protect eyes and wide-brimmed hats to shade the face during outdoor summer excursions.
• **Ice Cream and Cold Treats:** The universal childhood joy of eating ice cream and kulfi on a scorching hot summer afternoon.
• **Summer Vacation Concept:** Understanding that schools close for a summer break during the hottest weeks, allowing families to travel or rest indoors.
• **Tree Shade Benefits:** Seeking the cooling shade of large leafy trees when walking outside under the blazing midday sun.
• **Wildlife in Summer:** Observing birds panting or dipping in birdbaths, and street dogs resting lazily under parked cars to escape the heat.
• **Nighttime Cooling:** Noticing how evenings become slightly pleasant as the Sun sets, allowing families to sit on balconies or porches.
• **Dehydration Awareness:** Learning simple warning signs of feeling too hot, tired, or dizzy, and asking an adult for water immediately.
• **Swimming Activities:** The popular recreational sport of swimming in pools to beat the summer heat and stay physically active.
• **Cotton Plant Origin:** Connecting summer cotton clothes back to the soft white fluffy bolls of the cotton plant grown in fields.
• **Sunburn Prevention:** Basic awareness of protecting sensitive skin from turning red and painful under harsh ultraviolet sun rays.

• **Winter Season Deep Dive:** Exploring the winter season in detail, characterized by freezing temperatures, chilly winds, and early sunsets.
• **Winter Clothing:** Donning thick **woolen clothes** such as sweaters, mufflers, caps, gloves, and shawls to trap body heat and stay warm.
• **Wool Source:** Learning that wool comes from the fluffy fleece of sheep, which is processed and knitted into warm winter garments.
• **Hot Food and Beverages:** Enjoying piping hot soups, warm milk, tea, and freshly cooked comfort foods during chilly winter days.
• **Bedding and Blankets:** Transitioning to heavy quilts, blankets, and duvets at night to ensure a cozy, warm sleep during cold winter nights.
• **Bonfires and Heaters:** Utilizing room heaters or gathering around safe outdoor bonfires with family members to keep hands and feet warm.
• **Short Days and Long Nights:** Observing that the Sun rises late in the morning and sets much earlier in the evening during winter months.
• **Fog and Mist Formation:** **Fog** is introduced as thick, cloudy water vapor close to the ground that limits visibility on cold winter mornings.
• **Skin Care in Winter:** Understanding why skin feels dry and chapped in winter, requiring cold creams, lotions, and lip balms for protection.
• **Sunbathing Pleasure:** Sitting out in the warm midday winter sunshine to absorb gentle heat and enjoy the pleasant outdoor environment.
• **Hot Water Baths:** Using warm or lukewarm water for daily bathing to maintain personal hygiene without catching a cold.
• **Winter Flora and Fauna:** Observing deciduous trees completely bare of leaves and noticing how many birds migrate to warmer regions.
• **Hot Water Bottles:** Utilizing rubber hot water bags tucked under bedsheets to pre-warm sleeping spaces on exceptionally freezing nights.
• **Cold Prevention:** Learning to cover the head and ears with woolen caps to prevent catching common winter colds and earaches.
• **Winter Festivals:** Connecting the winter season with joyous cultural celebrations, festive decorations, and family gatherings.

• **Monsoon and Plant Kingdom:** Exploring how the rainy season breathes vibrant new life into flora, turning brown landscapes lush and green.
• **Seed Germination:** Rainwater awakens sleeping seeds buried in the soil, prompting them to sprout roots and push tiny green shoots upward.
• **Muddy Play Precautions:** Understanding why playing in deep mud requires supervision to avoid slips, falls, and stepping on sharp hidden objects.
• **Thunder and Lightning:** Introduction to majestic atmospheric phenomena where flashing electric sparks (**lightning**) are followed by booming **thunder**.
• **Safety During Thunderstorms:** Learning that when thunder roars, children must stay indoors away from tall trees, open windows, and electrical appliances.
• **Puddle Observations (Biology):** Noticing tiny insects, tadpoles, or earthworms emerging from the soil or swimming in temporary rainwater pools.
• **Frog Choruses:** Listening to the loud, cheerful croaking of frogs who love damp weather and gather in large numbers during the monsoon.
• **Umbrella Sharing Concept:** Teaching moral values of sharing an umbrella with a friend or sibling who got caught in an unexpected downpour.
• **Rainwater Harvesting (Basic):** Simple awareness that rainwater can be collected in buckets or tanks for watering plants and cleaning purposes.
• **Preventing Water Stagnation:** Understanding why collected puddles must not be left stagnant for too long around homes to prevent mosquito breeding.
• **Paper Boat Racing:** Participating in group play activities where children cheer for their paper boats floating down flowing rainwater channels.
• **Monsoon Hygiene:** Emphasizing clean habits of washing hands and feet thoroughly after coming indoors from muddy or wet outdoor environments.
• **Cloud Shapes Game:** Looking up at the sky during monsoon transition days and identifying animal or object shapes formed by passing clouds.
• **Wet Weather Footwear Care:** Learning to wash and dry rubber shoes or sandals properly after a trip outside in the rain.
• **The Petrichor Sensation:** Experiencing the delightful, earthy aroma that fills the air when raindrops fall upon dry, sun-baked earth.

• **Inter-Season Connections:** Reviewing how nature smoothly transitions from hot summers to rainy monsoons and cool winters in a continuous yearly circle.
• **The Calendar Year Cycle:** Understanding that there are twelve months in a year, during which all seasons visit in a predictable, repeating order.
• **Animals Adjusting to Weather:** Recapping how various animals adapt, such as birds building nests, bears hibernating, and dogs growing thick winter fur.
• **Human Adaptability:** Recognizing human ingenuity in inventing clothing, housing, and temperature control to live comfortably across all climates.
• **Plant Adaptability:** Observing how deciduous trees shed leaves to conserve water and energy during harsh seasons before blooming anew.
• **Weather Forecasting (Simplified):** Looking at daily weather reports on television or mobile devices with parents to check if rain or sun is expected.
• **Clothing Sorting Activity:** Classroom exercises where children sort pictures of clothes into summer, winter, and rainy categories.
• **Food Sorting Activity:** Matching seasonal foods like watermelons (summer) and hot soup (winter) to their correct weather conditions.
• **Storytelling Integration:** Reading classic stories and nursery rhymes related to rain ("Rain, Rain, Go Away") and sunshine to reinforce chapter concepts.
• **Art and Craft Integration:** Creating cotton-ball clouds, paper umbrellas, and yellow sun cutouts to build tactile memory of chapter themes.
• **Outdoor Observation Journal:** Encouraging parents and children to maintain a simple weekly weather log with stickers representing sun, cloud, or rain.
• **Respect for Nature:** Instilling an early understanding that weather patterns are natural, necessary, and vital for life on planet Earth.
• **Water Conservation Value:** Emphasizing that water from rain is precious, teaching children not to waste clean tap water needlessly.
• **Energy Conservation Value:** Turning off lights and cooling fans when not needed, connecting daily habits to weather and environmental care.
• **Chapter Summary Concept:** Bringing together all learnings about the Sun's warmth, rainy days, and seasonal clothing into a unified environmental view.

• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Sun:** The bright, hot star in the sky that gives us light and warmth during the daytime.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Rain:** Water drops that fall from the clouds in the sky down onto the earth.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Season:** A specific time of the year that has its own special weather conditions like summer or winter.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Summer:** The hot season of the year when we wear light cotton clothes and eat ice cream.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Winter:** The cold season of the year when we wear woolen sweaters and caps to stay warm.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Monsoon:** The rainy season when dark clouds bring heavy showers and cool breezes.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Umbrella:** A hand-held folding canopy used to protect oneself from rain or strong sunlight.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Raincoat:** A waterproof coat worn to keep the body dry during heavy rainstorms.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Puddle:** A small pool of liquid water, especially of rainwater on a street or ground.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Cloud:** A visible mass of condensed water vapor floating high up in the atmosphere.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Weather:** The day-to-day state of the atmosphere, whether hot, cold, sunny, cloudy, or rainy.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Cotton:** Soft white fiber harvested from plants, used to make cool summer garments.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Wool:** Warm textile fiber obtained from the fleece of sheep, used for winter clothing.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Thunder:** A loud rumbling or crashing sound heard in the sky following a flash of lightning.
• **DAV Dictionary Glossary - Rainbow:** A multi-colored curved arch appearing in the sky when sunlight shines through falling rain.
