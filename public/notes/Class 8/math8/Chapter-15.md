* [RED] **FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE 1: THE NATURE AND ORGANIZATION OF DATA**
  * **Data Definition:** Numerical facts, figures, or measurements collected for a specific analytical or administrative purpose.
  * **Raw Data:** Unorganized, unprocessed data points directly as recorded from an observation or survey (e.g., marks of 10 students: $45, 32, 67, 89, \dots$).
  * **Arrayed Data:** Raw data arranged in ascending or descending order of magnitude to facilitate quick visual inspection and range determination.
  * **Frequency ($f$):** The exact count of the number of times a specific observation or class interval repeats within a given dataset.

* [GOLD] **FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE 2: FREQUENCY DISTRIBUTION DYNAMICS**
  * **Ungrouped Frequency Distribution:** A tabular arrangement where individual, distinct values of variables are paired directly with their respective frequencies.
  * **Grouped Frequency Distribution:** A condensed tabular format where data points are aggregated into continuous or discontinuous ranges called **Class Intervals**.
  * **Exclusive Form:** Class intervals where the upper limit of one interval is the lower limit of the next (e.g., $10-20, 20-30$). The upper limit value is excluded from that interval.
  * **Inclusive Form:** Class intervals where both lower and upper limits are included within the same interval (e.g., $10-19, 20-29$). Requires conversion to exclusive form via adjustment factors ($\pm 0.5$) before continuous graphical plotting.

* [GREEN] **FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE 3: PROBABILITY THEORY & DETERMINISM**
  * **Random Experiment:** An action or trial whose outcome cannot be predicted with certainty prior to its execution, though all possible outcomes are known.
  * **Sample Space ($S$):** The exhaustive set of all possible, mutually exclusive outcomes resulting from a random experiment.
  * **Event ($E$):** A specific subset of the sample space representing a desired outcome or combination of outcomes.
  * **Equally Likely Outcomes:** Outcomes that possess the exact same theoretical probability of occurrence in a single trial of an unbiased experiment.

* [RED] **STATISTICAL FORMULAE: MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY**
  * **Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$) for Raw Data:**
    $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + \dots + x_n}{n}$$
    *(Where $\sum x_i$ is the algebraic sum of all observations, and $n$ is the total count of observations.)*
  * **Arithmetic Mean for Ungrouped Frequency Distributions ($x_i$ with frequencies $f_i$):**
    $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum (f_i \cdot x_i)}{\sum f_i} = \frac{f_1x_1 + f_2x_2 + \dots + f_nx_n}{f_1 + f_2 + \dots + f_n}$$

* [GOLD] **MEDIAN FORMULAE FOR UNGROUPED DATA**
  * **Case 1: When Total Number of Observations ($n$) is Odd**
    $$\text{Median} = \left(\frac{n + 1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ term}$$ *(Data must be arranged in ascending/descending order first).*
  * **Case 2: When Total Number of Observations ($n$) is Even**
    $$\text{Median} = \frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ term} + \left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ term}}{2}$$

* [GREEN] **PROBABILITY LAWS & EQUATIONS**
  * **Classical (Theoretical) Probability Formula:**
    $$P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of Favorable Outcomes to Event } E}{\text{Total Number of Possible Outcomes in Sample Space}} = \frac{n(E)}{n(S)}$$
  * **Law of Complementary Events:**
    $$P(E) + P(\text{not } E) = 1 \implies P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)$$
  * **Probability Bounds Theorem:** For any event $E$:
    $$0 \le P(E) \le 1$$
    *(Probability is strictly bounded between $0$ (impossible event) and $1$ (certain event).)*

* [RED] **TECHNICAL GLOSSARY — STATISTICS**
  * **Statistics:** The branch of mathematics concerned with the collection, organization, presentation, analysis, and interpretation of numerical data.
  * **Class Limit:** The extreme boundary values (Lower Class Limit and Upper Class Limit) defining a class interval.
  * **Class Size (Width):** The arithmetic difference between the upper class limit and the lower class limit of a given class interval ($\text{Upper Limit} - \text{Lower Limit}$).
  * **Class Mark (Mid-point):** The exact arithmetic average of the upper and lower limits of a class interval:
    $$\text{Class Mark} = \frac{\text{Lower Limit} + \text{Upper Limit}}{2}$$
  * **Histogram:** A graphical display of continuous grouped frequency data using adjacent rectangular bars where area is proportional to frequency.
  * **Frequency Polygon:** A line graph constructed by plotting the frequencies against the class marks and joining consecutive points with straight line segments.

* [GOLD] **TECHNICAL GLOSSARY — PROBABILITY**
  * **Probability:** A quantitative measure ranging from $0$ to $1$ expressing the mathematical likelihood that a specific event will occur.
  * **Trial:** A single performance of a random experiment.
  * **Elementary Event:** An event consisting of a single, indivisible outcome from the sample space.
  * **Sure Event (Certain Event):** An event whose occurrence is absolute and guaranteed, possessing a probability value of exactly $1$.
  * **Impossible Event:** An event that cannot under any circumstance occur during an experiment, possessing a probability value of exactly $0$.

* [GREEN] **TECHNICAL GLOSSARY — MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY**
  * **Central Tendency:** A single statistical value that identifies the central or typical position within a dataset.
  * **Arithmetic Mean:** The balance point or average of a dataset, calculated by dividing the sum of values by the total count.
  * **Median:** The exact middle positional value of an arrayed dataset dividing it into two equal halves.
  * **Mode:** The value that appears most frequently in a dataset (i.e., the observation possessing the maximum frequency).

* [RED] **CONCEPTUAL BREAKDOWN: CONVERTING INCLUSIVE TO EXCLUSIVE CLASSES**
  * **The Problem:** Class intervals given as $10-19, 20-29, 30-39$ create a gap between $19$ and $20$.
  * **The Adjustment Principle:** Calculate the gap value ($g = \text{Lower Limit of Class } 2 - \text{Upper Limit of Class } 1$). Here, $20 - 19 = 1$.
  * **Correction Factor:** Divide the gap by $2$ ($1 / 2 = 0.5$).
  * **Adjustment Formula:** Subtract $0.5$ from all Lower Limits and add $0.5$ to all Upper Limits.
  * **Resulting Exclusive Intervals:** $9.5-19.5, 19.5-29.5, 29.5-39.5$. Class continuity is successfully restored for histogram construction.

* [GOLD] **PRACTICAL APPLICATION: COMPUTING MEAN FROM GROUPED DATA**
  * **Step 1:** Construct a vertical analysis table with columns: Class Interval, Frequency ($f_i$), Class Mark ($x_i$), and Product ($f_i \cdot x_i$).
  * **Step 2:** Compute each class mark: $x_i = \frac{\text{Lower Limit} + \text{Upper Limit}}{2}$.
  * **Step 3:** Multiply frequency by class mark for each row to obtain $f_i \cdot x_i$.
  * **Step 4:** Sum all frequencies to find $\sum f_i$ (total observations, $N$) and sum all products to find $\sum (f_i \cdot x_i)$.
  * **Step 5:** Apply the formula $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum (f_i \cdot x_i)}{\sum f_i}$.

* [GREEN] **PRACTICAL APPLICATION: PROBABILITY OF DICE AND COIN COMBINATIONS**
  * **Two-Dice Sample Space Analysis:** Rolling two fair 6-faced dice simultaneously yields $6 \times 6 = 36$ total outcomes.
  * **Example Favorable Outcome:** Sum of numbers equals $8$.
  * **Favorable Pairs:** $(2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2)$ — Total count = $5$.
  * **Probability Calculation:** $P(\text{Sum} = 8) = \frac{5}{36}$.

* [RED] **DAV BOARD EXAM: TOP-TIER CONCEPTUAL TRAPS**
  * **Trap 1:** Forgetting to arrange raw data in ascending or descending order before calculating the Median. Doing so yields a completely incorrect positional term.
  * **Trap 2:** Confusing Class Mark ($x_i$) with Class Size ($h$). Class mark is an average value; class size is a difference value.
  * **Trap 3:** Assuming the sum of probabilities of all non-mutually exclusive events can exceed $1$. The total probability sum across all elementary events in a sample space must equal *exactly* $1$.

* [GOLD] **DAV BOARD EXAM: FREQUENTLY TESTED THEORETICAL PROOFS & FACTS**
  * **Fact A:** The algebraic sum of deviations of all observations from their arithmetic mean is *always* zero:
    $$\sum (x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$$
  * **Fact B:** If each observation in a dataset is increased, decreased, multiplied, or divided by a constant $k$, the arithmetic mean is correspondingly increased, decreased, multiplied, or divided by $k$.
  * **Fact C:** An empirical probability value approaches the theoretical probability value as the number of experimental trials approaches infinity (Law of Large Numbers).

* [GREEN] **DAV BOARD EXAM: GOLDEN SCORING CHECKLIST**
  * Always label graph axes clearly with variable names and measurement units (e.g., "Marks (Frequency)", "Number of Students").
  * Always write down the general formula before substituting numerical values in Mean, Median, and Probability problems.
  * Reduce all probability fractions to their simplest lowest terms (e.g., write $\frac{2}{4}$ as $\frac{1}{2}$).
  * Verify that the sum of individual frequencies equals the total sample size ($N = \sum f_i$) before concluding frequency table calculations.

| Feature Parameter | Bar Graph | Histogram |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

* [GOLD] **STRUCTURAL SUMMARY: MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY CHARACTERISTICS**

| Measure | Definition / Property | Best Used When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mean ($\bar{x}$)** | Mathematical average utilizing all data points | Data has no extreme outliers; symmetrical distribution |
| **Median** | Positional middle value dividing data in half | Dataset contains extreme outliers or open-ended intervals |
| **Mode** | Most frequently occurring observation value | Identifying peak popularity or modal category is required |

* [GREEN] **STRUCTURAL MATRIX: PROBABILITY EVENT CATEGORIES**

| Event Category | Mathematical Definition | Probability Value ($P(E)$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Impossible Event** | Event that can never occur under any condition | $P(E) = 0$ |
| **Possible (Likely) Event** | Event that may or may not occur | $0 < P(E) < 1$ |
| **Certain (Sure) Event** | Event that is guaranteed to occur | $P(E) = 1$ |

* [RED] **CRUCIAL DISTINCTION 1: EXPERIMENTAL VS. THEORETICAL PROBABILITY**
  * **Experimental (Empirical) Probability:**
    * Based strictly on direct physical experimentation, historical records, and past observational data.
    * Varies slightly depending on the specific sample size and number of conducted trials.
    * Calculated as: $\frac{\text{Number of times event occurred}}{\text{Total number of trials conducted}}$.
  * **Theoretical (Classical) Probability:**
    * Based on logical reasoning, mathematical symmetry, and a priori analysis of sample spaces.
    * Remains constant and does not require physical experimentation.
    * Calculated as: $\frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes in sample space}}$.

* [GOLD] **CRUCIAL DISTINCTION 2: DISCRETE DATA VS. CONTINUOUS DATA**
  * **Discrete Data:** Consists of distinct, separate numerical values obtained by counting (e.g., number of students in a classroom: $35$, number of cars sold: $4$). Gaps exist between possible values.
  * **Continuous Data:** Consists of measurements that can take any numerical value within a specified finite or infinite interval (e.g., height, weight, time). Can be subdivided infinitely.

* [GREEN] **COMMON CONCEPTUAL PITFALLS & CORRECTIONS**
  * **Pitfall:** Forgetting to check if class intervals are inclusive before plotting a histogram.
    * *Correction:* Always convert inclusive classes to exclusive classes by adjusting limits ($\pm 0.5$) before plotting continuous histogram bars.
  * **Pitfall:** Confusing the median position formula $\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)$ with the median value itself.
    * *Correction:* The formula yields the *rank/position* of the term in the sorted array, not the final numerical median value. Match that rank back to the sorted data sequence.
