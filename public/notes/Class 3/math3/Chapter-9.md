• **Concept of Weight:** Weight is the measure of how heavy an object is, which is a fundamental property in physical science and mathematics.
• **Heavier vs. Lighter:** The primary method of comparing two objects is by determining which one exerts more downward force, categorized as 'heavier' or 'lighter'.
• **Standard Units:** To measure weight accurately, we use standard units like Grams (g) and Kilograms (kg) to ensure consistency across the globe.
• **Gram (g):** The gram is the standard unit used for measuring the weight of light objects, such as a pencil, an eraser, or a small fruit.
• **Kilogram (kg):** The kilogram is the standard unit used for measuring the weight of heavy objects, such as a sack of rice, a human being, or a large bag of flour.
• **Relationship:** There is a fixed mathematical relationship between these units: 1 Kilogram is equal to 1000 Grams.
• **Conversion Rule:** To convert kilograms into grams, one must multiply the number of kilograms by 1000.
• **Measurement Tool:** A **Physical Balance** or **Weighing Scale** is the instrument used to determine the exact weight of an object.
• **Beam Balance:** A traditional tool consisting of two pans; when the pans are level, the weights on both sides are equal.
• **Electronic Scale:** A modern digital device that provides a precise numerical reading of weight, commonly found in grocery stores and kitchens.
• **Estimation:** Before measuring, we often estimate weight based on the size and material density of an object.
• **Heaviness:** An object is considered 'heavy' if it has a large mass, requiring more force to lift or move.
• **Lightness:** An object is considered 'light' if it has a small mass, requiring very little force to lift or move.
• **Standard Weights:** Metal blocks of fixed masses (e.g., 50g, 100g, 200g, 500g, 1kg) are used as references on a balance scale.
• **Consistency:** Using standard units prevents confusion in trade and daily life, ensuring that a 'kilogram' means the same amount everywhere.

• **Unit Selection:** Choosing the correct unit is essential; we use grams for small items and kilograms for large items.
• **Small Objects:** Items like a safety pin, a coin, or a feather are measured in grams because their mass is significantly less than 1kg.
• **Large Objects:** Items like a school bag, a watermelon, or a chair are measured in kilograms because their mass exceeds 1kg.
• **The 1kg Benchmark:** Any object weighing 1000 grams is equivalent to 1 kilogram, serving as the bridge between units.
• **Addition of Weights:** When adding weights, we must ensure all values are in the same unit before performing the calculation.
• **Addition Rule:** If adding 500g and 600g, the result is 1100g, which can be expressed as 1kg and 100g.
• **Subtraction of Weights:** Similar to addition, subtraction requires common units to find the difference between two weights.
• **Difference Calculation:** To find how much heavier one object is than another, we subtract the smaller weight from the larger weight.
• **Word Problem Logic:** In real-life scenarios, identifying the 'total' weight involves addition, while 'difference' involves subtraction.
• **Capacity vs. Weight:** It is important to distinguish between capacity (how much a container holds) and weight (how heavy an object is).
• **Mass Consistency:** The weight of an object remains constant unless material is added to or removed from it.
• **Practical Application:** Grocery shopping involves calculating the total weight of various items to determine the cost.
• **Visualizing Grams:** A small paperclip weighs approximately 1 gram, helping students visualize the unit size.
• **Visualizing Kilograms:** A standard textbook or a liter of water weighs approximately 1 kilogram, helping students visualize the unit size.
• **Precision:** Accuracy in weighing is vital for recipes, medicine dosages, and commercial transactions.

• **Weighing Scales:** Various types of scales exist, including spring balances, digital scales, and pan balances.
• **Spring Balance:** A device that uses a spring to measure weight by the extent to which the spring is stretched.
• **Digital Scale:** A device that uses sensors to convert weight into an electrical signal, displayed as a number on a screen.
• **Pan Balance:** A classic tool where the object is placed on one side and standard weights are added to the other until they balance.
• **Equilibrium:** When the pointer of a balance scale is in the center, the weight on both sides is perfectly equal.
• **Standard Weights (Weights):** These are standardized metal pieces used as the 'known' weight in a balance scale.
• **Common Weights:** Common denominations include 50g, 100g, 200g, 500g, 1kg, 2kg, and 5kg.
• **Combination of Weights:** To weigh an object of 700g, one might use a 500g weight and a 200g weight together.
• **Reading a Scale:** Learning to read the markings on a scale is a critical skill for determining weight accurately.
• **Scale Increments:** Some scales have markings for every 10g, while others might have markings for every 100g.
• **Tare Function:** On digital scales, the 'tare' button resets the scale to zero, allowing the weight of a container to be ignored.
• **Net Weight:** The weight of the actual product inside a container, excluding the weight of the packaging.
• **Gross Weight:** The total weight of the product plus its packaging or container.
• **Calibration:** The process of adjusting a scale to ensure it provides accurate readings.
• **Safety:** Always handle weighing equipment with care to maintain its accuracy and longevity.

• **Conversion Process:** Converting kilograms to grams is a multiplication process by 1000.
• **Example 1:** 3kg = 3 × 1000 = 3000g.
• **Example 2:** 5kg 250g = (5 × 1000) + 250 = 5250g.
• **Reverse Conversion:** Converting grams to kilograms involves dividing by 1000 or separating the thousands place.
• **Example 3:** 4000g = 4000 ÷ 1000 = 4kg.
• **Example 4:** 2750g = 2000g + 750g = 2kg 750g.
• **Place Value Role:** The thousands digit in a gram measurement represents the number of kilograms.
• **Remainder Rule:** The remaining digits after the thousands place in a gram measurement represent the grams.
• **Addition Strategy:** Align the kilograms and grams in separate columns when performing addition.
• **Carrying Over:** If the sum of grams exceeds 999, convert the excess into kilograms and carry it over to the kilogram column.
• **Subtraction Strategy:** Align the kilograms and grams in separate columns, ensuring the larger value is on top.
• **Borrowing:** If the grams to be subtracted are greater than the grams available, borrow 1kg (1000g) from the kilogram column.
• **Verification:** Always check the result of a conversion by performing the reverse operation.
• **Mental Math:** Practice converting multiples of 1000g to kg mentally to build speed and confidence.
• **Logical Sequence:** Always convert to the smallest unit (grams) before performing complex calculations if needed.

• **Addition of Weights (Step-by-Step):** Write the weights in two columns: Kilograms (kg) and Grams (g).
• **Column Alignment:** Ensure that the grams column always has three digits (e.g., 50g is written as 050g).
• **Summation:** Add the grams column first, then add the kilograms column.
• **Regrouping:** If the sum of the grams column is 1000 or more, subtract 1000 from the grams and add 1 to the kilograms column.
• **Example:** Add 2kg 400g and 1kg 800g.
• **Calculation:** 400g + 800g = 1200g.
• **Conversion:** 1200g = 1kg 200g.
• **Final Result:** 2kg + 1kg + 1kg (carried) = 4kg. Total is 4kg 200g.
• **Subtraction of Weights (Step-by-Step):** Write the weights in two columns: Kilograms (kg) and Grams (g).
• **Borrowing Process:** If the grams in the top number are smaller than the grams in the bottom number, borrow 1kg from the kg column.
• **Conversion of Borrowed Unit:** The 1kg borrowed becomes 1000g in the grams column.
• **Example:** Subtract 1kg 600g from 3kg 200g.
• **Step 1:** Cannot subtract 600g from 200g.
• **Step 2:** Borrow 1kg from 3kg, leaving 2kg. Add 1000g to 200g to get 1200g.
• **Step 3:** Subtract 600g from 1200g to get 600g.
• **Step 4:** Subtract 1kg from 2kg to get 1kg. Final result is 1kg 600g.

• **Word Problem Structure:** Every word problem involves identifying the given information and the goal.
• **Keyword Identification:** Look for words like 'total', 'sum', 'altogether' for addition.
• **Keyword Identification:** Look for words like 'difference', 'how much more', 'left', 'remaining' for subtraction.
• **Step 1: Data Extraction:** Write down the weights provided in the problem clearly.
• **Step 2: Unit Check:** Ensure all units are consistent or converted before calculation.
• **Step 3: Operation Selection:** Decide whether to add or subtract based on the context.
• **Step 4: Calculation:** Perform the arithmetic with attention to carrying or borrowing.
• **Step 5: Statement:** Write the final answer as a complete sentence including the units.
• **Example Problem:** A shopkeeper had 10kg of rice. He sold 4kg 500g. How much is left?
• **Solution:** 10kg 000g - 4kg 500g.
• **Borrowing:** Borrow 1kg from 10kg (becomes 9kg), add 1000g to 000g.
• **Subtraction:** 1000g - 500g = 500g; 9kg - 4kg = 5kg.
• **Result:** 5kg 500g of rice is left.
• **Complexity:** As students progress, problems may involve multiple items or multi-step calculations.
• **Consistency:** Always double-check the units in the final answer to avoid errors.

• **History of Measurement:** Ancient civilizations used stones and seeds as standard weights before modern units were developed.
• **The Metric System:** The system of grams and kilograms is part of the International System of Units (SI).
• **Global Standard:** The metric system is used by almost every country in the world, facilitating international trade.
• **The Prototype Kilogram:** Historically, a physical cylinder made of platinum-iridium was kept in France to define the exact mass of 1kg.
• **Modern Definition:** Today, the kilogram is defined by fundamental physical constants, ensuring it never changes.
• **Why 1000?** The metric system is based on powers of 10, making calculations and conversions simple and logical.
• **Prefixes:** 'Kilo-' is a prefix meaning 1000; thus, a kilometer is 1000 meters, and a kilogram is 1000 grams.
• **Subdivisions:** Smaller units like milligrams (mg) exist for extremely light objects, where 1g = 1000mg.
• **Scientific Importance:** Accurate weight measurement is the backbone of chemistry, physics, and engineering.
• **Health and Nutrition:** Understanding weight helps in maintaining a balanced diet and monitoring body mass.
• **Safety Regulations:** Vehicles and bridges have weight limits to prevent structural failure.
• **Space Exploration:** Weight changes in space (microgravity), but mass remains constant; this is a concept for higher grades.
• **Environmental Awareness:** Measuring the weight of waste produced by a household helps in tracking recycling efforts.
• **Technological Evolution:** From balance scales to laser-based weighing, technology has made measurement faster and more accurate.
• **Academic Foundation:** Mastering weight in Class 3 provides the base for understanding volume, area, and more complex physics later.
