• **Introduction to DAV Class 5 Social Studies**: Welcome to the comprehensive academic study notes for Class 5, Subject: "We and Our World" (Social Studies), published by the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV) Public School Board.
• **Chapter Overview**: This document provides an exhaustive, in-depth academic breakdown of Chapter 13, titled "India's Neighbours," designed for rigorous student mastery and conceptual clarity.
• **Geographical Significance of India**: India is a vast and culturally diverse country located in the southern part of the continent of Asia, holding a unique strategic position that influences regional geopolitics and trade.
• **Defining "Neighbours"**: In a geographical context, a nation's neighbours are countries that share direct land borders or maritime boundaries across seas and oceans, facilitating close diplomatic, cultural, and economic interactions.
• **The Subcontinent Concept**: India, along with its immediate contiguous neighbours, forms a distinct geographical unit known as the Indian Subcontinent due to its physical isolation by towering mountains and vast oceans.
• **Natural Boundaries of India**: The northern frontiers of India are naturally protected by the majestic Himalayan mountain ranges, which act as a formidable climatic and physical barrier against Central Asia.
• **Peninsular Nature of India**: The southern half of India projects into the Indian Ocean, flanked by the Arabian Sea to the west and the Bay of Bengal to the east, defining its peninsular geography.
• **Land Frontiers**: India shares its land boundaries with several sovereign nations located to its northwest, north, northeast, and east, creating complex geopolitical relationships.
• **Maritime Boundaries**: Beyond land frontiers, India shares maritime boundaries across the seas with island nations located in the Indian Ocean, fostering maritime cooperation and security partnerships.
• **Role of DAV Curriculum**: These study notes strictly adhere to the DAV academic philosophy, emphasizing factual precision, geographical literacy, and global citizenship.
• **Importance of Regional Awareness**: Studying India's neighbours helps students understand regional cooperation, South Asian history, cross-border trade, and cultural shared heritages.
• **Diversity in Neighbourhood**: India’s neighbours present a fascinating mix of governmental systems, including constitutional republics, federal democracies, and monarchies.
• **Size Comparison**: India is the largest country in South Asia in terms of geographic area, population, and economic output, making it a central anchor for the entire region.
• **Linguistic and Cultural Ties**: Many neighbouring countries share deeply intertwined linguistic, religious, culinary, and historical ties with India that date back millennia.
• **Economic Interdependence**: Trade routes, bilateral agreements, and resource sharing bind India and its neighbours in a web of mutual economic dependency.

• **Comprehensive List of Neighbours**: India shares its land borders with seven primary countries: Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar.
• **Northwestern Neighbour (Pakistan)**: Pakistan is located to the immediate northwest of India, sharing a long, heavily guarded land border that emerged following the partition of British India in 1947.
• **Capital and Currency of Pakistan**: Islamabad serves as the capital city of Pakistan, and the official currency used within the country is the Pakistani Rupee.
• **Geographical Link with Afghanistan**: India shares a very short, historically significant land border with Afghanistan to the extreme northwest in the region administered as Pakistan-administered Kashmir (PoK).
• **Capital and Currency of Afghanistan**: Kabul is the historic capital of Afghanistan, and the national currency utilized by its citizens is the Afghani.
• **Northern Giants (China)**: To the north and northeast, India shares a massive, sprawling land border with the People's Republic of China, separated largely by the Himalayan mountain ranges.
• **Capital and Currency of China**: Beijing is the political and cultural capital of China, and the currency of exchange is the Renminbi, commonly referred to as the Yuan.
• **Himalayan Kingdom (Nepal)**: Nepal is a landlocked Himalayan nation nestled directly between India and China, sharing open-border arrangements and deep cultural affinities with India.
• **Capital and Currency of Nepal**: Kathmandu is the vibrant capital city of Nepal, situated in the Kathmandu Valley, and the national currency is the Nepalese Rupee.
• **Land of the Thunder Dragon (Bhutan)**: Bhutan is another small, mountainous, landlocked country located in the eastern Himalayas, sharing its northern border with China and all other sides with India.
• **Capital and Currency of Bhutan**: Thimphu is the capital of Bhutan, known for its strict adherence to Gross National Happiness, and its currency is the Ngultrum (pegged to the Indian Rupee).
• **Eastern Neighbour (Bangladesh)**: Bangladesh is situated to the east of India, almost entirely surrounded by Indian territory on three sides, emerging as an independent nation in 1971.
• **Capital and Currency of Bangladesh**: Dhaka serves as the bustling capital of Bangladesh, and the official monetary unit of the country is the Bangladeshi Taka.
• **Gateway to Southeast Asia (Myanmar)**: Myanmar (formerly Burma) lies to the far east of India, serving as a crucial geographical and cultural bridge connecting South Asia with Southeast Asia.
• **Capital and Currency of Myanmar**: Naypyidaw is the administrative capital of Myanmar, while Yangon remains its commercial hub, and the national currency is the Kyat.

• **Focus on Pakistan**: Pakistan shares its borders with Indian states such as Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat, and the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir.
• **The Radcliffe Line**: The international border demarcating India and Pakistan is historically known as the Radcliffe Line, drawn by Sir Cyril Radcliffe during partition in 1947.
• **Cultural Parities with Pakistan**: Despite political complexities, populations on both sides of the India-Pakistan border share immense linguistic commonalities, particularly in Punjabi, Urdu, and Hindi (Hindustani).
• **Historical Sites**: Regions close to the India-Pakistan border hold immense historical significance, including the ancient ruins of the Indus Valley Civilization, such as Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro (now in Pakistan).
• **Focus on Afghanistan**: Although the direct land border with Afghanistan is currently disputed and spans a remote mountainous corridor known as the Wakhan Corridor, bilateral ties remain historically robust.
• **Historical Silk Road**: Afghanistan served as a vital crossroads on the ancient Silk Road, facilitating trade and cultural diffusion between India, Central Asia, Persia, and Europe.
• **Developmental Assistance**: India has historically contributed extensively to infrastructure development in Afghanistan, constructing roads, dams, hospitals, and the Afghan Parliament building.
• **Focus on China**: India and China are the two most populous nations on earth, making their bilateral relationship one of the most critical geopolitical axes in the modern world.
• **The McMahon Line**: The boundary line separating India's northeastern region (Arunachal Pradesh) and China is historically referred to as the McMahon Line, established during the Simla Convention of 1914.
• **Line of Actual Control (LAC)**: The effective military-held border between India and China is known as the Line of Actual Control (LAC), spanning rugged Himalayan terrains including Ladakh and Uttarakhand.
• **Ancient Civilizational Contact**: India and China share ancient linkages facilitated by Buddhist monks, scholars, and traders who traveled across treacherous mountain passes like the Silk Road.
• **Trade Dynamics with China**: China is one of India's largest trading partners in terms of volume of goods, though bilateral trade balances present ongoing economic and strategic debates.
• **Cultural Exchanges**: Cultural exchanges between India and China include shared philosophies, art forms, martial arts traditions, and the widespread popularity of yoga and cinema.
• **Geopolitical Challenges**: Border disputes and differing strategic interests in the Indian Ocean and South Asia periodically strain India-China diplomatic relations.
• **Regional Stability**: Peaceful coexistence between India and China is deemed vital for Asian economic growth, continental stability, and global trade security.

• **Focus on Nepal**: Nepal shares an open border with India under the 1950 Indo-Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship, allowing citizens of both nations to move freely and work without strict visa restrictions.
• **Gorkha Regiments**: Citizens of Nepal serve with immense valour and distinction in the Gorkha Regiments of the Indian Army, cementing deep military brotherhood.
• **Mount Everest**: Nepal is world-famous for hosting Mount Everest (known locally as Sagarmatha), the highest peak on Earth, located on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet).
• **Pashupatinath and Lumbini**: Nepal is home to sacred pilgrimage sites including Pashupatinath Temple for Hindus and Lumbini, the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama (Lord Buddha), for Buddhists.
• **Rivers Flowing from Nepal**: Several major tributaries of the Ganges River, such as the Kosi, Gandaki, and Karnali, originate in Nepal and flow into India, providing vital irrigation and hydroelectric potential.
• **Focus on Bhutan**: Bhutan is officially known as the "Land of the Thunder Dragon" and maintains uniquely close, mutually beneficial diplomatic and economic ties with India.
• **Environmental Preservation**: Bhutan's constitution mandates that at least 60 percent of its land remain under forest cover, making it the world's only carbon-negative country.
• **Hydropower Cooperation**: India actively collaborates with Bhutan to harness its rushing Himalayan rivers for hydroelectric power generation, exporting excess electricity back to India.
• **Monarchy to Democracy**: Bhutan successfully transitioned from an absolute monarchy to a constitutional democratic monarchy, guided by the vision of successive Kings of Bhutan.
• **Cultural Homogeneity**: Tibetan Buddhism is the state religion and dominant cultural force in Bhutan, creating deep historical and spiritual ties with Buddhist traditions in India.
• **Focus on Bangladesh**: Bangladesh emerged on the global map in December 1971 following the Bangladesh Liberation War, with significant diplomatic and military support from India.
• **The Bengal Delta**: Bangladesh is primarily formed by the massive delta of the Padma (Ganges) and Jamuna (Brahmaputra) rivers, making it one of the most fertile yet flood-prone regions globally.
• **Sunderbans Shared Heritage**: The Sunderbans, the largest mangrove forest in the world and home to the Royal Bengal Tiger, is shared geographically between India (West Bengal) and Bangladesh.
• **Linguistic and Literary Pride**: Bangladesh shares the Bengali language and rich literary heritage of Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore with the Indian state of West Bengal.
• **Economic Partnerships**: India and Bangladesh share extensive land and river transit routes, energy grids, and robust textile-manufacturing trade partnerships.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Focus on Myanmar**: Myanmar serves as India's land bridge to Southeast Asia, strategically connecting South Asian economies with the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) bloc.
• **Historical Connection (Burma)**: Myanmar was historically administered as part of British India until it was officially separated in 1937, leaving deep administrative, legal, and demographic footprints.
• **Indian Diaspora in Myanmar**: Yangon and other major cities in Myanmar host a vibrant and historic population of people of Indian origin who have contributed significantly to local commerce and culture.
• **Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Project**: India is developing the Kaladan Project to connect the port of Kolkata in India with Sittwe port in Myanmar, facilitating seamless cargo transport to India's northeastern states.
• **Religious Ties with Myanmar**: Theravada Buddhism is widely practiced in Myanmar, drawing continuous pilgrimage traffic from followers in India and across the Buddhist world.
• **Introduction to Island Neighbours**: Apart from land-sharing countries, India shares crucial maritime boundaries with two major island nations in the Indian Ocean: Sri Lanka and the Maldives.
• **Strategic Importance of the Indian Ocean**: The Indian Ocean acts as a global maritime highway, and India's island neighbours play a critical role in maintaining regional maritime security and trade routes.
• **Focus on Sri Lanka**: Sri Lanka is an island nation situated in the Indian Ocean, separated from the southern tip of India (Tamil Nadu) by a narrow strip of shallow water known as the Palk Strait.
• **Capital and Currency of Sri Lanka**: Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte is the official legislative and administrative capital of Sri Lanka, Colombo is its commercial hub, and the currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee.
• **Ramayana Connection**: Sri Lanka holds deep mythological significance in the Indian epic Ramayana, being identified as the kingdom of Lanka ruled by Ravana, connected to India via the ancient Ram Setu (Adam's Bridge).
• **Buddhism in Sri Lanka**: Buddhism was introduced to Sri Lanka from India during the reign of Mauryan Emperor Ashoka through his son Mahinda and daughter Sanghamitta.
• **Economic Ties with Sri Lanka**: India and Sri Lanka have historical trade relations, cooperating closely in infrastructure development, tourism, agriculture, and educational exchanges.
• **Focus on the Maldives**: The Maldives is an archipelago nation consisting of nearly 1,200 coral islands grouped into 26 atolls, located southwest of the Indian mainland in the Indian Ocean.
• **Capital and Currency of Maldives**: Malé is the densely populated capital city of the Maldives, and the national currency used by its inhabitants is the Maldivian Rufiyaa.
• **Tourism and Economy**: The Maldivian economy relies heavily on high-end international tourism and sustainable fisheries, with India serving as a major economic partner and security guarantor.

• **Concept of Maritime Boundaries**: Unlike land borders marked by fences, pillars, or natural features, maritime borders are governed by international law, specifically the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).
• **Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZ)**: Coastal nations, including India and its maritime neighbours, possess sovereign rights over exploration and use of marine resources within their designated Exclusive Economic Zones.
• **The Palk Strait**: The Palk Strait is a strait between the Tamil Nadu state of India and the Mannar district of the Northern Province of Sri Lanka, serving as a vital fishing and navigation zone.
• **Adam's Bridge (Ram Setu)**: A chain of limestone shoals, stretching between Pamban Island (also known as Rameswaram Island) off the southeastern coast of Tamil Nadu and Mannar Island off the northwestern coast of Sri Lanka.
• **SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation)**: Founded in December 1985, SAARC is an economic and geopolitical organization of eight countries in South Asia: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka.
• **Objectives of SAARC**: SAARC aims to promote welfare economics, collective self-reliance, accelerate social progress, and foster cultural development within the South Asian region.
• **India's Leadership in SAARC**: As the largest economy and geographic entity in SAARC, India plays a pivotal role in driving regional infrastructure projects, disaster management cooperation, and technological assistance.
• **Bilateral vs. Multilateral Relations**: India maintains both bilateral (one-on-one country) relationships and multilateral engagements through platforms like SAARC, BIMSTEC, and the Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA).
• **BIMSTEC Framework**: The Bay of Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation (BIMSTEC) bridges South Asia and Southeast Asia, including India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, and Thailand.
• **Cultural Diplomacy**: India actively leverages its rich cultural heritage—including classical music, dance, film, spiritual philosophy, and cricket—to build enduring bonds of friendship with its neighbours.
• **Educational Scholarships**: The Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR) offers thousands of scholarships annually to students from neighbouring countries to pursue higher education in premier Indian universities.
• **Medical Tourism**: India serves as a premier healthcare hub for patients from neighbouring nations seeking advanced medical treatments, surgeries, and specialized healthcare services.
• **Technical Assistance and Training**: Through the Indian Technical and Economic Cooperation (ITEC) programme, India provides specialized civil and military training and capacity-building to professionals from friendly neighbouring countries.
• **Disaster Management Cooperation**: India frequently acts as a first responder during regional natural disasters, deploying naval ships, aircraft, medical teams, and relief supplies to neighbouring countries affected by earthquakes, floods, or cyclones.
• **The "Neighbourhood First" Policy**: Modern Indian foreign policy places supreme priority on the "Neighbourhood First" doctrine, emphasizing peace, shared prosperity, connectivity, and mutual trust.

• **Physical Geography of the Subcontinent**: The Indian subcontinent features remarkable geographical diversity, ranging from the world's highest mountains in the north to tropical rainforests, vast river basins, arid deserts, and deep-sea coastlines.
• **Role of Himalayan Rivers**: Perennial rivers originating from the Himalayas—such as the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra—traverse multiple countries, sustaining hundreds of millions of people with fresh water and alluvial soil.
• **Transboundary Water Management**: Managing shared river basins requires delicate international treaties and cooperative basin-wide governance between India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and China.
• **Monsoon Climatic Unity**: The South Asian monsoon system unites India and its neighbours under a singular weather phenomenon, dictating agricultural cycles, water resource availability, and regional food security.
• **Shared Historical Legacies**: For centuries, the Indian subcontinent experienced overlapping empires, including the Mauryan, Gupta, Mughal, and British eras, leaving behind shared legal systems, administrative traditions, and architectural marvels.
• **Religious and Philosophical Birthplace**: South Asia is the cradle of major world religions—Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism—whose holy sites attract millions of pilgrims across national boundaries.
• **Language Families**: Linguistic studies reveal deep shared roots among South Asian languages originating from Indo-Aryan and Dravidian linguistic stocks, facilitating cross-border cultural understanding.
• **Trade and Commerce Routes**: Ancient trade routes like the Grand Trunk Road connected regions spanning from modern-day Bangladesh through India to Afghanistan and Central Asia, fostering long-distance commerce.
• **Colonial Impact and Partition**: The withdrawal of British colonial rule in the mid-20th century resulted in the redrawing of borders, leading to the creation of independent nation-states accompanied by complex political legacies.
• **Post-Independence Nation Building**: Following independence, India and its neighbours embarked on distinct trajectories of nation-building, grappling with challenges of poverty alleviation, industrialization, and democratic governance.
• **Cross-Border Infrastructure**: Modern initiatives focus heavily on building transnational highways, railway links, energy grids, and digital connectivity pipelines across borders.
• **Energy Grids Integration**: Power-sharing agreements allow surplus electricity generated in nations like Bhutan and Nepal to be transmitted seamlessly into the Indian national power grid.
• **Maritime Security and Collaboration**: India works alongside island neighbours like Sri Lanka and the Maldives to conduct joint naval patrols, combat piracy, and secure sea lanes of communication in the Indian Ocean.
• **People-to-People Connections**: Tourism, family ties, academic exchanges, and cultural festivals generate enduring grassroots-level friendships that transcend political friction.
• **The Vision of Regional Harmony**: A peaceful, prosperous, and stable neighbourhood remains the cornerstone of India's long-term national security and developmental aspirations.

• **Detailed Country Profile: Pakistan**: Total land border length with India is approximately 3,323 kilometres, traversing diverse terrains from the arid deserts of Rajasthan to the rugged mountains of Kashmir.
• **Major Border Crossings**: The Attari-Wagah border near Amritsar is the most famous transit point between India and Pakistan, renowned for its daily military Beating Retreat ceremony.
• **Detailed Country Profile: China**: India shares a winding land border with China spanning approximately 3,488 kilometres, divided into Western (Ladakh), Middle (Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh), and Eastern (Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh) sectors.
• **Major Himalayan Passes**: Mountain passes such as Nathu La in Sikkim, Shipki La in Himachal Pradesh, and Lipulekh in Uttarakhand serve as historical trading gateways between India and China/Tibet.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Nepal**: Shares a porous, open land border measuring roughly 1,751 kilometres with Indian states including Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, and Sikkim.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Bhutan**: Shares an international land border of about 699 kilometres with Indian states comprising Sikkim, West Bengal, Assam, and Arunachal Pradesh.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Bangladesh**: Possesses the longest international land border that India shares with any neighbour, measuring approximately 4,096 kilometres, wrapping around West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram.
• **Enclaves and Boundary Agreements**: The historic India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement resolved complex enclave issues, granting citizenship and settled land rights to tens of thousands of border residents.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Myanmar**: Shares a rugged, hilly border of about 1,643 kilometres with Indian states including Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, and Mizoram.
• **Free Movement Regime (FMR)**: Border populations residing along the India-Myanmar frontier historically enjoyed traditional Free Movement Regime privileges to maintain indigenous community ties.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Sri Lanka**: Located just 31 kilometres off the southern coast of Tamil Nadu across the Palk Bay, Sri Lanka maintains deep maritime connectivity through passenger ferry services and flight routes.
• **Detailed Country Profile: Maldives**: Situated roughly 700 kilometres southwest of the Indian mainland, the Maldives is easily accessible via direct international flights from major Indian metropolitan airports.
• **Strategic Chokepoints**: The Indian Ocean houses critical maritime chokepoints, through which global energy supplies and container shipping pass, elevating the strategic importance of India's island neighbours.
• **Blue Economy Partnership**: India actively promotes the "Blue Economy" framework with maritime neighbours, focusing on sustainable marine resource harvesting, oceanographic research, and coastal tourism.
• **Space Diplomacy**: India's space agency (ISRO) launched the South Asia Satellite (GSAT-9) as a gift to its neighbours, providing vital communication, meteorology, and tele-education services across the region.

• **Glossary of Essential Geographical Terms**:
  - *Subcontinent*: A large, distinguishable part of a continent, such as the Indian Subcontinent, separated by natural physical barriers.
  - *Landlocked*: A country or region entirely enclosed by land with no direct access to the open sea or ocean.
  - *Archipelago*: A extensive group, chain, or cluster of islands scattered across a body of water.
  - *Strait*: A narrow passage of water connecting two large bodies of water, such as the Palk Strait.
  - *Porous Border*: A national border that is relatively easy to cross across rural, mountainous, or un-fenced terrains.
  - *Bilateral Relations*: Political, economic, or cultural relations established between two sovereign nations.
  - *Multilateral Relations*: Cooperation and agreements involving three or more nations working toward common goals.
