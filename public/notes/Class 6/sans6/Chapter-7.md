• **Chapter Title and Context**: Chapter 7 of the DAV Sanskrit textbook 'सुरभि' for Class 6 is titled **"प्रहेलिकाः" (Prahelikah)**, which translates to **Riddles** or **Enigmas**.
• **Literary Significance**: Riddles form an ancient and culturally rich part of Sanskrit literature, serving as a medium for intellectual stimulation, linguistic play, and sharp observation of nature and daily life.
• **Educational Objective**: The primary objective of this chapter is to enhance students' Sanskrit vocabulary, improve reading fluency, foster lateral thinking, and teach grammatical structures through playful puzzles.
• **Nature of Sanskrit Riddles**: Sanskrit riddles (*प्रहेलिकाः*) are typically composed in poetic meters (*श्लोकाः*), requiring the reader to decode hidden meanings, double entendres (*श्लेष*), or clever wordplay.
• **Structural Layout of the Chapter**: The chapter presents a series of verses, each posing a unique riddle about common objects, animals, natural phenomena, or everyday human experiences.
• **Role of the DAV Academic Notes**: These comprehensive study notes are designed according to the DAV Sanskrit curriculum guidelines to provide exhaustive linguistic analysis for Class 6 learners.
• **Vocabulary Building Focus**: Every verse in the chapter introduces multiple new nouns, verbs, and adjectives, expanding the active vocabulary base of the students.
• **Grammatical Integration**: The verses naturally incorporate various nominal declensions (*शब्दरूपाणि*), verbal conjugations (*धातुरूपाणि*), prefixes (*उपसर्गाः*), and suffixes (*प्रत्ययाः*).
• **Phonetic and Recitation Practice**: Students are expected to recite the *ślokas* with proper intonation (*स्वर*), rhythm (*लय*), and pronunciation (*उच्चारण*) to appreciate the poetic cadence of Sanskrit.
• **Analytical Thinking**: Solving these riddles demands moving beyond literal interpretations to analyze structural clues embedded within the poetic lines.
• **Cultural Roots**: Riddles in Sanskrit have historical roots in Vedic literature, classical epics, and ancient scholastic gatherings where wit was celebrated as a high intellectual virtue.
• **Interdisciplinary Connections**: The chapter connects Sanskrit language study with general science, environmental awareness, and logical reasoning.
• **Accessibility for Class 6**: Despite their classical nature, the riddles selected in this chapter use accessible vocabulary suited for beginner and intermediate learners of Sanskrit.

• **Introduction to Verse 1**: The first verse of the chapter introduces a classic riddle focusing on physical characteristics, lifestyle, and unique capabilities of an enigmatic entity.
• **Textual Opening**: The verse begins with descriptive phrases that paint a vivid mental picture, challenging the student to identify the subject based on its distinct attributes.
• **Term - अपदः (Apadaha)**: Means **'footless'** or **'without feet'**, highlighting an organism or object that moves without possessing traditional limbs.
• **Term - दूरगामी (Dooragaami)**: Translates to **'one who goes far'** or **'travels long distances'**, describing a high degree of mobility despite physical limitations.
• **Term - साक्षरः (Saaksharaha)**: Means **'literate'** or **'possessing letters'**, a clever pun in Sanskrit where an inanimate object can be 'literate' because it is inscribed with text.
• **Term - न च पण्डितः (Na cha Panditaha)**: Translates to **'and yet not a scholar'**, contrasting literacy with formal scholarly wisdom to confuse and amuse the reader.
• **Term - अमूखः (Amookhha)**: Means **'faceless'** or **'mouthless'**, indicating an absence of a conventional speaking apparatus.
• **Term - स्फुटरक्ता (Sphutarakta)**: Refers to **'clearly red'** or possessing distinct red features, often used in descriptive poetic imagery within Sanskrit compositions.
• **Grammatical Breakdown of Verse 1**: Features masculine nominative singular forms like *पण्डितः* and compound adjectives like *दूरगामी*.
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 1**: The clues of being footless yet traveling far, literate yet not a scholar, and mouthless yet vocal point directly to a specific everyday object.
• **The Answer to Verse 1**: The correct answer to this famous Sanskrit riddle is **'पत्रम्' (Patram)**, which means a **letter** or a **leaf** written upon.
• **Explanation of the Answer**: A letter travels far across distances without feet, contains letters (words) without being a human scholar, and conveys messages without having a physical mouth.
• **Cognitive Skill Developed**: Teaches students the art of metaphor and how inanimate objects are personified in classical Sanskrit literature.
• **Syntax Appreciation**: The placement of negative particles like *न* emphasizes negation in a playful, contradictory manner.
• **Memorization Aid**: Students should memorize Verse 1 by breaking it down into individual word meanings: *अपदः* (footless) + *दूरगामी* (long-distance traveler).

• **Introduction to Verse 2**: The second verse shifts focus to an animate subject, using biological and ecological clues to construct a captivating riddle about living creatures in nature.
• **Textual Theme**: This verse explores the theme of residence, dwelling places, and unique behavioral traits found in the animal kingdom.
• **Term - वृक्षारोही (Vrikshaarohi)**: Means **'tree-climber'** or **'one who ascends trees'**, pointing toward arboreal animals.
• **Term - न च पक्षीराजः (Na cha Pakshiraajaha)**: Translates to **'and not the king of birds (Garuda/Eagle)'**, eliminating birds from the list of possible answers.
• **Term - त्रिनेत्रधारी (Trinetradhaari)**: Means **'wearer of three eyes'** or **'possessing three eyes'**, a striking physiological description that immediately invokes mythological or unique biological imagery.
• **Term - न च शूलपाणिः (Na cha Shoolapaanihi)**: Translates to **'and not Lord Shiva'** (who holds a trident and possesses a third eye), cleverly ruling out deities.
• **Term - त्वग् वस्त्रधारी (Twag Vastradhaari)**: Means **'wearing skin as clothing'** or **'clad in bark/hide'**, referring to natural outer coverings.
• **Term - न च सिद्धयोगी (Na cha Siddhayogi)**: Translates to **'and not an ascetic yogi'**, further narrowing down the identity away from human holy men.
• **Term - जलं बिभ्रत् (Jalam Bibhrat)**: Means **'holding water'** or **'carrying water'**, describing the internal composition or storage capacity of the subject.
• **Term - न च घटः (Na cha Ghataha)**: Translates to **'and not an earthen pot'**, eliminating artificial water containers despite the capacity to hold water.
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 2**: The combination of climbing trees (not a bird), having three 'eyes' (not Lord Shiva), wearing bark (not a yogi), and holding water (not a pot) creates a brilliant web of contradictions.
• **The Answer to Verse 2**: The correct answer to this intricate riddle is **'नारिकेलम्' (Naarikelam)**, which means the **coconut**.
• **Explanation of the Answer**: The coconut grows on tall trees (tree-climber), has three distinct dark spots on its shell resembling eyes (three-eyed), has a rough fibrous husk resembling coarse clothing (skin-clad), and contains refreshing sweet water inside its hard shell (holding water).
• **Botanical Connection in Sanskrit**: Highlights how ancient Sanskrit poets observed nature with remarkable botanical accuracy.
• **Grammatical Focus**: Demonstrates the use of masculine and neuter nouns along with participial adjectives ending in *-धारी* (possessing/wearing).

• **Introduction to Verse 3**: The third verse introduces a riddle centered on consumption, digestion, transformation, and physical states of matter, presenting a fascinating puzzle about daily necessities.
• **Textual Theme**: Focuses on dietary habits, culinary processes, and the transformation of food items through cooking or natural changes.
• **Term - भोजनम् (Bhojanam)**: Means **'food'** or **'meal'**, establishing the primary context of sustenance and eating.
• **Term - स्वादु (Swaadu)**: Translates to **'delicious'**, **'sweet'**, or **'pleasant-tasting'**, describing the agreeable flavor of the subject in question.
• **Term - दुर्लभम् (Durlabham)**: Means **'difficult to obtain'**, **'rare'**, or **'hard to acquire'**, indicating high value or scarcity.
• **Term - पक्वम् (Pakwam)**: Translates to **'cooked'**, **'ripe'**, or **'mature'**, highlighting a state of readiness for consumption.
• **Term - अपक्वम् (Apakwam)**: Means **'uncooked'**, **'raw'**, or **'unripe'**, contrasting with the processed or mature state.
• **Term - कुरुते (Kurute)**: Present tense (Lat Lakaara) Atmanepada verb meaning **'does'**, **'makes'**, or **'performs'**.
• **Term - क्षुधा (Kshudhaa)**: Translates to **'hunger'**, the fundamental biological drive that makes food desirable.
• **Term - शमने (Shamane)**: Locative singular form meaning **'in pacifying'**, **'in calming'**, or **'in satisfying'** (hunger or affliction).
• **Grammatical Analysis**: Utilizes neuter adjectives (*स्वादु, दुर्लभम्, पक्वम्*) agreeing with the implied subject, and noun declensions in locative and instrumental cases.
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 3**: The clues revolve around what is eaten, what satisfies hunger, and the conditions under which it is prepared or consumed.
• **Contextual Understanding**: Sanskrit riddles often use culinary and agricultural metaphors because food production and preparation were central to ancient agrarian society.
• **Linguistic Nuance**: The juxtaposition of antonyms (*पक्वम्* and *अपक्वम्*) sharpens the student's comprehension of polar descriptive terms in Sanskrit.
• **Moral and Practical Wisdom**: Teaches students the importance of nourishment and the transformation of raw materials into consumable goods.
• **Recitation Practice**: Students must practice the correct articulation of compound words like *भोजनस्वादिष्ट* and ensure proper nasalization (*अनुस्वार*) pronunciation.

• **Introduction to Verse 4**: The fourth verse presents a dynamic riddle involving motion, direction, travel, and the paradox of stationary movement or cyclical journeys.
• **Textual Theme**: Explores the concepts of space, time, paths, and the tireless movement of natural or mechanical entities.
• **Term - गच्छति (Gachchhati)**: Present tense verb (Lat Lakaara) meaning **'goes'** or **'moves'**, indicating continuous action.
• **Term - तिष्ठति (Tishtthati)**: Present tense verb meaning **'stands'** or **'stays'**, creating an immediate paradox when paired with movement.
• **Term - धावति (Dhaavati)**: Translates to **'runs'** or **'speeds'**, emphasizing rapid motion across distances.
• **Term - भ्रमति (Bhramati)**: Means **'wanders'**, **'revolves'**, or **'rotates'**, describing circular or erratic paths.
• **Term - पन्थाः (Panthaaha)**: Masculine nominative singular noun meaning **'path'**, **'road'**, or **'journey'**.
• **Term - दिशः (Dishaha)**: Feminine plural noun meaning **'directions'** or **'quarters of the universe'** (East, West, North, South, etc.).
• **Grammatical Structure**: Features a rapid succession of third-person singular present tense verbs (*गच्छति, तिष्ठति, धावति, भ्रमति*), making it an excellent exercise for verb conjugation recognition.
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 4**: The verse questions what it is that constantly moves yet appears fixed, or what travels across all directions without resting.
• **Philosophical Undertone**: Reflects the Indian philosophical concept of *काल* (Time) or rivers (*नद्यः*) that flow incessantly while their riverbeds remain in place.
• **Vocabulary Expansion**: Exposes students to essential verbs of motion and spatial nouns that appear frequently in classical Sanskrit narratives.
• **Sentence Formation Practice**: Students can use the verbs learned in this verse to construct simple sentences about their daily movements (e.g., *बालकः विद्यालयं गच्छति*).
• **Oral Drill**: Teachers use this verse for rapid oral recitation drills to test students' grip on verbal roots (*धातवः*) such as *गम्*, *स्था*, *धाव्*, and *भ्रम्*.
• **Comprehension Check**: Students must explain how an object can simultaneously exhibit characteristics of standing still and running fast.

• **Introduction to Verse 5**: The fifth verse introduces a riddle focusing on interpersonal relationships, familial bonds, and human characteristics described through metaphorical puzzles.
• **Textual Theme**: Explores societal structures, human interactions, and kinship terms (*सम्बन्धिनः*) within the framework of playful verse.
• **Term - जननी (Janani)**: Means **'mother'** or **'procreator'**, representing the primary maternal figure in family terminology.
• **Term - जनकः (Janakah)**: Means **'father'** or **'procreator'**, representing the paternal figure.
• **Term - भगिनी (Bhagini)**: Translates to **'sister'**, an essential familial bond in Sanskrit culture.
• **Term - भ्राता (Bhraataa)**: Means **'brother'**, representing sibling relationships.
• **Term - परिवारः (Parivaaraha)**: Translates to **'family'** or **'household'**, encompassing all relatives living together.
• **Term - स्नेहः (Snehaha)**: Means **'affection'**, **'love'**, or **'oil/lubricant'**, showcasing a classic Sanskrit word with multiple distinct meanings depending on context.
• **Grammatical Categories**: Highlights masculine (*जनकः, भ्राता*) and feminine (*जननी, भगिनी*) nouns in the nominative singular case.
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 5**: Riddles in this category test whether students can identify relationships or metaphorical kinship between seemingly unrelated concepts or objects.
• **Cultural Context**: Emphasizes the deep-rooted importance of family (*कुटुम्ब*) and relational terminology in ancient Indian society.
• **Linguistic Precision**: Demonstrates how Sanskrit maintains distinct, highly specific vocabulary for every tier of familial relationship.
• **Writing Exercise**: Students are encouraged to write a paragraph describing their own family (*मम परिवारः*) using the vocabulary acquired from this verse.
• **Pronunciation Guide**: Special attention is paid to retroflex and dental sounds in words like *जननी* and *भ्राता*.

• **Introduction to Verse 6**: The sixth verse delves into the realm of time, seasons, day and night, and the eternal cycle of nature that governs all living beings.
• **Textual Theme**: Focuses on temporal progression, celestial movements, and the continuous wheel of time (*कालचक्रम्*).
• **Term - सूर्यः (Sooryaha)**: Means **'the sun'**, the primary source of light, heat, and life on Earth.
• **Term - चन्द्रः (Chandraha)**: Means **'the moon'**, governing the night and associated with coolness and poetic beauty.
• **Term - निशा (Nishaa)**: Translates to **'night'**, the period of darkness and rest.
• **Term - दिवसः (Divasaha)**: Means **'day'**, the period of illumination and activity.
• **Term - कालः (Kaalaha)**: Translates to **'time'** or **'death'**, the ultimate regulator of all worldly events.
• **Term - चक्रम् (Chakram)**: Means **'wheel'** or **'cycle'**, symbolizing perpetual motion without beginning or end.
• **Grammatical Forms**: Features standard masculine and feminine nominative singular nouns (*सूर्यः, चन्द्रः, निशा, दिवसः*).
• **Decoding the Riddle - Part 6**: Poses questions about what governs the alternation of light and darkness, or what moves forward inexorably without ever turning back.
• **Scientific Observation**: Reflects ancient Indian astronomy and timekeeping systems (*पञ्चाङ्ग*), where days and nights were meticulously calculated.
• **Metaphorical Usage**: Time is frequently depicted as a spinning wheel (*कालचक्र*) in Sanskrit epics, a concept introduced subtly through this riddle.
• **Vocabulary Enrichment**: Expands the student's lexicon regarding celestial bodies and temporal units.
• **Cross-Curricular Link**: Connects Sanskrit literary studies with elementary geography and science concepts regarding day-night cycles.

• **Introduction to Grammar and Syntax in Chapter 7**: A comprehensive study of Sanskrit riddles requires a firm grasp of underlying grammatical patterns, sentence structures, and morphological forms.
• **Nominal Declensions (*शब्दरूपाणि*)**: The chapter heavily utilizes masculine words ending in *-अ* (e.g., *पण्डितः, घटः*), feminine words ending in *-आ* (e.g., *क्षुधा, निशा*), and neuter words ending in *-म्* (e.g., *पत्रम्, नारिकेलम्*).
• **Verbal Roots (*धातवः*)**: Key verbal roots appearing across the verses include **गम** (to go), **स्था** (to stand), **धाव्** (to run), **भ्रम्** (to wander), and **कृ** (to do).
• **Lat Lakaara (Present Tense)**: Used extensively to describe ongoing actions and timeless truths within the riddles (e.g., *गच्छति, तिष्ठति, करोति*).
• **Use of Negative Particles**: The particle **'न'** (not) is used repeatedly in adversative structures (e.g., *न च पण्डितः, न च पक्षीराजः*) to create misleading clues.
• **Conjunctions (*व्ययपदानि*)**: Words like **'च'** (and) play a crucial role in linking descriptive attributes in a single continuous thought.
• **Compound Words (*समासाः*)**: Sanskrit riddles frequently employ descriptive compounds (*कर्मधारय* and *बहुव्रीहि*), such as *दूरगामी* (far-traveler) and *त्रिनेत्रधारी* (three-eye-bearer).
• **Significance of Compounds**: Compounds condense complex ideas into single, melodious words that fit the strict metrical constraints of Sanskrit poetry.
• **Interrogative Pronouns (*प्रश्वाचकाः*)**: Words like **'कः'** (who - masculine), **'का'** (who - feminine), and **'किम्'** (what - neuter) form the basis of solving and discussing these riddles.
• **Syntax Flexibility**: Illustrates how Sanskrit word order (*अन्वय*) can be rearranged for poetic effect without altering the core grammatical meaning.
• **Punctuation and Intonation**: Proper pauses (*विराम*) during recitation are essential for capturing the rhetorical question format of riddles.
• **Student Application**: Learners are trained to identify the subject (*कर्ता*), verb (*क्रिया*), and modifiers (*विशेषण*) within complex poetic lines.
