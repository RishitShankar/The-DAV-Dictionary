• **Chapter Introduction:** The chapter "चाँद का कुर्ता" (Chand ka Kurta) is an iconic poetic composition included in the DAV Class 5 Hindi 'भाषा अभ्यास' and 'वापस मधुप' curriculum, written by the legendary poet Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar'.
• **Poetic Genre:** The poem belongs to the realm of children's literature (बाल साहित्य), crafted with simple yet rhythmic phrasing designed to captivate young minds while delivering profound imaginative concepts.
• **Core Theme:** The central theme revolves around the innocent desire of the moon (चाँद) asking his mother for a warm winter kurta (garment) to protect himself from the biting cold weather.
• **Main Character - The Moon (चाँद):** Personified in the poem as a little child who feels cold during winter nights and approaches his mother with a childlike, innocent demand.
• **Main Character - The Mother (माता):** Depicted as an affectionate, wise, and practical mother who patiently listens to her child's demands and explains the practical difficulties of fulfilling them.
• **Setting the Scene:** The poem portrays the winter season (जाड़े का मौसम), highlighting how severe cold affects everyone, prompting the moon to look for protective clothing.
• **Poetic Device - Personification (मानवीकरण):** The moon and the stars are given human attributes, feelings, and speech, making the celestial bodies relatable to children.
• **Linguistic Style:** The language used by Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar' in this poem is standard Khariboli Hindi, enriched with colloquial sweetness and effortless rhyming schemes.
• **Educational Objective:** To develop reading fluency, imagination, emotional empathy, and an appreciation for poetry among Class 5 students studying under the DAV Board framework.
• **Cultural Significance:** The poem reflects traditional Indian familial bonds, where a child expresses discomfort to a mother, and the mother responds with gentle reasoning and deep affection.
• **Symbolism of the Kurta:** The kurta symbolizes warmth, protection, and the universal need of living beings for shelter and clothing against harsh natural elements.
• **Celestial Observations:** The poem subtly introduces astronomical phases of the moon (waxing and waning) through a creative poetic lens rather than dry scientific facts.
• **Tone of the Poem:** The tone shifts dynamically from the moon's pleading innocence to the mother's gentle, explanatory, and loving persuasion.
• **Vocabulary Foundation:** The chapter introduces rich Hindi vocabulary related to weather, clothing, celestial bodies, and familial relationships, which forms the basis for subsequent grammar exercises.
• **Significance in DAV Curriculum:** It serves as a foundational literary text designed to bridge imaginative thinking with structured Hindi language comprehension and moral understanding.

• **Stanza-by-Stanza Analysis (Stanza 1):** The poem opens with the moon shivering in the cold night, expressing his plight directly to his mother with utmost innocence.
• **Key Expression:** 'हट कर बैठा चाँद एक दिन, माता से यह बोला' — captures the stubborn yet innocent insistence of a child demanding something special from his mother.
• **The Central Demand:** 'सिलवा दो मेरा मोटा सा, एक झगा ही गोरा-गोरा' — the moon specifically requests a thick, fair-colored garment (झगा/कुर्ता) to beat the cold.
• **Meaning of 'झगा' (Jhaga):** A traditional loose-fitting upper garment or kurta worn by children, highlighting the rural and traditional cultural flavor of the poetry.
• **The Weather Context:** 'सन-सन चलती हवा रात भर, जाड़े से मरता हूँ' — describes the piercing cold night winds that make the moon feel vulnerable and freezing.
• **Childlike Exaggeration:** The phrase 'जाड़े से मरता हूँ' is an innocent hyperbole typical of children emphasizing their discomfort to gain immediate attention and sympathy from parents.
• **Mother's Initial Reaction:** The mother smiles at the childish logic, realizing the unique nature of her child's body and the impossibility of a standard stitching solution.
• **Stanza 2 Progression:** The mother points out the constant physical changes of the moon's body as the core obstacle in getting a permanent garment stitched.
• **The Waxing and Waning Dilemma:** 'रोज़ बदन पर हर दिन आता, एक नया सा नाप' — the mother explains that the moon's size changes every single day without fail.
• **Unpredictable Measurements:** Because the moon is never of the same size for two consecutive days, tailoring a well-fitted kurta becomes a logistical impossibility.
• **Meaning of 'नाप' (Naap):** Measurement or sizing required by a tailor to stitch clothes, which fluctuates daily in the case of the celestial child.
• **The Crescent to Full Moon Cycle:** The poem touches upon the lunar cycle where the moon grows from a thin sliver (दूज का चाँद) to a full orb (पूर्णिमा) and back.
• **Mother's Practical Wisdom:** Instead of scolding the moon for an impossible demand, the mother uses gentle logic to explain natural laws to her impatient child.
• **The Metaphor of Growth:** The changing size of the moon acts as a poetic metaphor for how children grow, though in the moon's case, the body shrinks and expands cyclically.
• **Visual Imagery:** The poet paints a vivid mental picture of a tailor scratching his head over how to take measurements of a constantly shifting celestial body.
