• **Chapter Title and Core Subject**: Welcome to the DAV Dictionary Academic Notes for Class 5 English Practice Book, Chapter 13, titled "Prepositions," designed to provide a comprehensive and exhaustive study guide for young learners.
• **Definition of a Preposition**: A preposition is a vital part of speech defined as a word placed before a noun or a pronoun to show its relation to some other word in the sentence.
• **Etymology and Positioning**: The word "preposition" is derived from "pre" (before) and "position" (placed), indicating that these words are typically positioned prior to the noun or pronoun they govern.
• **Primary Function in Sentences**: The fundamental function of a preposition is to establish relationships of time, space, direction, and spatial arrangement between different components of a sentence.
• **Object of the Preposition**: Every preposition requires a noun or pronoun to follow it, which is grammatically termed the "object of the preposition," forming a complete prepositional phrase.
• **Role in Sentence Modification**: Prepositional phrases act as modifiers, functioning adverbially or adjectivally to add rich descriptive detail regarding where, when, or how an action occurs.
• **Ubiquity in Daily Communication**: Prepositions are among the most frequently used words in the English language, serving as the linguistic glue that holds complex thoughts and spatial descriptions together.
• **Categories of Prepositions**: Prepositions can be broadly classified into multiple functional categories, including prepositions of time, prepositions of place, prepositions of direction, and prepositions of position.
• **DAV Curriculum Objective**: The DAV English Practice Book curriculum for Class 5 focuses on enabling students to identify, understand, and accurately apply prepositions in everyday writing and speech.
• **Avoiding Common Errors**: A key learning objective in this chapter is to eradicate common errors related to the interchange of confusing prepositions such as 'in', 'on', 'at', 'between', and 'among'.
• **Importance of Context**: The exact meaning and correct usage of a preposition often depend entirely on the context of the sentence and the specific relationship being described.
• **Distinction from Other Parts of Speech**: Students must learn to distinguish prepositions from adverbs and conjunctions, as many words can function as different parts of speech depending on their sentential context.
• **Foundational Grammar Skill**: Mastery of prepositions in Class 5 lays the critical foundation for advanced syntactic structures, sentence diagramming, and expressive creative writing in higher grades.
• **Scope of the Chapter**: This comprehensive 10-page study note will methodically break down every nuance, rule, and application of prepositions as mandated by the DAV Public School Board syllabus.

• **Introduction to Prepositions of Place**: Prepositions of place are words used to indicate the exact physical location or position of an object, person, or place in relation to another object.
• **Core Spatial Preposition 'In'**: The preposition 'in' is primarily used to express position within a bounded space, container, enclosure, or large geographical area like a city or a country.
• **Examples of 'In' Usage**: Common applications of 'in' include phrases such as "in the room," "in the box," "in Delhi," "in the water," and "in the school library."
• **Core Spatial Preposition 'On'**: The preposition 'on' is utilized to denote a position that is physically touching a surface, resting upon a flat plane, or located on a specific street or floor.
• **Examples of 'On' Usage**: Standard examples of 'on' encompass expressions like "on the table," "on the wall," "on the floor," "on the roof," and "on MG Road."
• **Core Spatial Preposition 'At'**: The preposition 'at' specifies a precise point, exact location, or specific address where someone or something is situated.
• **Examples of 'At' Usage**: Typical constructions involving 'at' include "at the door," "at the bus stop," "at the crossroads," "at home," and "at 42 Park Street."
• **Nuanced Contrast between 'In' and 'At'**: While 'in' implies being enclosed inside a space, 'at' refers to a general location or point of reference, such as being "at the school" (on the premises) versus "in the school" (inside the building).
• **Preposition 'Under'**: 'Under' is used to describe an object that is positioned directly beneath or lower than another object, often with the upper object covering or overshadowing the lower one.
• **Examples of 'Under'**: Illustrative phrases for 'under' feature constructs like "under the tree," "under the bridge," "under the bed," and "under the blanket."
• **Preposition 'Over'**: 'Over' indicates a position higher than something else, often implying movement across a space or vertical alignment without direct physical contact.
• **Examples of 'Over'**: Common instances of 'over' include "the lamp hanging over the table," "flying over the clouds," and "jumping over the fence."
• **Preposition 'Above'**: 'Above' signifies a higher position than another object, typically without touching it, focusing purely on relative vertical elevation rather than movement.
• **Examples of 'Above'**: Standard uses of 'above' comprise phrases like "the painting above the fireplace" and "temperatures above freezing point."
• **Preposition 'Below'**: 'Below' serves as the direct antonym of 'above', denoting a lower position than something else without necessarily implying direct contact.
• **Examples of 'Below'**: Familiar usages of 'below' include "the valley below the mountain" and "signing below the dotted line."

• **Introduction to Prepositions of Time**: Prepositions of time are essential grammatical tools used to indicate when an event, action, or state of being occurs, has occurred, or will occur.
• **Preposition 'In' for Time**: The preposition 'in' is deployed with longer, non-specific periods of time, including months, years, centuries, decades, and seasons.
• **Examples of 'In' for Time**: Standard time-based expressions with 'in' incorporate phrases like "in January," "in 2024," "in the summer," "in the 21st century," and "in the morning."
• **Preposition 'On' for Time**: The preposition 'on' is specifically reserved for exact days of the week, specific calendar dates, and particular days marked by an occasion.
• **Examples of 'On' for Time**: Common applications of 'on' for time feature constructs such as "on Monday," "on 15th August," "on my birthday," "on Christmas Day," and "on a rainy morning."
• **Preposition 'At' for Time**: The preposition 'at' is utilized to pinpoint precise times of the day, clock times, meal times, and specific festivals without the word 'day'.
• **Examples of 'At' for Time**: Typical time expressions using 'at' encompass phrases like "at 5 o'clock," "at noon," "at midnight," "at lunchtime," "at sunset," and "at Easter."
• **Preposition 'Before'**: 'Before' is used to indicate an earlier point in time relative to a specific event, deadline, or time marker.
• **Examples of 'Before'**: Illustrative phrases for 'before' include "before sunset," "before class begins," "before Monday," and "wash your hands before eating."
• **Preposition 'After'**: 'After' denotes a later point in time subsequent to a specific event, action, or time marker, acting as the temporal opposite of 'before'.
• **Examples of 'After'**: Familiar usages of 'after' comprise expressions like "after school," "after dinner," "after the rain stopped," and "after a long journey."
• **Preposition 'By'**: 'By' indicates a deadline or the latest point in time by which an action must be completed or accomplished.
• **Examples of 'By'**: Common instances of 'by' include "submit the project by Friday," "return the book by 4 PM," and "finish the homework by tomorrow."
• **Preposition 'For'**: 'For' is used to express the duration of time over which an action extends or continues.
• **Examples of 'For'**: Standard constructions for duration include "for two hours," "for a week," "for many years," and "waiting for ten minutes."
• **Preposition 'Since'**: 'Since' marks the specific starting point in time for an action that began in the past and continues into the present.
• **Examples of 'Since'**: Typical examples of 'since' feature phrases like "since morning," "since 2010," "since last Sunday," and "living here since childhood."

• **Introduction to Prepositions of Direction and Movement**: Prepositions of direction and movement are used to show the path along which someone or something moves, travels, or is directed.
• **Preposition 'To'**: The preposition 'to' indicates movement in the direction of a specific destination, goal, or endpoint.
• **Examples of 'To'**: Common applications of 'to' include sentences and phrases such as "going to school," "walking to the park," "traveling to London," and "handing the notebook to the teacher."
• **Preposition 'Towards'**: 'Towards' expresses movement in the general direction of a person, place, or object, without necessarily reaching the final destination.
• **Examples of 'Towards'**: Illustrative instances of 'towards' comprise expressions like "walking towards the building," "running towards the finish line," and "looking towards the horizon."
• **Preposition 'Into'**: 'Into' denotes movement directed to the interior of a bounded space, container, or enclosure from the outside.
• **Examples of 'Into'**: Familiar usages of 'into' feature phrases such as "jumping into the swimming pool," "pouring water into the glass," and "walking into the classroom."
• **Preposition 'Out of'**: 'Out of' serves as the direct antonym of 'into', signifying movement from the interior of a confined space to the exterior.
• **Examples of 'Out of'**: Standard applications of 'out of' include "coming out of the house," "taking a book out of the bag," and "stepping out of the car."
• **Preposition 'Through'**: 'Through' indicates movement from one side of a three-dimensional space, tunnel, opening, or obstacle to the other side.
• **Examples of 'Through'**: Typical examples of 'through' encompass phrases like "walking through the forest," "the train passing through the tunnel," and "looking through the window."
• **Preposition 'Across'**: 'Across' describes movement from one side of a two-dimensional surface, line, road, or open area to the opposite side.
• **Examples of 'Across'**: Common instances of 'across' comprise "walking across the street," "swimming across the river," and "drawing a line across the page."
• **Preposition 'Along'**: 'Along' signifies movement following a line, path, road, river, or continuous boundary.
• **Examples of 'Along'**: Illustrative constructions for 'along' include "walking along the beach," "strolling along the railway track," and "planting trees along the roadside."
• **Preposition 'Round' or 'Around'**: 'Around' or 'round' denotes circular movement or positioning encompassing the perimeter of an object or area.
• **Examples of 'Around'**: Familiar examples feature phrases like "running around the track," "sitting around the campfire," and "traveling around the world."

• **Introduction to Advanced Relational Prepositions**: Beyond space, time, and direction, several prepositions express complex interrelationships between multiple entities, such as position relative to multiple objects.
• **Preposition 'Between'**: 'Between' is used to indicate a position in the middle of two distinct people, objects, or points in space and time.
• **Examples of 'Between'**: Standard applications of 'between' include phrases like "sitting between two friends," "the book lying between the notebooks," and "choosing between tea and coffee."
• **Preposition 'Among'**: 'Among' is utilized to express a position surrounded by more than two people, objects, or a group, where individual identities are merged into a collective whole.
• **Examples of 'Among'**: Common instances of 'among' comprise expressions such as "hidden among the trees," "distributing sweets among the students," and "a leader among peers."
• **Rule Distinction: Between vs. Among**: A crucial DAV grammar rule specifies that 'between' is strictly for two distinct entities, while 'among' is used for a collective group of three or more entities.
• **Preposition 'Behind'**: 'Behind' denotes a position at the back of or at the rear side of another person or object.
• **Examples of 'Behind'**: Illustrative phrases for 'behind' include "hiding behind the door," "the garden behind the house," and "standing behind my friend in the queue."
• **Preposition 'In front of'**: 'In front of' is a phrasal preposition indicating a position directly ahead of the face or front side of another entity.
• **Examples of 'In front of'**: Familiar usages of 'in front of' comprise "standing in front of the mirror," "the car parked in front of the gate," and "the teacher standing in front of the class."
• **Preposition 'Beside'**: 'Beside' means next to, by the side of, or immediately adjacent to another person or object.
• **Examples of 'Beside'**: Typical applications of 'beside' feature phrases such as "sitting beside the window," "placing the glass beside the plate," and "walking beside my mother."
• **Preposition 'Besides'**: 'Besides' is a frequently confused word that functions as a preposition meaning "in addition to" or "apart from," distinct from 'beside'.
• **Examples of 'Besides'**: Standard examples of 'besides' include "besides English, she speaks French" and "who was there besides you?"
• **Preposition 'Against'**: 'Against' indicates physical contact supported by a surface, opposition to a force, or leaning posture.
• **Examples of 'Against'**: Common instances of 'against' encompass "leaning against the wall," "swimming against the current," and "pushing against the heavy door."

• **Introduction to Agency, Instrument, and Association**: Certain prepositions are specially designed to express the agent performing an action, the instrument used, or the association between entities.
• **Preposition 'By' for Agency**: The preposition 'by' is used in passive voice constructions to introduce the doer or agent of the action.
• **Examples of 'By' for Agency**: Illustrative phrases include "the book was written by Ruskin Bond," "the picture was painted by an artist," and "the window was broken by a stray ball."
• **Preposition 'With' for Instruments**: The preposition 'with' is employed to denote the instrument, tool, or weapon used by an agent to perform a specific action.
• **Examples of 'With' for Instruments**: Common examples comprise "cutting paper with scissors," "writing with a fountain pen," "hitting the nail with a hammer," and "painting with a brush."
• **Contrast between 'By' and 'With'**: Students must master the distinction that 'by' introduces the living agent performing the action, while 'with' introduces the inanimate tool used by that agent.
• **Preposition 'By' for Means of Transport**: 'By' is also utilized to indicate the method or mode of transportation used for traveling.
• **Examples of 'By' Transport**: Standard expressions include "traveling by train," "going by bus," "flying by airplane," and "commuting by metro."
• **Exception in Transport Prepositions**: When a determiner (like 'a', 'the', or 'my') precedes the vehicle name, the preposition shifts from 'by' to 'in' or 'on', such as "in a car" or "on the bus."
• **Preposition 'With' for Accompanying**: 'With' indicates accompaniment, meaning together with someone or in the company of another person.
• **Examples of 'With' for Association**: Familiar usages feature phrases like "going to the market with my mother," "playing football with my friends," and "sharing lunch with my classmate."
• **Preposition 'Without'**: 'Without' serves as the antonym of 'with', indicating the absence of something or someone.
• **Examples of 'Without'**: Typical applications of 'without' include "tea without sugar," "coming to school without a notebook," and "living without water."
• **Preposition 'Of' for Possession and Association**: 'Of' is heavily used to denote possession, connection, origin, or association between nouns.
• **Examples of 'Of'**: Common instances of 'of' comprise "the roof of the house," "a cup of tea," "page of the book," and "a friend of mine."
• **Preposition 'From' for Origin and Source**: 'From' is deployed to indicate the point of origin, starting source, or separation from a place.
• **Examples of 'From'**: Illustrative constructions feature "coming from Mumbai," "water flowing from the tap," and "borrowing a book from the library."

• **Introduction to Phrasal Prepositions**: A phrasal preposition consists of two or more words—typically a combination of adverbs, adjectives, or nouns with prepositions—that function as a single prepositional unit.
• **Definition and Structure**: Phrasal prepositions act just like simple prepositions, establishing relational links between nouns/pronouns and other sentence elements with enhanced descriptive precision.
• **Prepositional Phrase 'In front of'**: Meaning directly ahead of, this phrasal preposition is widely used to denote precise relative positioning.
• **Examples of 'In front of'**: Sentential applications include "the car parked in front of the gate" and "the teacher standing in front of the blackboard."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'In spite of'**: Meaning regardless of or despite an obstacle, 'in spite of' introduces a contrasting circumstance.
• **Examples of 'In spite of'**: Illustrative sentences feature "in spite of the heavy rain, we played the match" and "in spite of working hard, he missed the prize."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'Because of'**: Indicating cause, reason, or origin, 'because of' connects an action to its motivating factor.
• **Examples of 'Because of'**: Common usages comprise "the match was cancelled because of rain" and "she was late because of heavy traffic."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'Instead of'**: Meaning in place of or as an alternative to, 'instead of' indicates substitution.
• **Examples of 'Instead of'**: Standard instances include "I drank milk instead of tea" and "he chose to walk instead of taking a bus."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'According to'**: Used to attribute information to a source, authority, or rule, 'according to' means as stated or reported by.
• **Examples of 'According to'**: Familiar phrases comprise "according to the weather forecast, it will rain" and "according to the school rules, uniforms are compulsory."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'In addition to'**: Meaning as well as or besides, 'in addition to' adds extra elements to a statement.
• **Examples of 'In addition to'**: Typical applications include "in addition to English, he studies Sanskrit" and "in addition to being smart, she is very polite."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'By means of'**: Denoting the method or instrument utilized to achieve a result, 'by means of' provides explanatory depth.
• **Examples of 'By means of'**: Illustrative sentences feature "he lifted the heavy box by means of a rope" and "communication is achieved by means of language."
• **Prepositional Phrase 'On account of'**: Functioning synonymously with 'because of', 'on account of' introduces formal reasons for an action or state.
• **Examples of 'On account of'**: Sentential examples include "the office remained closed on account of a national holiday."

• **Introduction to Prepositional Verbs and Adjectives**: Many verbs, adjectives, and nouns in English are habitually followed by specific prepositions to form fixed idiomatic pairings.
• **Importance of Fixed Prepositions**: Understanding fixed prepositions is crucial because translating word-for-word from regional languages often leads to ungrammatical prepositional choices.
• **Verbs Followed by 'To'**: Certain verbs require the preposition 'to' to direct their action toward an object, such as "listen to," "speak to," "talk to," and "write to."
• **Verbs Followed by 'For'**: Common verbs taking the preposition 'for' include "wait for," "search for," "ask for," "hope for," and "look for."
• **Verbs Followed by 'From'**: Verbs indicating separation, prevention, or origin often take 'from', such as "prevent from," "protect from," "differ from," and "hide from."
• **Verbs Followed by 'Of'**: Specific verbs expressing mental states or possession take 'of', such as "think of," "dream of," "approve of," and "warn of."
• **Adjectives Followed by 'Of'**: Many descriptive adjectives take the preposition 'of', including "afraid of," "fond of," "proud of," "ashamed of," and "sure of."
• **Examples in Sentences for Adjectives of 'Of'**: Illustrative examples comprise "he is fond of reading books" and "she is proud of her achievements."
• **Adjectives Followed by 'To'**: Common adjectives requiring the preposition 'to' include "kind to," "polite to," "similar to," "grateful to," and "obedient to."
• **Examples in Sentences for Adjectives of 'To'**: Sentential applications feature "we should be kind to animals" and "he is grateful to his teacher."
• **Adjectives Followed by 'With'**: Adjectives expressing feelings or associations often take 'with', such as "angry with," "pleased with," "satisfied with," and "familiar with."
• **Examples in Sentences for Adjectives of 'With'**: Standard instances include "the teacher was pleased with my work" and "are you familiar with this computer?"
• **Adjectives Followed by 'At'**: Adjectives denoting skill levels or emotional reactions frequently take 'at', such as "good at," "clever at," "surprised at," and "shocked at."
• **Examples in Sentences for Adjectives of 'At'**: Familiar usages feature "she is good at mathematics" and "we were surprised at the sudden news."
• **DAV Pedagogical Focus**: The DAV Class 5 curriculum emphasizes memorizing and practicing these fixed prepositional pairings to ensure fluency and grammatical precision.

• **Introduction to Common Prepositional Errors**: Language learners frequently commit predictable errors when utilizing prepositions due to mother tongue interference and overlapping definitions.
• **Error 1: Confusing 'In' and 'At' for Places**: Incorrectly saying "he lives at Delhi" instead of the correct form "he lives in Delhi" (using 'in' for large areas).
• **Error 2: Confusing 'On' and 'In' for Transport**: Saying "he is in the bus" when referring to public transport where one stands or walks, which requires "he is on the bus."
• **Error 3: Incorrect Use of 'To' with 'Reach'**: Erroneously writing "he reached to the station" instead of the correct idiom "he reached the station" (as 'reach' does not take 'to').
• **Error 4: Redundant Prepositions**: Adding unnecessary prepositions, such as saying "return back" instead of "return" or "enter inside" instead of "enter."
• **Error 5: Misusing 'Since' and 'For'**: Confusing specific starting points with durations, such as saying "raining for morning" instead of "raining since morning."
• **Error 6: Confusing 'Beside' and 'Besides'**: Mistakenly interchanging "beside" (next to) with "besides" (in addition to) in descriptive sentences.
• **Error 7: Incorrect Preposition after 'Marry'**: Saying "she was married with John" instead of the correct grammatical structure "she was married to John."
• **Error 8: Confusing 'Between' and 'Among'**: Using 'between' for a group of four students instead of the correct plural collective preposition 'among'.
• **Error 9: Incorrect Use of 'Than' with 'Superior'**: Saying "this is superior than that" instead of the correct comparative prepositional form "this is superior to that."
• **Error 10: Misusing 'Off' and 'Of'**: Confusing the preposition of separation 'off' (falling off a chair) with the possessive preposition 'of'.
• **DAV Diagnostic Strategies**: The DAV English Practice Book employs targeted error-spotting exercises to train students to self-correct these common pitfalls systematically.
• **Importance of Proofreading**: Cultivating the habit of careful proofreading helps young learners catch and rectify misplaced or incorrect prepositions before final submissions.
• **Mastery through Practice**: Consistent exposure to correct models of spoken and written English remains the most effective antidote to prepositional confusion.
