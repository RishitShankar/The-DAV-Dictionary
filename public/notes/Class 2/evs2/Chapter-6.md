• **Neighbourhood Definition**: The area around our house, including the nearby houses and the people living in them, is called our neighbourhood.
• **Neighbours**: The people who live near our house are called our neighbours.
• **Community Living**: Living in a neighbourhood helps us develop a sense of community, cooperation, and mutual support.
• **Importance of Neighbourhood**: A good neighbourhood provides us with essential services, safety, and social interaction.
• **Basic Needs**: A neighbourhood must have facilities that cater to our daily requirements like food, health, and education.
• **Market**: A place in the neighbourhood where we go to buy daily necessities like vegetables, fruits, milk, and bread.
• **Shopkeeper**: A person who sells goods in a shop within the neighbourhood market.
• **Post Office**: A public utility service where we go to post letters, send parcels, and buy stamps or postcards.
• **Postman**: The official who collects letters from the post box and delivers them to our homes.
• **Letter Box**: A red-colored box placed in the neighbourhood where people drop their letters for collection.
• **Hospital/Clinic**: A place where sick or injured people go to get medical treatment from doctors and nurses.
• **Doctor**: A medical professional who examines patients and prescribes medicines to help them recover.
• **Nurse**: A healthcare worker who assists the doctor and takes care of patients in the hospital.
• **Ambulance**: A specialized vehicle used to carry patients to the hospital quickly during emergencies.
• **Pharmacy/Chemist**: A shop in the neighbourhood where we buy medicines prescribed by the doctor.
• **Safety in Neighbourhood**: It is important to know the emergency contact numbers of the local hospital and police station.

• **Police Station**: A vital place in the neighbourhood that maintains law and order and ensures the safety of the residents.
• **Police Officer**: A person who protects us from thieves, helps in emergencies, and keeps the neighbourhood peaceful.
• **Traffic Police**: Officers who manage the flow of vehicles on the roads to prevent accidents and congestion.
• **Dial 100/112**: The emergency telephone numbers used to call the police for immediate help.
• **Fire Station**: A facility that houses fire engines and firefighters who rush to extinguish fires.
• **Fire Engine**: A specialized truck equipped with water hoses, ladders, and tools to put out fires.
• **Firefighters**: Brave individuals who risk their lives to save people and property from fire accidents.
• **Bank**: A place where people keep their money and jewelry safe and perform financial transactions.
• **ATM (Automated Teller Machine)**: A machine located in the neighbourhood that allows people to withdraw cash at any time.
• **School**: An educational institution in the neighbourhood where children go to learn, read, write, and play.
• **Teacher**: A person who imparts knowledge and guides students in their academic and personal growth.
• **Park**: A public space with greenery, swings, and slides where children play and adults go for walks.
• **Importance of Parks**: Parks provide fresh air, exercise, and a space for social interaction among neighbours.
• **Cleanliness**: It is the duty of every resident to keep their neighbourhood clean and green.
• **Public Property**: Facilities like parks, roads, and streetlights belong to everyone and must be protected.
• **Civic Sense**: Being a good neighbour means respecting others, keeping noise levels low, and following community rules.

• **Places of Worship**: Locations such as temples, mosques, churches, and gurdwaras are often found in a neighbourhood.
• **Community Harmony**: People of different religions living in the same neighbourhood celebrate festivals together, promoting peace.
• **Railway Station**: A place in larger neighbourhoods or cities where trains arrive and depart for travel.
• **Bus Stop**: A designated area where people wait for buses to travel from one place to another.
• **Public Transport**: Buses, autos, and taxis are common modes of transport available in a well-connected neighbourhood.
• **Library**: A quiet place in the neighbourhood where people can go to read books, newspapers, and magazines.
• **Community Centre**: A hall or building where residents gather for meetings, celebrations, and social functions.
• **Garbage Disposal**: A system where waste is collected from houses to keep the neighbourhood hygienic.
• **Dustbins**: Containers placed in public areas to ensure that litter is not thrown on the streets.
• **Street Lights**: Electric lamps installed along the roads to provide visibility and safety during the night.
• **Water Supply**: Essential infrastructure that provides clean drinking water to all houses in the neighbourhood.
• **Electricity**: A basic service provided to homes, schools, and shops to power appliances and lights.
• **Telephone Exchange**: A facility that manages the communication network, though now largely replaced by mobile towers.
• **Petrol Pump**: A station where vehicles are refueled with petrol or diesel.
• **Vegetable Market (Sabzi Mandi)**: A specific area where farmers and vendors sell fresh produce.
• **Bakery**: A shop that specializes in selling bread, cakes, biscuits, and other baked goods.

• **Neighbourhood Helpers**: People who provide services that make our lives easier and more comfortable.
• **Milkman**: A person who delivers fresh milk to our doorsteps every morning.
• **Gardener (Mali)**: A person who takes care of the plants and trees in the neighbourhood parks or private gardens.
• **Sweeper**: A worker who cleans the streets and ensures the neighbourhood remains free of dirt and waste.
• **Watchman (Guard)**: A person who keeps a watch on the neighbourhood at night to ensure the safety of our homes.
• **Electrician**: A skilled worker who repairs electrical faults, wiring, and appliances in our houses.
• **Plumber**: A professional who fixes leaking pipes, taps, and water-related issues in our homes.
• **Tailor**: A person who stitches and repairs our clothes.
• **Cobbler**: A person who mends our shoes and sandals.
• **Barber**: A person who cuts and styles our hair.
• **Laundry Man (Dhobi)**: A person who washes and irons our clothes.
• **Newspaper Vendor**: A person who delivers the daily newspaper to our homes early in the morning.
• **Importance of Helpers**: We should respect all helpers because they work hard to provide us with essential services.
• **Gratitude**: We should always be polite and thankful to the people who help us in our daily lives.
• **Cooperation**: Helping our neighbours in times of need strengthens the bond of friendship.
• **Mutual Respect**: Treating neighbours with kindness creates a happy and peaceful living environment.

• **Festivals in Neighbourhood**: Celebrating festivals like Diwali, Eid, Christmas, and Holi together brings neighbours closer.
• **Sharing**: Sharing sweets and gifts during festivals is a tradition that builds strong relationships.
• **Helping in Emergencies**: Neighbours are the first people to help us during accidents, illnesses, or other crises.
• **Safety Rules**: We must teach children not to talk to strangers and to stay within the safe limits of the neighbourhood.
• **Traffic Awareness**: Children should learn to walk on the footpath and cross the road only at the zebra crossing.
• **Zebra Crossing**: A series of white stripes on the road where pedestrians can cross safely.
• **Traffic Lights**: Red means stop, yellow means wait, and green means go.
• **Playground Safety**: While playing in the park, children should follow the rules of the games and be careful on swings.
• **Respecting Elders**: It is a good habit to greet our neighbours and show respect to the elderly people in the area.
• **Noise Control**: We should avoid playing loud music or making unnecessary noise that might disturb our neighbours.
• **Pet Etiquette**: If we have pets, we must ensure they do not cause trouble or mess in the neighbourhood.
• **Planting Trees**: Encouraging neighbours to plant more trees helps in keeping the air clean and the environment cool.
• **Saving Resources**: We should not waste water or electricity, as these are shared resources in the neighbourhood.
• **Reporting Issues**: If we see a broken street light or a leaking pipe, we should inform the local authorities.
• **Community Meetings**: Participating in meetings helps in discussing and solving common problems of the neighbourhood.
• **Unity**: A united neighbourhood is a safe and happy place to live.
