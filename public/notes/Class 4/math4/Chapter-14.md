• **Definition of Pattern:** A pattern is a repeated arrangement of numbers, shapes, colors, or symbols that follow a specific rule or logic.
• **Observation Skills:** Identifying patterns requires keen observation to determine how elements change or repeat in a sequence.
• **Types of Patterns:** Patterns can be broadly categorized into visual patterns (shapes/colors) and numerical patterns (sequences of numbers).
• **Repeating Patterns:** These are sequences where a specific set of elements repeats itself in a fixed order.
• **Growing Patterns:** These are sequences where the elements increase in size, number, or complexity according to a specific rule.
• **Shrinking Patterns:** These are sequences where the elements decrease in size or quantity based on a consistent mathematical rule.
• **Symmetry in Patterns:** Many patterns are based on symmetry, where one half of the design is a mirror image of the other half.
• **Tessellation:** A pattern made of identical shapes that fit together perfectly without any gaps or overlaps, often seen in floor tiles.
• **Number Sequences:** A list of numbers that follow a particular rule, such as adding or subtracting a constant value.
• **Skip Counting:** A fundamental form of a number pattern where we jump over a fixed number of digits (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8).
• **Arithmetic Progression:** A sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant, forming the basis of many school-level patterns.
• **Pattern Recognition:** The cognitive process of identifying the underlying rule that governs a sequence of events or objects.
• **Real-life Application:** Patterns are found everywhere, from the petals of a flower to the arrangement of bricks in a wall.
• **Predictive Logic:** Once a rule is identified, one can predict the next term in the sequence with absolute certainty.
• **Visual Sequencing:** Arranging objects in a specific order based on size, orientation, or color to create a logical flow.

• **Geometric Patterns:** Patterns created using geometric shapes like triangles, squares, circles, and rectangles.
• **Rotation in Patterns:** A pattern can involve rotating a shape by 90, 180, or 270 degrees to create a new visual effect.
• **Reflection in Patterns:** A pattern created by flipping a shape horizontally or vertically to create a mirror image.
• **Translation in Patterns:** Moving a shape from one position to another without changing its orientation or size.
• **Color Sequences:** Patterns where colors alternate in a specific cycle, such as Red-Blue-Red-Blue.
• **Size Variation:** A pattern where the same shape is repeated but in increasing or decreasing sizes.
• **Nature’s Patterns:** Examples include the arrangement of leaves on a stem, the stripes on a zebra, or the rings inside a tree trunk.
• **Mathematical Rules:** Every numerical pattern is governed by an operation, such as addition (+), subtraction (-), multiplication (×), or division (÷).
• **Constant Difference:** In a simple number pattern, the difference between any two consecutive numbers remains the same throughout the series.
• **Increasing Patterns:** A sequence where each subsequent number is larger than the previous one by a fixed amount.
• **Decreasing Patterns:** A sequence where each subsequent number is smaller than the previous one by a fixed amount.
• **Pattern Blocks:** Manipulative tools used to create complex designs by combining basic geometric shapes.
• **Symmetry Line:** An imaginary line that divides a pattern into two identical halves, ensuring balance.
• **Pattern Complexity:** Patterns can range from simple one-step repetitions to complex multi-step logical sequences.
• **Logical Reasoning:** The core skill developed by studying patterns, which helps in solving complex mathematical problems later.

• **Number Pyramids:** A structure where numbers are arranged in rows, and the number above is the sum of the two numbers below it.
• **Addition Rule in Pyramids:** The fundamental mechanism of a number pyramid is that each block is the sum of the two blocks directly beneath it.
• **Base of the Pyramid:** The bottom row of a number pyramid provides the foundation for all calculations above it.
• **Apex of the Pyramid:** The single number at the very top of the pyramid, which is the result of all additions in the structure.
• **Missing Numbers:** Using the pyramid rule to find a hidden value by subtracting a known number from the total sum.
• **Multiplication Patterns:** Sequences where each term is multiplied by a constant factor to get the next term (e.g., 2, 4, 8, 16).
• **Division Patterns:** Sequences where each term is divided by a constant factor to get the next term (e.g., 100, 50, 25).
• **Odd Number Patterns:** Sequences consisting only of odd numbers (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) which increase by a constant difference of 2.
• **Even Number Patterns:** Sequences consisting only of even numbers (2, 4, 6, 8, 10) which increase by a constant difference of 2.
• **Multiples:** Patterns based on the multiples of a number (e.g., 5, 10, 15, 20 are multiples of 5).
• **Pattern Extension:** The act of continuing a sequence by applying the identified rule to the last known term.
• **Error Detection:** Identifying a break in a pattern where a number or shape does not follow the established rule.
• **Pattern Correction:** Replacing an incorrect element in a sequence with the correct one based on the governing rule.
• **Sequence Notation:** Writing down a series of numbers or shapes clearly to analyze the progression.
• **Pattern Analysis:** Breaking down a complex pattern into smaller, manageable parts to understand the underlying logic.

• **Magic Squares:** A grid of numbers where the sum of each row, column, and diagonal is the same.
• **Magic Constant:** The specific sum that every row, column, and diagonal in a magic square must equal.
• **3x3 Magic Square:** The most common introductory magic square, containing numbers from 1 to 9.
• **Grid Layout:** The structure of a magic square, consisting of rows (horizontal) and columns (vertical).
• **Diagonal Sums:** In a magic square, the two main diagonals must also add up to the magic constant.
• **Number Placement:** Strategic placement of numbers in a grid to ensure the magic constant is maintained across all directions.
• **Symmetry in Grids:** Magic squares often exhibit internal symmetry in how numbers are distributed around the center.
• **Problem Solving:** Solving magic squares requires trial and error combined with logical deduction.
• **Mathematical Properties:** Magic squares demonstrate the commutative and associative properties of addition.
• **Historical Context:** Magic squares have been used in various cultures for centuries, often associated with puzzles and games.
• **Grid Constraints:** The rules of a magic square limit where specific numbers can be placed, making it a challenging logic puzzle.
• **Summation Logic:** Understanding that the total sum of the grid is divided equally among the rows or columns.
• **Verification:** Checking every row, column, and diagonal to ensure the magic square is valid.
• **Pattern Variation:** Changing the numbers in a magic square while maintaining the same magic constant.
• **Mental Math:** Solving magic squares improves mental arithmetic and addition speed.

• **Alphabet Patterns:** Sequences where letters of the alphabet follow a specific rule (e.g., A, C, E, G skipping one letter).
• **Letter-Number Combinations:** Patterns that mix letters and numbers, such as A1, B2, C3, D4.
• **Code Breaking:** Using pattern recognition to decipher simple codes where letters are replaced by numbers or other letters.
• **Cipher Logic:** Understanding that a pattern can act as a key to unlock a hidden message.
• **Positional Patterns:** Patterns based on the position of letters in the alphabet (e.g., A=1, B=2, C=3).
• **Reverse Alphabet Patterns:** Sequences that move backward through the alphabet (e.g., Z, Y, X, W).
• **Skipping Patterns:** Patterns that skip a fixed number of letters (e.g., A, D, G, J skipping two letters each time).
• **Pattern Complexity in Codes:** Increasing the difficulty by using multiple rules simultaneously (e.g., shifting letters and changing numbers).
• **Cryptographic Basics:** Introduction to the concept that patterns are the foundation of secure communication.
• **Pattern Mapping:** Creating a table to map letters to their corresponding numerical values for easier analysis.
• **Logical Sequences:** Applying the same logic used in number patterns to the English alphabet.
• **Pattern Consistency:** Ensuring that the rule applied to the first two elements is applied consistently throughout the sequence.
• **Pattern Variation:** Introducing patterns that change rules halfway through, requiring higher-level observation.
• **Alphabetical Order:** The most basic pattern, which serves as the foundation for more complex letter sequences.
• **Encoding Rules:** Defining the rule clearly before attempting to create or solve an alphabet-based pattern.

• **Tessellation Definition:** A pattern of shapes that covers a plane surface without any gaps or overlaps.
• **Tiling:** The practical application of tessellation in architecture, flooring, and art.
• **Regular Tessellation:** A pattern made using only one type of regular polygon (e.g., all squares or all hexagons).
• **Semi-regular Tessellation:** A pattern made using two or more types of regular polygons that fit together.
• **Gapless Fit:** The primary requirement of a tessellation is that the interior angles of the shapes meeting at a point must sum to 360 degrees.
• **Polygon Properties:** Understanding which shapes can tessellate (e.g., triangles, squares, hexagons) and which cannot (e.g., circles).
• **Escher-style Patterns:** Complex tessellations where shapes are modified to look like animals or objects while still fitting together.
• **Visual Harmony:** Tessellations create a sense of balance and order in design.
• **Geometric Constraints:** Not all shapes can tessellate; the shape must be able to surround a point completely.
• **Pattern Repetition:** Tessellations are essentially infinite patterns that can be extended in any direction.
• **Artistic Patterns:** Using colors and shapes to create visually appealing tessellated designs.
• **Symmetry in Tessellation:** Many tessellations rely on reflection and rotation to maintain the pattern.
• **Real-world Examples:** Honeycombs in beehives are a natural example of hexagonal tessellation.
• **Tessellation Design:** Creating a pattern by repeating a single tile in a grid or offset arrangement.
• **Pattern Analysis:** Identifying the repeating unit (the "tile") within a larger tessellated design.

• **Angular Patterns:** Patterns based on the size of angles (e.g., 45, 90, 135, 180 degrees).
• **Movement Sequences:** Describing a pattern as a series of steps or movements in a grid.
• **Spatial Reasoning:** The ability to visualize how a pattern changes its position or orientation in space.
• **Grid Navigation:** Using patterns to navigate through a grid by following a set of directional instructions.

• **Pattern Identification Strategy:** Step 1: Observe the sequence. Step 2: Identify the difference between terms. Step 3: Test the rule. Step 4: Apply the rule.
• **Difference Method:** Subtracting the first term from the second, the second from the third, etc., to find the common difference.
• **Ratio Method:** Dividing the second term by the first to find a common multiplier in geometric sequences.
• **Trial and Error:** A valid method for solving complex patterns when the rule is not immediately obvious.
• **Pattern Documentation:** Keeping a record of the sequence and the rule found to avoid errors.
• **Verification Step:** Always check the last term of the sequence against the rule to ensure consistency.
• **Pattern Generalization:** Moving from specific examples to a general rule that applies to any term in the sequence.
• **Visualizing Patterns:** Drawing the next few steps of a pattern to see if the logic holds up.
• **Pattern Complexity Management:** Breaking down a multi-step pattern into two simpler, single-step patterns.
• **Pattern Flexibility:** Recognizing that some patterns might have more than one valid rule or interpretation.
• **Logical Consistency:** A pattern is only valid if the rule is applied without exception throughout the entire series.
• **Pattern Documentation:** Using tables or lists to organize the elements of a pattern for easier analysis.
