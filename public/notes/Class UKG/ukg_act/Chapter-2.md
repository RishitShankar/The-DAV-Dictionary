• **Chapter Title**: Chapter 2: "Healthy Habits, Hygiene & Cleanliness" from the DAV Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board Class UKG Integrated Activity Book.
• **Academic Context**: Designed specifically for young learners in Upper Kindergarten (UKG) to establish fundamental life skills, daily routines, and personal well-being practices.
• **Core Subject Domain**: Early childhood health education, personal hygiene, self-care routines, sanitation, and holistic physical development.
• **Pedagogical Objective**: To transition toddlers from dependent toddlers to self-sufficient preschoolers who understand the value of keeping their bodies, clothes, and environments clean.
• **Foundational Principle**: **Health is Wealth**: The DAV framework emphasizes that a clean body houses an active, sharp, and joyful mind capable of effective learning.
• **Scope of Study**: The chapter comprehensively covers morning rituals, bathroom habits, dental hygiene, hair care, nail maintenance, proper clothing, handwashing techniques, and environmental cleanliness.
• **Definition of Healthy Habits**: **Healthy Habits**: Daily repetitive actions and routines performed consistently to maintain physical health, prevent diseases, and promote growth.
• **Definition of Personal Hygiene**: **Personal Hygiene**: The practice of keeping oneself and one's immediate surroundings clean to protect against harmful germs and infections.
• **Role of the School and Home**: Collaborative reinforcement between DAV educators and parents to ensure healthy practices learned in class are practiced daily at home.
• **Introduction to Germs**: Young learners are introduced to the concept of microscopic germs—invisible bugs that make us sick if we do not wash our hands and bodies.
• **Importance of Consistency**: Healthy habits must not be occasional treats but mandatory daily routines integrated into the child's morning-to-night schedule.
• **Visual Learning Approach**: The DAV curriculum utilizes sequential picture stories within the Integrated Activity Book to guide UKG students through correct hygienic behaviors.
• **Emotional Well-being**: Cleanliness contributes to feeling fresh, confident, and comfortable, enhancing positive social interactions among peers in the classroom.
• **Physical Readiness**: Good nutrition, adequate sleep, and clean habits prepare the child's body for active participation in sports, arts, and academic lessons.
• **Safety First**: Maintaining clean habits also prevents accidental poisonings, slips, falls, and infections within the school and home premises.

• **Morning Routine Initiation**: The day begins for a UKG student with waking up early, stretching the body, and greeting family members with a cheerful, positive mindset.
• **Bedmaking Habit**: **Bedmaking**: Children are encouraged to pull up their sheets and arrange their pillows to instill early responsibility, order, and independence.
• **Using the Toilet Correctly**: Learning proper toilet etiquette, including sitting correctly, flushing after use, and maintaining bathroom cleanliness for the next user.
• **Post-Toilet Handwashing**: Emphasizing that using the toilet must always be followed by thoroughly washing hands with soap and water to eliminate invisible waste bacteria.
• **Brushing the Teeth**: **Dental Hygiene**: Brushing teeth twice a day—once in the morning and once before going to bed—using a soft-bristled toothbrush and child-safe toothpaste.
• **Toothbrushing Technique**: Moving the toothbrush in gentle up-and-down and circular motions to clean the front, back, and chewing surfaces of every tooth.
• **Tongue Cleaning**: Gently scraping the tongue with a soft tongue cleaner or the back of the toothbrush to remove leftover food particles and morning breath bacteria.
• **Rinsing the Mouth**: Thoroughly swishing clean water inside the mouth after brushing and spitting it out into the basin without swallowing the paste or dirty water.
• **Saving Water**: Turning off the water tap while brushing teeth to practice water conservation—an important eco-friendly value taught in DAV schools.
• **Bath Time Routine**: Taking a daily bath using clean water and mild soap to wash away sweat, dust, pollution, and dead skin cells accumulated during play.
• **Washing All Body Parts**: Paying special attention to hidden areas like behind the ears, under the arms, between toes, and the neck during bath time.
• **Hair Care**: Washing hair with child-friendly shampoo regularly and using a clean comb or brush to keep hair untangled, neat, and free from dirt or lice.
• **Wiping Dry**: Using a personal, clean towel to dry the body thoroughly after a bath to prevent skin rashes, fungal infections, and catching a cold.
• **Clean Undergarments**: Wearing freshly laundered undergarments every single day to maintain intimate hygiene and personal freshness.
• **Dressing Up**: Wearing clean, ironed, and weather-appropriate clothes that fit comfortably, allowing free movement for running, jumping, and classroom activities.

• **Handwashing Fundamentals**: **Handwashing**: The single most effective defense against catching and spreading infectious diseases like colds, flu, and stomach bugs.
• **When to Wash Hands**: Handwashing is mandatory before eating meals, after using the restroom, after playing outdoors, after touching pets, and after coughing or sneezing.
• **The Step-by-Step Handwashing Process**: Wetting hands with clean running water, applying adequate soap, and rubbing palms together vigorously.
• **Interlocking Fingers**: Scrubbing between fingers and thumbs to ensure germs hiding in tight spaces are completely washed away with soap bubbles.
• **Cleaning Fingernails**: Scratching fingertips gently against palms with soap to clean underneath nails where dirt and bacteria frequently accumulate.
• **Wrist Scrubbing**: Remembering to wash wrists thoroughly as dirt and sweat easily settle on sleeve-covered or exposed wrist areas.
• **Rinsing Off Soap**: Rinsing hands under clean running water until all soap lather and loosened dirt are completely washed down the drain.
• **Drying Hands Properly**: Drying hands using a clean individual towel, paper towel, or air dryer to prevent wet hands from picking up new environmental germs.
• **The 20-Second Rule**: Encouraging children to take their time—roughly the time it takes to sing a short rhyme—while scrubbing their hands with soap.
• **Nail Care and Trimming**: **Nail Hygiene**: Keeping fingernails and toenails short and clean to prevent dirt buildup and accidental scratching of skin or classmates.
• **Parental Assistance in Trimming**: Recognizing that UKG students must have their nails trimmed regularly by parents or guardians using safe baby nail clippers.
• **No Biting Nails**: Discouraging the harmful habit of nail-biting, which transfers accumulated dirt, germs, and parasites directly from under the nails into the digestive system.
• **Ear Hygiene**: Cleaning outer ears gently with a soft, damp washcloth during bath time while strictly avoiding inserting sharp objects, pins, or cotton buds into the ear canal.
• **Eye Care**: Washing eyes gently with clean, splash water in the morning and avoiding rubbing eyes with dirty hands or unwashed sleeves to prevent infections like conjunctivitis.
• **Nose Hygiene**: Using a clean handkerchief or tissue to wipe a runny nose or blow the nose gently, and immediately disposing of used tissues in a covered dustbin.

• **Proper Attire for School**: Wearing the complete, neat, and well-pressed DAV school uniform every single day to foster discipline, equality, and school pride.
• **Uniform Maintenance**: Ensuring that socks and shoes are clean, polished, and free from unpleasant odors before wearing them to school or public places.
• **Shoe Hygiene**: Keeping shoes tidy, storing them properly on a shoe rack, and wearing clean cotton socks to absorb foot sweat and prevent fungal infections.
• **Pocket Essentials**: Carrying a clean handkerchief or tissue paper inside the uniform pocket at all times for wiping hands, face, or nose when required.
• **Hair Presentation**: Keeping hair neatly combed, oiled, and tied back securely with bands or clips so that stray strands do not disturb vision during studies.
• **Avoiding Torn Clothes**: Checking clothes for loose buttons, torn seams, or missing parts and getting them mended promptly to maintain a tidy appearance.
• **Eating Habits and Table Manners**: Integrating personal hygiene with mealtime behavior to ensure food is consumed safely and respectfully.
• **Pre-Meal Handwashing**: Making it an absolute rule to wash hands with soap and water immediately before sitting down to eat any meal or snack.
• **Clean Eating Surfaces**: Ensuring the dining table, desk, or lunchbox placement area is clean before opening food containers.
• **Using Cutlery and Napkins**: Learning how to use spoons, forks, and table napkins properly to avoid messy eating and keep clothes free from food stains.
• **Chewing with Mouth Closed**: Practicing polite eating habits by chewing food thoroughly with the mouth closed and avoiding talking with food inside the mouth.
• **Water Consumption**: Drinking clean, boiled, or filtered water from a personal, washed water bottle rather than sharing bottles to prevent the transmission of germs.
• **Post-Meal Cleanup**: Cleaning the mouth and hands with a damp napkin after eating and rinsing the mouth with water to remove food particles from teeth.
• **Lunchbox Hygiene**: Washing lunchboxes and water bottles daily with soap and warm water after returning home from school to prevent mold and bacterial growth.
• **No Junk Food Focus**: Understanding that clean and healthy eating includes fresh fruits, green vegetables, dal, and chapatis while avoiding stale or unhygienic street food.

• **Environmental Cleanliness**: Recognizing that personal hygiene is incomplete without maintaining cleanliness in the surrounding environment, classrooms, and home.
• **Use of Dustbins**: **The Golden Rule of Waste Disposal**: Always throwing paper scraps, pencil shavings, fruit peels, and wrappers into a dustbin and never on the floor.
• **Segregation Awareness**: Introduction to dry waste (paper, plastic) and wet waste (food leftovers) bins to teach early recycling and waste management concepts.
• **Classroom Cleanliness**: Keeping school desks, chairs, and learning corners neat, organized, and free from clutter to create a pleasant atmosphere for studying.
• **No Scribbling on Walls**: Understanding that walls, desks, and school property should not be drawn upon, defaced, or damaged with crayons or pencils.
• **Playground Hygiene**: Keeping school playgrounds, parks, and gardens clean by picking up litter and disposing of it properly after outdoor play sessions.
• **Plant Care**: Respecting nature by not plucking leaves, breaking flower stems, or damaging school plants, as greenery contributes to a clean and healthy environment.
• **Water Conservation in School**: Turning off school restroom taps completely after washing hands to prevent water wastage and keep bathroom floors dry and safe.
• **Keeping Rooms Ventilated**: Opening windows and doors at home and school to let fresh air and sunlight enter, which naturally kills germs and freshens indoor spaces.
• **Home Cleanliness Duties**: Helping parents by keeping toys, books, and shoes in their designated places instead of leaving them scattered on the floor.
• **Bedroom Organization**: Making the bed, folding blankets, and organizing school bags every evening to build systematic and orderly habits.
• **Kitchen Cleanliness**: Staying away from hot stoves, sharp knives, and gas cylinders while understanding that the kitchen must be kept clean to keep food safe.
• **Bathroom Safety and Cleanliness**: Keeping bathroom floors dry to prevent slipping accidents and ensuring soap and shampoo bottles are kept neatly on shelves.
• **Pet Hygiene**: If pets are kept at home, washing hands thoroughly after petting them, and keeping pet living areas clean and free from pests.
• **Community Cleanliness**: Learning the value of keeping neighborhood streets, parks, and public spaces clean for the well-being of the entire community.

• **Healthy Sleep Habits**: **Rest and Sleep**: A clean body needs adequate, restful sleep to grow, heal, and prepare for another active day of learning.
• **Early Sleep Schedule**: Going to bed early at night (by 8:30 or 9:00 PM) to ensure young children get 10 to 12 hours of uninterrupted, restorative sleep.
• **Bedtime Routine**: Washing feet, brushing teeth, changing into clean nightclothes, and reading a quiet bedtime story to signal the brain that it is time to sleep.
• **Clean Bedding**: Sleeping on clean bedsheets, pillowcases, and blankets that are washed and sun-dried regularly to prevent dust mites and skin irritations.
• **Good Posture**: Practicing good posture while sitting on classroom chairs, standing in assembly lines, and walking to prevent back pain and support bone growth.
• **Exercise and Play**: Engaging in daily physical activities, running, skipping, and yoga to keep muscles strong, joints flexible, and the heart healthy.
• **Sunlight and Fresh Air**: Spending time outdoors in the morning sunlight to absorb natural Vitamin D, which is essential for strong bones and teeth.
• **Screen Time Limits**: Limiting time spent watching television, mobile phones, or tablets to protect eyesight and ensure active physical play and social interaction.
• **Mental Cleanliness**: Cultivating positive thoughts, kindness, sharing with peers, and cheerful communication as part of overall mental health and hygiene.
• **Saying Magic Words**: Practicing polite habits—such as "Please," "Thank You," "Sorry," and "Good Morning"—which reflect a clean, disciplined, and beautiful character.
• **Handling Sickness**: Understanding that when someone falls sick, they must rest at home, take medicines prescribed by a doctor, and cover their mouth when coughing.
• **Preventing Disease Spread**: Using a tissue or elbow to cover the mouth and nose while sneezing or coughing to prevent respiratory droplets from spreading germs.
• **Isolation of Sick Items**: Keeping a sick child's towel, cup, and utensils separate from other family members to stop infections from spreading across the household.
• **First Aid Awareness**: Knowing that minor cuts and scrapes must be washed with clean water and shown immediately to a teacher or parent for antiseptic application and a bandage.
• **Doctor and Dentist Visits**: Visiting a pediatrician for regular check-ups and a dentist every six months to ensure teeth and body development are on the right track.

• **Teacher as a Role Model**: Teachers modeling exemplary personal hygiene—clean hands, neat uniforms, and organized desks—serving as immediate behavioral templates.
• **Parental Partnership**: Sending regular communication notes to parents detailing the week's cleanliness topic so families can practice the same routines at home.
• **Health Check-up Camps**: Participating in school-organized health camps where doctors examine children's eyes, teeth, and general growth parameters.
• **Nutrition and Cleanliness Link**: Discussing how eating washed fruits and vegetables with clean hands prevents stomach aches and keeps the tummy happy and strong.
• **Hydration Habits**: Emphasizing the importance of drinking clean water throughout the day to flush toxins out of the body and maintain skin hydration.

• **Summary of Daily Hygiene Checklist**: A comprehensive review of the daily routine expected from every DAV UKG student to maintain optimal health.
• **Checklist Item 1 - Morning**: Wake up early, brush teeth twice, use the toilet, and wash hands thoroughly with soap.
• **Checklist Item 2 - Bathing**: Take a daily bath, wash hair regularly, wipe dry with a clean towel, and wear fresh undergarments and a clean uniform.
• **Checklist Item 3 - Grooming**: Comb hair neatly, check that nails are trimmed and clean, and ensure shoes are polished and tidy.
• **Checklist Item 4 - Eating**: Wash hands before every meal, eat healthy home-cooked food, use a napkin, and drink clean water from a personal bottle.
• **Checklist Item 5 - School & Environment**: Throw all trash into dustbins, keep the desk and classroom organized, and respect plants and property.
• **Checklist Item 6 - Evening/Night**: Play outdoors, limit screen time, help parents tidy up toys, wash feet and hands, and sleep early for rest.
• **Key Vocabulary Term - Germs**: Tiny, invisible living things that can cause illness if personal hygiene and cleanliness are neglected.
• **Key Vocabulary Term - Sanitation**: The process of keeping places clean and healthy, especially through sewage and garbage disposal systems.
• **Key Vocabulary Term - Antiseptic**: A gentle liquid or cream applied to minor cuts to kill germs and prevent infection.
• **Key Vocabulary Term - Routine**: A sequence of regular actions performed habitually to build discipline and maintain well-being.
• **Key Vocabulary Term - Posture**: The position in which someone holds their body while standing, sitting, or walking.
