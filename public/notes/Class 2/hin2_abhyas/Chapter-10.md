• **Chapter Overview:** The chapter "दो तोते" (Two Parrots) is a moral-based narrative designed for Class 2 students to understand the impact of environment and company on character development.
• **Core Theme:** The story focuses on the concept of 'Sangat' (Company) and how it shapes the behavior, language, and personality of living beings.
• **Character Introduction:** The story features two parrots who are siblings, born from the same mother, but separated by circumstances early in their lives.
• **Setting the Scene:** The narrative begins in a dense forest where a hunter captures the two young parrots.
• **The Separation:** Due to a sudden storm and the hunter’s actions, the two parrots get separated, leading them to two entirely different environments.
• **Parrot One's Destination:** The first parrot reaches the vicinity of a group of robbers (dacoits), which becomes its primary learning environment.
• **Parrot Two's Destination:** The second parrot finds shelter near an Ashram (Hermitage) where sages and saints reside, influencing its growth.
• **Concept of Environment:** The chapter illustrates that the environment acts as a teacher, silently molding the habits of individuals.
• **Behavioral Contrast:** The first parrot learns the harsh, aggressive, and rude language used by the robbers.
• **Linguistic Development:** The second parrot learns the polite, calm, and welcoming language used by the sages in the Ashram.
• **The King's Arrival:** The story introduces a King who enters the forest while hunting and loses his way, becoming the catalyst for the parrots' interaction.
• **The First Encounter:** The King reaches the robbers' hideout and hears the first parrot shouting commands to rob the traveler.
• **The King's Reaction:** The King is shocked by the parrot's aggressive tone and realizes the danger of the location.
• **The Second Encounter:** The King moves further and reaches the Ashram, where he hears the second parrot inviting him to rest and drink water.
• **The Lesson of Speech:** The chapter highlights that speech is a reflection of the company one keeps.
• **Moral Foundation:** The DAV curriculum emphasizes 'Sanskars' (values), and this chapter serves as a foundational lesson in choosing good friends.
• **Vocabulary Focus:** Students learn terms like 'लुटेरे' (Robbers), 'आश्रम' (Hermitage), and 'सत्संग' (Good Company).

• **Character Analysis - Parrot 1:** This parrot represents the influence of negative company, characterized by harsh words like "पकड़ो" (catch) and "मारो" (hit).
• **Character Analysis - Parrot 2:** This parrot represents the influence of positive company, characterized by gentle words like "स्वागत है" (welcome) and "बैठिए" (please sit).
• **The Role of the Hunter:** The hunter serves as the initial agent of separation, highlighting how external events can change the trajectory of life.
• **The Concept of 'Sangat':** The Hindi term 'संगति' (Sangat) is central to the chapter, meaning the association or company one keeps.
• **The Proverb Connection:** The chapter implicitly teaches the Hindi proverb "जैसी संगति, वैसी रंगति" (As the company, so the color/character).
• **The King as an Observer:** The King acts as the bridge between the two environments, allowing the reader to compare the two parrots side-by-side.
• **The Ashram Environment:** The Ashram is depicted as a place of peace, meditation, and kindness, which directly influences the parrot's vocabulary.
• **The Robbers' Den Environment:** The den is depicted as a place of chaos and greed, which directly influences the first parrot's vocabulary.
• **Linguistic Nuance:** The chapter teaches students that language is not innate but learned through imitation of those around us.
• **The Power of Words:** The story demonstrates how words can either threaten (Parrot 1) or comfort (Parrot 2).
• **The King’s Realization:** Upon meeting the second parrot, the King realizes that the same species can behave differently based on their upbringing.
• **The Sage's Explanation:** The sage explains to the King that the parrots are brothers, but their different environments led to their different behaviors.
• **Educational Objective:** To teach students the importance of choosing friends who are kind and well-mannered.
• **Value Education:** The chapter promotes the DAV value of 'Satsang' (being in the company of good people).
• **Narrative Structure:** The story follows a logical progression: Separation -> Different Environments -> King's Journey -> Comparison -> Moral Conclusion.
• **Vocabulary Building:** Students are introduced to descriptive adjectives used to define the nature of the two parrots.
• **Critical Thinking:** The chapter encourages students to think about how their own friends influence their daily behavior.
