• **Concept of Muhavare:** A Muhavara is a group of words that functions as a single unit to convey a specific, often metaphorical, meaning.
• **Literal vs. Figurative:** Students must distinguish between the literal meaning (shabdik arth) and the special meaning (vishesh arth) of an idiom.
• **Example 1: 'आँख का तारा' (Aankh ka tara):** Literally means 'star of the eye,' but the figurative meaning is 'bahut pyara' (very dear/beloved).
• **Example 2: 'दाँत खट्टे करना' (Daant khatte karna):** Literally means 'to make teeth sour,' but the figurative meaning is 'buri tarah harana' (to defeat soundly).
• **Example 3: 'नौ दो ग्यारह होना' (Nau do gyarah hona):** Literally means 'nine and two make eleven,' but the figurative meaning is 'bhag jana' (to run away/escape).
• **Example 4: 'घी के दीये जलाना' (Ghee ke diye jalana):** Literally means 'lighting lamps of ghee,' but the figurative meaning is 'khushi manana' (to celebrate/rejoice).
• **Example 5: 'हाथ बँटाना' (Haath bantana):** Literally means 'to share a hand,' but the figurative meaning is 'madad karna' (to help/assist).
• **Example 6: 'आसमान सिर पर उठाना' (Aasman sir par uthana):** Literally means 'to lift the sky on one's head,' but the figurative meaning is 'bahut shor machana' (to make a lot of noise).
• **Example 7: 'पेट में चूहे दौड़ना' (Pet mein chuhe daudna):** Literally means 'mice running in the stomach,' but the figurative meaning is 'bahut bhook lagna' (to be very hungry).
• **Example 8: 'फूला न समाना' (Phoola na samana):** Literally means 'not being able to fit due to swelling,' but the figurative meaning is 'bahut khush hona' (to be overjoyed).
• **Example 9: 'अक्ल का दुश्मन' (Akl ka dushman):** Literally means 'enemy of intelligence,' but the figurative meaning is 'murkh' (foolish person).
• **Example 10: 'ईंट से ईंट बजाना' (Eent se eent bajana):** Literally means 'to strike brick with brick,' but the figurative meaning is 'vinash karna' (to destroy completely).
• **Sentence Construction:** The chapter emphasizes that idioms must be used in the 'Vishesh Arth' (Special Meaning) when creating sentences, not the literal one.
• **Grammatical Rule:** Idioms do not change their form; they remain fixed expressions in the Hindi language.
• **Usage Tip:** Always ensure the idiom fits the context of the sentence naturally to maintain the flow of the narrative.
