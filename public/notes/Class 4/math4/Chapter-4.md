• **Division**: The mathematical process of repeated subtraction or splitting a total quantity into equal groups.
• **Dividend**: The number that is being divided or the total amount available to be shared.
• **Divisor**: The number by which the dividend is divided, representing the size of each group or the number of groups.
• **Quotient**: The final result obtained after performing the division operation.
• **Remainder**: The amount left over when a number cannot be divided exactly into equal groups.
• **Division Symbol**: Represented by the sign (÷), indicating the operation of sharing or partitioning.
• **Inverse Operation**: Division is the inverse or opposite operation of multiplication.
• **Repeated Subtraction**: A method of division where the divisor is repeatedly subtracted from the dividend until zero or a remainder is reached.
• **Equal Sharing**: The core concept of division where items are distributed so that every group receives the exact same amount.
• **Equal Grouping**: The concept of forming groups of a specific size from a larger set.
• **Zero Property of Division**: Any number divided by 1 equals the number itself (e.g., 5 ÷ 1 = 5).
• **Division by Self**: Any non-zero number divided by itself always results in 1 (e.g., 8 ÷ 8 = 1).
• **Division of Zero**: Zero divided by any non-zero number always results in 0 (e.g., 0 ÷ 7 = 0).
• **Undefined Division**: Division by zero is mathematically undefined or impossible in basic arithmetic.
• **Basic Fact**: Division facts are derived directly from multiplication tables (e.g., if 3 × 4 = 12, then 12 ÷ 4 = 3).

• **Long Division**: A systematic method for dividing larger numbers by breaking the process into smaller, manageable steps.
• **Division Algorithm**: The fundamental relationship expressed as: Dividend = (Divisor × Quotient) + Remainder.
• **Verification**: The process of checking the accuracy of a division result using the algorithm formula.
• **Place Value in Division**: Division always begins from the highest place value digit of the dividend (leftmost digit).
• **Step 1 (Divide)**: Determine how many times the divisor fits into the current digit or digits of the dividend.
• **Step 2 (Multiply)**: Multiply the quotient digit by the divisor to find the value to be subtracted.
• **Step 3 (Subtract)**: Subtract the product from the dividend part to find the difference.
• **Step 4 (Bring Down)**: Bring down the next digit from the dividend to continue the process.
• **Remainder Constraint**: The remainder must always be strictly smaller than the divisor.
• **Zero Remainder**: When the remainder is 0, the division is considered "exact" or "complete."
• **Non-Zero Remainder**: When the remainder is greater than 0, the division is considered "with remainder."
• **Mental Division**: The ability to divide numbers quickly using basic multiplication facts and place value understanding.
• **Dividing by 10**: When dividing by 10, the last digit of the dividend becomes the remainder, and the remaining digits form the quotient.

• **Dividing by 1000**: When dividing by 1000, the last three digits of the dividend become the remainder, and the remaining digits form the quotient.
