• **Concept of a Fraction**: A fraction represents a part of a whole object or a part of a collection of objects.
• **The Whole**: When an object is considered as a single unit, it is referred to as the 'whole'.
• **Equal Parts**: Fractions are only defined when a whole is divided into equal-sized parts.
• **Fractional Parts**: If a whole is divided into two equal parts, each part is called 'one-half'.
• **One-Half Representation**: One-half is written numerically as 1/2.
• **Division into Three**: When a whole is divided into three equal parts, each part is called 'one-third'.
• **One-Third Representation**: One-third is written numerically as 1/3.
• **Division into Four**: When a whole is divided into four equal parts, each part is called 'one-fourth' or a 'quarter'.
• **One-Fourth Representation**: One-fourth is written numerically as 1/4.
• **Numerator**: The top number in a fraction that tells us how many parts of the whole we are considering.
• **Denominator**: The bottom number in a fraction that tells us the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into.
• **Fraction Bar**: The horizontal line that separates the numerator and the denominator.
• **Reading Fractions**: A fraction like 2/3 is read as 'two-thirds'.
• **Visualizing Fractions**: Shading parts of a shape is the primary method to visualize fractional values.
• **Equality Requirement**: If parts are unequal, they do not represent a standard fraction.

• **Numerator Function**: The numerator acts as the 'counter' of the fractional parts.
• **Denominator Function**: The denominator acts as the 'divider' or the 'total count' of parts.
• **Unit Fraction**: A fraction where the numerator is always 1, such as 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, etc.
• **Non-Unit Fraction**: A fraction where the numerator is greater than 1, such as 2/3, 3/4, or 5/6.
• **Two-Thirds**: This represents two parts out of three equal parts of a whole.
• **Three-Fourths**: This represents three parts out of four equal parts of a whole.
• **Writing Fractions**: To write a fraction, first count the total parts (denominator) and then count the shaded parts (numerator).
• **Fraction of a Collection**: Fractions can also represent parts of a group, such as 2 apples out of 5 apples.
• **Group Denominator**: In a collection, the denominator is the total number of items in the group.
• **Group Numerator**: In a collection, the numerator is the number of items being selected or highlighted.
• **Example of Collection**: If there are 4 balls and 1 is red, the fraction of red balls is 1/4.
• **Example of Collection**: If there are 5 stars and 3 are blue, the fraction of blue stars is 3/5.
• **Importance of Equality**: In collections, each item is treated as one 'part' of the total group.
• **Fractional Language**: Using terms like 'half', 'third', 'fourth', 'fifth', and 'sixth' is essential for verbal communication of math.
• **Denominator Significance**: As the denominator increases, the size of each individual part decreases, assuming the whole remains the same.

• **Comparing Fractions**: Comparing fractions with the same denominator is done by looking at the numerator.
• **Rule of Same Denominator**: When denominators are the same, the fraction with the larger numerator is the larger fraction.
• **Visual Comparison**: Drawing two identical shapes and shading different amounts helps in comparing fractions visually.
• **Example of Comparison**: 3/5 is greater than 2/5 because 3 parts are more than 2 parts of the same size.
• **Ordering Fractions**: Fractions can be arranged in ascending order (smallest to largest) or descending order (largest to smallest).
• **Ascending Order**: Start by identifying the smallest numerator when denominators are identical.
• **Descending Order**: Start by identifying the largest numerator when denominators are identical.
• **Equivalent Fractions**: Fractions that represent the same part of a whole even if the numbers look different.
• **Visualizing Equivalence**: 1/2 of a circle is the same amount as 2/4 of the same circle.
• **Simplification Concept**: Though advanced, understanding that 2/4 covers the same area as 1/2 is a foundational skill for Class 3.
• **Fractional Parts of a Shape**: A square divided into 4 equal triangles means each triangle is 1/4 of the square.
• **Fractional Parts of a Rectangle**: A rectangle divided into 2 equal rectangles means each is 1/2 of the whole.
• **Identifying Fractions**: Always check if the parts are equal before naming the fraction.
• **Common Errors**: Mistaking 1/4 for 1/3 because of visual estimation without counting parts.
• **Precision**: Always count the total number of parts carefully to determine the correct denominator.
