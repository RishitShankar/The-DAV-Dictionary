• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to Chapter 3 of Class LKG Environmental Studies (EVS) for The DAV Dictionary, titled "Healthy Food & Good Habits".
• **Core Objective**: This chapter introduces young learners to the fundamental concepts of nutrition, personal hygiene, and daily routines that promote physical well-being.
• **Target Audience**: Designed specifically for four-to-five-year-old students under the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board curriculum framework.
• **Holistic Development**: The curriculum integrates dietary awareness with hygienic practices to foster early childhood development and healthy lifestyle choices.
• **Nutritional Foundation**: Children learn to differentiate between food choices that nourish the body and those that cause lethargy or illness.
• **Habit Formation**: The chapter emphasizes repetitive daily actions that turn into lifelong habits of cleanliness and self-care.
• **DAV Philosophy**: Aligns with the DAV ethos of balanced living, purity of mind and body, and respect for nature's bounty.
• **Visual Learning Strategy**: Concepts are presented through vivid descriptions, encouraging teachers to use real-life objects, fruits, and hygiene kits in the classroom.
• **Parental Partnership**: Encourages parents to reinforce school lessons at home by preparing nutritious tiffins and supervising hygiene routines.
• **Classroom Environment**: Promotes a clean, organized, and health-conscious classroom environment where students practice sharing and handwashing together.
• **Sensory Engagement**: Encourages children to explore food through taste, smell, color, and texture to build an instinctive appreciation for natural foods.
• **Age-Appropriate Language**: Uses simple, repetitive terminology to ensure maximum comprehension and vocabulary retention for LKG learners.
• **Sequential Flow**: The chapter systematically moves from defining food to categorizing it, understanding meal times, practicing table manners, transitioning to personal hygiene, and finally establishing a daily routine.
• **Preventive Health**: Early introduction to cleanliness helps minimize common childhood illnesses like stomach infections and the common cold.
• **Emotional Well-being**: Proper nutrition and healthy habits directly correlate with a happy, energetic mood and better focus during learning activities.

• **Definition of Food**: **Food** is any nutritious substance that people eat or drink in order to maintain life, grow, and stay active.
• **Energy Source**: Food acts as the primary fuel for the human body, providing the necessary energy to run, jump, play, and think.
• **Growth Catalyst**: Nutritious food supplies essential building blocks like proteins and vitamins that help children grow taller and stronger every day.
• **Immunity Booster**: A balanced diet strengthens the body's immune system, helping little kids fight off germs and harmful bacteria.
• **Source of Freshness**: Fresh food revitalizes the body and keeps the mind alert, preventing fatigue during school hours.
• **Natural vs. Processed**: The DAV curriculum emphasizes eating natural foods directly from nature rather than heavily manufactured, packaged items.
• **Plant-Based Foods**: A vast majority of healthy foods come from plants, including delicious fruits, crisp vegetables, and wholesome grains.
• **Animal-Based Foods**: Animals provide essential food items like milk, eggs, and dairy products that are crucial for growing children.
• **Water as Food Companion**: Clean drinking water is treated as an essential companion to food, keeping the body hydrated and flushing out toxins.
• **Hunger and Satiety**: Children learn to recognize the natural feeling of hunger and understand when their tummy is comfortably full.
• **The Tiffin Box Concept**: The school tiffin box is introduced as a portable source of home-cooked, healthy energy for the school day.
• **Sharing is Caring**: DAV values encourage children to share healthy portions of their food with classmates, fostering friendship and empathy.
• **Gratitude for Food**: Students are taught to feel thankful for the food on their plates, recognizing the hard work of farmers and parents.
• **No Food Waste**: An early lesson in sustainability teaches children to take only as much food as they can finish without wasting.
• **Chewing Properly**: The digestive process begins in the mouth; children learn the importance of chewing their food slowly and thoroughly.

• **Healthy Food Classification**: **Healthy Food** encompasses natural, wholesome items that provide optimal nutrition without harmful chemical additives.
• **Role of Fruits**: Fruits are sweet, juicy plant products packed with natural sugars, vitamins, and fiber that keep the body glowing and healthy.
• **Specific Fruit Benefits**: Apples, bananas, papayas, and mangoes are highlighted as powerhouse fruits that provide instant energy and aid digestion.
• **Role of Vegetables**: Vegetables are savory plant parts, including roots, leaves, and stems, that supply vital minerals and protect against diseases.
• **Specific Vegetable Benefits**: Leafy greens like spinach, root vegetables like carrots, and cruciferous veggies like broccoli build strong bones and clear vision.
• **Role of Grains**: Wheat, rice, and corn form the grain group, supplying complex carbohydrates that keep children energetic throughout the day.
• **Role of Pulses and Lentils**: Dals and legumes are packed with plant proteins that repair body tissues and support muscle growth.
• **Importance of Milk**: Milk is hailed as a complete food containing calcium for strong teeth and bones, essential for growing LKG students.
• **Dairy Products**: Curd, paneer, and butter derived from milk offer delicious and nutritious alternatives that children love to eat.
• **Eggs and Nutrition**: For non-vegetarian families, eggs provide high-quality protein and essential fatty acids for brain development.
• **Homemade Meals**: Freshly cooked rotis, dal, rice, and vegetable curry prepared at home are identified as the gold standard of healthy eating.
• **Color Diversity**: Children are taught to eat a "rainbow" of fruits and vegetables to ensure a wide spectrum of vitamins and minerals.
• **Fiber and Digestion**: High-fiber foods like whole grains and raw salads help keep the tummy clean and prevent constipation.
• **Natural Sweetness**: Natural sugars found in fruits are promoted as healthy alternatives to refined white sugar.
• **Hydration through Food**: Juicy fruits like watermelons and oranges help maintain the body's water balance naturally.

• **Junk Food Definition**: **Junk Food** refers to commercially processed food items that are high in calories, unhealthy fats, sugar, and salt, but low in nutritional value.
• **Lack of Nutrients**: Junk foods do not help children grow tall or strong; instead, they fill the stomach with empty calories.
• **Common Examples**: Burgers, pizzas, french fries, potato chips, and instant noodles are classified as typical junk foods.
• **Sugary Treats**: Candies, lollipops, chocolates, and carbonated soft drinks fall under harmful junk items due to excessive sugar content.
• **Tooth Decay Risk**: High sugar intake from junk food sticks to teeth, inviting bacteria that cause painful cavities and toothaches.
• **Lethargy and Fatigue**: Eating heavy, greasy junk food makes children feel sluggish, lazy, and tired instead of active and playful.
• **Digestive Distress**: Processed foods lack dietary fiber, leading to stomach aches, indigestion, and unhealthy bowel habits.
• **Weight Issues**: Unchecked consumption of junk food can lead to early childhood obesity and related health complications.
• **Addictive Nature**: Artificial flavors and excessive sodium make junk foods intensely addictive, making it harder for children to choose healthy options.
• **Occasional Treat Rule**: While complete restriction is difficult, junk food must be treated strictly as an occasional, rare treat rather than a daily habit.
• **Marketing Awareness**: Children are gently guided to understand that glossy advertisements for junk food do not represent true health benefits.
• **Alternative Snacks**: Healthy swaps are introduced, such as replacing potato chips with roasted makhana (fox nuts) or fruit slices.
• **Role Models**: Teachers and parents must model good eating behavior by avoiding junk food consumption in front of young children.
• **School Canteen Policy**: DAV schools promote strict guidelines ensuring only wholesome, nutritious snacks are available on campus.
• **Developing Discernment**: LKG students practice identifying whether a food item belongs to the "Healthy" group or the "Junk" group using flashcards.

• **Meal Times Structure**: The day is divided into distinct, regular meal times to regulate the body's internal clock and metabolism.
• **Breakfast Definition**: **Breakfast** is the first and most important meal of the day, breaking the overnight fasting period after sleep.
• **Breakfast Energy**: A hearty breakfast fuels the brain, helping LKG students concentrate, learn, and play actively during morning classes.
• **Lunch Definition**: **Lunch** is the midday meal that replenishes declining energy levels halfway through the day.
• **Lunch Composition**: A balanced lunch typically includes chapati, rice, dal, and cooked vegetables to sustain afternoon activities.
• **Evening Snack**: A light, nutritious evening snack like a glass of milk with dry fruits or a fresh fruit prevents excessive hunger before dinner.
• **Dinner Definition**: **Dinner** is the final evening meal, kept light and easily digestible to ensure a peaceful night's sleep.
• **Timely Eating**: Eating meals at fixed, regular times every day prevents stomach upset and promotes smooth digestion.
• **No Skipping Meals**: Children learn that skipping meals, especially breakfast, makes them weak, cranky, and unable to focus.
• **Mindful Eating**: Meals should be consumed in a calm environment without the distraction of television, mobile phones, or toys.
• **Sitting Posture**: Children are taught to sit upright and comfortably at the dining table or floor mat while eating their meals.
• **Pacing the Meal**: Eating slowly and mindfully allows the brain to register fullness, preventing overeating.
• **Water Timing**: Drinking large amounts of water immediately before or during heavy meals should be avoided to aid proper digestive enzyme action.
• **Family Meals**: Encouraging children to eat dinner with family members promotes social bonding and healthy conversation habits.
• **Pre-Bed Routine**: Brushing teeth after dinner and before sleep is emphasized as the final food-related hygiene rule.

• **Table Manners Definition**: **Table Manners** are the polite rules of behavior we follow while eating food with others.
• **Hand Hygiene First**: The primary rule of dining is washing hands thoroughly with soap and water before touching any food item.
• **Clean Napkin Use**: Keeping a clean napkin or handkerchief on the lap or nearby helps wipe fingers and mouth cleanly.
• **Using Cutlery Gracefully**: Learning to hold spoons correctly and using them without making clattering noises is a key social skill.
• **No Talking with Full Mouth**: Children are taught never to speak or laugh while food is inside their mouth to prevent choking hazards.
• **Chewing with Closed Mouth**: Good manners dictate that food should be chewed quietly with the mouth firmly closed.
• **No Spilling**: Making a conscious effort to keep the plate and dining area tidy without dropping food grains everywhere.
• **Passing Dishes Politely**: Instead of grabbing food across the table, children learn to ask politely for items to be passed to them.
• **Using Magic Words**: Saying "Please" when asking for food and "Thank You" when served by parents or helpers is mandatory.
• **Finishing the Portion**: Respecting food by eating everything served on the plate without leaving unfinished leftovers.
• **Sitting Until Everyone Finishes**: Cultivating patience by waiting politely at the table until older family members or peers finish their meals.
• **Clearing the Plate**: Learning to place the empty plate or tiffin box in the designated washing area after eating.
• **Rinsing the Mouth**: Swishing clean water in the mouth and spitting it out after eating helps remove leftover food particles.
• **No Playing with Food**: Food is precious; tossing, throwing, or playing with vegetables and grains is strictly discouraged.
• **Respecting Servers**: Acknowledging the efforts of mothers, school cooks, and servers with gratitude and polite manners.

• **Good Habits Definition**: **Good Habits** are positive, repetitive daily actions that keep our mind, body, and surroundings clean and healthy.
• **Morning Routine**: Waking up early in the morning with a cheerful attitude sets a positive tone for the entire day.
• **Greeting Elders**: Saying "Good Morning" to parents, grandparents, and teachers fosters warmth, respect, and positive social interaction.
• **Brushing Teeth Twice**: **Dental Hygiene** requires brushing teeth every morning after waking up and every night before going to bed.
• **Proper Brushing Technique**: Moving the toothbrush in gentle up-and-down and circular motions cleans all tooth surfaces effectively.
• **Tongue Cleaning**: Gently cleaning the tongue using a soft cleaner removes bacteria and ensures fresh breath.
• **Bath and Freshness**: Taking a bath daily with clean water and soap washes away sweat, dust, and invisible germs from the skin.
• **Hair Care**: Combing hair neatly every morning prevents tangles, keeps pests away, and presents a tidy appearance.
• **Nail Hygiene**: Trimming fingernails and toenails short every week prevents dirt and dangerous germs from hiding underneath.
• **Clean Clothing**: Wearing freshly washed, ironed clothes every day makes the child look neat and feel comfortable.
• **Toilet Training & Hygiene**: Using the toilet properly and flushing afterwards is a crucial independent life skill for LKG students.
• **Handwashing After Toilet**: Washing hands thoroughly with soap after using the restroom is a non-negotiable health rule.
• **Footwear Care**: Removing shoes outside the living area and keeping footwear neatly arranged prevents dirt from tracking indoors.
• **Clean Environment**: Throwing waste paper, pencil shavings, and fruit peels strictly into the dustbin keeps the surroundings clean.
• **Sleep Hygiene**: Going to bed early at night ensures 10-11 hours of restful sleep necessary for rapid childhood growth.

• **Handwashing Mechanism**: **Handwashing** is the single most effective defense mechanism against the spread of infectious germs and diseases.
• **When to Wash Hands**: Hands must be washed before eating, after using the toilet, after playing outside, and after touching pets.
• **Step 1 - Wetting**: Wet hands thoroughly under clean, running tap water.
• **Step 2 - Laps of Soap**: Apply enough liquid soap or bar soap to cover all hand surfaces completely.
• **Step 3 - Palm to Palm**: Rub hands palm to palm vigorously to create a rich, germ-trapping lather.
• **Step 4 - Between Fingers**: Interlace fingers and rub back and forth to clean hidden spaces between digits.
• **Step 5 - Back of Hands**: Rub the back of each hand with the palm of the opposite hand, including the knuckles.
• **Step 6 - Thumb Cleaning**: Clasp each thumb in the opposite palm and rotate to clean thoroughly.
• **Step 7 - Fingernails**: Scratch fingertips gently against the opposite palm to clean underneath the nails.
• **Step 8 - Wrist Scrubbing**: Rotate hands around the wrists to remove hidden dust and perspiration.
• **Step 9 - Rinsing**: Rinse both hands thoroughly under clean running water until all soap residue disappears.
• **Step 10 - Drying**: Dry hands completely using a clean, personal towel, handkerchief, or air dryer.
• **Germ Visibility**: Children learn that germs are microscopic "invisible monsters" that make us sick if we do not wash them away.
• **Preventing Illness**: Proper handwashing stops stomach bugs, colds, and flu from passing from person to person.
• **Independence in Hygiene**: LKG students practice independent handwashing drills daily under teacher supervision until the steps become second nature.

• **Personal Grooming Standards**: **Personal Grooming** involves maintaining a neat, clean, and respectable physical appearance every single day.
• **Hair Grooming**: Applying a few drops of clean oil and combing hair into a neat style prevents dandruff and scalp infections.
• **Ear Care**: Cleaning outer ears gently with a soft, damp towel under parental supervision prevents wax accumulation and hearing issues.
• **Eye Care**: Washing eyes with cool, clean water each morning refreshes vision and removes sleep residue.
• **Nose Hygiene**: Using a clean handkerchief or tissue to blow the nose gently prevents the spread of nasal mucus and germs.
• **Foot Hygiene**: Washing feet thoroughly after playing outdoors and wearing clean cotton socks prevents fungal infections and foot odor.
• **Uniform Neatness**: DAV students take pride in wearing their school uniform neatly, keeping buttons fastened and shoes polished.
• **Pocket Essentials**: Every child should carry a clean handkerchief in their uniform pocket for wiping hands, nose, or mouth.
• **Posture and Walking**: Standing straight and walking with a balanced posture prevents skeletal strain during developmental years.
• **Smile and Attitude**: Wearing a bright smile and maintaining a cheerful, polite attitude completes the ideal grooming of a DAV child.
• **Protecting Skin**: Applying sunscreen or wearing hats when playing out in harsh sunlight protects tender young skin from burns.
• **Independent Dressing**: LKG children practice buttoning shirts, wearing socks, and pulling up shorts independently to build motor skills.
• **Seasonal Hygiene**: Adjusting hygiene habits according to seasons—such as staying hydrated in summer and moisturizing in winter—is taught.
• **Sharing Ban**: Emphasizing that personal grooming items like combs, toothbrushes, and handkerchiefs should never be shared with others.
• **Daily Checklist**: Teachers use visual charts to help children check off their daily grooming items each morning upon arrival.
