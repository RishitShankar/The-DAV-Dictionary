• **Number Concept (0-100)**: This chapter focuses on the foundational understanding of numbers, their sequence, and their representation in the decimal system.
• **The Concept of Zero (0)**: Zero represents the absence of objects or a null quantity, serving as the essential placeholder in our number system.
• **Single-Digit Numbers**: Numbers from 0 to 9 are known as single-digit numbers, representing individual units.
• **Counting Sequence**: The natural counting sequence begins at 1 and proceeds incrementally by adding one to the previous number.
• **One-to-One Correspondence**: This is the process of matching each object in a set to a specific number, ensuring accurate counting.
• **Cardinal Numbers**: These numbers (1, 2, 3...) tell us "how many" objects are present in a given collection.
• **Ordinal Numbers**: These numbers (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) indicate the position or rank of an object in a specific sequence.
• **Number Names**: Every digit and number has a corresponding word form, such as 'One' for 1, 'Two' for 2, and so on, up to 100.
• **The Number 10**: The number 10 is the first two-digit number, formed by combining one 'ten' and zero 'ones'.
• **Place Value Foundation**: Understanding that the position of a digit determines its value is critical for mastering numbers up to 100.
• **The Ones Place**: The rightmost position in a two-digit number represents the count of individual units.
• **The Tens Place**: The position to the left of the ones place represents groups of ten units.
• **Grouping by Tens**: Organizing objects into bundles of ten simplifies the counting and representation of larger quantities.
• **Base-10 System**: Our number system is based on groups of ten, where ten ones are regrouped to form one ten.
• **Number Representation**: Numbers can be represented using physical objects, tally marks, or numerical symbols.

• **Numbers 11 to 20**: These numbers are formed by adding units to a base of ten, such as 10 + 1 = 11 (eleven).
• **Teen Numbers**: Numbers from 13 to 19 are referred to as 'teen' numbers, all sharing the common suffix '-teen'.
• **The Number 20**: This represents two groups of ten and zero ones, written as 20 (twenty).
• **Place Value of 20**: In the number 20, the digit '2' occupies the tens place, and the digit '0' occupies the ones place.
• **Counting Forward**: This is the process of moving from a smaller number to a larger number by adding one repeatedly.
• **Counting Backward**: This is the process of moving from a larger number to a smaller number by subtracting one repeatedly.
• **Number Line**: A visual tool consisting of a straight line with numbers marked at equal intervals to represent the order of numbers.
• **Using the Number Line**: The number line helps in visualizing the distance between numbers and understanding the concept of 'greater than' and 'less than'.
• **Comparing Numbers**: Comparing involves determining if a number is larger, smaller, or equal to another number.
• **Greater Than (> )**: A symbol used to indicate that the number on the left is larger than the number on the right.
• **Less Than (< )**: A symbol used to indicate that the number on the left is smaller than the number on the right.
• **Equal To (= )**: A symbol used to indicate that two numbers represent the exact same quantity.
• **Comparing Tens**: When comparing two-digit numbers, always compare the digits in the tens place first.
• **Comparing Ones**: If the digits in the tens place are identical, compare the digits in the ones place to determine the larger number.
• **Ascending Order**: Arranging numbers from the smallest value to the largest value.

---PAGE-BREAK---

• **Descending Order**: Arranging numbers from the largest value to the smallest value.
• **Successor**: The number that comes immediately after a given number, found by adding 1.
• **Predecessor**: The number that comes immediately before a given number, found by subtracting 1.
• **Between Numbers**: The number that lies in the middle of two given numbers in a sequence.
• **Expanded Form**: Breaking down a two-digit number into its tens and ones components, such as 34 = 30 + 4.
• **Standard Form**: The compact numerical representation of a number, such as 34.
• **Numbers 21 to 30**: These numbers follow the pattern of 'twenty' followed by the single-digit name (e.g., twenty-one, twenty-two).
• **The Tens Digit**: In the range 21-29, the tens digit remains '2', representing two groups of ten.
• **The Ones Digit**: In the range 21-29, the ones digit changes sequentially from 1 to 9.
• **Thirty (30)**: This number represents three groups of ten, written as 30.
• **Place Value of 30**: The digit '3' is in the tens place, and '0' is in the ones place.
• **Numbers 31 to 40**: These numbers follow the pattern of 'thirty' followed by the single-digit name.
• **Pattern Recognition**: Recognizing that the ones digit cycles from 1 to 9 while the tens digit remains constant within each decade.
• **Forty (40)**: This number represents four groups of ten, written as 40.
• **Place Value of 40**: The digit '4' is in the tens place, and '0' is in the ones place.

• **The Number 90**: This represents nine groups of ten, written as 90 (ninety).
• **Place Value of 90**: The digit '9' is in the tens place, and '0' is in the ones place.
• **Numbers 91 to 99**: These numbers follow the pattern of 'ninety' followed by the single-digit name.
• **The Number 100**: This is the first three-digit number, representing one hundred, or ten groups of ten.
• **Place Value of 100**: The digit '1' is in the hundreds place, '0' is in the tens place, and '0' is in the ones place.
• **Hundreds Place**: The position to the left of the tens place, representing groups of one hundred.
• **Reading Numbers**: To read a two-digit number, state the tens value followed by the ones value (e.g., 56 is fifty-six).
• **Writing Numbers**: To write a number, identify the tens digit and the ones digit and combine them into the correct word form.
• **Skip Counting by 2s**: Counting by skipping one number each time (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8, 10).
• **Skip Counting by 5s**: Counting by skipping four numbers each time (e.g., 5, 10, 15, 20).
• **Skip Counting by 10s**: Counting by skipping nine numbers each time (e.g., 10, 20, 30, 40).
• **Even Numbers**: Numbers that can be divided into two equal groups without any remainder (ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8).
• **Odd Numbers**: Numbers that cannot be divided into two equal groups without a remainder (ending in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9).
• **Identifying Odd/Even**: Look at the digit in the ones place to determine if a number is odd or even.
• **Zero as an Even Number**: By mathematical convention, 0 is considered an even number.

• **Abacus Representation**: A tool with rods and beads used to represent numbers by placing beads in the ones and tens columns.
• **Ones Rod**: The rightmost rod on an abacus where each bead represents one unit.
• **Tens Rod**: The rod to the left of the ones rod where each bead represents ten units.
• **Reading an Abacus**: Count the number of beads on each rod to determine the number represented.
• **Representing 0 on Abacus**: If a rod has no beads, it signifies that the value for that place is zero.
• **Maximum Capacity of Rods**: Each rod on a standard primary abacus can hold up to nine beads.
• **Regrouping on Abacus**: When a tenth bead is added to the ones rod, all ten beads are removed, and one bead is added to the tens rod.
