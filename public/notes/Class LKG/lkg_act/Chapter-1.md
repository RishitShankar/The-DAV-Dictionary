• **Chapter Overview**: Welcome to Chapter 1 of the DAV Class LKG Integrated Activity Book, titled "Know Me Better," which focuses on self-awareness, personal identity, and the fundamental exploration of the child's own body and uniqueness.
• **Core Objective**: The primary pedagogical aim of this chapter is to help young learners aged 3 to 4 years recognize their individuality, understand basic personal details, and build foundational self-esteem.
• **Concept of Self**: **Self-Identity** forms the cornerstone of this chapter, teaching students that every individual has a unique name, gender, age, and specific physical attributes that distinguish them from others.
• **The Human Body**: Introduction to the basic external human body parts, helping children locate, name, and understand the general function of major limbs and facial features.
• **Personal Data Awareness**: Children are guided to learn, memorize, and confidently articulate essential personal information such as their full name, age, and gender when prompted in a safe environment.
• **Role of the DAV Board**: In alignment with DAV Public School Board values, this chapter integrates moral development with self-care, emphasizing gratitude for the wonderful body given by nature.
• **Holistic Integration**: The Integrated Activity Book approach combines cognitive recognition with motor skill development, laying the groundwork for linguistic expression and emotional intelligence.
• **Visual Association**: The chapter utilizes pictorial representations and mirrors to help children connect their visual reflection with their spoken name and personal identity.
• **Teacher and Parent Partnership**: The content encourages facilitators and parents to engage children in daily interactive dialogues about their likes, dislikes, and physical traits to reinforce learning.
• **Inclusivity and Respect**: Recognizing that every child is unique, the chapter promotes an inclusive classroom environment where diverse names, appearances, and backgrounds are celebrated equally.
• **Cognitive Foundation**: Understanding "Who am I?" serves as the cognitive baseline for expanding outward into family, neighborhood, school, and the wider world in subsequent chapters.
• **Vocabulary Building**: Introduction of foundational vocabulary words including 'Name', 'Boy', 'Girl', 'Age', 'Face', 'Head', 'Hands', and 'Feet' within a structured, age-appropriate context.
• **Sensory Awareness**: Early exposure to how the body interacts with the physical world through touch, sight, and sound, setting the stage for detailed sensory lessons later in the book.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Personal Introduction (My Name)**: Every child must learn to state their full name clearly using the conversational prompt, "My name is [Student Name]."
• **Significance of Names**: Names are unique identifiers that give children a sense of belonging, personal dignity, and social recognition within their family and school community.
• **Practice of Articulation**: Encouraging shy learners to speak their names aloud builds auditory confidence, vocal projection, and early public-speaking poise in a classroom setting.
• **Gender Recognition**: **Gender Identity** is introduced gently at the LKG level, helping children understand the basic categories of being a 'boy' or a 'girl' using visual cues and clothing identifiers.
• **Statement of Gender**: Children learn to express their gender identity using simple, standardized sentences such as "I am a boy" or "I am a girl."
• **Age Awareness**: Understanding the concept of time passing through age, specifically learning to state "I am 3 years old" or "I am 4 years old" depending on their current developmental milestone.
• **Birthday Celebration Connection**: Linking the concept of age to birthday celebrations helps young learners comprehend that growing older is a joyous and natural biological process.
• **Physical Self-Recognition**: Using safe classroom mirrors to observe facial features, hair color, eye color, and skin tone, fostering observational skills and self-acceptance.
• **Uniqueness of Individuals**: Teaching the core value that no two people look or sound exactly alike, which plants the early seeds of appreciation for human diversity.
• **Body Ownership**: Instilling the concept of personal body boundaries, teaching children that their body belongs to them and they have a right to feel safe and respected.
• **Emotional Connection to Self**: Connecting personal identification with positive emotions, ensuring children feel proud, happy, and secure in their own identity.
• **Interactive Questioning**: Facilitators use prompts like "Can you tell me your name?" to transition children from passive listeners to active participants in classroom dialogues.
• **Peer Recognition**: Learning to look at classmates while they speak their names, fostering early social listening skills, empathy, and mutual classroom respect.

• **Introduction to Head and Face**: The head is identified as the uppermost part of the human body, housing vital sensory organs and acting as the control center for daily activities.
• **Hair Identification**: **Hair** is recognized as the protective covering on top of the head, varying in texture, length, and color among different children.
• **Forehead Recognition**: The forehead is introduced as the flat area above the eyebrows, completing the upper facial geography in early body-mapping exercises.
• **Eyes as Visual Organs**: **Eyes** are introduced as the precious organs of sight that allow children to see shapes, colors, bright lights, and the faces of loved ones.
• **Blinking and Protection**: Children learn that eyes have natural eyelids and eyelashes that blink automatically to protect them from dust, bright glare, and foreign particles.
• **Ears for Hearing**: **Ears** are identified as the bilateral organs located on the sides of the head that enable humans to hear sounds, music, voices, and environmental noises.
• **Nose as a Sensory Tool**: The **nose** is highlighted as the prominent central facial feature responsible for breathing air and detecting various pleasant and unpleasant smells.
• **Mouth and Lips**: The **mouth** is introduced as the cavity used for speaking, smiling, and consuming nutritious food, framed by soft, flexible lips.
• **Teeth and Smile**: **Teeth** are white, hard structures inside the mouth used for biting and chewing food, which must be kept clean through regular daily brushing.
• **Tongue Functions**: The **tongue** is introduced as a muscular organ inside the mouth that helps taste different flavors (sweet, sour, salty, bitter) and aids in swallowing.
• **Cheeks and Chin**: The fleshy sides of the face are identified as **cheeks**, and the lower point of the face beneath the mouth is recognized as the **chin**.
• **Facial Expressions**: Children learn how the face reflects emotions—smiling when happy, frowning when sad, or widening eyes when surprised—linking physical parts to emotional literacy.
• **Grooming the Face**: Basic hygiene habits related to the head and face, such as wiping the nose with a handkerchief, washing the face, and combing hair daily.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Trunk and Torso**: The central part of the human body connecting the head, neck, and limbs is introduced as the trunk or torso, providing core structural stability.
• **Neck Flexibility**: The **neck** is identified as the slender cylinder that connects the head to the torso, allowing the head to turn left, right, up, and down.
• **Shoulders**: The broad upper parts of the trunk where the arms attach are recognized as **shoulders**, useful for carrying small school bags and shrugging.
• **Chest Region**: The upper front portion of the torso is identified as the **chest**, which houses vital internal organs like the heart and lungs.
• **Stomach Identification**: The **stomach** (or tummy) is located in the lower front torso, where food goes after being swallowed and digested to give the body energy.
• **Back Support**: The rear side of the torso is the **back**, which provides structural support and allows children to bend, lean, and maintain an upright posture.
• **Upper Limbs (Arms)**: The arms are introduced as the upper limbs extending from the shoulders, crucial for reaching, lifting, hugging, and performing daily tasks.
• **Elbow Joint**: The **elbow** is identified as the middle joint of the arm that bends and straightens, facilitating flexible arm movements during play and writing.
• **Wrists**: The flexible connection between the forearm and the hand is named the **wrist**, allowing rotational and bending movements of the palm.
• **Hands and Palms**: The **hands** at the end of the arms are essential tools for grasping objects, holding pencils, eating, and expressing emotions through gestures.
• **Fingers and Thumb**: Every hand has five digits—four **fingers** and an opposable **thumb**—which enable precise gripping and fine motor manipulation.
• **Lower Limbs (Legs)**: The legs are introduced as the lower limbs that support the entire weight of the body and enable locomotion.
• **Knee Joint**: The **knee** is the middle joint of the leg that bends, allowing children to run, jump, sit down, and climb stairs safely.
• **Ankle and Feet**: The **ankle** connects the lower leg to the foot, while the **feet** provide a stable base of support for standing, walking, and balancing.
• **Toes**: Each foot has five **toes** at the front, helping with balance and grip while walking barefoot or wearing shoes.

• **Sensory Organs Overview**: Introduction to the "Five Senses" concept, teaching children that their body has five special organs to explore and understand the world around them.
• **Sense of Sight (Eyes)**: The eyes provide the sense of sight, allowing children to read books, appreciate nature's colors, and navigate safely in their surroundings.
• **Sense of Hearing (Ears)**: The ears provide the sense of hearing, enabling children to listen to their teacher's instructions, enjoy nursery rhymes, and hear warning sounds.
• **Sense of Smell (Nose)**: The nose provides the sense of smell, helping children identify fragrant flowers, delicious food cooking, and unpleasant odors.
• **Sense of Taste (Tongue)**: The tongue provides the sense of taste, allowing children to distinguish between sweet candies, sour lemons, salty snacks, and bitter medicine.
• **Sense of Touch (Skin)**: The **skin** is the largest organ of the body, covering all external surfaces and providing the sense of touch to feel hot, cold, soft, hard, and rough textures.
• **Interactive Sensory Exploration**: Encouraging children to use all five senses simultaneously to experience everyday objects, such as smelling and tasting an apple while feeling its smooth skin.
• **Health and Safety of Senses**: Teaching basic protective habits, such as not putting sharp objects in ears, not rubbing eyes with dirty hands, and avoiding foul chemical fumes.
• **Sensory Coordination**: Explaining how the brain processes signals sent by the five sensory organs to help the body make quick and safe decisions.
• **Observation Games**: Utilizing simple classroom games like "Guess the Sound" or "Touch and Tell" to reinforce sensory awareness and cognitive retention.
• **Vocabulary of Touch**: Introducing descriptive words for touch: soft (cotton), hard (wood), rough (bark), smooth (glass), hot (milk), and cold (ice cream).
• **Vocabulary of Taste**: Introducing descriptive words for taste: sweet (sugar), sour (lemon), salty (chips), and bitter (neem leaves).
• **Vocabulary of Sound**: Introducing descriptive words for sound: loud (horn), soft (whisper), musical (bell), and harsh (siren).
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Body Movements (Gross Motor)**: Children explore fundamental gross motor actions facilitated by their muscles and joints, including walking, running, hopping, jumping, and skipping.
• **Locomotion Control**: Learning how to move from one point to another in the classroom or playground while maintaining balance and avoiding collisions with peers.
• **Fine Motor Skills**: The DAV curriculum emphasizes fine motor control, using fingers and hands for tasks like holding crayons, turning book pages, and stacking blocks.
• **Pencil Grip Foundation**: Introducing the correct three-finger grip (tripod grasp) as a vital milestone for future writing readiness and hand muscle development.
• **Bilateral Coordination**: Teaching children to use both hands together cooperatively—such as holding a paper with one hand while coloring with the other.
• **Posture Awareness**: Early guidance on maintaining an upright posture while sitting on the floor or at a desk, preventing slouching and promoting spinal health.
• **Physical Exercise Integration**: Incorporating simple stretching exercises into the daily routine to keep muscles flexible and joints active.
• **Rest and Recovery**: Emphasizing that after physical play or intense classroom activities, the body requires rest and relaxation to regain energy.
• **Spatial Awareness**: Helping children understand their physical position relative to classroom objects, learning concepts like 'near', 'far', 'front', and 'back'.
• **Directional Basics**: Introducing simple body-based directional cues such as lifting the 'right hand' or stamping the 'left foot' to build spatial orientation.
• **Hand-Eye Coordination**: Engaging in activities like catching a soft ball or placing pegs in a board to synchronize visual input with physical hand movements.
• **Balance Exercises**: Simple balancing tasks, such as standing on one foot like a stork, to strengthen core muscles and vestibular balance systems.

• **Personal Hygiene Basics**: "Know Me Better" extends into personal cleanliness, teaching children that keeping their body clean is an essential part of self-care.
• **Morning Routine**: Children learn the importance of waking up, brushing teeth twice a day, taking a bath, and wearing clean school clothes.
• **Handwashing Habits**: Detailed steps on washing hands with soap and water before eating meals and after using the restroom to eliminate harmful germs.
• **Nail Care**: Understanding that fingernails and toenails must be kept short and clean by parents to prevent dirt and bacteria accumulation.
• **Hair Maintenance**: Learning the habit of washing hair regularly and brushing or combing it neatly before coming to school.
• **Oral Hygiene Deep-Dive**: Explaining how brushing teeth in circular motions removes food particles and prevents tooth decay, keeping smiles bright and healthy.
• **Use of Handkerchief**: Teaching children to always carry a clean handkerchief to wipe their nose, clean their mouth, or cover a cough or sneeze.
• **Toilet Training & Etiquette**: Reinforcing independent bathroom habits and the importance of flushing and washing hands afterward.
• **Footwear Hygiene**: Learning to keep shoes clean, wiping feet before entering a room, and storing footwear neatly in designated racks.
• **Cleanliness Pride**: Instilling a sense of personal pride in looking neat, tidy, and well-groomed every single day.
• **Health and Sickness Prevention**: Linking basic hygiene practices directly to staying healthy, strong, and away from common illnesses like colds and stomach bugs.
• **Role of Parents and Teachers**: Acknowledging that adults assist children in learning these habits until they can perform them independently.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Nutritional Awareness (Fuel for the Body)**: Introducing the concept that the human body needs healthy food, clean water, and adequate rest to grow big and strong.
• **Healthy Eating Habits**: Promoting fresh fruits, green vegetables, milk, lentils, and chapatis as essential building blocks for physical growth.
• **Hydration Importance**: Teaching children to drink sufficient clean water throughout the day to keep their body hydrated and functioning smoothly.
• **Junk Food Limitation**: Gently introducing the idea that chips, candies, and sugary sodas should be eaten rarely because they do not help the body grow strong.
• **Table Manners**: Basic etiquette during eating, such as chewing with the mouth closed, not talking with food inside, and eating without spilling.
• **Wash Before Eating**: Emphasizing the golden rule of washing hands thoroughly with soap before touching any food item.
• **Gratitude for Food**: Encouraging children to express thankfulness for the food they receive, aligning with DAV moral values and cultural traditions.
• **Rest and Sleep**: Explaining that the body works hard all day and needs 10 to 12 hours of peaceful sleep at night to repair muscles and grow tall.
• **Energy for Play**: Connecting proper food and sleep directly to having enough energy to run, jump, play, and learn new things in school.
• **Role of Milk**: Highlighting milk as a complete and essential food for growing children, helping build strong bones and healthy teeth.
• **Fresh vs. Stale Food**: Early awareness of eating freshly prepared, home-cooked meals rather than uncovered or stale street food.
• **Sharing with Friends**: Encouraging sharing healthy snacks with classmates during recess to build friendship, kindness, and social bonding.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Emotional Self-Awareness**: Helping children identify, name, and express their basic emotions—such as happiness, sadness, anger, and surprise—safely and constructively.
• **The Power of a Smile**: Teaching children that smiling makes them feel good, spreads joy to others, and creates a welcoming classroom atmosphere.
• **Expressing Feelings**: Encouraging children to use words like "I am happy," "I am sad," or "I need help" instead of crying or throwing tantrums.
• **Managing Sadness or Anger**: Introducing gentle coping mechanisms, such as taking a deep breath or talking to a teacher when feeling upset.
• **Empathy and Compassion**: Learning to notice when a classmate is sad or hurt, and offering a comforting word or sharing a toy.
• **Building Self-Confidence**: Reinforcing positive affirmations like "I am special," "I am smart," and "I can do it" to build robust self-esteem.
• **Appreciating Differences**: Recognizing that every child has unique feelings, likes, and dislikes, and respecting those differences in peers.
• **Friendship and Sharing**: Understanding that knowing oneself better helps in forming warm, cooperative friendships with other children in school.
• **Calmness and Patience**: Introducing short moments of quiet time or deep breathing exercises to help children center their minds and emotions.
• **Expressing Gratitude**: Teaching children to say "Thank you" when helped, "Please" when asking, and "Sorry" when making a mistake.
• **Positive Reinforcement**: Teachers and parents using praise and encouragement to validate emotional milestones and personal growth achievements.
• **Safe Adults**: Reminding children that they can always share their feelings, worries, or fears with trusted adults at home and school.
