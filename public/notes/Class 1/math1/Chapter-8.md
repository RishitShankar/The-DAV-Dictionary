• **Concept of Weight:** Weight is a measure of how heavy or light an object is.
• **Heavier Objects:** An object that has more mass and requires more force to lift is classified as heavy.
• **Lighter Objects:** An object that has less mass and is easy to lift or move is classified as light.
• **Comparison:** To determine weight, we compare two objects by holding them or using a balance scale.
• **Direct Observation:** Visualizing the size of an object often helps in guessing its weight, though it is not always accurate.
• **Heavier vs. Lighter:** When two objects are compared, the one that pulls down more is the heavier one.
• **Everyday Examples:** A school bag filled with books is heavier than an empty school bag.
• **Everyday Examples:** A feather is lighter than a stone.
• **Everyday Examples:** A watermelon is heavier than an apple.
• **Everyday Examples:** A pencil is lighter than a geometry box.
• **Tactile Perception:** Our hands act as natural sensors to feel the weight of objects by lifting them.
• **Relative Nature:** Weight is relative; an object can be heavy compared to one thing and light compared to another.
• **The Balance Scale:** A simple tool used to compare the weights of two objects by placing them on opposite pans.
• **Balance Scale Mechanism:** The pan that goes down holds the heavier object.
• **Balance Scale Mechanism:** The pan that stays up holds the lighter object.
• **Equilibrium:** When both pans are at the same level, the objects have equal weight.

• **Standard Units:** While we compare weights, we eventually need standard units to measure them accurately.
• **Kilogram (kg):** The standard unit used to measure the weight of heavy objects.
• **Gram (g):** The standard unit used to measure the weight of light objects.
• **Relationship:** 1 kilogram is equal to 1000 grams.
• **Measuring Instruments:** A weighing machine or a physical balance is used to find the exact weight.
• **Weighing Machine:** A digital or analog device that displays the weight of an object placed on it.
• **Physical Balance:** A device with two pans used to compare weights using standard weights.
• **Standard Weights:** Metal blocks of fixed weight (e.g., 1kg, 500g, 200g) used on a balance scale.
• **Grocery Shopping:** Shopkeepers use weighing scales to measure items like rice, sugar, and pulses.
• **Vegetable Market:** Vegetables are weighed in kilograms or grams depending on the quantity.
• **Body Weight:** Human weight is typically measured in kilograms using a weighing scale.
• **Capacity vs. Weight:** It is important to distinguish between how much a container holds (capacity) and how heavy it is (weight).
• **Density Factor:** Sometimes a small object can be heavier than a large object if it is denser (e.g., a small iron ball vs. a large balloon).
• **Estimation:** Before measuring, we often estimate the weight based on our previous experiences.
• **Safety:** Always handle heavy objects with care to avoid injury.
• **Precision:** Using standard weights ensures fairness in trade and scientific accuracy.
