• **Chapter Title**: General Awareness from the DAV Dictionary Integrated Activity Book for Class LKG.
• **Primary Objective**: To introduce foundational knowledge about the immediate environment, personal identity, and surroundings to early learners.
• **Target Audience**: Class LKG students under the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board framework.
• **Curricular Design**: Structured learning approach utilizing visual recognition, association, and basic cognitive mapping.
• **Core Concept**: Understanding "Self" as the starting point of general awareness in early childhood education.
• **Self-Identification**: Recognizing one's full name, age, and gender as foundational blocks of personal identity.
• **Personal Data**: Memorizing basic personal information builds confidence and enhances social security awareness.
• **Body Awareness**: Recognizing that the human body is an amazing machine with various external and internal parts.
• **Basic Anatomy**: Identifying primary body parts such as eyes, ears, nose, mouth, hands, and legs.
• **Sensory Organs**: Introduction to the five primary sense organs: eyes for seeing, ears for hearing, nose for smelling, tongue for tasting, and skin for feeling.
• **Sensory Functions**: Linking each sense organ to its specific daily life application and environmental interaction.
• **Family Unit**: Understanding the concept of a family as a group of people related by blood, marriage, or adoption living together.
• **Family Members**: Identifying immediate family roles including father, mother, brother, sister, grandfather, and grandmother.
• **Family Values**: Inculcating DAV values of respect, love, and obedience towards elders and affection for younger ones.
• **Home Environment**: Recognizing different rooms inside a house such as the bedroom, living room, kitchen, and bathroom.
• **Home Safety**: Basic awareness of household items that are safe versus those that require adult supervision.
• **Daily Routine**: Understanding chronological sequences of daily activities from waking up in the morning to sleeping at night.
• **Personal Hygiene**: Learning essential cleanliness habits like brushing teeth, bathing daily, washing hands before meals, and wearing clean clothes.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Second Module Focus**: Expanding general awareness from the immediate home environment to the broader school and community setting.
• **School Introduction**: Recognizing the school as a place of joyful learning, sharing, and growing under the guidance of teachers.
• **School Name and Location**: Memorizing the name of the DAV Public School and appreciating its campus environment.
• **Classroom Objects**: Identifying common objects found inside a classroom including blackboard, chalk, duster, tables, chairs, and books.
• **School Staff**: Recognizing key school personnel such as the Principal, class teacher, helpers, and security staff.
• **Playground Activities**: Understanding the importance of outdoor play, sharing toys, and following turn-taking rules on slides and swings.
• **Community Helpers**: Recognizing people who provide essential services to society to make daily life smooth and comfortable.
• **Doctor**: Identifying the doctor as a healthcare professional who examines sick people and prescribes medicines.
• **Teacher**: Acknowledging the teacher as an educator who imparts knowledge, good manners, and moral values.
• **Policeman**: Understanding the role of the policeman in maintaining law, order, and community safety.
• **Firefighter**: Recognizing the firefighter as a brave helper who puts out accidental fires and rescues people.
• **Postman**: Identifying the postman who delivers letters, parcels, and important documents to homes.
• **Farmer**: Appreciating the farmer who works hard in fields to grow food grains, vegetables, and fruits for everyone.
• **Shoemaker/Cobbler**: Recognizing the cobbler who mends shoes and keeps footwear in usable condition.
• **Tailor**: Identifying the tailor who stitches clothes for people of all ages.
• **Sweeper/Cleaner**: Understanding the vital role of sanitation workers in keeping neighborhoods and public spaces clean.
• **Community Gratitude**: Learning to respect and thank all community helpers for their dedicated services.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Third Module Focus**: Exploring the natural world, plants, animals, and seasonal changes in the immediate surroundings.
• **Nature Awareness**: Developing an early appreciation for nature, greenery, open spaces, and natural beauty.
• **Plant Kingdom**: Introduction to plants as living things that provide oxygen, shade, flowers, and delicious fruits.
• **Parts of a Plant**: Basic visual recognition of roots, stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits on common garden plants.
• **Tree Concept**: Differentiating between small plants, shrubs, and large tall trees found in parks and neighborhoods.
• **Flower Recognition**: Identifying common and bright flowers such as rose, lotus, sunflower, marigold, and jasmine.
• **Animal Kingdom**: Categorizing animals into domestic, wild, and water-dwelling creatures based on habitat.
• **Domestic Animals**: Identifying animals kept at home or farms such as cows, dogs, cats, goats, horses, and sheep.
• **Animal Utility**: Understanding how domestic animals help humans (e.g., cows give milk, dogs guard homes, horses carry loads).
• **Wild Animals**: Recognizing animals that live naturally in forests and jungles such as lions, tigers, elephants, bears, and monkeys.
• **King of the Jungle**: Identifying the lion as a majestic wild animal known for its strength and loud roar.
• **National Animal**: Recognizing the tiger as the national animal of India, notable for its orange coat and black stripes.
• **National Bird**: Identifying the peacock as the national bird of India, famous for its colorful and magnificent feathers.
• **Bird Characteristics**: Understanding that birds have feathers, wings, and beaks, and can fly high up in the sky.
• **Water Animals**: Introduction to aquatic animals like fish, whales, sharks, and turtles that live entirely in water.
• **Insects**: Recognizing tiny crawling or flying creatures such as butterflies, honeybees, ants, and ladybugs.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Fourth Module Focus**: Understanding colors, shapes, patterns, and basic mathematical awareness within the physical environment.
• **Color Recognition**: Identifying and naming primary and secondary colors seen in everyday objects and nature.
• **Primary Colors**: Recognizing Red, Blue, and Yellow as fundamental primary colors that form the basis of the color spectrum.
• **Secondary Colors**: Identifying Green, Orange, Purple, Pink, Brown, Black, and White in common objects.
• **Color Association (Red)**: Associating red color with apples, roses, tomatoes, and stop signs.
• **Color Association (Blue)**: Associating blue color with the clear sky, ocean water, and blueberries.
• **Color Association (Yellow)**: Associating yellow color with the sun, bananas, lemons, and sunflowers.
• **Color Association (Green)**: Associating green color with grass, leaves, parrots, and unripe mangoes.
• **Shape Awareness**: Recognizing basic 2D geometric shapes present in everyday toys, household items, and buildings.
• **Circle Shape**: Identifying circular objects such as coins, bangles, wheels, and clocks that have no corners.
• **Square Shape**: Recognizing square objects such as carrom boards, sandwich slices, and tiles having four equal sides.
• **Triangle Shape**: Identifying triangular items like slice of pizza, sandwich cuts, and party hats having three sides.
• **Rectangle Shape**: Recognizing rectangular items like doors, books, blackboards, and mobile phones with opposite equal sides.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Fifth Module Focus**: Understanding modes of transport, vehicles, and rules of travel safety in daily life.
• **Transportation Concept**: Defining transport as the movement of people and goods from one place to another.
• **Land Transport**: Identifying vehicles that move on roads with wheels, such as cars, buses, bicycles, motorcycles, and trucks.
• **Two-Wheelers**: Recognizing bicycles and motorcycles as lightweight personal transport vehicles with two wheels.
• **Four-Wheelers**: Identifying cars, jeeps, and vans as multi-passenger vehicles with four wheels.
• **Heavy Vehicles**: Recognizing buses and trucks as large transport vehicles carrying many passengers or heavy loads.
• **Rail Transport**: Identifying trains as long vehicles running on metal tracks to connect distant cities and towns.
• **Water Transport**: Recognizing boats, ships, and steamers that float and sail across rivers, lakes, and oceans.
• **Air Transport**: Identifying aeroplanes and helicopters as fast-moving flying machines that travel through the sky.
• **Traffic Safety**: Introducing basic pedestrian rules such as walking on the footpath and holding an adult's hand while crossing roads.
• **Traffic Lights**: Understanding the significance of traffic signal colors for road safety.
• **Red Light (Traffic)**: Learning that the red signal means 'Stop' and vehicles must halt completely.
• **Yellow Light (Traffic)**: Understanding that the yellow signal means 'Wait' or get ready to move.
• **Green Light (Traffic)**: Knowing that the green signal means 'Go' and traffic can proceed safely.
• **Zebra Crossing**: Recognizing black and white stripes on the road meant for pedestrians to cross safely.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Sixth Module Focus**: Weather conditions, seasons of the year, and dressing according to atmospheric changes.
• **Weather Awareness**: Observing daily changes in the sky and atmosphere such as sunshine, rain, wind, and clouds.
• **Sunny Day**: Recognizing a bright sunny day when the sun shines warmly and people wear light cotton clothes.
• **Rainy Day**: Identifying rainy days when dark clouds fill the sky, water falls from above, and umbrellas are used.
• **Monsoon Season**: Understanding the monsoon period when plants flourish, puddles form, and paper boats are floated.
• **Winter Season**: Recognizing cold winter days when chilly winds blow, warm woolen clothes are worn, and hot soups are enjoyed.
• **Summer Season**: Identifying hot summer days when ceiling fans and air conditioners run, and cold drinks and ice creams are consumed.
• **Spring Season**: Appreciating spring as a pleasant season when colorful flowers bloom and butterflies flutter around gardens.
• **Clothing Alignment**: Learning to match appropriate clothes with prevailing weather conditions (e.g., sweaters in winter, raincoats in monsoon).
• **Water Cycle Awareness**: Simple observation of rain coming from clouds and filling rivers, lakes, and ponds.
• **Day and Night**: Differentiating between daytime when the sun gives light and warmth, and nighttime when the moon and stars appear.
• **Celestial Bodies**: Basic visual recognition of the Sun, Moon, and Stars in the sky during their respective times.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Seventh Module Focus**: Developing civic sense, good manners, and moral values promoted by the DAV educational philosophy.
• **Magic Words**: Learning essential polite expressions that foster harmonious social interactions and positive communication.
• **Please**: Using "Please" when making a polite request for assistance or borrowing an item.
• **Thank You**: Expressing gratitude with "Thank You" when receiving a gift, help, or a compliment.
• **Sorry**: Saying "Sorry" immediately when making a mistake, hurting someone unintentionally, or causing an inconvenience.
• **Excuse Me**: Using "Excuse Me" to politely gain attention or pass through a crowded space.
• **Sharing Habits**: Understanding the joy of sharing toys, colors, and tiffin boxes with classmates and siblings.
• **Respect for Elders**: Inculcating DAV values of respecting parents, grandparents, and teachers through words and actions.
• **Helping Nature**: Developing a helpful attitude toward peers, family members, and community helpers.
• **Cleanliness Habits**: Committing to keeping surroundings clean by throwing waste paper and wrappers into dustbins.
• **Truthfulness**: Learning the importance of always speaking the truth and being honest in thoughts and actions.
• **Compassion for Animals**: Developing kindness toward street animals and birds by offering them food and water.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Eighth Module Focus**: Fostering holistic health, nutritional awareness, and good dietary habits in early childhood.
• **Food Importance**: Understanding that food gives us energy to play, study, run, and grow strong and healthy.
• **Healthy Meals**: Recognizing home-cooked, nutritious meals as essential for overall physical and mental development.
• **Fruits**: Identifying nutritious fruits such as apples, bananas, mangoes, grapes, oranges, and papayas loaded with vitamins.
• **Vegetables**: Recognizing healthy vegetables like carrots, spinach, potatoes, peas, and tomatoes essential for a balanced diet.
• **Dairy Products**: Identifying milk, curd, cheese, and butter as calcium-rich foods that strengthen bones and teeth.
• **Water Intake**: Understanding the importance of drinking plenty of clean, filtered water throughout the day to stay hydrated.
• **Junk Food Awareness**: Recognizing unhealthy fast foods like chips, burgers, cold drinks, and candies that should be eaten rarely.
• **Table Manners**: Learning basic dining etiquette such as chewing with the mouth closed and eating without spilling.
• **Hand Washing**: Practicing thorough hand washing with soap before and after eating meals to prevent germs.
• **Oral Care**: Brushing teeth twice a day—once in the morning and once before bedtime—to maintain sparkling clean teeth.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Ninth Module Focus**: Exploring hobbies, recreational activities, indoor and outdoor games, and creative expression.
• **Play Importance**: Recognizing that playing games is essential for physical fitness, mental alertness, and joyful social bonding.
• **Outdoor Games**: Identifying active games played outside in open spaces, such as running, tag, hide-and-seek, and football.
• **Indoor Games**: Recognizing calm games played inside rooms, such as ludo, snake and ladders, building blocks, and puzzles.
• **Toy Time**: Identifying favorite toys such as dolls, teddy bears, remote-controlled cars, and stacking rings.
• **Art and Craft**: Expressing creativity through drawing, coloring in books, paper folding (origami), and clay modeling.
• **Music and Dance**: Enjoying rhythmic movement, clapping to beats, singing nursery rhymes, and dancing with peers.
• **Storytelling**: Listening attentively to moral and fairy-tale stories narrated by teachers and parents to boost imagination.
• **Gardening Hobby**: Participating in small school activities like watering potted plants and watching seeds sprout.
• **Festivals Celebration**: Recognizing major Indian festivals celebrated with family and friends in school and community.
• **Diwali**: Identifying Diwali as the festival of lights, featuring diyas, rangoli patterns, sweets, and joyous celebrations.
• **Holi**: Recognizing Holi as the vibrant festival of colors, spring arrival, and joyful social harmony.
• **Independence Day**: Acknowledging India's Independence Day on 15th August, hoisting the national flag, and singing the national anthem.
• **Republic Day**: Recognizing Republic Day on 26th January as a proud national commemoration of the Constitution of India.

• **Tenth Module Focus**: Review, consolidation, cognitive reinforcement, and holistic wrap-up of the General Awareness chapter.
• **Comprehensive Review**: Synthesizing all learned concepts regarding self, family, school, community, nature, and safety rules.
• **Visual Memory Reinforcement**: Strengthening visual identification of colors, shapes, animals, vehicles, and weather types.
• **Social Skill Reflection**: Recalling polite magic words (Please, Thank You, Sorry) for daily interpersonal communication.
• **Health Habit Checklist**: Reviewing daily hygiene routines like brushing teeth, bathing, washing hands, and eating healthy food.
• **DAV Value Integration**: Reaffirming core moral values of honesty, respect, kindness, and environmental care.
• **Environmental Consciousness**: Committing to protect plants, save water, avoid plastic, and keep surroundings clean.
• **Safety Consciousness**: Remembering traffic rules, pedestrian safety, and home safety boundaries for self-protection.
• **Confidence Building**: Encouraging children to speak about themselves, their family, and their favorite things proudly.
• **Curiosity Encouragement**: Fostering an inquisitive mindset to ask questions about the wonderful world around them.
• **Parent-Teacher Partnership**: Encouraging collaborative reinforcement of general awareness concepts at home through practical observation.
• **Concluding Milestone**: Successfully completing the foundational General Awareness curriculum for Class LKG under DAV guidelines.
