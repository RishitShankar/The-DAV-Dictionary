• **Food**: The basic necessity of life that provides energy to work, play, and grow.
• **Energy Source**: Food acts as fuel for the human body, similar to how petrol acts as fuel for a car.
• **Growth**: Nutrients in food help the body build bones, muscles, and tissues, allowing children to grow taller and stronger.
• **Health Maintenance**: Proper nutrition helps the body fight against diseases and keeps the immune system strong.
• **Repair**: Food provides the necessary materials for the body to repair damaged cells and recover from injuries.
• **Daily Requirement**: The human body requires a consistent intake of food throughout the day to function optimally.
• **Hunger**: A natural signal from the body indicating that it needs more energy and nutrients to continue functioning.
• **Variety**: Humans consume a wide variety of food items, including grains, vegetables, fruits, and dairy products.
• **Sources of Food**: All food consumed by humans originates from two primary sources: plants and animals.
• **Plant-based Foods**: These include cereals, pulses, fruits, vegetables, nuts, and spices.
• **Animal-based Foods**: These include milk, eggs, meat, fish, and honey.
• **Cereals**: Grains like wheat, rice, and maize that provide carbohydrates for energy.
• **Pulses**: Legumes like lentils, beans, and peas that are rich in proteins for body growth.
• **Fruits**: Edible plant parts that provide essential vitamins and minerals for health.
• **Vegetables**: Plant parts such as roots, stems, leaves, and flowers that are vital for a balanced diet.
• **Dairy Products**: Food items derived from milk, such as curd, cheese, butter, and ghee.
• **Nutritional Balance**: Eating a mix of different food types ensures the body receives all necessary nutrients.
• **Dietary Habits**: The importance of eating at regular intervals to maintain metabolic stability.

• **Energy-giving Foods**: Food items rich in carbohydrates and fats that provide the energy required for physical activities.
• **Carbohydrates**: The primary source of quick energy; found in rice, wheat, potatoes, and sugar.
• **Fats**: Concentrated energy sources that keep the body warm; found in butter, ghee, oil, and nuts.
• **Body-building Foods**: Foods rich in proteins that help in the growth of muscles and bones.
• **Proteins**: Essential nutrients for building and repairing body tissues; found in pulses, milk, eggs, and meat.
• **Protective Foods**: Foods rich in vitamins and minerals that protect the body from illnesses and infections.
• **Vitamins**: Micronutrients that keep eyes, skin, and teeth healthy; found abundantly in fruits and green vegetables.
• **Minerals**: Essential elements like calcium and iron that strengthen bones and blood; found in leafy greens and dairy.
• **Water**: A vital component of the diet that helps in digestion and the removal of waste from the body.
• **Hydration**: The process of maintaining adequate water levels in the body to ensure proper organ function.
• **Balanced Diet**: A diet that contains the right amount of energy-giving, body-building, and protective foods.
• **Meal Structure**: The division of daily food intake into breakfast, lunch, and dinner.
• **Breakfast**: The most important meal of the day, consumed in the morning to break the overnight fast.
• **Lunch**: The midday meal that provides energy to continue activities throughout the afternoon.
• **Dinner**: The final meal of the day, which should be lighter than lunch to aid digestion before sleep.
• **Snacks**: Small portions of food consumed between main meals to keep energy levels stable.
• **Digestion**: The process of breaking down food into smaller components that the body can absorb.
• **Nutrient Absorption**: The mechanism by which the small intestine takes in nutrients from digested food into the bloodstream.

• **Milk**: Often called a 'complete food' because it contains almost all the nutrients required for growth.
• **Calcium**: A mineral abundant in milk that is crucial for the development of strong teeth and bones.
• **Vegetarian Diet**: A diet consisting primarily of plant-based foods, including fruits, vegetables, grains, and dairy.
• **Non-vegetarian Diet**: A diet that includes animal products such as meat, fish, and eggs in addition to plant-based foods.
• **Raw Foods**: Food items that can be eaten without cooking, such as many fruits and certain vegetables like carrots and cucumbers.
• **Cooked Foods**: Food items that require heat treatment to become soft, tasty, and safe to eat.
• **Cooking Benefits**: Cooking kills harmful germs and bacteria present in raw food items.
• **Digestibility**: Cooking makes food softer and easier for the human digestive system to process.
• **Flavor Enhancement**: Cooking improves the taste, aroma, and texture of food, making it more appetizing.
• **Cooking Methods**: Various techniques used to prepare food, including boiling, steaming, frying, roasting, and baking.
• **Boiling**: Cooking food in boiling water; commonly used for rice, potatoes, and eggs.
• **Steaming**: Cooking food using steam; a healthy method that retains most nutrients, used for idlis and dhoklas.
• **Frying**: Cooking food in hot oil; used for items like puris and pakoras, though it should be consumed in moderation.
• **Roasting**: Cooking food directly over heat or in an oven; used for papads, corn, and meats.
• **Baking**: Cooking food in an oven using dry heat; used for bread, cakes, and biscuits.
• **Hygiene in Cooking**: The necessity of washing vegetables and fruits thoroughly before cooking or eating.
• **Kitchen Cleanliness**: Maintaining a clean cooking area to prevent contamination of food.
• **Utensils**: The tools and containers used for cooking and serving food, which must be kept clean.

• **Food Habits**: The specific choices and patterns of eating that vary from person to person and culture to culture.
• **Regional Influence**: Food habits are often determined by the climate and crops grown in a specific region.
• **Cultural Influence**: Traditions and festivals often dictate the types of food prepared and consumed by families.
• **Healthy Eating Habits**: Practices that promote long-term health, such as eating at fixed times and chewing food properly.
• **Chewing**: The process of breaking food into smaller pieces with teeth, which is the first step of digestion.
• **Saliva**: A fluid in the mouth that mixes with food during chewing to begin the breakdown of carbohydrates.
• **Moderation**: Eating the right quantity of food; overeating can lead to health issues like obesity.
• **Junk Food**: Processed food items that are high in sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats but low in essential nutrients.
• **Negative Effects of Junk Food**: Regular consumption can lead to lethargy, weight gain, and poor health.
• **Examples of Junk Food**: Chips, colas, burgers, pizzas, and candies.
• **Freshness**: The importance of consuming fresh food to ensure maximum nutrient intake and avoid food poisoning.
• **Storage**: Proper storage of food in refrigerators or cool, dry places to prevent spoilage.
• **Food Preservation**: Techniques used to keep food edible for longer periods, such as pickling or drying.
• **Clean Water**: Drinking clean, filtered, or boiled water is essential for maintaining health alongside food.
• **Hand Hygiene**: Washing hands with soap before and after every meal to prevent the transfer of germs.
• **Table Manners**: Good habits like sitting properly, using a napkin, and not talking with a full mouth.
• **Sharing**: The social aspect of eating together, which promotes bonding and happiness.
• **Gratitude**: Appreciating the food on the plate and the effort taken to prepare it.

• **Proteins (Detailed)**: Known as 'grow-food', these are essential for the repair of body cells and the growth of children.
• **Sources of Protein**: Lentils (dal), beans, nuts, milk, cheese, eggs, fish, and lean meats.
• **Carbohydrates (Detailed)**: Known as 'energy-giving food', these provide the fuel for daily activities like running and studying.
• **Sources of Carbohydrates**: Wheat, rice, maize, potatoes, sugar, and honey.
• **Fats (Detailed)**: Provide more energy than carbohydrates and help in insulating the body.
• **Sources of Fats**: Butter, ghee, vegetable oils, nuts, and cream.
• **Vitamins (Detailed)**: Known as 'protective food', they keep the body's systems functioning correctly.
• **Vitamin A**: Essential for good eyesight and healthy skin; found in carrots and green vegetables.
• **Vitamin C**: Boosts the immune system and helps heal wounds; found in citrus fruits like oranges and lemons.
• **Vitamin D**: Necessary for strong bones and teeth; obtained from sunlight and fortified foods.
• **Minerals (Detailed)**: Inorganic substances that perform specific roles, such as iron for blood and calcium for bones.
• **Iron**: A mineral that helps in the formation of hemoglobin in red blood cells; found in spinach and apples.
• **Calcium**: A mineral that makes bones and teeth hard; found in milk, yogurt, and green leafy vegetables.
• **Roughage (Fiber)**: The indigestible part of plant foods that helps in the smooth movement of waste through the digestive tract.
• **Sources of Roughage**: Whole grains, fruits with skin, and raw vegetables like salads.
• **Importance of Roughage**: Prevents constipation and keeps the digestive system clean.
• **Water (Detailed)**: Makes up about 70% of the human body; essential for regulating body temperature and transporting nutrients.
• **Dehydration**: A condition caused by lack of water, leading to headaches, fatigue, and dizziness.

• **Meal Planning**: Organizing meals to ensure a balance of all food groups throughout the day.
• **Breakfast Importance**: Refills the body's energy stores after the long sleep period.
• **Breakfast Examples**: Milk, cereal, eggs, fruit, or parathas.
• **Lunch Importance**: Provides the energy needed for the afternoon's work and school activities.
• **Lunch Examples**: Roti, dal, rice, vegetables, and salad.
• **Dinner Importance**: Should be light to allow the body to rest and digest easily during sleep.
• **Dinner Examples**: Soup, light vegetables, and a small portion of grains.
• **Snacking**: Should consist of healthy items like fruits, nuts, or sprouts rather than junk food.
• **Food Groups**: Categorizing food based on their primary function in the body.
• **Group 1 (Energy)**: Carbohydrates and Fats.
• **Group 2 (Growth)**: Proteins.
• **Group 3 (Protection)**: Vitamins and Minerals.
• **The Food Pyramid**: A visual guide showing the recommended proportions of different food groups in a healthy diet.
• **Base of Pyramid**: Grains and cereals (should be eaten in the largest quantity).
• **Middle of Pyramid**: Fruits and vegetables (should be eaten in moderate quantity).
• **Top of Pyramid**: Fats, oils, and sweets (should be eaten in very small quantities).
• **Variety in Diet**: Eating different colors of fruits and vegetables ensures a wider range of vitamins and minerals.
• **Seasonal Eating**: Consuming fruits and vegetables that are in season provides the freshest and most nutritious produce.
• **Local Produce**: Buying locally grown food supports local farmers and ensures the food is fresh.

• **Food Safety**: Ensuring that food is free from harmful bacteria, viruses, and chemicals.
• **Contamination**: The presence of harmful substances in food that can cause illness.
• **Symptoms of Food Poisoning**: Stomach ache, vomiting, diarrhea, and fever.
• **Prevention of Contamination**: Covering food to protect it from flies, dust, and insects.
• **Refrigeration**: Keeping perishable foods like milk, meat, and cooked vegetables in the fridge to slow down bacterial growth.
• **Expiry Dates**: Always checking the date on packaged food to ensure it is safe for consumption.
• **Washing Produce**: Rinsing fruits and vegetables under running water to remove pesticides and dirt.
• **Cooking Thoroughly**: Ensuring meat and eggs are cooked until they are no longer raw to kill pathogens.
• **Cross-contamination**: Avoiding the use of the same cutting board for raw meat and vegetables to prevent germ spread.
• **Kitchen Hygiene**: Regularly cleaning countertops, sinks, and utensils with soap and water.
• **Personal Hygiene**: Washing hands before touching food and keeping nails short and clean.
• **Waste Disposal**: Properly disposing of food scraps to avoid attracting pests like rats and cockroaches.
• **Pest Control**: Keeping the kitchen free of insects and rodents that carry diseases.
• **Water Quality**: Using clean, boiled, or filtered water for drinking and cooking.
• **Storage Containers**: Using airtight containers to store dry goods like pulses and flour to keep them fresh.
• **Leftovers**: Storing leftover food in the refrigerator and reheating it thoroughly before eating.
• **Food Waste**: Avoiding the wastage of food by cooking only what is needed and storing extra portions properly.
• **Environmental Impact**: Reducing food waste helps the environment and saves resources.

• **Community Meals**: Eating together in schools or community centers fosters a sense of togetherness.
• **School Lunches**: Providing nutritious meals in schools ensures that children have the energy to learn.

• **Hunger Awareness**: Understanding that many people in the world do not have enough food to eat.
• **Responsibility**: It is our responsibility to eat mindfully and not waste food.
• **Healthy Choices**: Making informed decisions about what to eat based on nutritional value rather than taste alone.
• **Long-term Health**: Good food habits formed in childhood lead to a healthier adult life.

• **Digestion Process**: Begins in the mouth where teeth grind food and saliva starts the breakdown.
• **Esophagus**: The food pipe that carries chewed food from the mouth to the stomach.
• **Stomach**: A muscular organ that mixes food with digestive juices to break it down further.
• **Small Intestine**: The primary site for nutrient absorption into the bloodstream.
• **Large Intestine**: Absorbs water from the remaining undigested food and prepares waste for removal.
• **Excretion**: The final stage where waste is removed from the body.
• **Role of Fiber**: Keeps the digestive system moving, preventing blockages.
• **Role of Water in Digestion**: Helps in dissolving nutrients and softening food for easy passage.
• **Energy Expenditure**: The body uses energy even when resting (basal metabolism) and more when active.
• **Activity Levels**: Children who are physically active require more energy-giving foods.
• **Growth Spurts**: Periods of rapid growth in children require increased protein intake.
• **Brain Function**: The brain requires glucose (from carbohydrates) to function, focus, and learn.
• **Healthy Snacks**: Options like fruit slices, yogurt, nuts, or vegetable sticks are better than processed snacks.
• **Sugar Intake**: Excessive sugar consumption can lead to tooth decay and energy crashes.
• **Salt Intake**: Too much salt can lead to high blood pressure; it should be used sparingly.
• **Hydration Signs**: Dark-colored urine is a sign that the body needs more water.
• **Mindful Eating**: Paying attention to the taste, texture, and smell of food helps in better digestion.
• **Eating Environment**: Eating in a calm and peaceful environment improves the digestion process.
