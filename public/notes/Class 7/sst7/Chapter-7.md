• **Subject and Grade Context**: Welcome to the DAV Dictionary academic study notes for Class 7, We and Our World: Social Science, Chapter 7: "Human Environment (Settlement, Transport and Communication)".
• **Chapter Overview**: This chapter explores the dynamic relationship between humans and their environment, focusing on how people settle, move, and connect across the globe.
• **Core Definition of Human Environment**: The human environment encompasses all the physical and cultural modifications, structures, and systems created by human beings on Earth's surface.
• **Evolution of Human Settlement**: Early humans were nomadic wanderers who lived in caves and relied entirely on hunting and gathering for survival.
• **Transition to Sedentary Life**: The discovery and development of agriculture prompted humans to establish permanent dwellings, leading to the birth of early civilizations.
• **Definition of Settlements**: Settlements are clusters of dwellings of any type or size where human beings live, practice economic activities, and build their communities.
• **Role of Natural Environment**: Natural factors such as availability of water, fertile soil, favorable climate, and topography heavily influence the location and growth of settlements.
• **Early River Valley Civilizations**: The earliest permanent settlements flourished along major river valleys like the Indus, Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, and Hwang-He due to abundant water and fertile silt.
• **Temporary Settlements**: Temporary settlements are dwellings occupied for short periods, commonly practiced by nomadic pastoralists, shifting cultivators, and seasonal laborers.
• **Permanent Settlements**: Permanent settlements are long-term human habitations where people build permanent homes, schools, markets, and infrastructure.
• **Classification of Settlements**: Settlements are broadly categorized into two major types based on their activities and spatial distribution: rural settlements and urban settlements.
• **Rural Settlements Definition**: Rural settlements are communities where people are primarily engaged in primary activities such as agriculture, forestry, fishing, and animal husbandry.
• **Life in Rural Areas**: In rural settlements, lifestyles are closely linked to nature, population density is low, and social ties among community members are generally strong and intimate.
• **Compact Settlements**: Compact or nucleated settlements are clusters of houses built very close to each other in fertile plain regions where flat land is readily available.
• **Dispersed Settlements**: Dispersed settlements consist of isolated houses scattered over vast areas, typically found in rugged terrain, hilly regions, dense forests, or extreme climates.

• **Urban Settlements Definition**: Urban settlements are densely populated areas where the majority of the working population is engaged in secondary and tertiary economic activities.
• **Characteristics of Urban Areas**: Urban centers feature advanced infrastructure, multi-storied buildings, specialized markets, industrial hubs, educational institutions, and administrative offices.
• **Towns and Cities**: Urban settlements vary in size and complexity, ranging from small towns and bustling cities to massive metropolitan areas and megalopolises.
• **Interdependence of Rural and Urban**: Rural and urban areas are mutually dependent; rural zones supply raw materials and food, while urban centers provide manufactured goods, healthcare, and employment.
• **Concept of Transport**: Transport is the system and process of moving people, raw materials, and finished goods from one geographic location to another.
• **Evolution of Transport**: Transport systems evolved dramatically from human porterage and animal transport to the invention of the wheel, steam engines, automobiles, and supersonic airplanes.
• **Significance of Transport**: Efficient transport networks shrink time and distance, promote trade, foster national integration, and facilitate emergency relief and cultural exchange.
• **Four Major Means of Transport**: The modern global transport network is classified into four principal modes: roadways, railways, waterways, and airways.
• **Roadways Definition**: Roadways are paved or unpaved pathways utilized by wheeled vehicles like cars, buses, trucks, bicycles, and motorcycles for short and medium-distance travel.
• **Advantages of Roadways**: Roads offer door-to-door service, connect remote terrains, and act as feeders to railways, ports, and airports.
• **Types of Roads**: Roads are categorized into metalled (pucca) roads made of concrete or bitumen and unmetalled (kutcha) roads made of mud and gravel.
• **National Highways**: National highways are major arterial roads maintained by the central government that connect capital cities, major ports, and industrial centers across a nation.
• **Expressways and Freeways**: Expressways are multi-lane, high-speed access-controlled highways designed to handle heavy vehicular traffic with minimal interruptions.
• **Mountain Roads**: Mountain roads are engineered as zig-zag hairpin bends to negotiate steep gradients, such as the Leh-Manali Highway in the Himalayas.
• **Subways and Underpasses**: Urban transport relies heavily on subways, flyovers, and underpasses to ease traffic congestion in densely populated metropolitan zones.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Railways Definition**: Railways are systems of transport operating on fixed parallel metal tracks (rails), highly efficient for carrying heavy bulk freight and large numbers of passengers over long distances.
• **Invention of Steam Engine**: The invention of the steam engine by James Watt during the Industrial Revolution revolutionized railway systems globally.
• **Advantages of Railways**: Railways are cheaper for long-distance travel, unaffected by minor weather disruptions, and indispensable for industrial freight movement.
• **Trans-Continental Railways**: Trans-continental railways span entire continents, such as the Trans-Siberian Railway in Russia and the Trans-Australian Railway.
• **Underground Railways (Metro)**: Metro rail systems provide rapid, high-frequency transit beneath congested city streets, significantly reducing urban air pollution and traffic jams.
• **Bullet Trains (High-Speed Rail)**: High-speed rail networks, like Japan's Shinkansen, operate at speeds exceeding 300 km/h, bridging distant cities within hours.
• **Indian Railways Network**: Indian Railways is one of the largest railway networks in the world, serving as the economic lifeline of the nation.
• **Waterways Definition**: Waterways utilize natural and artificial water bodies—rivers, lakes, seas, and oceans—for navigating boats, ships, and barges.
• **Economic Efficiency of Waterways**: Waterways represent the cheapest mode of transport for bulky, heavy, and non-perishable goods over intercontinental distances because they require no track construction.
• **Two Categories of Waterways**: Waterways are fundamentally divided into inland waterways and ocean/sea routes.
• **Inland Waterways**: Inland waterways comprise navigable rivers and lakes, such as the Ganga-Brahmaputra river system in India and the Great Lakes-St. Lawrence Seaway in North America.
• **Significance of Inland Waterways**: Inland waterways facilitate regional trade, reduce highway congestion, and support local economies living along river basins.
• **Ocean Routes**: Ocean routes connect major maritime nations across the globe, facilitating international trade and globalized commerce.
• **Key Oceanic Lanes**: Important ocean routes include the North Atlantic Sea Route, the Mediterranean-Asiatic Route, and the Cape of Good Hope Route.
• **Major Canals in Transport**: Artificial canals like the Suez Canal and the Panama Canal drastically reduced maritime travel time by creating shortcuts between major oceans.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Suez Canal Impact**: The Suez Canal connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, eliminating the need to circumnavigate the entire continent of Africa.
• **Panama Canal Impact**: The Panama Canal links the Atlantic Ocean with the Pacific Ocean across the Isthmus of Panama, transforming global shipping lanes.
• **Airways Definition**: Airways are the fastest, most advanced, and most expensive mode of transport, utilizing aircraft such as aeroplanes and helicopters to traverse the skies.
• **Invention of Aeroplane**: The Wright brothers successfully flew the first powered airplane in 1903, marking the dawn of modern aviation.
• **Advantages of Airways**: Airways can cross impassable geographical barriers like towering mountains, vast oceans, thick deserts, and dense forests with unmatched speed.
• **Role in Emergencies**: Airways are vital during natural disasters, wars, and medical crises for airlifting supplies, rescuing stranded victims, and transporting critical patients.
• **Disadvantages of Airways**: High operational costs, weather dependence (fog, storms), and massive infrastructure requirements (runways, air traffic control) limit universal accessibility.
• **Major International Airports**: Global cities are connected by mega international airports that act as critical aviation hubs for passenger and cargo transit.
• **Helicopters in Remote Access**: Helicopters can take off and land vertically, making them indispensable for high-altitude rescue missions, inaccessible terrain, and remote areas.
• **Concept of Communication**: Communication is the transmission, reception, and exchange of information, ideas, messages, opinions, and emotions between individuals or groups.
• **Evolution of Communication**: Communication progressed from primitive smoke signals, drum beats, and messenger pigeons to postal services, print media, telecommunications, and the internet.
• **Role of Communication in Modern Society**: Rapid communication keeps societies informed, unites global economies, coordinates disaster management, and accelerates technological progress.
• **Mass Media Definition**: Mass media refers to technological tools and communication platforms designed to reach massive audiences simultaneously across geographical borders.
• **Print Media**: Print media includes newspapers, magazines, journals, books, and pamphlets that provide detailed news, educational content, and literature.
• **Electronic Media**: Electronic media encompasses radio, television, and cinema, providing audio-visual broadcasting of news, entertainment, and public awareness campaigns.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Role of Radio**: Radio is an accessible mass medium that delivers news, music, and educational programs even to remote, illiterate, or electricity-deprived rural communities.
• **Role of Television**: Television combines audio and visual elements, bringing live news, documentaries, sports, and cultural events directly into homes worldwide.
• **Personal Communication**: Personal communication involves direct, one-to-one interaction between two or more specific individuals via letters, telephone calls, emails, or chat apps.
• **Postal Services**: Postal networks handle physical letters, parcels, money orders, and documents nationally and internationally, managed by government postal departments.
• **Telecommunication Revolution**: The invention of the telegraph, telephone, and mobile phone revolutionized personal communication by enabling instant voice interaction over vast distances.
• **Mobile Phones and Smartphones**: Modern smartphones act as portable computers, combining voice calling, messaging, photography, navigation, and internet browsing in one device.
• **The Internet**: The internet is a global network of interconnected computer systems that facilitates instantaneous worldwide data sharing and communication.
• **World Wide Web (WWW)**: The World Wide Web allows users to access a vast repository of web pages, documents, and multimedia content through web browsers.
• **Email and Digital Messaging**: Electronic mail (email) and instant messaging apps have virtually replaced traditional mail for business and personal correspondence due to speed and zero paper waste.
• **Social Media Platforms**: Social media platforms like blogs, networking sites, and video-sharing portals enable users to create, share content, and build global communities.
• **Satellites in Communication**: Artificial satellites orbiting Earth receive and transmit signals for television broadcasting, telecommunications, weather forecasting, and GPS navigation.
• **Global Positioning System (GPS)**: GPS technology utilizes satellite networks to provide real-time location tracking, navigation maps, and spatial data for transport.
• **Cyber Security and Digital Ethics**: With the expansion of digital communication, safeguarding personal data, preventing cybercrime, and practicing online etiquette have become critical.
• **Information Age**: Contemporary society is often designated as the Information Age because data flows instantly across the globe, transforming economies into knowledge-based systems.
• **Environmental Impact of Transport and Communication**: While technology connects the world, increased vehicular traffic and electronic infrastructure present serious environmental challenges like pollution.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Traffic Congestion**: Rapid urbanization and rising vehicle ownership lead to severe traffic jams in major cities, wasting fuel and increasing commuter stress.
• **Air Pollution from Vehicles**: Exhaust fumes from cars, trucks, and buses release harmful gases like carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, and particulate matter into the atmosphere.
• **Noise Pollution**: Honking, heavy engines, and railway operations contribute to urban noise pollution, negatively impacting human health and wildlife.
• **Greenhouse Gas Emissions**: Burning fossil fuels in transport engines releases massive amounts of carbon dioxide, accelerating global warming and climate change.
• **Sustainable Transport Solutions**: Promoting eco-friendly travel options like electric vehicles (EVs), carpooling, cycling, and walking helps reduce carbon footprints.
• **Public Transport Promotion**: Expanding efficient public transport systems (metros, buses, suburban trains) discourages private vehicle usage and eases road congestion.
• **Green Corridors and Urban Planning**: Designing sustainable smart cities involves creating green corridors, pedestrian zones, and dedicated bicycle tracks.
• **Role of Renewable Energy**: Transitioning transport and communication infrastructure to renewable energy sources like solar and wind power minimizes environmental degradation.
• **Digital Divide**: The disparity in access to internet and communication technology between urban and rural populations, or rich and poor nations, is known as the digital divide.
• **Bridging the Digital Divide**: Governments and NGOs work to bridge this gap by providing affordable internet access, digital literacy programs, and rural computer labs.
• **Globalization and Connectivity**: Advanced transport and communication weave the world into a single interconnected global village where borders blur for trade and culture.
• **Cultural Exchange**: Instant communication and easy travel foster cross-cultural understanding, appreciation of diverse cuisines, arts, and global citizenship.
• **Economic Globalization**: Multinational corporations rely on fast shipping and telecommunication to manage global supply chains, outsourcing, and international markets.
• **Interdependent World Economy**: Financial markets in New York, London, Tokyo, and Mumbai operate simultaneously through digital networks, forming a unified global economy.
• **Summary of Chapter Themes**: Human settlements, transport networks, and communication systems are the three pillars shaping the human environment and societal evolution.
