• **Addition**: The mathematical process of combining two or more numbers to find their total or sum.
• **Addends**: The individual numbers being added together in an addition operation.
• **Sum**: The final result obtained after performing the addition of two or more addends.
• **Place Value**: The value represented by a digit based on its position (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) in a number.
• **Alignment**: The process of vertically arranging numbers according to their place values before adding or subtracting.
• **Regrouping (Carrying)**: The process of moving a value from a lower place value column to a higher one when the sum exceeds 9.
• **Addition Property of Zero**: Adding zero to any number results in the number itself (e.g., 456 + 0 = 456).
• **Commutative Property of Addition**: Changing the order of addends does not change the sum (e.g., 120 + 340 = 340 + 120).
• **Associative Property of Addition**: The way in which addends are grouped does not change the sum (e.g., (1+2)+3 = 1+(2+3)).
• **Addition with Regrouping**: Occurs when the sum of digits in a column is 10 or greater, requiring the tens digit to be carried over.
• **Column Addition**: A method where numbers are written one below the other, aligning digits by place value.
• **Horizontal Addition**: Adding numbers written in a line, often by breaking them into expanded forms.
• **Expanded Form**: Writing a number as the sum of its place values (e.g., 452 = 400 + 50 + 2).
• **Mental Addition**: The technique of adding numbers quickly in one’s head using strategies like splitting or rounding.
• **Estimation**: Rounding numbers to the nearest ten or hundred to find an approximate sum before calculating the exact total.

• **Subtraction**: The mathematical process of taking one number away from another to find the difference.
• **Minuend**: The larger number from which another number is subtracted.
• **Subtrahend**: The number that is being subtracted from the minuend.
• **Difference**: The final result obtained after performing the subtraction operation.
• **Subtraction Property of Zero**: Subtracting zero from any number leaves the number unchanged (e.g., 500 - 0 = 500).
• **Subtraction Property of Self**: Subtracting a number from itself always results in zero (e.g., 89 - 89 = 0).
• **Borrowing (Regrouping)**: The process of taking a value from a higher place value column when the digit in the minuend is smaller than the digit in the subtrahend.
• **Decomposition**: Breaking down a number into smaller units to make subtraction easier, especially when borrowing is required.
• **Subtraction of 4-Digit Numbers**: Involves subtracting ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands sequentially from right to left.
• **Checking Subtraction**: A method to verify the answer by adding the difference to the subtrahend; the result must equal the minuend.
• **Relationship between Addition and Subtraction**: They are inverse operations; if A + B = C, then C - B = A and C - A = B.
• **Subtraction Across Zeros**: A specific technique where borrowing occurs across multiple columns containing zeros (e.g., 1000 - 456).
• **Regrouping Thousands**: When the thousands digit must be reduced to provide value to the hundreds column.
• **Place Value Alignment in Subtraction**: Essential for accuracy; failing to align digits leads to incorrect differences.
• **Standard Algorithm**: The formal step-by-step procedure for solving multi-digit subtraction problems.
