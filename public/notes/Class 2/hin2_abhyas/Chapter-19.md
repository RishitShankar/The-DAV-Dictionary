• **तिरंगा (Tiranga):** The term refers to the National Flag of India, which is a symbol of national pride, sovereignty, and unity.
• **Three Colors:** The name 'Tiranga' is derived from the Sanskrit word 'Tri' (three) and 'Ranga' (color), signifying the three horizontal bands.
• **Saffron (Kesariya):** The top band of the flag is saffron, which represents courage, sacrifice, and the spirit of renunciation.
• **White (Safed):** The middle band is white, symbolizing peace, purity, and truth, serving as a guiding light for the nation.
• **Green (Hara):** The bottom band is green, representing faith, fertility, growth, and the auspiciousness of the land.
• **Ashoka Chakra:** Located in the center of the white band is a navy-blue wheel known as the Ashoka Chakra.
• **Wheel of Progress:** The Ashoka Chakra represents the 'Dharmachakra' or the wheel of law, signifying constant movement and progress.
• **24 Spokes:** The Ashoka Chakra contains 24 spokes, which symbolize the 24 hours of the day and the continuous march of time.
• **National Identity:** The Tiranga is the most important symbol of our national identity and is respected by every citizen of India.
• **Respect for Flag:** It is the duty of every student and citizen to show utmost respect to the national flag at all times.
• **Flag Hoisting:** The flag is hoisted during national festivals like Independence Day and Republic Day to commemorate our freedom.
• **Symbol of Freedom:** The flag represents the long struggle for independence and the sacrifices made by our freedom fighters.
• **Unity in Diversity:** The three colors together represent the diverse cultures, religions, and regions of India united under one banner.
• **Patriotism:** Learning about the Tiranga fosters a sense of patriotism and love for the motherland among young students.
• **National Pride:** Displaying the flag brings a sense of belonging and collective pride to the entire nation.

• **Design Specifications:** The national flag must be rectangular in shape, with a length-to-width ratio of 3:2.
• **Fabric Standards:** Traditionally, the flag is made of 'Khadi', which is hand-spun cotton or silk, symbolizing self-reliance.
• **Historical Significance:** The current design of the Tiranga was adopted by the Constituent Assembly of India on July 22, 1947.
• **Pingali Venkayya:** The credit for the original design of the Indian national flag goes to Pingali Venkayya, a freedom fighter.
• **Flag Code of India:** There is a strict 'Flag Code' that dictates how the flag should be displayed, handled, and stored.
• **Respectful Handling:** The flag should never touch the ground or be dipped in front of any person or thing.
• **No Damage:** A damaged or disheveled flag should never be displayed, as it is considered disrespectful to the nation.
• **Sunrise to Sunset:** Generally, the flag is hoisted from sunrise to sunset, though modern rules allow for night display under specific lighting.
• **Placement:** When displayed with other flags, the Tiranga must always occupy the position of honor.
• **State Funerals:** The flag is flown at half-mast only during periods of national mourning as directed by the government.
• **Public Display:** Citizens are encouraged to display the flag in their homes and offices to express their national spirit.
• **Educational Value:** Understanding the flag helps children connect with the history and values of their country.
• **Visual Symbolism:** The colors are not just decorative; they are deeply rooted in the philosophy of Indian culture.
• **Saffron Meaning:** Saffron is also associated with the spiritual heritage of India, representing the pursuit of truth.
• **Green Meaning:** Green represents the agricultural prosperity of India, which is the backbone of the nation's economy.
