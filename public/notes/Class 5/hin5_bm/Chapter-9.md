• **Chapter Overview**: **'अजंता की सैर'** (Ajaanta Ki Sair - Journey to Ajanta) is an informative and travelogue-style chapter from the DAV Public School Class 5 **भाषा माधुरी** text book.
• **Literary Genre**: The chapter is written in the form of a travelogue (यात्रा-वृत्तांत), detailing a personal or guided journey to one of India's most historical and artistic treasures.
• **Primary Subject**: The caves of Ajanta, their location, historical significance, architectural brilliance, and world-renowned paintings and sculptures are the core subjects of this chapter.
• **Educational Objective**: To introduce young learners to the glorious ancient heritage, Indian art, architecture, and historical monuments of India through the DAV curriculum.
• **Geographical Location**: Ajanta caves are located in the Aurangabad district of the Indian state of Maharashtra, nestled in a horse-shoe shaped rock gorge.
• **Natural Setting**: The caves are carved out of perpendicular rock cliffs along a loop of the Waghora River, providing a serene and picturesque natural backdrop.
• **Discovery and History**: These ancient rock-cut caves date back from the 2nd century BCE to about 480 CE, representing a golden period of Indian history.
• **UNESCO World Heritage**: The chapter highlights the global importance of Ajanta, which was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1983 due to its universal artistic value.
• **Buddhist Connection**: Most of the Ajanta caves are Buddhist monasteries (Viharas) and prayer halls (Chaityas), reflecting the deep roots of Buddhism in ancient India.
• **Artistic Mastery**: The text emphasizes the exceptional mastery of ancient Indian artists who carved massive sanctuaries out of solid basalt rock using only primitive tools like chisels and hammers.
• **Rock-Cut Architecture**: **Rock-cut architecture** (शैलकृत वास्तुकला) is a prominent theme, explaining how entire temples and halls were excavated top-down from living rock mountains.
• **Narrative Tone**: The narrative style of the chapter invites students to imagine traveling along with the narrator on an educational excursion to witness these wonders firsthand.
• **Cultural Pride**: A core underlying value of the chapter is to instill a sense of pride and responsibility among students toward preserving India's rich cultural monuments.
• **Integration of History and Geography**: The chapter seamlessly blends historical timelines of the Gupta and Vakataka dynasties with geographical facts about Maharashtra's terrain.
• **Visual Imagery**: The descriptive language used in the text paints a vivid mental picture of ancient murals, dim-lit cave interiors, and magnificent stone-carved pillars.

• **The Horseshoe Gorge**: The chapter describes the geographical formation of the gorge, shaped like a horse's shoe, carved by the meandering flow of the Waghora River.
• **Waghora River Significance**: The Waghora River acts as a natural boundary and a source of life for the valley, with a picturesque waterfall located near the end of the cave horseshoe.
• **Approach Path**: Visitors walk along a paved pathway constructed by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) that runs parallel to the cliff face, connecting all the caves.
• **Numbering of Caves**: The text explains the systematic numbering of the Ajanta caves from west to east, ranging from Cave No. 1 to Cave No. 29 (or 30), assigned by archaeologists.
• **Two Main Types of Caves**: The architecture of Ajanta is broadly categorized into two types: **Chaityas** (places of worship/prayer halls) and **Viharas** (residential monasteries for monks).
• **Chaitya Halls (चैत्य गृह)**: These are large, rectangular prayer halls featuring a stupa at one end, high vaulted ceilings, and aisles separated by massive stone pillars.
• **Vihara Monasteries (विहार)**: These are residential complexes comprising a central square hall surrounded by a series of small, cell-like rooms where Buddhist monks lived and meditated.
• **Monolithic Creation**: The chapter underscores the astonishing engineering feat that every single statue, pillar, wall, and ceiling was carved out of a single, continuous piece of rock.
• **No Addition of Material**: None of the structures were built by adding bricks or mortar; rather, everything was created entirely through subtraction—chiseling away excess rock.
• **Lighting in Ancient Times**: The text prompts curiosity about how ancient artists worked in the pitch-dark interiors of the caves using polished copper mirrors to reflect sunlight or torches.

• **The Paintings of Ajanta**: A major highlight of the chapter is the description of the world-famous wall paintings (frescos) that adorn the walls and ceilings of the caves.
• **Technique of Painting**: The ancient technique involved applying a layer of mud, clay, and cow dung mixed with plant fibers, followed by a layer of lime plaster before painting.
• **Natural Pigments**: All colors used in the Ajanta paintings were derived from natural sources such as minerals, rocks, plant extracts, colored earths, and carbon.
• **Longevity of Colors**: Despite being centuries old, many of the colors in Ajanta paintings have retained their vibrancy, showcasing advanced chemical knowledge of ancient artisans.
• **Themes of the Murals**: The paintings primarily depict the life of Gautama Buddha, various **Jataka Tales** (stories of Buddha's previous births), and scenes from contemporary royal and daily life.
• **Human Emotions and Expressions**: The text notes that the faces of the figures in the paintings capture a profound range of human emotions—joy, sorrow, compassion, devotion, and contemplation.
• **Padmapani Bodhisattva**: The chapter specifically draws attention to the iconic painting of **Padmapani** (Avalokiteshvara), renowned for its grace, gentle expression, and spiritual aura.
• **Vajrapani Bodhisattva**: Another masterpiece highlighted is **Vajrapani**, depicting strength, divine protection, and serene majesty alongside Padmapani.
• **Portrayal of Nature**: The murals meticulously detail flora and fauna, including blooming lotuses, flowering trees, birds, monkeys, elephants, and deer, reflecting a deep harmony with nature.
• **Depiction of Royal Courts**: Some panels vividly portray royal processions, court dancers, musicians playing ancient instruments, and queens gazing thoughtfully out of balconies.
• **Global Artistic Influence**: The Ajanta art style influenced artistic traditions across Asia, leaving its indelible mark on paintings in Tibet, China (Dunhuang caves), Japan, and Sri Lanka (Sigiriya).
• **Sculptural Mastery**: Alongside paintings, the chapter discusses the magnificent stone sculptures carved directly into the rock walls, displaying intricate ornamentation and drapery.
• **Reclining Buddha**: One of the most awe-inspiring sculptures mentioned is the grand statue of the Buddha in Mahaparinirvana (final liberation) in a reclining posture.
• **Pillar Carvings**: The stone pillars inside the Chaityas are not plain; they are intricately carved with floral motifs, flying celestial beings (Gandharvas), and geometric patterns.
• **Preservation Challenges**: The text touches upon the historical neglect, natural weathering, and damage caused by moisture, bats, and smoke over centuries before modern conservation began.

• **Historical Timeline**: The creation of Ajanta caves spanned across two distinct historical phases separated by several centuries, reflecting changing political patronage.
• **First Phase (Satavahana Period)**: The earlier phase dates back to the 2nd century BCE to 1st century CE, executed under the patronage of the **Satavahana dynasty**.
• **Hinayana Buddhism**: Caves built during the Satavahana period strictly belonged to the Hinayana sect, where Buddha was represented only through symbols like footprints, thrones, or stupas.
• **Second Phase (Vakataka Period)**: The later, more elaborate phase occurred in the 5th century CE during the reign of the **Vakataka dynasty** and Emperor Harishena.
• **Mahayana Buddhism**: The second phase marked the rise of Mahayana Buddhism, where statues of Buddha in human form (sitting, standing, and reclining) were introduced.
• **Royal Patronage**: Feudatories and ministers of the Vakataka kings generously funded the excavation and decoration of these magnificent caves, as evidenced by inscriptions.
• **Sudden Abandonment**: The chapter notes the mystery surrounding the sudden halting of work and abandonment of the caves around the late 5th or early 6th century CE.
• **Re-discovery in Modern Times**: For centuries, the caves remained forgotten by the outside world, swallowed by dense jungle growth and known only to local tribal communities.
• **British Officer's Discovery**: The modern re-discovery occurred in April **1819**, when a British cavalry officer named **John Smith** stumbled upon Cave No. 10 while hunting tigers.
• **Inscribing a Name**: Officer John Smith famously scratched his name and the date on a pillar in Cave No. 10, a historical mark that can still be seen today.
• **Spread of Awareness**: Following Smith's discovery, news of the magnificent hidden art treasure spread rapidly across scholarly and archaeological circles in the West and India.
• **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)**: The chapter acknowledges the crucial role played by ASI in clearing debris, restoring crumbling plaster, and protecting the site from further decay.
• **Tourist Attraction**: Today, Ajanta stands as a premier global tourist destination, welcoming history enthusiasts, art historians, students, and travelers from every corner of the world.
• **Guided Tours and Lighting**: Modern conservation includes regulated artificial lighting inside the caves to protect the delicate paintings from heat and humidity while allowing visitors to view them.
• **Educational Value for Students**: The chapter concludes segments by emphasizing that visiting such sites bridges the gap between textbook history and tangible reality for young learners.

• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 1**: Cave No. 1 is presented in the chapter as one of the finest and most visually stunning Vihara monasteries in Ajanta.
• **Facade and Porch**: The entrance porch of Cave No. 1 features intricately carved pillars and stone beams adorned with ornamental friezes of animals, flowers, and dwarfs.
• **Shrine Buddha Image**: Inside Cave No. 1, the central shrine houses a colossal, serene image of Lord Buddha in the **Dharmachakra Pravartana Mudra** (setting the wheel of law in motion).
• **Masterpiece Murals of Cave 1**: This cave is home to the world-famous murals of the two great Bodhisattvas—Padmapani and Vajrapani—flanking the inner shrine doorway.
• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 2**: Located next to Cave 1, Cave No. 2 is famous for its elaborately carved ceiling patterns, ceiling murals, and unique pillar designs.
• **Ceiling Art in Cave 2**: The ceiling of Cave No. 2 displays a riot of geometric designs, fruit motifs, flying geese, and mythological creatures painted with extraordinary precision.
• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 9 and 10**: These are identified as the earliest Chaitya (prayer) halls in Ajanta, dating back to the Satavahana period.
• **Simplicity of Early Caves**: Unlike the ornate later caves, Caves 9 and 10 feature simpler architectural lines, plain pillar designs, and early symbols of Buddhism.
• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 16**: Cave No. 16 is a magnificent Vihara known for its famous narrative paintings, particularly the **'Dying Princess'** (मरणसन्न राजकुमारी).
    • **The Dying Princess Narrative**: This poignant mural depicts a princess collapsing in grief upon hearing that her husband has renounced the world to become a Buddhist monk.
• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 17**: Cave No. 17 is celebrated as a virtual art gallery containing the highest number of well-preserved and diverse wall paintings.
• **Mother and Child Mural**: Cave 17 features the famous touching mural of **'Mother and Child before Buddha'**, showing Yashodhara and Rahula seeking alms from Gautama Buddha.
• **The Wheel of Life (Bhavachakra)**: Also found in Cave 17 is an early depiction of the Wheel of Life, illustrating the Buddhist concept of Samsara (cycle of rebirth and karma).
• **Detailed Walkthrough of Cave No. 19**: Cave No. 19 is an exquisite Chaitya hall featuring an intricately carved facade, ornamental windows, and standing Buddha figures.
• **Stupa Architecture in Cave 19**: The monolithic stupa inside Cave 19 features a standing relief image of the Buddha carved directly onto the front of the stupa itself.

• **The Role of Monks in Ajanta**: The chapter sheds light on the daily lives of Buddhist monks who lived, studied, and meditated in the Viharas of Ajanta.
• **Center of Learning**: Ajanta was not just a residential cave complex; it functioned as a vibrant university and center for Buddhist philosophy, meditation, and art.
• **Meditation Practices**: Monks spent hours in deep meditation inside the dark, quiet stone cells, seeking spiritual enlightenment and inner peace away from worldly distractions.
• **Patronage of Merchants and Kings**: The construction and maintenance of these massive caves were heavily supported by wealthy merchants, guilds, and royal families seeking spiritual merit.
• **Inscriptions as Historical Records**: Numerous stone inscriptions found inside the caves provide historians with names of donors, ruling monarchs, and the chronological sequence of excavation.
• **The Decline of Buddhism**: With shifting political powers, revival of Hinduism, and changing economic conditions, Buddhism gradually lost royal patronage in the region.
• **Desertion of the Valley**: As monks migrated to other regions or monasteries, the Ajanta valley fell silent, leaving the halls to nature, bats, and creeping vines.
• **Nature's Cloak**: Over a millennium, thick tropical forests enveloped the cliff faces, effectively hiding the cave entrances from passing travelers and armies.
• **Significance of Conservation**: The chapter highlights why modern governments and international bodies spend immense resources on chemical conservation of the paintings.
• **Threats from Moisture and Humidity**: Water seepage through rock fissures is identified as a major threat that causes mineral salt crystallization and flaking of the plaster.
• **Tourism Management**: Managing tourist footfall is discussed as a delicate balance between allowing public access and controlling humidity, carbon dioxide, and temperature inside the caves.
• **Lighting Restrictions**: Flash photography is strictly prohibited inside the Ajanta caves because camera light flashes emit ultraviolet and infrared rays that degrade delicate pigments.
• **Educational Trips in DAV Culture**: The chapter encourages DAV schools to organize heritage excursions, reinforcing the DAV ethos of experiential learning and cultural rootedness.
• **Connecting Past and Present**: Students are inspired to view historical monuments not as dead stones, but as living testaments to human creativity and perseverance.

• **Summary of Key Facts - Location**: Ajanta caves are located near Aurangabad in Maharashtra, carved into a horse-shoe shaped ravine along the Waghora River.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Number of Caves**: There are approximately 29 to 30 rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments spanning two distinct historical periods.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Cave Types**: The caves are divided into **Chaityas** (prayer halls) and **Viharas** (monasteries).
• **Summary of Key Facts - Discovery**: Re-discovered in **1819** by British officer **John Smith** during a hunting expedition.
• **Summary of Key Facts - UNESCO Status**: Declared a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** in **1983** in recognition of its universal cultural and artistic importance.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Artistic Mediums**: Features masterclass stone sculptures, monolithic pillars, and vibrant mural paintings (frescos) created using natural pigments.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Major Themes**: Artworks depict the life of Gautama Buddha, Jataka tales, celestial beings, royal processions, and rich flora and fauna.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Iconic Murals**: Most famous paintings include **Padmapani Bodhisattva**, **Vajrapani Bodhisattva**, and **The Dying Princess**.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Engineering Marvel**: Carved entirely by hand from solid basalt rock cliffs using chisels and hammers without any structural assembly.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Historical Eras**: Developed in two phases—Satavahana period (2nd century BCE onwards) and Vakataka period (5th century CE).
• **Summary of Key Facts - Linguistic Value**: Enriches Class 5 Hindi vocabulary with heritage-related terminology, descriptive adjectives, and travelogue phrasing.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Environmental Harmony**: Built amidst serene natural surroundings, reflecting the ancient Indian ethos of co-existing peacefully with nature.
• **Summary of Key Facts - Conservation Need**: Requires rigorous scientific preservation against humidity, water seepage, and visitor-induced atmospheric changes.
