• **Chapter Title:** Community Services (DAV Public School Board, Class 5, We and Our World).
• **Core Subject:** Social Studies emphasizing civic responsibility, community interdependence, and social welfare.
• **Definition of Community:** A community is a social group of people living in the same place, sharing a common environment, culture, and mutual interests.
• **Concept of Community Life:** Humans are social beings who cannot live in isolation and depend on one another for daily survival, safety, and growth.
• **What is Community Service?:** Community service refers to unpaid work or voluntary activities performed by individuals or groups for the betterment of the local community.
• **Primary Objective:** The main goal of community service is to improve the quality of life for residents, foster social cohesion, and assist the underprivileged.
• **Interdependence:** Community members rely on different professionals and service providers, such as teachers, doctors, sanitation workers, and police officers.
• **Role of the Individual:** Every citizen has a fundamental moral and civic duty to contribute positively to the welfare of their immediate surroundings.
• **DAV Values Integration:** Imbibing the Arya Samaj principle of working for the physical, spiritual, and social upliftment of all human beings.
• **Scope of Services:** Community services range from basic sanitation and health care to disaster management, education, and environmental protection.
• **Voluntary Nature:** Most community services are driven by altruism, compassion, and the intrinsic desire to help others without expecting monetary rewards.
• **Historical Roots:** Traditional Indian society deeply valued community work, often seen in cooperative farming, building village wells, and organizing community kitchens.
• **Civic Pride:** Active participation in community service builds a strong sense of pride, ownership, and belonging among residents.
• **Social Harmony:** Working together for common causes bridges social, economic, and cultural divides among diverse groups of people.
• **Holistic Development:** Engaging in community service helps students develop empathy, leadership qualities, teamwork, and social responsibility.

• **Basic Civic Amenities:** Essential facilities provided within a community to ensure a healthy, comfortable, and civilized standard of living for all citizens.
• **Water Supply Systems:** Local authorities ensure the treatment and distribution of clean, potable drinking water to every household in the community.
• **Sanitation and Waste Management:** Proper collection, segregation, and disposal of garbage to maintain public hygiene and prevent the outbreak of diseases.
• **Electricity and Power:** Reliable supply of electricity for domestic, commercial, and industrial purposes, managed by local or state electricity boards.
• **Roads and Transport:** Well-maintained roads, streetlights, and public transportation networks that connect different parts of the community safely and efficiently.
• **Healthcare Infrastructure:** Provision of hospitals, dispensaries, primary health centers, and mobile medical vans for immediate medical attention.
• **Educational Institutions:** Schools, libraries, and vocational training centers established to impart knowledge and build skills among the youth.
• **Parks and Recreation:** Playgrounds, public gardens, and community halls that promote physical fitness, mental relaxation, and social gatherings.
• **Fire and Safety Services:** Fire stations equipped with modern machinery and trained personnel to tackle accidental fires and rescue trapped citizens.
• **Law and Order:** Police stations and chowkis that maintain peace, protect citizens from crime, and enforce the rule of law.
• **Postal and Communication Services:** Post offices, courier services, and telecom infrastructure that facilitate seamless communication within and outside the community.
• **Drainage and Sewage Systems:** Underground networks designed to carry waste water away safely, preventing water stagnation and mosquito breeding.
• **Public Distribution System (PDS):** Fair-price shops that supply subsidized food grains and essential commodities to economically weaker sections.
• **Animal Care Services:** Veterinary hospitals and animal shelters managed by the community or government to protect stray and domestic animals.
• **Maintenance and Upkeep:** Regular repair and maintenance of public property by civic bodies to prevent decay and ensure long-term utility.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Civic Agencies:** Government or semi-government organizations established to administer towns and cities and provide civic amenities.
• **Municipal Corporation (Nagar Nigam):** The apex local governing body for big cities and metropolitan areas, responsible for large-scale urban administration.
• **Municipal Council (Nagar Palika):** The local governing body responsible for smaller towns and urban areas with moderate population density.
• **Nagar Panchayat:** The governing body that looks after areas transitioning from rural to urban settlements (semi-urban regions).
• **Gram Panchayat:** The primary unit of local self-government in rural areas, operating at the village level to manage local affairs.
• **Mayor:** The elected political head of a Municipal Corporation who presides over corporation meetings and guides policy decisions.
• **Municipal Commissioner:** An administrative official appointed by the state government to execute the policies and decisions of the Municipal Corporation.
• **Sources of Municipal Revenue:** Funds collected through property taxes, water taxes, toll taxes, vehicle registration fees, and grants from the state government.
• **Role of Ward Councilors:** Elected representatives from specific wards who voice the local concerns and developmental needs of their constituents.
• **Safai Karmacharis:** Dedicated sanitation workers responsible for sweeping streets, clearing drains, and collecting household garbage daily.
• **Health Officers:** Officials who monitor food safety in local markets, inspect restaurants, and lead vaccination or vector-control drives.
• **Town Planning Department:** A specialized wing that regulates building construction, zoning laws, and commercial development to prevent haphazard urban sprawl.
• **Public Works Department (PWD):** Agency responsible for constructing and repairing major public roads, bridges, and government buildings.
• **Disaster Response Units:** Specialized municipal teams trained to handle emergencies like floods, building collapses, and major accidents.
• **Citizen Grievance Redressal:** Toll-free helplines and online portals set up by civic bodies to address complaints regarding streetlights, water leakage, and garbage dumps.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Voluntary Organizations (NGOs):** Non-Governmental Organizations operating independently of the government to address social, environmental, and humanitarian issues.
• **Characteristics of NGOs:** Driven by ideals of social service, flexibility, community participation, and reliance on donations or grants.
• **Red Cross Society:** A prominent international organization providing emergency assistance, disaster relief, and blood donation camps within communities.
• **St. John Ambulance:** An organization that trains citizens in first aid, home nursing, and emergency response during accidents or calamities.
• **Neighborhood Watch Groups:** Community-led initiatives where residents cooperate to monitor suspicious activities and enhance local safety and security.
• **Residents' Welfare Associations (RWAs):** Formal bodies of apartment or colony residents that manage shared utilities, security guards, and community festivals.
• **Youth Clubs and Scouts/Guides:** Organizations that instill discipline, leadership, and a spirit of volunteerism among children and young adults.
• **Environmental Action Groups:** Grassroots organizations that conduct tree-plantation drives, cleanliness campaigns, and anti-plastic awareness rallies.
• **Old Age Homes and Orphanages:** Institutions run by charitable trusts to provide shelter, food, care, and dignity to abandoned elderly people and orphans.
• **Free Medical Camps:** Periodic health check-up camps organized by voluntary bodies in slums and rural areas to provide free medicines and consultations.
• **Blood Donation Drives:** Organized events where healthy citizens voluntarily donate blood to replenish hospital blood banks for emergencies.
• **Literacy Campaigns:** Volunteer-driven educational programs aimed at teaching basic reading and writing skills to underprivileged adults and street children.
• **Self-Help Groups (SHGs):** Community networks, particularly among women, formed to promote financial savings, micro-credit, and small-scale entrepreneurship.
• **Animal Welfare NGOs:** Groups dedicated to rescuing injured stray animals, promoting anti-cruelty laws, and organizing mass vaccination and sterilization drives.
• **Relief and Rehabilitation:** Immediate mobilization of food, clothing, and shelter materials by voluntary groups during natural disasters like earthquakes and floods.

• **Concept of Public Property:** Infrastructure, buildings, parks, monuments, and utilities owned collectively by the public and maintained by the government.
• **Examples of Public Property:** Government hospitals, public buses, trains, railway stations, historical monuments, public parks, streetlights, and public schools.
• **Significance of Public Property:** These facilities are built using taxpayers' money to ensure equal access and convenience for every member of the society.
• **Shared Ownership:** Every citizen is a co-owner of public property, implying a collective responsibility to protect and preserve it.
• **Vandalism:** The deliberate destruction, defacement, or damaging of public property through graffiti, breaking benches, or tearing bus seats.
• **Misuse of Public Utilities:** Leaving public taps running, wasting electricity in public buildings, and littering historical sites or parks.
• **Civic Sense:** The unwritten social code that guides individuals to behave respectfully and responsibly in public spaces.
• **Conservation of Heritage:** Protecting historical monuments from defacement (like writing names on walls) and preserving their archaeological value.
• **Role of Students:** Children must be taught early not to write on school desks, tear library books, or damage school playground equipment.
• **Legal Penalties:** Strict laws and fines imposed by the government against individuals caught damaging public property or littering public spaces.
• **Cleanliness Campaigns:** Initiatives like Swachh Bharat Abhiyan that emphasize the importance of keeping public places clean and hygienic.
• **Graffiti and Posters:** Unauthorized sticking of posters or painting on public walls, which spoils the visual aesthetics of the community.
• **Reporting Damage:** Citizens should promptly report leaking pipes, broken streetlights, or damaged public property to the respective civic authorities.
• **Respecting Public Transport:** Keeping buses and trains clean, offering seats to the elderly, and avoiding ticketless travel.
• **Intergenerational Duty:** Preserving public infrastructure in good condition so that future generations can benefit from it without extra financial burden.

• **Health and Sanitation Services:** Crucial community services focused on maintaining personal and public hygiene to prevent disease outbreaks.
• **Role of Hospitals:** Providing diagnosis, treatment, emergency care, and surgeries for various illnesses and injuries.
• **Primary Health Centers (PHCs):** Grassroots medical units in rural areas that offer basic healthcare, maternal care, and child immunization programs.
• **Immunization Programs:** Government-led vaccination drives (like Pulse Polio) that protect children from deadly infectious diseases.
• **Vector Control:** Municipal measures to control vectors like mosquitoes and rodents by spraying insecticides, fogging, and clearing stagnant water.
• **Safe Drinking Water Testing:** Regular laboratory testing of municipal water supplies to check for bacterial contamination and chemical pollutants.
• **Food Safety Inspection:** Checking grocery stores, dairies, and restaurants to ensure that food items are not adulterated or expired.
• **Mental Health Services:** Counseling centers and psychiatric departments in hospitals addressing mental well-being and stress management.
• **Mobile Health Vans:** Medical units that travel to remote, inaccessible villages or urban slums to provide doorstep healthcare.
• **Disposal of Bio-Medical Waste:** Scientific incineration and disposal protocols for hazardous waste generated by hospitals and clinics.
• **Awareness Campaigns:** Educational programs on personal hygiene, handwashing, menstrual hygiene, and nutritional balance.
• **First Aid Training:** Community workshops teaching basic life-saving techniques such as CPR, bandage wrapping, and handling shock.
• **Role of Anganwadis:** Childcare centers under the Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) providing nutrition and preschool education.
• **Blood Banks:** Specialized storage facilities that collect, test, and preserve blood for surgeries and trauma cases.
• **Combating Epidemics:** Coordinated rapid-response efforts by health authorities to contain outbreaks of diseases like dengue, malaria, and cholera.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Education and Literacy Services:** Community infrastructure and programs dedicated to empowering citizens through knowledge and skill development.
• **Right to Education (RTE) Act:** A landmark legislation ensuring free and compulsory education for all children aged 6 to 14 years in India.
• **Public vs. Private Schools:** Government-run schools offering subsidized education versus private institutions managed by trusts or societies.
• **Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA):** A national program aimed at universalizing elementary education and bridging social and gender gaps.
• **Mid-Day Meal Scheme:** A government initiative providing free hot lunches to school children to improve nutrition and boost attendance rates.
• **Public Libraries:** Community hubs that provide free access to books, newspapers, encyclopedias, and quiet study spaces for all age groups.
• **Adult Literacy Centers:** Evening classes designed to teach reading, writing, and basic arithmetic to adults who missed formal schooling.
• **Vocational Training Institutes:** Centers offering practical skill development in tailoring, computer operations, plumbing, and electrical repair.
• **Special Education Schools:** Specialized institutions catering to children with visual, auditory, or cognitive disabilities.
• **Science Centers and Museums:** Educational institutions that promote scientific curiosity and experiential learning among students.
• **Role of Teachers:** Educators act as pillars of the community by shaping young minds, instilling moral values, and promoting critical thinking.
• **Scholarship Programs:** Financial assistance provided by government and NGOs to meritorious and economically disadvantaged students.
• **Distance Education:** Open schooling and university boards that allow working adults and rural youth to continue their education.
• **Extracurricular Facilities:** Sports complexes, debate clubs, and art academies supported by the community for holistic student growth.
• **Digital Literacy Initiatives:** Programs teaching computer skills, internet safety, and digital payments to bridge the technological divide.

• **Disaster Management and Emergency Services:** Structured plans and specialized services to protect communities from natural and man-made disasters.
• **Natural Disasters:** Catastrophic events caused by natural forces, including earthquakes, floods, cyclones, droughts, and landslides.
• **Man-Made Disasters:** Emergencies caused by human negligence or error, such as major industrial accidents, train derailments, and urban fires.
• **Role of the Fire Department:** Quick deployment of fire engines, water tankers, and hydraulic ladders to extinguish fires and rescue victims.
• **Police and Law Enforcement:** Maintaining order, managing traffic during emergencies, securing disaster zones, and preventing looting.
• **Disaster Response Force (NDRF):** A specialized national force trained in rescue operations during collapsed structures, floods, and earthquakes.
• **First Responders:** Local community members trained in basic rescue and first aid who act immediately before professional help arrives.
• **Early Warning Systems:** Meteorological departments and weather satellites that predict storms, cyclones, and floods in advance.
• **Evacuation Drills:** Regular mock drills conducted in schools and offices to prepare citizens for safe evacuation during emergencies.
• **Relief Camps:** Temporary shelters set up with tents, blankets, drinking water, and community kitchens for displaced victims.
• **Medical Emergency Helplines:** Dedicated emergency numbers (such as 112, 100, 101, 108) for instant dispatch of police, fire, or ambulance services.
• **Post-Disaster Rehabilitation:** Long-term rebuilding of houses, restoration of power lines, psychological counseling, and livelihood support.
• **Community Preparedness:** Educating households on stocking emergency kits, keeping emergency documents safe, and knowing safe exit routes.
• **Animal Rescue during Floods:** Specialized teams deployed to rescue livestock and stray animals stranded in flooded regions.
• **Psychological First Aid:** Emotional support provided to survivors who have suffered trauma, loss of family members, or property destruction.

• **Environmental Protection and Community Action:** Collaborative efforts to preserve natural resources, reduce pollution, and ensure sustainable development.
• **Tree Plantation Drives:** Community-led initiatives to plant saplings along roadsides, in parks, and on school campuses to increase green cover.
• **Waste Segregation at Source:** Separating household waste into biodegradable (wet) and non-biodegradable (dry) categories for efficient recycling.
• **Ban on Single-Use Plastics:** Community awareness campaigns and local regulations discouraging the use of plastic carry bags and disposable cutlery.
• **Water Conservation Practices:** Rainwater harvesting installations, repairing leaking public pipes, and reviving traditional water bodies like stepwells (baolis).
• **Air Pollution Control:** Promoting public transport, carpooling, cycling, and checking vehicular emissions through Pollution Under Control (PUC) checks.
• **Noise Pollution Regulation:** Enforcing strict guidelines on loudspeaker usage during festivals and discouraging unnecessary honking in residential zones.
• **Composting Organic Waste:** Converting kitchen scraps and garden leaves into nutrient-rich compost for community gardens.
• **Wildlife and Bird Conservation:** Creating artificial water nests for birds in summer and protecting local wetlands and green belts.
• **Eco-Clubs in Schools:** Student-led groups that organize poster-making competitions, rallies, and cleanliness drives to promote environmental awareness.
• **Energy Conservation:** Encouraging the switch to LED lights, solar power, and turning off electrical appliances when not in use.
• **River Cleaning Campaigns:** Community drives to clear plastic and garbage from local rivers, lakes, and ponds.
• **The 3 R's Principle:** Adopting Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle in daily life to minimize waste generation and conserve raw materials.
• **Community Gardens:** Shared green spaces where residents grow vegetables, medicinal plants, and flowers collectively.
• **Sustainable Living:** Adopting lifestyles that meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

• **Role of Citizens in Community Development:** Active participation, cooperation, and social responsibility are the foundations of a progressive society.
• **Active Citizenship:** Going beyond voting in elections to participate in neighborhood meetings, voluntary work, and civic problem-solving.
• **Paying Taxes Honestly:** Prompt payment of income tax, property tax, and municipal dues enables governments to fund public infrastructure.
• **Following Civic Rules:** Adhering to traffic signals, zebra crossings, no-parking zones, and speed limits to ensure road safety for all.
• **Promoting Social Equality:** Treating fellow citizens with dignity, eliminating caste or gender discrimination, and supporting marginalized groups.
• **Respecting Diversity:** Celebrating multi-religious, multi-cultural festivals together to strengthen national integration and secular harmony.
• **Reporting Illegal Activities:** Cooperating with law enforcement by reporting illegal dumping, child labor, and suspicious movements.
• **Elderly Care within Neighborhoods:** Checking on elderly neighbors living alone, assisting them with groceries, and ensuring their safety.
• **Promoting Girl Child Education:** Encouraging families in the community to educate daughters and fighting against regressive social practices.
• **Blood and Organ Donation Pledges:** Voluntarily signing up for organ donation and regular blood donation to save human lives.
• **Maintaining Neighborhood Hygiene:** Ensuring that one's garbage does not spill onto streets and participating in community clean-up drives.
• **Constructive Dialogue:** Resolving neighborhood disputes through peaceful discussions and mediation rather than hostility.
• **Supporting Local Artisans:** Buying pottery, handloom, and local crafts to sustain traditional livelihoods within the community.
• **Inculcating Values in Children:** Parents and teachers instilling empathy, honesty, and a spirit of service in the younger generation.
• **Vision of a Welfare State:** Working collectively toward a society where every individual enjoys peace, prosperity, security, and equal opportunity.
