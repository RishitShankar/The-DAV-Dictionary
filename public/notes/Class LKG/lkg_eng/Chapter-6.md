• **Chapter Title:** Chapter 6: Action Words and Daily Vocabulary is a foundational unit in the DAV Class LKG "Fun with English" curriculum designed to introduce young learners to verbs and everyday operational language.
• **Core Learning Objective:** The primary pedagogical goal is to help students identify, comprehend, and visually associate physical actions with corresponding English action words (verbs) used in daily routines.
• **Target Age Group Context:** Tailored for LKG students (approx. 3.5 to 4.5 years old), the methodology relies heavily on multisensory learning, linking spoken words directly to tangible classroom and home movements.
• **Definition of Action Words:** Action words, formally known as verbs, are introduced to LKG students simply as "doing words" that describe what people, animals, or objects are doing at any given moment.
• **Role of Daily Vocabulary:** Daily vocabulary encompasses essential nouns, basic adjectives, and polite expressions that children encounter in their immediate environments, bridging home language and school English.
• **Visual Association Principle:** The DAV workbook pairs each action word with large, colorful, and engaging illustrations to ensure pre-readers can grasp the semantic meaning of the verb without relying on text decoding.
• **Kinesthetic Integration:** Teachers and parents are guided to use physical gestures (Total Physical Response) alongside these notes to help students embody the action words kinesthetically.
• **Foundation for Sentence Building:** Mastery of Chapter 6 concepts acts as the crucial stepping stone toward future sentence formation, allowing children to eventually combine a naming word (noun) with an action word (verb).
• **Inclusive Scope:** The vocabulary selected covers high-frequency verbs that are universally experienced by children globally, regardless of their regional or linguistic backgrounds.
• **Observational Skills Enhancement:** Students are encouraged to observe their surroundings—such as classmates playing, teachers writing, or birds flying—and mentally map them to the vocabulary learned in this chapter.
• **Sequential Progression:** The chapter systematically progresses from single-word body movements to complex daily routines, ensuring a gentle, cognitive load-balanced learning curve.
• **Phonetic Readiness:** While primarily focused on semantics, the words introduced in this chapter also serve as early sight words and phonetic blending targets for upcoming phonics units.
• **Classroom Management Utility:** Many of the action words taught (e.g., sit, stand, listen, look) double as essential classroom management commands, reinforcing discipline through English acquisition.
• **Parental Engagement Framework:** The vocabulary lists are curated to be easily practiced at home during routine activities like eating, bathing, playing, and sleeping.
• **Holistic Development:** Beyond linguistic skills, this chapter supports motor skill development by connecting the cognitive understanding of a word to the execution of the physical movement.

• **Core Concept - Movement Verbs:** The first major sub-section of Chapter 6 focuses on gross motor movement verbs that describe how children move their bodies through space.
• **Term: 'Run' (•):** Defined as moving at a speed faster than a walk, never having both or all feet on the ground at the same time; illustrated in the workbook by a child sprinting on a playground.
• **Term: 'Walk' (•):** Defined as moving at a regular pace by lifting and setting down each foot in turn, never having both feet off the ground simultaneously.
• **Term: 'Jump' (•):** Defined as pushing oneself off a surface into the air using the muscles in legs and feet; a high-energy action word kids associate with joy and play.
• **Term: 'Hop' (•):** Defined as moving by jumping on one foot; distinguished from 'jump' to teach LKG students precision in bodily control and vocabulary distinction.
• **Term: 'Skip' (•):** Defined as a movement combining a step and a hop with a light spring on alternating feet, introducing rhythmic movement vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Sit' (•):** Defined as resting the body weight on the buttocks rather than the feet, with the torso upright; a fundamental classroom posture command.
• **Term: 'Stand' (•):** Defined as having or maintaining an upright position supported by one's feet; the direct physical and semantic opposite of 'sit'.
• **Term: 'Fall' (•):** Defined as moving downward, typically rapidly and freely without control, introduced cautiously through safe workbook illustrations of soft toy drops.
• **Term: 'Climb' (•):** Defined as mounting or scaling something, typically using both hands and feet, such as climbing jungle gyms or stairs.
• **Pedagogical Significance of Gross Motor Verbs:** These verbs allow children to express their physical states and intentions clearly to teachers and peers.
• **Pronunciation Focus (•):** Teachers emphasize the clear articulation of initial consonant blends in words like 'skip' and 'stand' to blend phonics with vocabulary acquisition.
• **Action-Reaction Pairing:** Workbook exercises frequently pair 'sit' and 'stand' together to teach antonymous relationships through physical enactment.
• **Classroom Integration (•):** Teachers use 'stand up' and 'sit down' as continuous transition cues throughout the school day, embedding the vocabulary into muscle memory.
• **Visual Representation Strategy (•):** Each gross motor verb is depicted with directional arrows in the workbook to indicate the trajectory or nature of the movement.

• **Core Concept - Hands and Arms Actions:** The second major sub-section shifts focus to manipulative verbs performed primarily using hands, fingers, and arms.
• **Term: 'Clap' (•):** Defined as striking the palms of the hands together repeatedly to express applause, joy, or rhythm; a common participatory classroom action.
• **Term: 'Catch' (•):** Defined as intercepting and holding something that has been thrown, propelled, or dropped; develops hand-eye coordination vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Throw' (•):** Defined as propelling something violently through the air using a motion of the hand and arm; the conceptual counterpart to 'catch'.
• **Term: 'Push' (•):** Defined as exerting force on an object in order to move it away from one's body; illustrated using doors, toy carts, or blocks.
• **Term: 'Pull' (•):** Defined as exerting force to cause something to move toward or follow the source of the force; taught alongside 'push' to contrast directional vectors.
• **Term: 'Hold' (•):** Defined as carrying, supporting, or keeping something in one's grasp; foundational for discussing handling objects like pencils, books, and toys.
• **Term: 'Write' (•):** Defined as marking letters, words, or symbols on a surface with a pen, pencil, or crayon; a core academic action word for LKG students.
• **Term: 'Draw' (•):** Defined as producing a picture or diagram by making lines on a surface with a pencil, pen, or chalk; encourages creative expression vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Open' (•):** Defined as moving a door, window, or container so as to allow access into or out of an interior; applied to school bags, lunch boxes, and books.
• **Term: 'Close' (•):** Defined as moving a door, lid, or barrier to shut an opening; taught as the direct functional inverse of 'open'.
• **Fine Motor Skill Connection:** Hand and arm action words directly correlate with the fine motor skill development mandated in the DAV preschool framework.
• **Bilateral Coordination:** Words like 'clap' and 'hold' require bilateral physical coordination, which is mirrored in the linguistic processing of these verbs.
• **Object Interaction:** Unlike gross motor verbs that move the whole body, hand/arm verbs emphasize subject-object interaction and manipulation.
• **Classroom Routines (•):** Instructions such as "open your book" and "close your pencil box" rely entirely on this subset of daily action vocabulary.
• **Visual Cueing (•):** Hand action icons are standardized in DAV workbooks to cue students when a writing or drawing task is required in subsequent chapters.

• **Core Concept - Facial and Sensory Actions:** The third major sub-section explores verbs related to the five senses, facial expressions, and oral communication.
• **Term: 'Look' (•):** Defined as directing one's gaze in a specified direction to perceive visual information; the primary command word used by teachers to direct attention.
• **Term: 'See' (•):** Defined as discerning or perceiving with the eyes; distinguished subtly from 'look' to teach intentional observation versus passive sight.
• **Term: 'Listen' (•):** Defined as giving one's attention to a sound, reinforcing auditory focus essential for phonics and verbal instruction in class.
• **Term: 'Hear' (•):** Defined as perceiving sound with the ear; paired with 'listen' to help children understand active versus passive auditory experiences.
• **Term: 'Speak' (•):** Defined as uttering words or communicating vocally; encourages verbal participation in the LKG interactive classroom environment.
• **Term: 'Talk' (•):** Defined as engaging in conversational speech with one or more people; builds social-emotional vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Eat' (•):** Defined as putting food into the mouth, chewing it, and swallowing it; an essential daily routine verb practiced during lunch break.
• **Term: 'Drink' (•):** Defined as taking liquid into the mouth and swallowing it; taught alongside 'eat' under the umbrella of nourishment vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Smile' (•):** Defined as forming one's features into a pleased or kind expression by curving the lips upward; introduces positive emotional vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Laugh' (•):** Defined as making spontaneous sounds and movements of the face and body expressive of lively amusement.
• **Sensory Integration Focus:** These verbs help children articulate their internal sensory experiences and communicate their needs effectively to adults.
• **Phonetic Awareness in Sensory Words (•):** Words like 'look' and 'listen' feature prominent initial consonant sounds that link directly to phonics blending exercises.
• **Social-Emotional Learning (SEL):** Facial action words ('smile', 'laugh') promote emotional literacy, helping students recognize and express feelings safely.
• **Daily Routine Mapping:** Eating and drinking verbs structure the mid-day school schedule, giving English labels to biological necessities.
• **Non-Verbal Communication:** Workbook images emphasize facial cues, ensuring children understand that actions extend beyond limbs to facial expressions.

• **Core Concept - Daily Hygiene and Self-Care Vocabulary:** The fourth major sub-section introduces verbs and nouns associated with personal hygiene, grooming, and morning preparation routines.
• **Term: 'Wash' (•):** Defined as cleaning the body, hands, or objects using water and soap; emphasized before meals and after restroom use.
• **Term: 'Brush' (•):** Defined as cleaning or smoothing something with a brush; specifically applied in the workbook to teeth and hair grooming routines.
• **Term: 'Bath' (or 'Bathe') (•):** Defined as washing the body by immersion in water or under a shower; a fundamental evening or morning routine word.
• **Term: 'Comb' (•):** Defined as untangling and arranging hair with a handheld toothed implement; teaches personal grooming independence.
• **Term: 'Wear' (•):** Defined as having clothing, shoes, or accessories on one's body; used when discussing getting dressed for school.
• **Term: 'Sleep' (•):** Defined as resting in a state of natural suspension of consciousness; represents the end-of-day routine in the workbook's timeline charts.
• **Term: 'Wake' (or 'Wake up') (•):** Defined as emerging from sleep; the starting point of the child's daily chronological narrative in the workbook.
• **Term: 'Clean' (•):** Defined as free from dirt, marks, or stains; taught both as an action verb and a descriptive daily vocabulary word.
• **Term: 'Tidy' (•):** Defined as arranging things neatly; used in contexts like "tidy your toys" or "tidy your desk" to build organizational habits.
• **Chronological Sequencing:** Self-care vocabulary is taught in the natural chronological order of a day: wake up, brush, bathe, wear clothes, eat, sleep.
• **Independence Building (•):** DAV curriculum emphasizes that mastering self-care words empowers young children to take ownership of their daily hygiene habits.
• **Vocabulary Expansion (•):** Nouns associated with hygiene (soap, toothbrush, comb, towel) are introduced alongside the verbs to build comprehensive semantic fields.
• **Health and Sanitation Education:** These terms bridge English language learning with basic health education, reinforcing cleanliness standards.
• **Parental Reinforcement:** Parents are encouraged in workbook notes to use these exact English verbs during home routines to ensure consistent reinforcement.
• **Visual Timelines (•):** Workbook pages utilize sequential comic-strip style panels to show a character performing these daily self-care actions in order.

• **Core Concept - Home and Environment Vocabulary:** The fifth major sub-section expands beyond direct body actions to include essential nouns and action verbs linked to domestic life.
• **Term: 'House' / 'Home' (•):** Defined as a building for human habitation; introduced as the central setting where many daily actions take place.
• **Term: 'Room' (•):** Defined as a part of a building enclosed by walls, floor, and ceiling; leads into sub-vocabulary for bedroom, kitchen, and bathroom.
• **Term: 'Bed' (•):** Defined as a piece of furniture for sleeping or resting on, directly linked to the verb 'sleep' and 'wake'.
• **Term: 'Table' and 'Chair' (•):** Furniture items linked to 'sit', 'eat', 'write', and 'draw', teaching the functional relationship between objects and action words.
• **Term: 'Door' and 'Window' (•):** Architectural features paired with the verbs 'open' and 'close' to teach spatial mechanics in the home.
• **Term: 'Toy' (•):** An object for a child to play with; integrated with action words like 'play', 'share', 'hold', and 'throw'.
• **Term: 'Book' (•):** A written or printed work consisting of pages glued together; linked with 'read', 'look', 'open', and 'hold'.
• **Term: 'Water' (•):** A transparent, odorless, tasteless liquid essential for life; paired with 'drink', 'wash', and 'bath'.
• **Term: 'Food' (•):** Any nutritious substance that people eat or drink; paired with 'eat', 'cook', and 'share'.
• **Contextualized Learning:** Home vocabulary grounds abstract English words in the concrete reality of the child's domestic environment.
• **Object-Verb Mapping:** LKG students learn that every object has a specific set of actions associated with it (e.g., books are for reading, water is for drinking).
• **Spatial Awareness:** Words like in, on, under, and around are introduced gently through the placement of home objects relative to one another.
• **Safety Vocabulary (•):** Introduction of home items includes cautionary concepts (hot, sharp) to ensure vocabulary acquisition supports child safety.
• **Categorization Skills:** Students learn to group home vocabulary into categories such as furniture, toys, food items, and clothing.

• **Core Concept - School and Classroom Vocabulary:** The sixth major sub-section focuses on nouns and action verbs specific to the DAV school environment and early educational settings.
• **Term: 'School' (•):** Defined as an institution for educating children; the primary community center introduced in the LKG social environment curriculum.
• **Term: 'Teacher' (•):** Defined as a person who helps students acquire knowledge, competence, or virtue; central figure in classroom action commands.
• **Term: 'Classmate' / 'Friend' (•):** Defined as a peer sharing the same classroom environment; introduces social interaction and cooperative vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Bag' (or 'School Bag') (•):** A container used for carrying books and stationery; associated with 'pack', 'open', 'close', and 'carry'.
• **Term: 'Pencil' (•):** An instrument for writing or drawing, typically consisting of a thin graphite core embedded in wood; linked with 'write' and 'draw'.
• **Term: 'Eraser' (•):** A piece of rubber used for rubbing out marks made by a pencil; introduces corrective action vocabulary.
• **Term: 'Crayon' (•):** A pencil or stick of colored wax used for drawing; tied directly to artistic activities and color identification.
• **Term: 'Board' (or 'Blackboard' / 'Whiteboard') (•):** A large vertical surface for writing or drawing on in a classroom; paired with 'look', 'see', and 'read'.
• **Term: 'Playground' (•):** An outdoor area provided for children to play on; associated with gross motor verbs like 'run', 'slide', 'swing', and 'play'.
• **Term: 'Share' (•):** Defined as using or enjoying something jointly with others; a vital social-emotional vocabulary word emphasized in DAV schools.
• **Institutional Socialization:** School vocabulary helps children transition smoothly from home-centric thinking to community-centric social awareness.
• **Instructional Literacy (•):** Familiarity with school terms ensures students understand multi-step instructions given by teachers during lessons.
• **Stationery Accountability:** Children learn to identify and care for their personal stationery items through dedicated workbook matching exercises.
• **Cooperative Play Integration:** The verb 'share' is highlighted on the playground to encourage empathy, turn-taking, and conflict resolution among peers.
• **Environmental Print:** DAV classrooms utilize labeling on objects (door, window, board) to reinforce this vocabulary through constant visual exposure.

• **Core Concept - Polite Expressions and Daily Courtesies:** The seventh major sub-section covers essential social phrases and vocabulary that form the backbone of moral and character education in DAV institutions.
• **Term: 'Please' (•):** An polite exclamation used when making a request; taught as a mandatory linguistic modifier for all classroom and home demands.
• **Term: 'Thank you' (•):** A polite expression used to express gratitude or appreciation for a gift, help, or service rendered.
• **Term: 'Sorry' (•):** An expression of apology used when one has made a mistake, bumped into someone, or accidentally caused disruption.
• **Term: 'Excuse me' (•):** A polite phrase used to attract attention, pass by someone, or request clearance to speak.
• **Term: 'Hello' / 'Good Morning' (•):** Greeting expressions used upon arrival at school or meeting teachers and peers at the start of the day.
• **Term: 'Goodbye' (•):** A parting formula used when leaving school or bidding farewell to parents and teachers.
• **Term: 'Welcome' (•):** A polite response to being thanked, or an expression of greeting extended to visitors entering the classroom.
• **Moral Foundations (•):** DAV curriculum integrates Vedic values into early English learning, ensuring language acquisition goes hand-in-hand with good manners.
• **Role-Playing Methodology (•):** Workbook exercises utilize cartoon dialogues and role-play scenarios to demonstrate when and how to use polite expressions.
• **Emotional Regulation:** Words like 'sorry' and 'please' help de-escalate minor conflicts among 4-year-olds in the classroom environment.
• **Habit Formation (•):** Consistent teacher modeling ensures these polite expressions become automatic conversational habits for LKG students.
• **Contextual Appropriateness:** Students learn that 'Good Morning' is used early in the day, while 'Goodbye' is reserved for departure times.
• **Inclusivity and Respect:** Courtesies establish an atmosphere of mutual respect, creating a secure psychological space for language learning.
• **Home-School Continuity:** Parents are provided with courtesy checklists to ensure children use 'please' and 'thank you' during family meals and interactions.
• **Reinforcement Stories (•):** Short narrative vignettes in the workbook illustrate the positive social outcomes of using polite words versus ignoring them.

• **Core Concept - Nature and Outdoor Vocabulary:** The eighth major sub-section introduces basic nouns and action verbs related to the natural world, weather, animals, and plants.
• **Term: 'Sun' (•):** The star around which the earth orbits, providing light and warmth; associated with daytime vocabulary and bright colors.
• **Term: 'Tree' (•):** A woody perennial plant having a single stem or trunk; serves as a habitat reference in early science-English integration.
• **Term: 'Flower' (•):** The seed-bearing structure in angiosperms; associated with colors, sweet smells, and visual beauty.
• **Term: 'Bird' (•):** A warm-blooded egg-laying vertebrate characterized by feathers and wings; paired with the action verb 'fly'.
• **Term: 'Fly' (•):** Defined as moving through the air using wings; a specialized action word distinct from walking or running.
• **Term: 'Swim' (•):** Defined as propelling the body through water using limbs or fins; illustrated with aquatic animals or children in pools.
• **Term: 'Rain' (•):** Moisture condensed from the atmosphere that falls in separate drops; associated with weather vocabulary and umbrellas.
• **Term: 'Play' (•):** Engaging in activity for enjoyment and recreation rather than a serious or practical purpose; a universal child verb.
• **Term: 'Grass' (•):** Vegetation consisting of short plants with narrow leaves, covering lawns and fields where children run and sit.
• **Term: 'Animal' (•):** A living organism that feeds on organic matter; umbrella category for basic fauna introduced in LKG (dog, cat, cow, bird).
• **Interdisciplinary Learning:** Nature vocabulary bridges English with Environmental Studies (EVS), reinforcing scientific concepts through linguistic tags.
• **Animal Action Matching:** Workbook activities require students to match animals to their specific movement verbs (birds fly, fish swim, dogs run, rabbits hop).
• **Sensory Engagement (•):** Nature terms evoke sensory descriptions—bright sun, soft grass, sweet flowers—expanding the child's descriptive lexicon.
• **Outdoor Observation Walks:** Teachers lead nature walks around the school campus, prompting students to point and name trees, birds, and grass in English.
• **Weather Awareness:** Simple weather descriptors (sunny, rainy) are introduced to help children describe daily atmospheric conditions during morning circle time.

• **Core Concept - Review, Consolidation, and Cross-Matching:** The ninth major sub-section consolidates all preceding categories through integrative review pages, matching exercises, and pictorial sorting tasks.
• **Synthesizing Actions and Nouns:** Review pages require students to mentally connect a noun (e.g., 'dog') with its corresponding action (e.g., 'run' or 'bark').
• **Categorization Drills (•):** Students practice sorting vocabulary into distinct conceptual buckets: action words (verbs) versus naming words (nouns).
• **Visual Discrimination Practice:** Workbooks feature busy scene illustrations where students must locate specific items (e.g., find the book, find the door) and state their functions.
• **Memory Retention Strategies:** Repetitive exposure to core verbs ('run', 'jump', 'eat', 'sleep') across multiple chapters ensures long-term cognitive retention.
• **Peer-to-Peer Quizzing:** Interactive classroom activities involve students taking turns acting out a verb while peers guess the correct action word in English.
• **Flashcard Integration:** Teachers utilize flashcards with the action word on one side and the picture on the other for rapid-fire vocabulary recall drills.
• **Error Analysis (•):** Common confusion points—such as mixing up 'push' and 'pull' or 'sit' and 'stand'—are explicitly addressed through corrective demonstration.
• **Sentence Stem Introduction (•):** Advanced consolidation exercises introduce simple two-word sentence stems like "Boy runs" or "Girl eats" to preview future grammar.
• **Active Listening Checks:** Teachers read descriptive short stories and ask students to perform a specific physical action whenever they hear a targeted action word.
• **Comprehensive Vocabulary Audit:** The review section serves as an internal checkpoint for educators to assess whether every student has mastered the core word list.
• **Confidence Building:** Success in matching and identifying vocabulary boosts self-esteem, encouraging hesitant speakers to participate verbally.
• **Holistic Application:** Students apply their cumulative vocabulary to describe what is happening in complex workbook story panels without adult prompting.
• **Kinesthetic Memory Lock:** Pairing physical movement with final review tasks locks the vocabulary into the child's procedural memory.
• **Preparation for Summative Assessment:** These consolidation pages directly prepare students for chapter-end evaluations and oral viva examinations.

• **Encouraging Exploratory Speech:** Children are praised when they spontaneously use action words to describe their play or express their physical needs.
• **Multilingual Bridging:** Recognizing regional mother tongues, teachers permit contextual translation while gently guiding students back to the English target vocabulary.
• **Continuous Vocabulary Growth:** Chapter 6 establishes a permanent mental framework that will be expanded in Class UKG with tense variations, adverbs, and adjectives.
• **Behavioral Reinforcement:** Using classroom action commands consistently helps maintain a calm, orderly, and engaged learning environment.
• **Inclusivity in Pacing:** Teachers utilize differentiated instruction, providing extra tactile and visual support for students needing additional time with motor verbs.
• **Digital and Media Supplementry Support:** DAV recommended audio-visual rhymes and action songs reinforce chapter vocabulary through catchy melodies and rhythm.
• **Physical-Cognitive Synergy:** The core philosophy of Chapter 6 remains the inseparable link between physical movement and cognitive linguistic acquisition in early childhood.
• **Formative Assessment Philosophy:** Assessment in LKG remains entirely stress-free, relying on observation of participation, enthusiasm, and correct physical response.
• **Empowerment Through Language:** By mastering daily vocabulary, young learners gain the linguistic tools necessary to navigate their immediate world with confidence and independence.
• **Foundation of Literacy:** Comprehensive understanding of action words and daily nouns sets the bedrock upon which all future reading, writing, and grammar skills are built.
