• **MS Paint Introduction**: This is the primary software used in Class 2 for digital drawing and creative expression.
• **The Paint Window**: The interface of MS Paint consists of the Ribbon, the Drawing Area, and the Quick Access Toolbar.
• **Ribbon**: The Ribbon contains various tabs and groups that hold all the tools needed to create a drawing.
• **Drawing Area**: This is the large white space in the center of the screen where the actual drawing takes place.
• **Tools Group**: This section contains essential instruments like the Pencil, Eraser, Fill with Color, and Text tool.
• **Pencil Tool**: Used for freehand drawing, similar to how one uses a real pencil on paper.
• **Eraser Tool**: Used to remove mistakes or unwanted parts of a drawing from the canvas.
• **Fill with Color Tool**: Represented by a paint bucket icon, it is used to fill closed shapes with a selected color instantly.
• **Shapes Group**: This contains pre-defined shapes like lines, rectangles, ovals, and triangles that can be drawn with a single click and drag.
• **Color Palette**: Located at the top of the MS Paint window, it allows the user to choose from a wide variety of colors for their artwork.
• **Size Selector**: This feature allows the user to change the thickness of the lines drawn by the Pencil or the size of the Eraser.
• **Mouse Actions**: Mastering the 'Click and Drag' action is essential for drawing shapes and using tools effectively.
• **Undo Feature**: A very important tool that allows the user to reverse the last action performed if a mistake is made.
• **Redo Feature**: Allows the user to bring back an action that was previously undone.
• **Saving Work**: Learning to save digital art is a crucial step to ensure that creative work is not lost after closing the program.

• **Mouse Control**: The mouse is the primary input device for "Fun with Computers," acting as the user's hand on the screen.
• **Pointer**: The small arrow on the screen that moves as you move the mouse is called the Mouse Pointer.
• **Clicking**: A single click is used to select an icon or a tool in the software.
• **Double-Clicking**: Used to open programs or files by pressing the left mouse button twice in quick succession.
• **Right-Clicking**: Used to open a shortcut menu that provides additional options for the selected item.
• **Dragging**: This involves holding down the left mouse button and moving the mouse to draw shapes or move objects across the screen.
• **Mouse Pad**: A flat surface that provides a smooth area for the mouse to glide, ensuring accurate pointer movement.
• **Ergonomics**: Holding the mouse correctly with the index finger on the left button and the middle finger on the right button is vital for comfort.
• **Precision**: Using the mouse for drawing helps children develop the precision required for digital design.
• **Cursor**: In text-based programs, the blinking line that shows where the next character will appear is known as the cursor.
• **Icon Selection**: Clicking on icons allows the computer to understand which program or tool the user wants to activate.
• **Menu Navigation**: Using the mouse to click on menu items allows the user to access advanced features of the software.
• **Scroll Wheel**: The wheel located between the two buttons helps in moving up and down through long pages or documents.
• **Clicking Sound**: The distinct sound made by the mouse buttons provides tactile feedback that the action has been registered.
• **Care for Mouse**: Keeping the mouse clean and using it on a flat surface prevents damage to the internal sensor.

• **Digital Art Benefits**: Creating art on a computer is eco-friendly as it saves paper and physical art supplies.
• **Color Mixing**: Computers allow users to experiment with millions of colors that might not be available in a standard set of crayons.
• **Editing**: Unlike paper drawings, digital drawings can be edited, resized, and modified without starting from scratch.
• **Shapes Tool**: Using the Shapes tool ensures that geometric figures like circles and squares are perfect every time.
• **Line Tool**: The Line tool helps in creating straight lines, which are difficult to draw perfectly by hand.
• **Curve Tool**: This advanced tool allows for the creation of smooth, artistic curves for drawing landscapes or organic shapes.
• **Text Tool**: Allows the user to type names or messages directly onto their drawing, combining art with literacy.
• **Brushes Tool**: Provides different textures like calligraphy pens, oil brushes, and spray cans to add depth to the artwork.
• **Zoom Tool**: A magnifying glass icon that allows the user to look closely at specific parts of the drawing for detailed work.
• **Magnifier**: Essential for pixel-level editing, ensuring that every corner of the drawing is colored correctly.
• **Color Picker**: A tool that allows the user to pick a color from an existing part of the drawing to use elsewhere.
• **Canvas Resizing**: Users can change the size of their drawing area to fit larger or smaller projects.
• **Artistic Freedom**: The computer removes the fear of making mistakes, as everything can be corrected instantly.
• **Digital Gallery**: Finished drawings can be saved as image files and shared with friends and family digitally.
• **Creativity Boost**: The ease of use encourages children to try new styles and color combinations they might otherwise avoid.

• **Listening to Music**: Computers act as media players, allowing users to organize and play their favorite songs and rhymes.
• **Media Player**: Software designed to play audio and video files on the computer.
• **Volume Control**: A feature that allows the user to adjust how loud or soft the sound output is.
• **Play/Pause**: The basic controls for managing the playback of audio and video content.
• **Stop Button**: Used to end the playback of a media file completely.
• **Next/Previous**: Buttons used to skip to the next track or go back to the previous one in a playlist.
• **Playlist**: A list of songs or videos arranged in a specific order for continuous playback.
• **Educational Audio**: Listening to stories or poems helps in improving pronunciation and listening skills.
• **Headphones**: Devices used to listen to audio privately without disturbing others in the room.
• **Speakers**: External devices that amplify the sound from the computer so that many people can listen together.
• **Digital Library**: Computers allow for the storage of a vast collection of music and videos in a small space.
• **Visualizers**: Animated patterns that move to the rhythm of the music, adding a visual element to audio playback.
• **File Formats**: Common audio formats include MP3, while video formats include MP4, which the computer can easily process.
• **Streaming**: The ability to watch or listen to content directly from the internet without needing to download it.
• **Entertainment Balance**: While music is fun, it should be enjoyed at a moderate volume to protect hearing.

• **Typing Skills**: Learning to type on a computer is a foundational skill that prepares students for future academic and professional tasks.
• **Keyboard Layout**: The standard keyboard layout is known as QWERTY, named after the first six letters on the top row.
• **Alphabet Keys**: These are the keys from A to Z, used for typing letters and words.
• **Number Keys**: Located above the alphabet keys and on the numeric keypad, used for typing numbers.
• **Spacebar**: The longest key on the keyboard, used to insert a space between words.
• **Enter Key**: Used to move the cursor to the next line or to confirm a command.
• **Backspace Key**: Used to delete the character to the left of the cursor, helping to fix typing errors.
• **Caps Lock**: A key that allows the user to type in capital letters when activated.
• **Shift Key**: Used in combination with other keys to type symbols or capital letters temporarily.
• **Arrow Keys**: Used to move the cursor up, down, left, or right on the screen.
• **Typing Games**: Software designed to make learning the position of keys fun and engaging through interactive challenges.
• **Finger Placement**: Learning to use the correct fingers for specific keys improves typing speed and accuracy.
• **Posture**: Sitting straight while typing is important for maintaining good health and preventing strain.
• **Accuracy vs. Speed**: In the beginning, it is more important to focus on typing correctly than typing fast.
• **Practice**: Regular, short practice sessions are more effective than long, infrequent ones for mastering the keyboard.

• **Computer Etiquette**: Using a computer requires following certain rules to ensure safety and respect for the machine.
• **Cleanliness**: Keep the computer area clean and avoid eating or drinking near the keyboard to prevent spills.
• **Gentle Handling**: Press keys and click mouse buttons gently to avoid damaging the hardware.
• **Posture**: Sit at a comfortable distance from the monitor to avoid eye strain and maintain good posture.
• **Screen Time**: Take regular breaks from the screen to rest your eyes and stretch your body.
• **Sharing**: If using a shared computer, be mindful of others and leave the workspace tidy for the next person.
• **Asking for Help**: Always ask a teacher or parent before opening new programs or changing settings.
• **Internet Safety**: Never share personal information like your name, address, or school name on the internet.
• **Password Security**: Keep your passwords private and do not share them with anyone except your parents.
• **Reporting Issues**: If something goes wrong or if you see something strange on the screen, tell an adult immediately.
• **Turning Off**: Always follow the correct procedure to shut down the computer to prevent data loss or system errors.
• **Respecting Work**: Do not delete or change files that belong to others on a shared computer.
• **Focus**: Use the computer for the intended task, whether it is learning, drawing, or playing an educational game.
• **Environment**: Keep the computer room well-lit and ventilated for a comfortable working environment.
• **Responsibility**: Taking care of the computer shows that you are a responsible and smart user.
