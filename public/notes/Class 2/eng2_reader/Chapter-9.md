• **Chapter Overview:** "The Rainbow" is a lyrical poem that explores the beauty of nature, specifically focusing on the bridge-like structure formed by clouds and rivers.
• **Core Theme:** The poem emphasizes the natural connection between the earth and the sky through the imagery of a rainbow.
• **Poetic Device:** The author uses **Personification** and **Metaphor** to describe natural elements as if they were living beings or man-made structures.
• **Key Imagery:** The poem invites the reader to visualize the sky, the clouds, the rivers, and the colorful arc of the rainbow.
• **Vocabulary Focus:** **Bridges** are defined as structures built over rivers to help people cross from one side to the other.
• **Nature’s Architecture:** The poem contrasts man-made bridges with the natural "bridge" created by the rainbow.
• **The Sky’s Role:** The sky is depicted as a vast canvas where the rainbow makes its appearance.
• **The River’s Role:** Rivers are described as flowing bodies of water that require bridges for traversal.
• **The Rainbow’s Path:** The rainbow is described as a bridge that connects the earth to the heavens.
• **Visual Appeal:** The rainbow is noted for its vibrant colors, which stand out against the backdrop of the sky.
• **Atmospheric Conditions:** Rainbows typically appear after rainfall, which is a key scientific fact relevant to the poem's context.
• **The Concept of 'Over':** The poem uses the preposition 'over' to describe the position of the rainbow relative to the rivers and trees.
• **The Concept of 'Across':** The poem highlights the distance covered by the rainbow as it spans across the horizon.
• **Nature’s Beauty:** The poem encourages children to appreciate the aesthetic value of the natural world.
• **Poetic Rhythm:** The poem follows a simple, melodic structure suitable for Class 2 learners to recite and memorize.

• **The Appearance of the Rainbow:** It always appears in the part of the sky directly opposite the sun.
• **The Shape of the Rainbow:** It is actually a full circle, but from the ground, we only see an arc or a bow.
• **The Temporary Nature:** Rainbows are fleeting; they disappear as the clouds move or the sun sets.
• **The Symbolism of the Rainbow:** Often seen as a symbol of hope, peace, and beauty in various cultures.
• **The Connection to the Poem:** The poem captures this fleeting, beautiful moment in a permanent literary form.

• **The Contrast with 'Boats':** Boats are heavy and move on water; the rainbow is light and moves through the air.
• **The Contrast with 'Ships':** Ships are large and carry cargo; the rainbow carries only color and light.
• **The Contrast with 'Clouds':** Clouds are white or grey; the rainbow is colorful and vibrant.
