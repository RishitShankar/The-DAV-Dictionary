• **Addition**: The mathematical process of combining two or more numbers or quantities to find their total or sum.
• **Sum**: The final result obtained after performing the operation of addition.
• **Plus Sign (+)**: The mathematical symbol used to represent the operation of addition between two numbers.
• **Equals Sign (=)**: The symbol used to denote that the values on both sides of the equation are equivalent.
• **Addends**: The individual numbers that are being added together to form a sum.
• **Counting Forward**: A fundamental mental strategy for addition where one starts at the larger number and counts forward by the value of the smaller number.
• **Number Line**: A visual representation of numbers arranged in order on a straight line, used to demonstrate addition by jumping forward.
• **Single-Digit Addition**: The process of adding two numbers, each consisting of only one digit (0-9).
• **Basic Addition Facts**: Memorized combinations of single-digit numbers that form the foundation for all complex addition.
• **Order Property of Addition**: A rule stating that changing the order of the addends does not change the sum (e.g., 3 + 4 = 7 and 4 + 3 = 7).
• **Commutative Property**: The formal mathematical name for the Order Property of Addition.
• **Zero Property of Addition**: A rule stating that when zero is added to any number, the sum is the number itself (e.g., 5 + 0 = 5).
• **Identity Property**: Another name for the Zero Property, as zero is the additive identity.
• **One Property of Addition**: A rule stating that adding one to any number results in the next consecutive number (the successor).
• **Successor**: The number that comes immediately after a given number in the counting sequence.

• **Two-Digit Addition**: The process of adding numbers that consist of both tens and ones places.
• **Place Value**: The value assigned to a digit based on its position in a number (Ones place and Tens place).
• **Column Addition**: A method of arranging numbers vertically, aligning the ones digits under the ones column and tens digits under the tens column.
• **Alignment**: The critical practice of keeping digits in their correct columns to ensure accurate calculation.
• **Right-to-Left Rule**: The standard procedure in addition where calculation always begins at the ones column before moving to the tens column.
• **Adding Ones**: The first step in two-digit addition, where the digits in the ones place are combined first.
• **Adding Tens**: The second step in two-digit addition, where the digits in the tens place are combined after the ones are summed.
• **Summing Tens**: When adding tens, the result represents groups of ten, which are placed in the tens column.
• **No-Regrouping Addition**: A scenario where the sum of the digits in the ones place is less than 10, requiring no carry-over.
• **Mental Math**: The ability to perform addition calculations in one's head without the use of paper or physical counters.
• **Decomposition**: Breaking a number into its tens and ones parts to make addition easier (e.g., 24 = 20 + 4).
• **Grouping**: Combining numbers into sets of ten to simplify the addition process.
• **Base-10 Blocks**: Physical or visual tools representing units (ones) and rods (tens) used to model addition.
• **Expanded Form**: Writing a number as the sum of its place values (e.g., 35 = 30 + 5) to facilitate addition.
• **Horizontal Addition**: Writing an addition problem in a single line (e.g., 12 + 13 = 25) rather than vertically.
---PAGE-BREAK---
• **Regrouping**: The process of carrying over a value from one place value column to the next when the sum exceeds 9.
• **Carrying Over**: A common term for regrouping, where a '1' is moved from the ones column to the tens column.
• **Sum Greater Than Nine**: The condition that triggers the need for regrouping in the ones column.
• **The 'Ten' Rule**: When the sum of the ones column is 10 or more, the '1' represents a new ten that must be added to the tens column.
• **Carrying Digit**: The small number written above the tens column to remind the student to include it in the final tens sum.
• **Step-by-Step Regrouping**: First, add the ones; second, regroup if the sum is 10 or more; third, add the tens including the carried digit.
• **Tens Column Adjustment**: Adding the carried digit to the existing digits in the tens column to find the final total.
• **Visualizing Regrouping**: Using blocks to show that 10 ones can be exchanged for 1 ten rod.
• **Accuracy in Regrouping**: Ensuring the carried digit is added to the tens column to avoid errors in the final sum.
• **Double-Digit Sums**: When the final sum of the tens column also results in a two-digit number, extending the addition into the hundreds place.
• **Hundreds Place**: The position to the left of the tens place, which holds the value of hundreds.
• **Three-Digit Sums**: The result of adding two-digit numbers that regroup into the hundreds place.
• **Verification**: Checking the work by adding the numbers in reverse order to ensure the sum remains the same.
• **Error Prevention**: Double-checking the ones column sum before moving to the tens column.
• **Consistency**: Maintaining the same column alignment throughout the entire addition process.

• **"In All"**: A phrase used to ask for the sum of multiple groups or quantities.
• **"Combined"**: A term implying that two or more separate groups are being joined together.
• **"Increase"**: A term suggesting that a quantity is being added to, resulting in a larger total.
• **Problem Analysis**: The process of reading a word problem, identifying the numbers, and determining the operation required.
• **Data Extraction**: Pulling the relevant numbers from a word problem to set up the mathematical equation.

• **Sequential Addition**: Adding the first two numbers to get a sub-total, then adding the third number to that sub-total.
• **Grouping Strategy**: Choosing which two numbers to add first to make the calculation easier (e.g., adding numbers that make a 10).
• **Associative Property**: The rule that states the way in which numbers are grouped in addition does not change the sum.

• **Summing the Tens**: When adding three two-digit numbers, the tens column may include multiple carried digits.

• **Common Mistakes in Addition**: Identifying frequent errors to improve accuracy.
• **Misalignment**: Failing to line up the ones and tens columns, leading to incorrect sums.
• **Forgetting the Carry**: Neglecting to add the carried digit in the tens column.
• **Calculation Errors**: Simple mistakes in basic addition facts (e.g., 7 + 5 = 13 instead of 12).
• **Misreading the Sign**: Confusing the plus sign (+) with the minus sign (-).
• **Starting from the Wrong Side**: Attempting to add the tens column before the ones column.
• **Ignoring Zero**: Miscalculating when zero is involved in the addition.
• **Regrouping Errors**: Carrying a number when the sum is less than 10, or failing to carry when it is 10 or more.
• **Writing Numbers Illegibly**: Poor handwriting leading to confusion between digits (e.g., 4 and 9).
• **Over-complicating**: Using complex methods when a simple mental math strategy would suffice.
• **Lack of Verification**: Not checking the answer, which allows errors to go unnoticed.
• **Rushing**: Performing calculations too quickly, which increases the likelihood of simple mistakes.
• **Lack of Focus**: Losing track of the numbers being added in a multi-step problem.
• **Misinterpreting Word Problems**: Identifying the wrong numbers or the wrong operation from the text.
• **Corrective Practice**: Reviewing mistakes to understand the 'why' behind the error and preventing it in the future.
