• **Chapter Identification**: Welcome to the official DAV Academic Notes for Class 7 Hindi - ज्ञान सागर (Gyan Sagar), Chapter 15 titled "देशभक्त पुरु" (Deshbhakt Puru), prepared according to the curriculum guidelines of the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board.
• **Literary Genre**: This chapter belongs to the genre of historical drama and plays (एकाग्र / नाटक), which uses dialogues and stage directions to bring historical events and patriotic figures to life for young learners.
• **Core Theme**: The central theme of this chapter revolves around patriotism, unyielding courage, self-respect, national pride, and the indomitable spirit of an ancient Indian king facing a foreign conqueror.
• **Historical Setting**: The events depicted in the chapter are set around 326 BCE during the invasion of the Indian subcontinent by the Macedonian conqueror Alexander the Great (सिकंदर).
• **Geographical Context**: The narrative unfolds on the banks of the Jhelum River (ancient Hydaspes River) in the Punjab region, which served as the battleground and camp site for historical confrontations.
• **Primary Protagonist**: King Puru (राजा पुरु), also known historically as Porus, is the hero of the chapter, celebrated for his exceptional bravery, righteous governance, and fierce love for his motherland.
• **Antagonist/Opponent**: Alexander (सिकंदर), the ambitious king of Macedonia who conquered much of the known western world, serves as the primary foreign antagonist in this historical encounter.
• **Significance of Title**: The title "देशभक्त पुरु" directly translates to "The Patriotic Puru," establishing right from the beginning that the character's primary defining attribute is his absolute devotion to his nation.
• **Pedagogical Objectives**: The chapter aims to instill values of national pride, moral integrity, fearlessness in the face of adversity, and respect for historical figures who defended Indian soil.
• **Dramaturgical Form**: Being written in play format, the text employs scenes (दृश्य), character names, dialogues (संवाद), and stage directions (रंगमंच निर्देश) to convey the psychological tension of war and diplomacy.
• **Character Dynamics**: The text highlights the stark contrast between Alexander's aggressive imperialism and Puru's defensive nationalism and commitment to preserving his people's sovereignty.
• **Linguistic Tone**: The language of the chapter is chaste, formal, and elevated Hindi, enriched with vocabulary appropriate for conveying high emotions of dignity, challenge, and royal authority.
• **Historical Background of Puru**: King Puru ruled the region between the Jhelum and Chenab rivers and was renowned for his physical prowess, tactical acumen, and fierce independence.
• **Historical Background of Alexander**: Alexander crossed the Hindu Kush, defeated various tribes, and marched into Punjab expecting easy submissions from regional kings due to his legendary reputation.
• **Role of Allies and Traitors**: The historical context implicitly touches upon the fractured political landscape of ancient India, where unity was often lacking, making Puru's standalone resistance even more heroic.
• **Value Education Matrix**: Students through these notes will analyze how true patriotism transcends the fear of death and demands uncompromising dignity when dealing with foreign invaders.

• **Scene Opening**: The dramatic action of the chapter opens in Alexander’s military tent/camp set up near the banks of the Jhelum River following a fierce and grueling battle.
• **Atmosphere of the Camp**: The stage directions set a tense and formal atmosphere, reflecting the gravity of the situation where a victorious invader is about to meet a captured monarch.
• **Alexander's Mental State**: Alexander is portrayed not merely as a ruthless conqueror, but as an astute and curious leader who is deeply impressed by the extraordinary valor displayed by Puru on the battlefield.
• **Consultation with Generals**: Alexander is shown discussing the day's battle outcomes with his trusted Greek generals and commanders, expressing astonishment at the fierce resistance offered by the Indian army.
• **Battle Assessment**: The text notes that despite Alexander's ultimate victory due to superior numbers and tactical surprises, the battle of the Hydaspes was one of the toughest campaigns Alexander ever fought.
• **Puru's Capture**: King Puru, severely wounded yet maintaining his regal posture, is brought before Alexander’s tribunal as a captive bound in chains, escorted by heavily armed Greek guards.
• **Initial Visual Contrast**: The striking visual contrast is established between the conquering emperor sitting on his throne and the captive Indian king standing tall, unbowed, and radiating intense self-respect.
• **Alexander's Opening Remarks**: Alexander breaks the silence by addressing Puru, acknowledging the sheer ferocity of Puru's resistance and expressing his amazement at how a defeated king can still look so fearless.
• **Puru's Unflinching Stance**: Instead of pleading for mercy or showing signs of defeat, Puru responds with absolute calmness, proving that physical captivity cannot shackle a free and patriotic soul.
• **Concept of Duty (कर्तव्य)**: Puru articulates that as an independent king and protector of his motherland, fighting to the last breath was his supreme sacred duty, regardless of the final outcome.
• **Alexander's Query**: Intrigued by Puru's lack of intimidation, Alexander poses a direct question to the captive king regarding how he expects to be treated as a prisoner of war.
• **Puru's Legendary Reply**: Puru gives the iconic, historically resonant reply that an enemy king should treat another king in the exact manner that one king treats another king—implying honorable and respectful treatment.
• **Depth of the Reply**: This response stuns Alexander because it displays complete parity in dignity, refusing to acknowledge any inferiority despite being physically captured and chained.
• **Alexander's Admiration**: The Greek conqueror is deeply struck by Puru's fearlessness, quick wit, and unwavering royal dignity, which awakens a sense of professional and personal respect within Alexander.
• **Dramatic Tension**: The interaction builds intense dramatic tension, shifting the dynamic from a victor-captive interrogation to a mutual dialogue between two equal minds of the ancient world.
• **Symbolism of Chains**: The chains binding Puru's hands are symbolically rendered irrelevant by the sheer majesty of his character, highlighting the thematic victory of moral strength over physical bondage.

• **Exploration of Self-Respect (आत्मसम्मान)**: The core philosophical discussion between Alexander and Puru centers heavily on the concept of *Atmasamman* (self-respect), which Puru values far above his own life or kingdom.
• **Alexander's Proposition**: Impressed beyond measure, Alexander proposes an alliance, asking Puru how he would like to be treated henceforth if he accepts Alexander's suzerainty and dominion.
• **Puru's Uncompromising Answer**: Puru reiterates without a moment's hesitation that his stance remains unchanged: he demands nothing less than treatment fit for an independent, sovereign monarch.
• **Alexander's Astonishment**: Alexander reminds Puru that he is a prisoner standing before the conqueror of the world, to which Puru calmly replies that a true patriot never considers himself conquered in spirit.
• **Definition of True Patriotism**: Through Puru's dialogues, the text defines true patriotism as an unconditional commitment to one's country that remains unaffected by victories, defeats, chains, or thrones.
• **Contrast of Ambitions**: The chapter sharply contrasts Alexander's imperial ambition of conquering the entire globe with Puru's localized, sacred dedication to defending his own sacred soil from foreign exploitation.
• **Greek Generals' Reaction**: The Greek commanders observing the exchange are initially perplexed and somewhat unsettled by Puru's defiant attitude, fearing it might undermine Alexander's absolute authority.
• **Alexander's Broad-Mindedness**: Demonstrating his appreciation for true greatness, Alexander silences any negative reactions from his generals, choosing instead to honor Puru's extraordinary spirit.
• **Dialogue as a Weapon**: Puru uses his words not to beg for life, but to educate the world conqueror on the limits of brute force and the infinite power of a righteous cause.
• **Concept of Sovereignty**: The dialogue reinforces the political science concept that true sovereignty resides in the will of the people and the integrity of the ruler, not merely in territorial expanse.
• **Psychological Victory**: Although Alexander won the battle on the field, the textual narrative emphasizes that Puru won the psychological and moral battle inside the royal tent.
• **Role of Dignity in Defeat**: The chapter teaches students that defeat in battle is a temporary material setback, but loss of moral dignity and national honor is a permanent spiritual death.
• **Puru's Connection to Motherland**: Every word uttered by Puru echoes his deep spiritual and emotional roots tied to the rivers, mountains, and people of Bharatvarsha.
• **Alexander's Internal Conflict**: Alexander experiences a rare moment of introspection, realizing that all his gold and conquered lands cannot buy the genuine loyalty and fearless spirit that Puru possesses.
• **Dramatic Climax Preparation**: The conversation builds steadily toward the historic resolution where enmity transforms into mutual admiration and respectful diplomatic partnership.
• **Language Enrichment**: Key literary phrases in Hindi such as 'आत्मसम्मान की रक्षा' (protection of self-respect) and 'वीरता का सम्मान' (respect for valor) are prominently highlighted in this segment.

• **Key Vocabulary Analysis - Term 7**: **प्रजा (Praja)** - Meaning subjects or citizens; the common people ruled by a king, whose safety and welfare remain the ultimate responsibility of the monarch.
• **Key Vocabulary Analysis - Term 8**: **कर्तव्य (Kartavya)** - Meaning duty or obligation; the moral and professional responsibility that guided Puru to defend his borders to his last breath.
