• **Chapter Overview:** "Riddle Time" is a foundational chapter in the DAV Class 2 English Reader designed to enhance cognitive development through linguistic puzzles.
• **Concept of Riddles:** A riddle is a statement, question, or phrase having a double or veiled meaning, put forth as a puzzle to be solved.
• **Educational Objective:** The chapter aims to improve vocabulary, critical thinking, and the ability to associate objects with their specific characteristics.
• **Linguistic Structure:** The chapter utilizes simple, descriptive sentences to provide clues about common objects found in a child's environment.
• **The Sun:** The first riddle focuses on the Sun, describing it as a celestial body that provides light and heat to the Earth.
• **Sun Characteristics:** The Sun is described as being "up in the sky," emphasizing its position relative to the ground.
• **Sun Function:** The text highlights the Sun’s role in providing warmth, which is essential for life and the changing of seasons.
• **The Moon:** The second riddle introduces the Moon, contrasting its appearance with that of the Sun.
• **Moon Characteristics:** The Moon is depicted as an object that appears at night, providing a soft, silvery light.
• **Moon Phases:** The chapter subtly introduces the concept of the changing shape of the Moon, often appearing as a crescent or a full circle.
• **The Star:** The third riddle describes stars as tiny, twinkling lights that appear in the night sky.
• **Star Association:** Stars are linked to the night sky, helping children differentiate between day and night phenomena.
• **The Tree:** The fourth riddle focuses on a tree, describing it as a living organism with branches, leaves, and a trunk.
• **Tree Function:** The chapter notes the tree's role in providing shade and acting as a home for birds and insects.
• **Vocabulary Building:** The chapter introduces specific nouns like 'sky', 'light', 'night', 'shade', and 'branches' to build descriptive language skills.
• **Cognitive Association:** Each riddle requires the student to map physical attributes (e.g., "I have leaves") to a specific object (e.g., "Tree").
• **Analytical Skills:** Students learn to process information sequentially, moving from general clues to specific identifiers.
