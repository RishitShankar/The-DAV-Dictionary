• **Point**: A basic unit of geometry representing a location in space, having no length, breadth, or thickness, denoted by a capital letter.
• **Line**: A straight path that extends infinitely in both directions, containing an infinite number of points and having no endpoints.
• **Line Segment**: A definite part of a line that has two fixed endpoints, allowing its length to be measured precisely.
• **Ray**: A part of a line that starts at a fixed initial point and extends infinitely in one direction only.
• **Plane**: A flat, two-dimensional surface that extends infinitely in all directions, such as the surface of a smooth wall or a tabletop.
• **Collinear Points**: Three or more points that lie on the same straight line are termed collinear; otherwise, they are non-collinear.
• **Coplanar Lines**: Lines that lie within the same two-dimensional plane are called coplanar lines.
• **Pairs of Lines**: In a single plane, two distinct lines can either intersect at exactly one point or never meet at all.
• **Intersecting Lines**: Two lines are said to be intersecting if they have exactly one common point, known as the point of intersection.
• **Non-intersecting Lines**: Lines in the same plane that do not meet, no matter how far they are extended, are classified as parallel lines.
• **Transversal Concept**: A line that intersects two or more given lines at distinct points is called a transversal.
• **Distinct Points**: For a line to be a transversal, it must cross the other lines at different locations; crossing at the same point makes them concurrent.
• **Geometry of Position**: The study of "Transversal and Pairs of Lines" focuses on the spatial relationship and the angles formed when lines interact.
• **Dimension of Lines**: Lines are considered one-dimensional figures, whereas the angles formed between them exist within a two-dimensional plane.
• **Symbolic Representation**: Lines are often named using lowercase letters (l, m, n) or by two points on the line (AB with a double arrow over it).
• **Infinite Extension**: The fundamental property of a line is its ability to be produced indefinitely in both directions, which is crucial for identifying transversals.
• **Vertex**: The common point where two lines or rays meet to form an angle is called the vertex.
• **Angle Formation**: When a transversal intersects two lines, it creates eight distinct angles at the two points of intersection.

• **Intersection Point**: The specific location where two intersecting lines cross each other and share a single common coordinate.
• **Concurrent Lines**: When three or more lines in a plane pass through the same point, they are called concurrent lines.
• **Point of Concurrence**: The single common point through which all concurrent lines pass is designated as the point of concurrence.
• **Parallel Lines Definition**: Two lines in the same plane are parallel if the perpendicular distance between them remains constant throughout their length.
• **Symbol for Parallelism**: The symbol '||' is used to denote that two lines are parallel, for example, line 'l' is parallel to line 'm' is written as l || m.
• **Perpendicular Distance**: The shortest distance between two parallel lines, measured along a line segment that is at a 90-degree angle to both.
• **Equidistant Property**: Parallel lines are always equidistant from each other at every point along their infinite length.
• **Non-Parallel Intersecting Lines**: If the distance between two lines changes as they extend, they will eventually meet at a point of intersection.
• **Real-World Examples of Parallel Lines**: Railway tracks, opposite edges of a ruler, and the rungs of a ladder are common physical representations.
• **Real-World Examples of Intersecting Lines**: The letter 'X', the crossing of two roads, and the blades of an open pair of scissors.
• **Transversal as a Connector**: A transversal acts as a bridge that relates the properties of one line to another line in the same plane.
• **Number of Intersections**: A transversal intersecting two lines creates two points of intersection; if it intersects three lines, it creates three points.
• **Linear Pair**: Two adjacent angles formed when two lines intersect, whose sum is always 180 degrees, forming a straight line.
• **Vertically Opposite Angles**: When two lines intersect, the angles opposite to each other at the vertex are equal in measure.
• **Importance of the Transversal**: The transversal is the primary tool used in geometry to prove whether two lines are parallel or not.
• **Plane Geometry Constraint**: All discussions regarding transversals and pairs of lines in Class 6 are restricted to Euclidean plane geometry.
• **Directionality**: Parallel lines must lie in the same plane; lines that do not intersect and are not in the same plane are called skew lines (not covered in Class 6).

• **Transversal Definition**: A line that cuts across two or more lines (which may or may not be parallel) at distinct points.
• **Identifying the Transversal**: In a diagram with three lines, the line that crosses the other two is the transversal relative to those two lines.
• **Points of Intersection**: If line 'n' intersects lines 'l' and 'm' at points P and Q respectively, then 'n' is the transversal.
• **Angle Labeling**: Conventionally, the eight angles formed by a transversal intersecting two lines are numbered 1 through 8 for easy identification.
• **Interior Region**: The space or area located between the two lines being intersected by the transversal.
• **Exterior Region**: The space or area located outside the two lines, above the upper line and below the lower line.
• **Interior Angles**: Angles that lie within the interior region (between the two lines) are called interior angles.
• **Exterior Angles**: Angles that lie in the exterior region (outside the two lines) are called exterior angles.
• **Classification by Position**: Angles are categorized based on their relative positions to the transversal and the lines it intersects.
• **Pairs of Angles**: The study of transversals focuses on specific pairs of angles: corresponding, alternate, and consecutive angles.
• **Transversal of Parallel Lines**: A special case where the transversal intersects two lines that are perfectly parallel to each other.
• **Transversal of Non-Parallel Lines**: A transversal can intersect non-parallel lines, but the specific equality properties of angles will not apply.
• **Visualizing the Transversal**: It is often helpful to think of the transversal as a diagonal or vertical line cutting through horizontal or slanted lines.
• **Angle 1 to Angle 8**: In a standard diagram, angles 3, 4, 5, and 6 are usually interior, while 1, 2, 7, and 8 are exterior.
• **Vertex Groups**: Four angles are formed at the upper vertex of intersection, and four angles are formed at the lower vertex.
• **Relationship between Vertices**: The transversal allows us to compare the group of angles at the first vertex with the group at the second vertex.
• **Straight Line Property**: At each vertex, the sum of adjacent angles on the transversal is 180 degrees because the transversal is a straight line.

• **Corresponding Angles Definition**: Angles that occupy the same relative position at each intersection where a straight line crosses two others.
• **Positioning of Corresponding Angles**: One angle is in the exterior region and the other is in the interior region, but both are on the same side of the transversal.
• **The 'F' Shape Rule**: Corresponding angles can be identified by looking for an 'F' shape (forwards, backwards, upside down, or rotated) in the diagram.
• **Pairs of Corresponding Angles**: When a transversal intersects two lines, there are exactly four pairs of corresponding angles.
• **Matching Positions**: If an angle is at the "top-right" of the first intersection, its corresponding angle is at the "top-right" of the second intersection.
• **Alternate Interior Angles**: A pair of angles on opposite sides of the transversal and between the two lines (in the interior region).
• **The 'Z' Shape Rule**: Alternate interior angles can be identified by looking for a 'Z' or 'N' shape in the geometric figure.
• **Non-Adjacent Property**: Alternate interior angles are non-adjacent; they do not share a common vertex or a common side.
• **Pairs of Alternate Interior Angles**: There are exactly two pairs of alternate interior angles formed by a transversal and two lines.
• **Alternate Exterior Angles**: A pair of angles on opposite sides of the transversal and outside the two lines (in the exterior region).
• **Positioning of Alternate Exterior Angles**: They are located in the exterior region, on opposite sides of the transversal, and at different vertices.
• **Pairs of Alternate Exterior Angles**: There are exactly two pairs of alternate exterior angles formed in the standard transversal diagram.
• **Consecutive Interior Angles**: A pair of angles on the same side of the transversal and inside the two lines; also known as Co-interior angles.
• **The 'C' or 'U' Shape Rule**: Consecutive interior angles can be identified by looking for a 'C' or 'U' shape between the lines.
• **Consecutive Exterior Angles**: A pair of angles on the same side of the transversal and outside the two lines; also known as Co-exterior angles.
• **Side of Transversal**: "Same side" refers to both angles being either to the left of the transversal or both being to the right.
• **Summary of Pairs**: A transversal across two lines creates 4 pairs of corresponding, 2 pairs of alternate interior, and 2 pairs of alternate exterior angles.

• **Interior Angles List**: In a standard intersection of lines 'l' and 'm' by transversal 'n', the interior angles are those situated between 'l' and 'm'.
• **Exterior Angles List**: The exterior angles are those situated above line 'l' and below line 'm' (assuming 'l' is the top line).
• **Identifying Interior Pairs**: If the angles are numbered 1-4 at the top and 5-8 at the bottom, angles 3, 4, 5, and 6 are typically interior.
• **Identifying Exterior Pairs**: In the same numbering system, angles 1, 2, 7, and 8 are typically exterior.
• **Same-Side Interior Angles**: These are interior angles that lie on the same side of the transversal (e.g., angle 4 and angle 6).
• **Same-Side Exterior Angles**: These are exterior angles that lie on the same side of the transversal (e.g., angle 1 and angle 7).
• **Alternate Interior Pair 1**: One angle on the left interior and the other on the right interior at different vertices (e.g., angle 3 and angle 6).
• **Alternate Interior Pair 2**: The second pair consisting of the remaining interior angles on opposite sides (e.g., angle 4 and angle 5).
• **Alternate Exterior Pair 1**: One angle on the left exterior and the other on the right exterior at different vertices (e.g., angle 1 and angle 8).
• **Alternate Exterior Pair 2**: The second pair consisting of the remaining exterior angles on opposite sides (e.g., angle 2 and angle 7).
• **Corresponding Pair 1**: Top-left angle of the first intersection and top-left angle of the second intersection (e.g., angle 1 and angle 5).
• **Corresponding Pair 2**: Top-right angle of the first intersection and top-right angle of the second intersection (e.g., angle 2 and angle 6).
• **Corresponding Pair 3**: Bottom-left angle of the first intersection and bottom-left angle of the second intersection (e.g., angle 3 and angle 7).
• **Corresponding Pair 4**: Bottom-right angle of the first intersection and bottom-right angle of the second intersection (e.g., angle 4 and angle 8).
• **Vertex Association**: Each pair of angles (corresponding, alternate, or consecutive) must involve one angle from the first vertex and one from the second.
• **Linear Pair vs. Transversal Pairs**: Linear pairs share a vertex; transversal pairs (like alternate or corresponding) do not share a vertex.
• **Visual Recognition**: Developing the ability to see 'F', 'Z', and 'C' shapes is essential for identifying these angle relationships quickly.

• **Parallel Line Axiom**: If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, then each pair of corresponding angles is equal in measure.
• **Equality of Corresponding Angles**: When l || m, then ∠1 = ∠5, ∠2 = ∠6, ∠3 = ∠7, and ∠4 = ∠8.
• **Alternate Interior Angle Theorem**: If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, then each pair of alternate interior angles is equal.
• **Equality of Alternate Interior Angles**: When l || m, then ∠3 = ∠6 and ∠4 = ∠5.
• **Alternate Exterior Angle Theorem**: If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, then each pair of alternate exterior angles is equal.
• **Equality of Alternate Exterior Angles**: When l || m, then ∠1 = ∠8 and ∠2 = ∠7.
• **Consecutive Interior Angle Theorem**: If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, then each pair of consecutive interior angles is supplementary.
• **Supplementary Definition**: Two angles are supplementary if the sum of their measures is exactly 180 degrees.
• **Sum of Co-interior Angles**: When l || m, then ∠4 + ∠6 = 180° and ∠3 + ∠5 = 180°.
• **Consecutive Exterior Angle Property**: If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, then each pair of consecutive exterior angles is also supplementary.
• **Sum of Co-exterior Angles**: When l || m, then ∠1 + ∠7 = 180° and ∠2 + ∠8 = 180°.
• **Logical Deduction**: These properties are interdependent; if one is true (given parallel lines), the others can be proven using vertically opposite angles and linear pairs.
• **Transitivity of Equality**: If ∠1 = ∠5 (corresponding) and ∠1 = ∠4 (vertically opposite), then ∠4 = ∠5 (alternate interior).
• **Application of Linear Pairs**: Since ∠3 + ∠4 = 180° (linear pair) and ∠4 = ∠5 (alternate interior), it follows that ∠3 + ∠5 = 180° (co-interior).
• **Constancy of Angle Measure**: No matter how the transversal is slanted, as long as the lines are parallel, these equality and supplementary relationships hold.
• **Crucial Condition**: These specific relationships (equality and 180° sum) *only* apply when the lines being intersected are parallel.
• **Foundation of Geometry**: These theorems form the basis for solving complex geometric problems involving triangles, parallelograms, and other polygons.

• **Testing for Parallelism**: The properties of transversals can be used in reverse to determine if two lines are parallel.
• **Converse of Corresponding Angles**: If a transversal intersects two lines such that a pair of corresponding angles is equal, then the lines must be parallel.
• **Converse of Alternate Interior Angles**: If a transversal intersects two lines such that a pair of alternate interior angles is equal, then the lines are parallel.
• **Converse of Alternate Exterior Angles**: If a transversal intersects two lines such that a pair of alternate exterior angles is equal, then the lines are parallel.
• **Converse of Co-interior Angles**: If a transversal intersects two lines such that a pair of consecutive interior angles is supplementary, then the lines are parallel.
• **Converse of Co-exterior Angles**: If a transversal intersects two lines such that a pair of consecutive exterior angles is supplementary, then the lines are parallel.
• **Single Pair Sufficiency**: To prove two lines are parallel, you only need to show that *one* of these conditions is met.
• **Verification Method**: If you measure ∠1 and ∠5 and find they are both 70°, you can mathematically conclude that line l is parallel to line m.
• **Non-Parallel Scenario**: If corresponding angles are not equal (e.g., 70° and 72°), the lines will eventually intersect if extended.
• **Perpendicular Transversal**: If a transversal is perpendicular to one of two parallel lines, it must be perpendicular to the other line as well.
• **90-Degree Angles**: In the case of a perpendicular transversal, all eight angles formed will be 90 degrees (right angles).
• **Parallelism to a Third Line**: If two lines are both parallel to the same third line, then they are parallel to each other (Transitive Property of Parallel Lines).
• **Line l || m and m || n**: If these two conditions are met, then it is logically certain that l || n.
• **Practical Construction**: Architects and engineers use the converse properties to ensure that walls or beams are perfectly parallel.
• **Geometric Proofs**: Using these properties allows for the formal proof of the angle sum property of a triangle (which is 180°).
• **Visual Cues in Problems**: Arrows drawn on lines in a geometry diagram indicate that those lines are parallel.
• **Importance of Accuracy**: Even a 1-degree difference in alternate angles means the lines are not parallel.

• **Angle Relationships Summary**: When lines are parallel, Corresponding = Equal, Alternate = Equal, Consecutive = Supplementary.
• **The "Z" Angle Equality**: The interior angles in the corners of the 'Z' shape are always equal if the top and bottom of the 'Z' are parallel.
• **The "F" Angle Equality**: The angles in the same relative corners of the 'F' shape are always equal if the horizontal bars of the 'F' are parallel.
• **The "C" Angle Supplement**: The interior angles inside the 'C' shape add up to 180° if the top and bottom of the 'C' are parallel.
• **Vertical Angles Consistency**: Regardless of parallelism, vertically opposite angles at any intersection are always equal.
• **Linear Pair Consistency**: Regardless of parallelism, angles forming a straight line always add up to 180°.
• **Calculating Unknown Angles**: If one angle in a parallel line-transversal system is known, the measures of all other seven angles can be determined.
• **Example Calculation**: If ∠1 = 110°, then ∠3 = 110° (vertically opposite), ∠5 = 110° (corresponding), and ∠7 = 110° (alternate exterior to ∠2's corresponding).
• **Supplementary Calculation**: If ∠1 = 110°, then ∠2 = 180° - 110° = 70° (linear pair).
• **Determining the Set**: In a parallel system, there are usually only two distinct angle measures (unless the transversal is perpendicular, then there is only one).
• **Acute and Obtuse Pairs**: Typically, four angles will be acute (less than 90°) and four will be obtuse (greater than 90°), provided the transversal is not perpendicular.
• **Equality of Acute Angles**: All acute angles in the parallel system will be equal to each other.
• **Equality of Obtuse Angles**: All obtuse angles in the parallel system will be equal to each other.
• **Sum of one Acute and one Obtuse**: Any one acute angle plus any one obtuse angle in the parallel system will equal 180°.
• **Transversal through Multiple Lines**: If a transversal cuts three parallel lines, the same angle properties apply across all three intersections.
• **Parallel Lines and Transversals in Polygons**: These concepts are used to identify properties of parallelograms, trapeziums, and rectangles.
• **Interior vs Exterior Logic**: Interior is "inside the sandwich" made by the two lines; exterior is "outside the sandwich."

• **Parallel Lines in 3D**: While Class 6 focuses on 2D, parallel lines are defined as lines in the same plane that never meet.
• **Distance between Parallel Lines**: This is the length of the perpendicular segment dropped from any point on one line to the other line.

• **Intersecting Lines and Angles**: When two lines intersect, they form four angles; the sum of these four angles is always 360°.

• **Key Term: Transversal**: A line intersecting two or more lines at distinct points.
• **Key Term: Parallel Lines**: Lines in a plane that never intersect and are equidistant.
• **Key Term: Intersecting Lines**: Lines that share exactly one common point.
• **Key Term: Corresponding Angles**: Pairs of angles in the same relative position at each intersection.
• **Key Term: Alternate Interior Angles**: Pairs of angles inside the lines on opposite sides of the transversal.
• **Key Term: Alternate Exterior Angles**: Pairs of angles outside the lines on opposite sides of the transversal.
• **Key Term: Co-interior Angles**: Pairs of angles inside the lines on the same side of the transversal.
• **Property 1**: If lines are parallel, Corresponding angles are equal.
• **Property 2**: If lines are parallel, Alternate Interior angles are equal.
• **Property 3**: If lines are parallel, Alternate Exterior angles are equal.
• **Property 4**: If lines are parallel, Co-interior angles are supplementary (sum = 180°).
• **Property 5**: If lines are parallel, Co-exterior angles are supplementary (sum = 180°).
• **Converse Property**: If any of the above angle relationships are found to be true, then the lines must be parallel.
