• **Chapter Title and Context**: The nineteenth chapter of DAV Class 6 Hindi Abhyas Sagar is titled "सिकंदर और साधु" (Sikandar aur Sadhu), focusing on the historical and moral encounter between the Greek conqueror Alexander the Great and an Indian saint.
• **Academic Objective**: This chapter is designed to enhance students' grammatical understanding, vocabulary expansion, contextual reading comprehension, and moral discernment through structured prose analysis.
• **Primary Theme**: The central theme revolves around the contrast between worldly ambition, material conquest, and true spiritual contentment, freedom, and wisdom.
• **Historical Background**: Alexander (Sikandar) of Macedonia invaded India with the ambition of conquering the entire world, believing that material dominion equated to ultimate greatness.
• **Introduction of the Sadhu**: The saint represents ancient Indian philosophical thought, detachment from worldly desires, and inner peace that remains undisturbed by royal threats or material allurements.
• **Grammatical Focus**: The exercises and textual segments of this chapter primarily focus on the structural analysis of sentences, identification of verbs, subject-predicate relationships, and suffix-prefix applications.
• **Vocabulary Enhancement**: Students are introduced to high-register Hindi vocabulary related to character traits, administrative authority, philosophical inquiry, and emotional states.
• **Narrative Progression**: The chapter transitions from an exposition of Alexander's military might to a philosophical debate between raw power and spiritual sovereignty.
• **Moral Lesson**: True greatness is not measured by the geographical expanse of an empire one rules, but by the mastery over one's own desires, ego, and greed.
• **Pedagogical Approach**: The DAV Abhyas Sagar framework uses this narrative to build language proficiency by prompting students to analyze sentence formations based on the actions of the characters.
• **Character Study of Alexander**: Alexander is portrayed as restless, ambitious, intelligent, yet fundamentally trapped in his own desire for eternal conquest and validation.
• **Character Study of the Sadhu**: The Sadhu is depicted as calm, fearless, unyielding to external coercion, and deeply rooted in spiritual truths that transcend transient earthly power.

• **Setting the Scene**: The chapter begins by establishing the tension-filled atmosphere of the Greek military camp established on Indian soil following various campaigns.
• **Alexander's Inquiries**: Alexander hears tales of a wise, fearless Indian saint who refuses to bow before conquerors or accept gifts of gold and territory.
• **The Royal Curiosity**: Driven by a mixture of pride and genuine intellectual curiosity, Alexander decides to visit the saint himself in disguise or with minimal escort.
• **The Encounter Location**: The meeting takes place in a quiet, natural setting away from the chaos of the military camp, symbolizing the clash between nature's tranquility and human ambition.

• **Alexander's Pride**: Alexander asserts his authority, reminding the Sadhu that his life and freedom hang by the thread of the emperor's absolute will.
• **The Philosophical Turn**: Rather than pleading for mercy, the Sadhu responds with calm wisdom, questioning the very definition of a true conqueror.
