### PAGE 2: Fundamental Principles & Theory Deep-Dive

- 📖 [BLUE] **Definition of Concord (Subject-Verb Agreement)**:
  - Concord is the grammatical harmony established between the subject (the doer or theme of the sentence) and the finite verb (the action or state of being).
  - If the subject is singular, the verb must display its singular inflections; if the subject is plural, the verb must match accordingly.

---

- ⚖️ [GOLD] **The Golden Rule of Grammatical Number**:
  - **Nouns**: Usually add *-s* or *-es* to become **Plural** (e.g., *dog* $\to$ *dogs*, *box* $\to$ *boxes*).
  - **Verbs**: Usually add *-s* or *-es* (in the Simple Present Tense) to become **Singular** (e.g., *run* $\to$ *runs*, *is*, *has*, *does*).
  - ⚠️ [RED] **Crucial Contrast**: Do not confuse noun pluralization rules with verb singularization rules. They operate in exact opposition.

---

- 🔬 [GREEN] **The Three Persons Framework**:
  - **First Person**: *I* (singular/takes singular/plural verbs depending on tense) / *We* (plural/takes plural verbs).
  - **Second Person**: *You* (singular and plural/always takes a **plural verb** e.g., *You are*, *You have*).
  - **Third Person**: *He/She/It/Singular Noun* (takes **singular verbs** e.g., *walks*, *is*) / *They/Plural Nouns* (takes **plural verbs** e.g., *walk*, *are*).

---

- 📝 [BLUE] **Theoretical Underpinnings of Proximity**:
  - Grammatical agreement must strictly ignore deceptive words placed *between* the subject and the verb.
  - The verb agrees with the **true grammatical head** of the subject phrase, not the noun immediately preceding the verb.
  - *Example*: "The box [of expensive imported chocolates] **was** stolen." (Head: *box* $\to$ singular verb *was*, ignoring *chocolates*).

---

- 🛠️ [RED] **Syntactic Hierarchy**:
  - Syntax dictates that word order cannot override agreement. 
  - Even in inverted structures, the subject remains the semantic anchor determining the verb's form.

### PAGE 3: Scientific Laws, Formulas, Equations & Principles

- ⚗️ [BLUE] **Law 1: The Basic Addition Formula**
  $$\text{Singular Subject} + [-\text{s/es} \text{ Verb}] \implies \text{Grammatically Correct (Simple Present)}$$
  $$\text{Plural Subject} + [\text{Base Verb}] \implies \text{Grammatically Correct (Simple Present)}$$

---

- ⚗️ [GOLD] **Law 2: The Conjunction Rule for "And"**
  $$\text{Noun A} + \text{"and"} + \text{Noun B} \implies \textbf{Plural Verb}$$
  - *Exception A (Single Idea/Hendiadys)*: Bread and butter **is** my favorite breakfast. (Acts as a single culinary unit).
  - *Exception B (Same Person/Title)*: The director and producer **has** arrived. (Refers to one individual possessing two roles; compare with: *The director and the producer **have** arrived*).

---

- ⚗️ [RED] **Law 3: The Correlative Conjunction Principle**
  - **Either...or / Neither...nor / Not only...but also**:
    $$\text{Subject 1} + [\text{Either/Neither}] + \text{Subject 2} \implies \textbf{Verb agrees with Subject 2 (the closer subject)}$$
  - *Formula*: Either the teacher or the **students are** responsible. / Either the students or the **teacher is** responsible.

---

- ⚗️ [GREEN] **Law 4: The Indefinite Pronoun Equation**
  - *Always Singular*: Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Somebody, Anyone, Anybody, No one, Nobody, Nothing, Either, Neither.
  - $\text{Indefinite Pronoun} \implies \textbf{Singular Verb}$ (*Everyone **is** ready*).

---

- ⚗️ [BLUE] **Law 5: Fractional and Quantifier Balances**
  - Fractions (*one-third of*, *half of*): Agreement depends on the noun that follows the preposition *of*.
    - One-third of the **cake is** eaten. (Singular noun $\to$ Singular verb).
    - One-third of the **apples are** rotten. (Plural noun $\to$ Plural verb).

### PAGE 4: Exhaustive Technical Glossary & Precision Definitions

- 📖 [BLUE] **Concord**: The formal grammatical term for the agreement or matching of a verb with its subject in person, number, and occasionally gender.
- 📖 [GOLD] **Finite Verb**: A verb form that has a subject and shows tense, person, and number. (The exact element targeted in Subject-Verb Agreement rules).
- 📖 [RED] **Notional Agreement (Synesis)**: Agreement according to the *meaning* of a word rather than its strict grammatical form (e.g., treating a collective noun as plural because the members act individually).
- 📖 [GREEN] **Proximity Agreement**: The erroneous or correct tendency to make a verb agree with the noun or pronoun closest to it rather than the actual head subject.
- 📖 [BLUE] **Compound Subject**: A subject consisting of two or more simple subjects joined by coordinating or correlative conjunctions.
- 📖 [GOLD] **Correlative Conjunctions**: Conjunctions used in pairs (*either...or*, *neither...nor*, *not only...but also*) that link balanced sentence elements.
- 📖 [RED] **Collective Noun**: A noun that denotes a group of individuals (e.g., *herd*, *committee*, *jury*, *team*). Can take singular or plural verbs based on collective unity versus individual division.
- 📖 [GREEN] **Ellipsis**: The omission of words easily understood from context, which can sometimes obscure the true subject-verb relationship.
- 📖 [BLUE] **Attributive Noun / Modifier**: Nouns placed before other nouns that can complicate the identification of the true subject.
- 📖 [GOLD] **Inverted Sentence**: A sentence structure where the verb precedes the subject (e.g., *Here lies the treasure*, *Down came the rain*), requiring extra care to locate the true subject.

### PAGE 5: Detailed Page-by-Page Textbook Highlights & Core Factoids

- 📄 [BLUE] **DAV English Practice Book – Unit Overview (Page 35-38)**:
  - Establishes the baseline exercises focusing on identifying singular vs. plural subjects in isolation.
  - Highlights the most common pitfall: confusing auxiliary verbs (*is/are*, *was/were*, *has/have*) with main verbs.

---

- 📄 [GOLD] **Textbook Highlight 1: Distance and Time Expressions**:
  - Expressions of time, money, distance, and weight usually take a **singular verb** when considered as a **single collective amount**.
  - *Factoid*: "Ten kilometers **is** a long distance to walk." / "Fifty dollars **is** too much for this book."

---

- 📄 [RED] **Textbook Highlight 2: Titles of Books, Movies, and Organizations**:
  - Proper nouns that are plural in form but represent a single entity take a **singular verb**.
  - *Factoid*: "*The Arabian Nights* **remains** a classic book." / "*United States* **is** a global superpower."

---

- 📄 [GREEN] **Textbook Highlight 3: Plural-Named Sciences and Academic Disciplines**:
  - Nouns ending in *-ics* (*Mathematics*, *Physics*, *Economics*, *Politics*) generally take a **singular verb** when treated as a field of study.
  - *Factoid*: "Physics **is** a fascinating branch of science."
  - ⚠️ *Caveat*: If preceded by a possessive pronoun (*their*, *your*), they can take plural verbs (*"His politics **are** radical"*).

---

- 📄 [BLUE] **Textbook Highlight 4: Indefinite Quantities ("A number of" vs. "The number of")**:
  - *A number of* $\implies$ Always takes a **plural verb** (Meaning: *many*). Example: "A number of students **are** absent."
  - *The number of* $\implies$ Always takes a **singular verb** (Meaning: *the counted sum*). Example: "The number of students **is** increasing."

### PAGE 6: Conceptual Breakdown, Practical Examples & Applications

- 💡 [BLUE] **Category 1: Compound Subjects with "And"**
  - *Rule*: Two or more singular subjects joined by *and* form a plural subject.
  - *Example*: Rahul and Priya **are** studying in the library.
  - *Nuance (Single Entity)*: Rice and curry **is** my favorite dish. (One meal).

---

- 💡 [GOLD] **Category 2: Indefinite Pronouns Deep-Dive**
  - *Rule*: Words like *each*, *every*, *everyone*, *someone* demand singular verbs.
  - *Example 1*: **Each** of the players **has** received a medal. (Trap alert: Do not look at *players*; look at *Each*).
  - *Example 2*: **Neither** of the answers **is** correct.

---

- 💡 [RED] **Category 3: Collective Nouns (Unity vs. Division)**
  - *Unity (Singular Verb)*: The committee **has** reached a unanimous decision.
  - *Division (Plural Verb)*: The committee **have** expressed differing opinions on the budget.

---

- 💡 [GREEN] **Category 4: Expressions of Quantity (*All*, *Some*, *None*, *Most*)**
  - *Rule*: The verb depends on whether the noun following the preposition *of* is countable (plural) or uncountable (singular).
  - *Uncountable*: **All** of the water **is** contaminated.
  - *Countable*: **All** of the students **are** present.

---

- 💡 [BLUE] **Category 5: Sentences Beginning with "There" or "Here"**
  - *Rule*: "There" and "Here" are never subjects. The subject follows the verb.
  - *Example*: There **is** a book on the table. / There **are** many books on the table.

### PAGE 7: DAV Board Exam High-Yield Factoids & Golden Highlights

- ⭐ [BLUE] **High-Yield Factoid 1: The "As well as / Along with / Together with" Trap**
  - When subjects are joined by *as well as*, *along with*, *together with*, *accompanied by*, *in addition to*, the verb agrees with the **first subject**, completely ignoring the second.
  - *DAV Exam Question Pattern*: "The captain, along with his crew members, **was** (not *were*) drowned."

---

- ⭐ [GOLD] **High-Yield Factoid 2: Nouns Joined by "Or / Nor" with Mixed Persons**
  - When a singular and a plural subject are joined by *or* or *nor*, place the **plural subject closer to the verb** to maintain natural-sounding English, and use a plural verb.
  - *DAV Exam Question Pattern*: "Neither the teacher nor the **students were** present."

---

- ⭐ [RED] **High-Yield Factoid 3: Expressions of Amount and Measurement**
  - Fractions, percentages, and measurements used with mass nouns take singular verbs; used with countable plural nouns, they take plural verbs.
  - *DAV Exam Question Pattern*: "Fifty percent of the **pie was** eaten." vs. "Fifty percent of the **pies were** eaten."

---

- ⭐ [GREEN] **High-Yield Factoid 4: Relative Pronoun Antecedent Agreement**
  - When a relative pronoun (*who*, *which*, *that*) acts as the subject, the verb agrees with the pronoun's **antecedent** (the noun it refers to).
  - *DAV Exam Question Pattern*: "It is I who **am** (not *is*) responsible for this mistake." / "She is one of those women who **work** (not *works*) tirelessly."

---

- ⭐ [BLUE] **High-Yield Factoid 5: Plural Forms with Singular Meaning**
  - Words like *measles*, *mumps*, *news*, *billiards*, *darts* end in *-s* but are strictly singular.
  - *DAV Exam Question Pattern*: "Measles **is** a contagious viral disease."

  - *Uncountable Nouns* (*advice*, *information*, *furniture*, *luggage*, *machinery*, *poetry*):
    - **Always take Singular Verbs**: "Your luggage **is** heavy." / "Good advice **is** priceless."
    - **Never take plural suffixes** (*furnitures*, *advices* are ungrammatical).

---

- 📉 [RED] **The "One of the + Plural Noun + Who" Structure**:
  - Formula: $\text{One of the} + \text{Plural Noun} + \text{Who} + \textbf{Plural Verb}$
  - *Application*: "He is one of those men who **trust** easily." (Because *who* refers to *men*, which is plural).

### PAGE 9: Crucial Distinctions, Conceptual Comparisons & Pitfalls

- ⚖️ [BLUE] **Distinction 1: "Every" vs. "All"**
  - **Every**: Focuses on individuals within a group separately $\implies$ Always takes a singular verb (*Every boy and girl **was** given a prize*).
  - **All**: Focuses on the collective totality $\implies$ Takes plural or singular depending on countability (*All the children **were** playing*).

---

- ⚖️ [GOLD] **Distinction 2: "None of" Concord Nuance**
  - *Traditional Rule*: "None" means "not one" and strictly takes a **singular verb**.
  - *Modern Usage*: "None" can also be treated as plural when referring to a plural antecedent.
  - *DAV Board Standard*: Stick to the traditional **singular verb** for formal examinations unless context overwhelmingly demands plural (*"None of the money **was** recovered"*).

---

- ⚖️ [RED] **Distinction 3: Expressions of Quantity (*A great deal of*, *Plenty of*)**
  - **A great deal of / Plenty of + Uncountable Noun** $\implies$ Singular Verb (*A great deal of effort **was** put in*).
  - **Plenty of + Plural Countable Noun** $\implies$ Plural Verb (*Plenty of books **are** available*).

---

- ⚖️ [GREEN] **Distinction 4: Inverted Locational Inversions ("Here/There") vs. Subject-First Sentences**
  - *Pitfall*: Students often mistake the introductory word *Here* or *There* for the subject.
  - *Correction*: Always search for the true noun performing the action after the verb. 
  - *Example*: "Here **comes** the bus." (*The bus* is the singular subject).

---

- ⚖️ [BLUE] **Distinction 5: Titles and Proper Names as Plurals**
  - *Pitfall*: Changing a verb to plural just because a book title ends with an *-s*.
  - *Correction*: "*The Adventures of Tom Sawyer* **is** an exciting read." (Treat the entire title as a single linguistic item).

### PAGE 10: 5-Minute Rapid Exam Booster Summary & Memory Anchors

- 🚀 [BLUE] **The 5-Minute DAV Exam Checklist**:
  1. **Locate the True Subject**: Cross out all prepositional phrases (*of*, *in*, *on*, *with*) between the subject and the verb.
  2. **Check for Conjunctions**: 
     - Joined by *and*? $\to$ Usually Plural.
     - Joined by *either/or* or *neither/nor*? $\to$ Agree with the closer subject.
     - Joined by *as well as*? $\to$ Agree with the first subject.
  3. **Check Indefinite Pronouns**: *Each*, *every*, *everyone*, *someone* $\to$ Always Singular.
  4. **Check Quantifiers**: *A number of* = Plural verb; *The number of* = Singular verb.
  5. **Check Plural-Form Singulars**: *Mathematics*, *Measles*, *News* $\to$ Singular verbs.

---

- 🧠 [GOLD] **Top 5 Memory Anchors (Mnemonics)**:
  - **S-S Rule**: **S**ingular **S**ubject loves verbs ending in **S** (*The cat sleep**s***).
  - **Proximity trap**: Ignore the noun in the middle, match the head at the beginning.
  - **Neither/Or Closer**: The verb hugs the noun closest to it.
  - **Each is Always Alone**: *Each* and *Every* always take singular verbs, no matter what follows.
  - **Title Unity**: A book title, no matter how long or plural, is just **one** book ($\to$ singular verb).

---

- 🏁 [RED] **Final Word of Caution for DAV Students**:
  - Always read the sentence completely before filling in the blank. 
  - Pay special attention to sentence inversions and relative clauses (*who*, *that*, *which*). 
  - Precision in concord is the hallmark of an exceptional English scholar!
