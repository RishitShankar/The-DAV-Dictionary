• **Digital Citizenship Definition:** The responsible, ethical, and safe use of technology, the internet, and digital devices by students, teachers, and global citizens.
• **DAV Academic Objective:** Cultivating moral values, digital empathy, and responsible behavior among Class 6 students while interacting in the digital world.
• **The Internet Ecosystem:** A vast, interconnected global network of computers that offers boundless educational resources, communication tools, and entertainment.
• **Need for Cyber Safety:** Essential awareness and protective measures required to prevent online threats, data theft, cyberbullying, and accidental exposure to inappropriate content.
• **Definition of Cyber Safety:** The safe and responsible use of information and communication technology to protect oneself and others from digital harms.
• **Digital Footprint Concept:** The permanent trail of data, history, and digital footprints left behind by users every time they browse websites, post comments, or send messages.
• **Active vs. Passive Footprint:** Active footprints are deliberately created by posting content, while passive footprints are collected automatically through browsing habits and IP tracking.
• **Permanence of Online Data:** The principle that anything posted online can be copied, saved, shared, and stored indefinitely, making caution mandatory before uploading.
• **Private vs. Public Information:** Understanding the strict boundary between data that must remain confidential and information that is safe to share publicly.
• **Personally Identifiable Information (PII):** Sensitive data such as full name, home address, phone number, school name, and parents' financial details that must never be shared online.
• **The Golden Rule of Sharing:** Never share personal information with strangers or on untrusted websites without explicit parental or teacher supervision.
• **Introduction to Cyber Threats:** Basic awareness of malicious online entities, including scams, fake profiles, and harmful software designed to exploit naive users.
• **Safe Surfing Habits:** Fundamental browsing practices that minimize risks, such as sticking to age-appropriate websites and avoiding unknown download links.
• **Role of Parents and Teachers:** Recognizing trusted adults as primary guides and safety nets when encountering confusing, scary, or inappropriate online situations.
• **Empowerment Through Knowledge:** Understanding that the internet is a magnificent tool for learning when navigated with awareness, caution, and adherence to moral values.

• **Password Security Fundamentals:** The first line of defense in cybersecurity, acting as a digital lock to protect personal accounts from unauthorized access.
• **Anatomy of a Strong Password:** A combination of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special symbols that is difficult for humans or computers to guess.
• **Weak Password Pitfalls:** Avoiding easily guessable passwords such as "123456", "password", pet names, or birth dates that hackers crack instantly.
• **Password Length Requirement:** Ensuring passwords are at least 8 to 12 characters long to increase cryptographic complexity and resistance to brute-force attacks.
• **Secret Password Policy:** The absolute rule that passwords must never be shared with friends, classmates, or anyone other than parents.
• **Uniqueness Principle:** Using different passwords for different online accounts so that a compromise on one platform does not breach others.
• **Password Managers:** Secure tools or browser features that help generate and store complex passwords safely without needing to memorize dozens of combinations.
• **Logging Out Safely:** The critical practice of signing out of personal accounts after finishing work, especially on shared school computers or library terminals.
• **Browser Save Password Prompt:** Understanding the risks of letting web browsers remember passwords on public or shared devices used by multiple people.
• **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** An extra layer of security requiring a second form of verification (like a text code or app prompt) beyond just the password.
• **Phishing Awareness via Passwords:** Recognizing fake login pages designed to trick users into typing their passwords into fraudulent websites.
• **Changing Passwords Regularly:** The healthy habit of updating important account passwords periodically to maintain long-term digital security.
• **Recognizing Lock Indicators:** Checking for padlock icons and HTTPS protocols in the browser address bar before entering login credentials anywhere.
• **Avoiding Write-Downs:** Never writing passwords on sticky notes attached to monitors or notebooks left lying around desks.
• **Recovery Options:** Setting up safe backup email addresses or security questions with parents to recover locked accounts legitimately.

• **Definition of Cyberbullying:** The use of digital technologies, communication devices, or online platforms to harass, threaten, humiliate, or target peers.
• **Forms of Cyberbullying:** Includes sending mean messages, spreading rumors online, creating fake profiles to mock someone, and posting embarrassing photos without consent.
• **Difference from Traditional Bullying:** Cyberbullying can happen 24/7, reach a massive audience instantly, and leave a permanent digital record that is hard to erase.
• **Impact on Victims:** Causes severe emotional distress, anxiety, drop in academic performance, and feelings of isolation among affected students.
• **The Bystander Effect Online:** The tendency of witnesses to ignore cyberbullying due to fear of getting involved or the false belief that it is not their problem.
• **Upstander Intervention:** The ethical responsibility of Class 6 students to stand up against cyberbullying by supporting the victim and reporting the behavior.
• **Golden Rule of Digital Communication:** "Treat others online as you would wish to be treated in real life" (The Digital Golden Rule).
• **Empathy in Cyberspace:** Remembering that behind every screen name, profile picture, and email address is a real human being with real feelings.
• **Rule of Pause:** Taking a deep breath and pausing for a few seconds before replying to an angry or provocative message to prevent escalating conflicts.
• **Text Tone Misunderstandings:** Recognizing that typed messages lack tone of voice and facial expressions, making misunderstandings common and careful wording essential.
• **Never Retaliate:** Avoiding the temptation to fight back with mean messages, which only worsens the situation and can get both parties into trouble.
• **Saving Evidence:** Taking screenshots of abusive messages, harmful comments, or threatening posts instead of deleting them immediately.
• **The Blocking Tool:** Utilizing built-in blocking features on chat apps, games, and social networks to prevent bullies from contacting you.
• **Reporting Mechanisms:** Knowing how to use report buttons on websites and apps to alert moderators about abusive behavior.
• **Involving Trusted Adults:** Immediately informing parents, teachers, or school counselors whenever cyberbullying occurs or causes distress.

• **Definition of Netiquette:** Short for "internet etiquette," it represents the set of professional, moral, and polite rules for behavior on the internet.
• **Respecting Others Online:** Treating fellow internet users with dignity, honoring diverse opinions, and maintaining polite conversation in all digital spaces.
• **Capital Letters Rule:** Avoiding typing entire sentences or paragraphs in ALL CAPS, as this is universally interpreted in netiquette as shouting or anger.
• **Punctuation and Tone:** Using punctuation carefully and avoiding excessive exclamation marks or question marks that can make messages sound aggressive.
• **Grammar and Clarity:** Writing clear, well-spelled messages so that other users can understand thoughts easily without confusion.
• **Copyright and Plagiarism:** Respecting intellectual property by never copying someone else's work, artwork, or text and claiming it as your own.
• **Giving Credit (Citations):** Acknowledging sources, authors, and creators when using information from the internet for school projects and assignments.
• **Spam and Chain Messages:** Avoiding the creation and forwarding of spam emails, junk messages, chain letters, and unverified viral hoaxes.
• **Respecting Privacy in Groups:** Never sharing screenshots of private group chats or personal messages with outsiders without explicit permission.
• **Online Gaming Etiquette:** Being a good sport in multiplayer games, avoiding trash talk, and encouraging teammates rather than blaming them for losses.
• **Constructive Feedback:** Offering polite and helpful suggestions when commenting on peer creations instead of harsh criticism.
• **Avoiding Trolls:** Recognizing internet "trolls"—individuals who post inflammatory comments just to provoke reactions—and choosing to ignore them entirely.
• **Language Choice:** Using clean, school-appropriate language in all chats, forums, message boards, and virtual classrooms.
• **Commercial Ethics:** Refraining from clicking suspicious ads or participating in unauthorized digital sales pitches inside educational platforms.
• **DAV Moral Integration:** Aligning daily netiquette practices with core human values like honesty, humility, kindness, and respect.

• **Definition of Malware:** Short for "malicious software," it refers to intrusive programs designed by cybercriminals to damage, disrupt, or steal data from computers.
• **Common Types of Malware:** Includes viruses, worms, trojan horses, spyware, adware, and ransomware that threaten system security.
• **Computer Viruses:** Programs that attach themselves to clean files, replicate, and spread throughout a system, corrupting data and slowing down performance.
• **Trojan Horse Mechanism:** Disguised software that appears useful or entertaining (like a game) but secretly contains harmful code to breach security.
• **Spyware Definition:** Secretive software installed without consent that tracks user keystrokes, browsing habits, and personal data to send to hackers.
• **Adware Introduction:** Software that automatically displays unwanted pop-up advertisements, slows down browsing, and redirects users to unsafe sites.
• **Safe Downloading Habits:** Never downloading files, games, or apps from untrusted, pirate, or unverified websites.
• **Email Attachment Dangers:** Avoiding opening email attachments from unknown senders, as they frequently conceal harmful malware scripts.
• **Software Updates Importance:** Regularly updating operating systems and applications to patch security vulnerabilities exploited by malware.
• **Antivirus Software Role:** Specialized protective software designed to detect, quarantine, and remove malware from computers and devices.
• **Real-Time Protection:** Keeping antivirus scans and shields turned on continuously to intercept threats before they infect the system.
• **Pop-Up Blocker Setup:** Configuring web browsers to block malicious pop-ups that attempt to trick users into clicking dangerous links.
• **Signs of Infection:** Recognizing symptoms of malware infection, such as sudden system slowness, frequent freezing, mysterious pop-ups, and missing files.
• **Teacher and IT Support:** Notifying school computer lab assistants or IT teachers immediately if a school PC behaves unusually.
• **Data Backup Protocol:** Maintaining regular backups of important school projects on secure external drives or school servers to prevent data loss.

• **Definition of Phishing:** A deceptive cybercrime technique where attackers disguise themselves as trustworthy entities to trick users into revealing sensitive data.
• **Common Phishing Channels:** Usually executed via fraudulent emails, fake text messages (smishing), or spoofed websites mimicking banks or schools.
• **Urgency Tactics:** Phishing messages often create false panic (e.g., "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!") to rush users into making careless mistakes.
• **Fake Login Links:** Directing users to lookalike websites designed to harvest usernames, passwords, and personal identification numbers.
• **Generic Greetings:** Spotting phishing emails that use vague greetings like "Dear User" instead of your actual name, indicating mass-sent scams.
• **Spelling and Grammar Errors:** Many phishing messages contain obvious spelling mistakes, poor grammar, and awkward phrasing that expose their fraudulent nature.
• **Too Good to Be True Offers:** Scam messages promising free smartphones, lottery winnings, or vacations designed to lure greedy or unsuspecting victims.
• **Verification Rule:** Never clicking links in unexpected emails claiming to be from official organizations; instead, navigate to the official website directly.
• **Checking Sender Addresses:** Inspecting the actual email address of the sender rather than just the display name to spot fake domains.
• **Definition of Scams:** Fraudulent schemes designed to trick people out of money, personal information, or digital assets through false promises.
• **Online Shopping Safety:** Ensuring online stores are legitimate, secure (HTTPS), and approved by parents before making any digital purchases.
• **Free Download Traps:** Avoiding sites offering "free" paid software, movie downloads, or game hacks, which are often breeding grounds for malware and scams.
• **Reporting Phishing:** Using built-in email tools to report phishing attempts and marking suspicious messages as spam.
• **Parental Oversight for Financials:** Ensuring children never input credit card numbers, bank details, or payment codes online without direct parental presence.
• **Awareness as Armor:** Knowing that skepticism and critical thinking are the best shields against online deception and phishing tricks.

• **Definition of Screen Time:** The total amount of time spent staring at digital screens, including computers, tablets, smartphones, televisions, and gaming consoles.
• **Balance in Daily Life:** Maintaining a healthy equilibrium between online digital activities and offline physical, social, and creative pursuits.
• **Physical Health Impacts:** Excessive screen time can lead to digital eye strain, blurred vision, headaches, neck pain, and poor posture (tech neck).
• **The 20-20-20 Rule:** A helpful vision-protection habit: every 20 minutes, look at an object at least 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds to rest eyes.
• **Sleep Disruption:** Staring at screens emitting blue light right before bedtime interferes with melatonin production, causing insomnia and fatigue.
• **Sedentary Lifestyle Risks:** Spending too many hours immobile in front of devices reduces physical exercise, contributing to weight gain and lethargy.
• **Mental Well-being:** Overuse of social media and gaming can lead to mood swings, anxiety, FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out), and reduced attention spans.
• **Setting Time Limits:** Establishing clear, daily screen time limits in agreement with parents for recreational browsing, gaming, and social media.
• **Screen-Free Zones:** Designating specific areas in the home, such as the dining table and bedrooms, as completely free of digital devices.
• **Bedtime Device Ban:** Turning off and removing all screens at least one hour before bedtime to ensure deep, restorative sleep.
• **Outdoor Play Priority:** Balancing screen activities with daily outdoor sports, cycling, reading physical books, and spending time with family.
• **Mindful Consumption:** Choosing high-quality educational videos, interactive learning apps, and creative digital tools over mindless scrolling.
• **Multitasking Myth:** Understanding that switching rapidly between homework, chats, and videos reduces productivity and concentration quality.
• **Ergonomics Basics:** Sitting upright with feet flat on the floor, screen at eye level, and elbows bent at 90 degrees while using computers.
• **Self-Regulation Skills:** Developing personal discipline to close games and apps when allocated time expires without needing parental reminders.

• **Definition of Copyright:** A legal right that grants creators and authors exclusive control over their original works, preventing unauthorized copying or distribution.
• **What Copyright Protects:** Applies to books, music, artwork, photographs, software code, videos, and website content created by individuals or companies.
• **Fair Use Concept:** Permitted, limited use of copyrighted material for educational purposes, criticism, or research without needing prior permission.
• **Public Domain:** Creative works whose copyrights have expired or been waived, making them freely available for anyone to use legally.
• **Creative Commons:** A flexible licensing system that allows creators to share their work publicly with specific permissions (e.g., free for educational use).
• **Plagiarism Definition:** The unethical act of stealing someone else's words, ideas, or creations and presenting them as your own original work.
• **Academic Integrity:** The DAV value of honesty in schoolwork, ensuring all sources used in projects are properly researched and referenced.
• **Image Rights:** Understanding that finding a picture on Google Images does not mean it is free to copy and publish without checking its license.
• **Citing Sources Properly:** Including author names, website URLs, and publication dates in bibliographies for school research reports.
• **Using Open Source Content:** Actively seeking out royalty-free images, music, and templates specifically licensed for student and educational projects.
• **Respecting Digital Creators:** Acknowledging the time, effort, and talent artists and writers invest in producing digital content.
• **Avoiding Illegal Downloads:** Never downloading copyrighted movies, games, or songs from pirate sites, which is both illegal and unsafe.
• **Parody and Transformation:** Understanding that modifying someone else's work slightly does not eliminate copyright violations unless categorized as fair use.
• **Watermarks and Attribution:** Recognizing digital watermarks on photos and honoring the photographer's request for credit.
• **Reporting Intellectual Theft:** Knowing how to report stolen content or copyright violations found on school portals or public platforms.

• **Emergency Action Plan:** Knowing the exact steps to take immediately when encountering cyber threats, scary content, or malicious attacks online.
• **The Golden Rule of Distress:** Never panic, never reply to threats, and never hide dangerous online incidents out of fear of punishment.
• **Screen Capturing Evidence:** Taking screenshots or photos of suspicious messages, harmful chats, or inappropriate images immediately before closing the window.
• **Closing the Browser:** Quickly closing browser tabs or shutting down the computer screen if confronted with shocking or explicit pop-ups.
• **Involving Parents First:** Immediately reporting cyber incidents to parents or guardians who can provide emotional support and handle external reporting.
• **Involving Teachers and Principals:** Informing school computer teachers, class teachers, or administrators if cyberbullying involves schoolmates or platform issues.
• **Contacting Law Enforcement:** Understanding that severe cybercrimes, identity theft, and severe threats require reporting to cyber cell police authorities.
• **Cyber Crime Reporting Portals:** Awareness of official government channels and helplines established for reporting cybercrimes safely.
• **Blocking and Unfriending:** Immediately using built-in account settings to block harassers, report spam accounts, and remove toxic connections.
• **Changing Compromised Credentials:** Instantly updating passwords and security settings if an account has been accessed or hacked by unauthorized users.
• **Emotional Resilience:** Remembering that victims of cyberbullying or scams are never at fault and that help is always available.
• **Digital Detox After Incidents:** Taking a break from screens and computers to calm down and talk through experiences with supportive family members.
• **Documenting Incidents:** Maintaining a written log of dates, times, and descriptions of recurring online harassment to assist investigations.
• **Helping Friends in Need:** Applying safety knowledge to help classmates who come forward with online problems or bullying experiences.
• **Promoting a Safe Culture:** Acting as a safety ambassador in DAV classrooms by sharing safe surfing ethics and reporting tips with peers.
