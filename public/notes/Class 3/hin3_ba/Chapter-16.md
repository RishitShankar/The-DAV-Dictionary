• **Interrogative Pronouns (प्रश्नवाचक सर्वनाम)**: These are words used to ask questions, such as 'कौन' (who) and 'क्या' (what).
• **Usage of 'कौन'**: This term is used specifically when inquiring about a person or a living being.
• **Usage of 'क्या'**: This term is used when inquiring about an object, an action, or a situation.
• **Usage of 'कहाँ'**: This term is essential for asking about the location or place of an object or person.
• **Usage of 'कब'**: This term is utilized to inquire about the time or the schedule of an event.
• **Usage of 'कैसे'**: This term is employed to understand the method, process, or condition of a task.
• **Usage of 'क्यों'**: This term is used to seek the reason or the cause behind an occurrence.
• **Sentence Formation**: The chapter teaches that the position of the question word can vary depending on the emphasis required in the sentence.
• **Direct Questions**: These are questions that can be answered with a simple 'yes' or 'no', often starting with 'क्या'.
• **Descriptive Questions**: These are questions that require a detailed explanation, usually involving 'कैसे' or 'क्यों'.
• **Grammar Rule**: Every question must begin with a capital letter (in English context) or follow standard Hindi sentence structure rules.
• **Punctuation Rule**: The 'पूर्ण विराम' (full stop - ।) is replaced by the 'प्रश्नवाचक चिह्न' (question mark - ?) in interrogative sentences.
• **Subject Identification**: The chapter helps students identify the 'कर्ता' (subject) who is performing the action in the question.
• **Object Identification**: It helps students identify the 'कर्म' (object) upon which the action is being performed.
• **Refining Speech**: By practicing these structures, students learn to frame their own queries more clearly and politely.

• **Grammar Focus - 'क्या'**: Used for asking about things or actions. Example: "तुम क्या खा रहे हो?" (What are you eating?).
• **Grammar Focus - 'कौन'**: Used for identifying a person. Example: "बाहर कौन खड़ा है?" (Who is standing outside?).
• **Grammar Focus - 'कहाँ'**: Used for identifying place. Example: "तुम्हारी किताब कहाँ है?" (Where is your book?).
• **Grammar Focus - 'कब'**: Used for identifying time. Example: "तुम स्कूल कब जाओगे?" (When will you go to school?).
• **Grammar Focus - 'कैसे'**: Used for identifying manner. Example: "तुम कैसे आए?" (How did you come?).
• **Grammar Focus - 'क्यों'**: Used for identifying reason. Example: "तुम क्यों रो रहे हो?" (Why are you crying?).
• **Sentence Structure - Subject-First**: In many Hindi questions, the subject comes first, followed by the question word.
• **Sentence Structure - Question-First**: Sometimes the question word is placed at the beginning for emphasis.
• **Punctuation - The Question Mark**: The '?' symbol is the universal indicator of a query in written Hindi.
• **Common Mistakes**: The chapter warns against using the wrong question word for a specific context (e.g., using 'क्यों' instead of 'कहाँ').

• **Concept: The Question Word (प्रश्नवाचक शब्द)**: These are the building blocks of any inquiry.
• **Concept: The Sentence Frame**: The structure `[Subject] + [Object] + [Question Word] + [Verb] + [?]`.
• **Concept: The 'क्या' at the start**: When 'क्या' is at the start, it turns the whole sentence into a Yes/No question.
• **Concept: The 'क्या' in the middle**: When 'क्या' is in the middle, it asks for specific information about an object.
• **Concept: The 'कौन' usage**: Always used with the verb in the singular or plural depending on the subject.
• **Concept: The 'कहाँ' usage**: Often used with verbs of motion like 'जाना' (to go) or 'आना' (to come).
• **Concept: The 'कब' usage**: Used with time markers like 'कल' (yesterday/tomorrow) or 'आज' (today).
• **Concept: The 'कैसे' usage**: Used to describe the state of being or the method of doing something.
• **Concept: The 'क्यों' usage**: Used to seek the reason behind a state or an action.
• **Concept: Punctuation Mastery**: The '?' must be placed immediately after the last word of the sentence.
• **Concept: Avoiding Redundancy**: Do not use two question words in the same sentence unless necessary.
• **Concept: Clarity of Thought**: A good question is one that is clear and focused on a specific piece of information.
• **Concept: The 'Savali' Mindset**: A mindset that refuses to accept things at face value without understanding them.
• **Concept: Classroom Etiquette**: While asking questions is good, it should be done at the appropriate time.
• **Concept: Reflective Practice**: Students are encouraged to reflect on their own questions during the day.
