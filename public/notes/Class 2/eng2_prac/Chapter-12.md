• **Chapter Theme:** The chapter "When I was a Baby..." focuses on the concept of growth, developmental milestones, and the passage of time from infancy to childhood.
• **Core Concept:** Growth is a natural, continuous process that every living being experiences as they transition through different life stages.
• **Narrative Perspective:** The chapter is written from the perspective of a young child reflecting on their past self, highlighting the contrast between being a baby and being a school-going child.
• **Key Vocabulary - Infancy:** The state of being a baby, characterized by total dependence on caregivers for basic needs like feeding and movement.
• **Key Vocabulary - Growth:** The physical and mental development that occurs as a child matures, leading to increased independence and capability.
• **Developmental Milestone - Mobility:** Babies start by crawling, then progress to standing, and eventually walking, which is a major shift in physical autonomy.
• **Developmental Milestone - Communication:** The transition from crying and babbling to forming single words and eventually complete sentences.
• **Developmental Milestone - Feeding:** The shift from liquid nutrition (milk) to solid foods as the digestive system matures and teeth begin to emerge.
• **Developmental Milestone - Motor Skills:** The development of fine motor skills, such as grasping objects, holding a spoon, and eventually writing or drawing.
• **Comparison Mechanism:** The chapter uses a comparative structure to contrast the limitations of infancy with the expanded abilities of a Class 2 student.
• **Emotional Connection:** The chapter encourages students to appreciate the care provided by parents and guardians during their early, vulnerable years.
• **Self-Awareness:** By reflecting on their past, students develop a sense of personal history and identity.
• **Physical Changes:** The chapter touches upon how physical size, strength, and coordination improve significantly over time.
• **Cognitive Development:** The shift from simple sensory experiences to complex learning, such as recognizing family members and understanding basic instructions.
• **The Concept of 'Then vs. Now':** A fundamental analytical tool used in the chapter to categorize behaviors and abilities into two distinct timeframes.

• **Infant Characteristics:** Babies are physically small, have soft skin, and require constant supervision for safety.
• **The Role of Caregivers:** Parents and family members act as the primary support system, providing nourishment, shelter, and emotional security to the infant.
• **Sensory Development:** Infants explore the world primarily through touch, taste, and sound before they develop the visual acuity of an older child.
• **The Concept of 'Dependency':** A baby cannot perform daily tasks like bathing, dressing, or eating independently, which is a key defining feature of infancy.
• **The Concept of 'Independence':** As a child reaches Class 2, they gain the ability to perform personal hygiene, dress themselves, and manage school tasks.
• **Language Acquisition:** The process begins with 'cooing' and 'babbling,' which are the foundational sounds that eventually evolve into vocabulary.
• **Teething Process:** The emergence of milk teeth is a significant physical milestone that allows the transition to a more varied diet.
• **Sleep Patterns:** Infants require significantly more sleep than school-aged children to support rapid brain and body development.
• **Emotional Expression:** Babies express needs primarily through crying, whereas older children learn to use words to express feelings like happiness, sadness, or frustration.
• **Social Interaction:** Infants interact mainly with immediate family, while older children expand their social circle to include teachers and classmates.
• **Motor Coordination:** The transition from uncoordinated movements to purposeful actions like picking up a toy or turning a page in a book.
• **The Importance of Play:** Play in infancy is sensory-based, while play in childhood becomes rule-based and collaborative.
• **Memory Formation:** As children grow, they begin to form lasting memories, whereas infancy is often a period that is remembered through stories told by parents.
• **Physical Stature:** The rapid increase in height and weight during the first few years of life is a hallmark of human growth.
• **The Concept of 'Change':** The chapter emphasizes that change is constant and that every stage of life has its own unique characteristics and joys.
