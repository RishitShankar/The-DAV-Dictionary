**Chapter 14: Living and Non-Living Things, Plants & Seasons**

---

•   **Chapter Introduction:** This chapter helps us understand the wonderful world around us by teaching us about different kinds of things we see every day.
•   **Main Topics:** We will learn about things that are alive, things that are not alive, special living things called plants, and the different types of weather we experience throughout the year, called seasons.
•   **Key Concept 1: Living Things:** These are things that can do many special actions all by themselves, making them different from other things.
•   **Characteristic 1: Growth:** Living things grow, which means they get bigger and change over time, just like you grow taller every year.
•   **Example of Growth:** A tiny baby grows into a big child, then an adult. A small puppy grows into a large dog. A tiny seed grows into a big plant.
•   **Characteristic 2: Movement:** Living things can move from one place to another on their own, or move parts of their body.
•   **Example of Movement:** Birds fly, fish swim, children run and play, and even plants can turn their leaves towards the sun.
•   **Characteristic 3: Eating:** Living things need food to get energy to grow, move, and stay healthy.
•   **Example of Eating:** Humans eat food like fruits, vegetables, and bread. Animals eat grass, other animals, or special animal food.
•   **Characteristic 4: Breathing:** Living things need to breathe air to live. They take in air and let it out.
•   **Example of Breathing:** We breathe in and out all the time. Animals also breathe, though sometimes we can't see it clearly.
•   **Characteristic 5: Reproduction:** Living things can make more of their own kind, like parents having children.
•   **Example of Reproduction:** A hen lays eggs from which chicks hatch. A mother cat has kittens. A plant produces seeds that can grow into new plants.
•   **Characteristic 6: Feeling:** Living things can feel things like pain, happiness, cold, or warmth.
•   **Example of Feeling:** If you touch something hot, you feel it. Animals react if they are happy or scared.
•   **Summary of Living Things:** They are active and change. They need food, water, and air to survive and can make more of themselves.
•   **Examples of Living Things:** Humans (like you, your family), Animals (like dogs, cats, birds, fish), and Plants (like trees, flowers, grass).
•   **Important Note:** Even though plants don't move around like animals, they are still living things because they grow, need food (from soil and sun), breathe, and can make new plants.

•   **Recap: What makes something a Living Thing?** It can grow, move on its own, eat, breathe, have babies, and feel.
•   **Humans as Living Things:** We are living things because we do all these actions: we grow, walk, eat, breathe, feel, and grow up to have families.
•   **Animals as Living Things:** All animals, from tiny insects to huge elephants, are living things because they grow, move, eat, breathe, and can have babies.
•   **Plants as Living Things (Special Case):** Plants are also living things. They grow from seeds, make their own food using sunlight, breathe air, and produce seeds or fruits to make new plants.
•   **Key Concept 2: Non-Living Things:** These are things that cannot do any of the special actions that living things can do. They are not alive.
•   **Characteristic 1: No Growth:** Non-living things do not grow bigger or change their size on their own.
•   **Example of No Growth:** A toy car will always stay the same size unless someone breaks it or adds parts to it. A rock does not grow.
•   **Characteristic 2: No Movement (on their own):** Non-living things cannot move from one place to another by themselves. They need someone or something to move them.
•   **Example of No Movement:** Your school bag cannot walk to school by itself. A chair stays in one place until you move it.
•   **Characteristic 3: No Eating:** Non-living things do not need food or water to survive.
•   **Example of No Eating:** A book does not get hungry. A pencil does not need water.
•   **Characteristic 4: No Breathing:** Non-living things do not breathe air.
•   **Example of No Breathing:** A table does not have lungs or a nose to breathe.
•   **Characteristic 5: No Reproduction:** Non-living things cannot make more of their own kind.
•   **Example of No Reproduction:** A toy doll cannot have baby dolls. A stone cannot produce more stones.
•   **Characteristic 6: No Feeling:** Non-living things cannot feel anything. They don't feel happy, sad, hot, or cold.
•   **Example of No Feeling:** If you drop a ball, it doesn't hurt the ball. A wall doesn't feel the rain.
•   **Summary of Non-Living Things:** They are inactive and do not change on their own. They do not need food, water, or air, and cannot make more of themselves.
•   **Examples of Non-Living Things:** Toys, furniture (tables, chairs), clothes, books, stones, cars, water, air, sunlight.
•   **Important Distinction:** Some non-living things, like cars or clocks, might move or make sounds, but they do so because a living thing (like a person driving) or a power source (like a battery) makes them work. They don't do it on their own.

•   **Understanding the Difference:** It's important to know if something is living or non-living because it helps us understand how the world works and how to care for living things.
•   **Activity Idea (Mental):** Look around your room. Can you point to something living? Can you point to something non-living?
•   **Example 1: A Pet Dog:** Is it living or non-living? It's living because it grows, eats, breathes, moves, and feels.
•   **Example 2: A Bicycle:** Is it living or non-living? It's non-living because it doesn't grow, eat, breathe, or move unless someone pedals it.
•   **Example 3: A Tree in the Park:** Is it living or non-living? It's living because it grows, makes its own food, breathes, and produces seeds.
•   **Example 4: A Stone on the Ground:** Is it living or non-living? It's non-living because it doesn't grow, eat, breathe, or move on its own.
•   **Key Concept 3: Plants:** Plants are very special and important living things that are all around us.
•   **Plants are Unique:** Unlike animals, most plants stay in one place, rooted in the ground. They don't run or fly.
•   **How Plants Get Food:** Plants are amazing because they can make their own food using sunlight, water, and air. This process is called photosynthesis (a big word for later, but know they make their own food!).
•   **What Plants Need to Grow:** Just like us, plants need certain things to grow big and strong.
•   **Plant Need 1: Sunlight:** Plants need sunlight to make their food. That's why they often grow towards the sun.
•   **Plant Need 2: Water:** Plants need water to drink, which they usually get from the soil through their roots.
•   **Plant Need 3: Air:** Plants need air, specifically a gas called carbon dioxide, to help them make food. They also give out oxygen, which we breathe.
•   **Plant Need 4: Soil:** Most plants need soil to hold them upright and to get water and nutrients (food from the earth).
•   **Caring for Plants:** Because plants are living things, we need to care for them by giving them enough water and sunlight.
•   **Types of Plants:** There are many different kinds of plants, like big trees, small bushes, colorful flowers, and green grass.
•   **Importance of Plants:** Plants are essential for all life on Earth. They give us food, clean air, and many other useful things.

•   **Parts of a Plant:** Just like our body has different parts (head, arms, legs), a plant also has different parts, each with a special job.
•   **Plant Part 1: Roots:** These are usually hidden underground. Roots are like anchors that hold the plant firmly in the soil.
•   **Function of Roots:** Roots also soak up water and nutrients (food) from the soil, which the plant needs to grow.
•   **Plant Part 2: Stem:** This is the main body of the plant that grows above the ground. It's like the plant's trunk or backbone.
•   **Function of Stem:** The stem holds the plant upright and carries water and nutrients from the roots up to the leaves and flowers.
•   **Plant Part 3: Leaves:** These are usually green and flat, growing from the stem or branches.
•   **Function of Leaves:** Leaves are like the plant's food factories! They use sunlight, water, and air to make food for the plant. They also help the plant breathe.
•   **Plant Part 4: Flowers:** These are often colorful and fragrant parts of the plant. Not all plants have big, showy flowers, but many do.
•   **Function of Flowers:** Flowers are important for making seeds, which can grow into new plants. They also attract insects like bees.
•   **Plant Part 5: Fruits:** After a flower is pollinated, it can turn into a fruit. Fruits often contain seeds.
•   **Function of Fruits:** Fruits protect the seeds inside them and help in spreading the seeds to grow new plants. Many fruits are also food for humans and animals.
•   **Example of Plant Parts:** Think of an apple tree: it has roots underground, a strong stem (trunk), green leaves, beautiful blossoms (flowers), and delicious apples (fruits) with seeds inside.
•   **Life Cycle of a Plant (Simple):** A tiny seed grows into a seedling, then a young plant, then a mature plant with flowers and fruits, which then produce more seeds.
•   **Plants Give Us Oxygen:** One of the most important things plants do is give us oxygen, the air we breathe to live. They take in a gas we don't need (carbon dioxide) and give out oxygen.
•   **Plants as Food:** Many parts of plants are food for us: we eat fruits (apples, bananas), vegetables (carrots, spinach, potatoes), and grains (rice, wheat).
•   **Plants for Shelter:** Big trees provide shelter for animals and sometimes even for people. Wood from trees is used to build houses.
•   **Plants for Clothes:** Some plants, like cotton, give us fibers to make clothes.

•   **Other Uses of Plants:** Plants are also used to make medicines, paper, furniture, and many other useful things.
•   **Respecting Plants:** Since plants are living things and so useful, we should always be kind to them, not pluck their leaves or flowers unnecessarily.
•   **Key Concept 4: Seasons:** The weather around us changes throughout the year. These different periods of weather are called seasons.
•   **Why Seasons Change:** The Earth moves around the sun, and this movement, combined with the tilt of the Earth, causes different parts of the world to get more or less sunlight at different times, leading to seasons. (Simplified for UKG: The weather changes over time).
•   **Main Seasons:** In many parts of the world, we experience four main seasons: Summer, Winter, Spring, and Autumn (or Fall). Some places also have a distinct Monsoon or Rainy Season.
•   **Season 1: Summer:** This is usually the hottest season of the year.
•   **Summer Weather:** Days are long and sunny, and the weather is very warm or hot.
•   **What We Wear in Summer:** We wear light clothes made of cotton, like t-shirts, shorts, and dresses, to stay cool.
•   **Summer Activities:** People enjoy going to the beach, swimming in pools, eating ice cream, and drinking cold juices to beat the heat.
•   **Summer Plants:** Some plants bloom in summer, and many fruits like mangoes and watermelons are ripe during this time.
•   **Summer Animals:** Many animals are active during summer, and some birds migrate to cooler places.
•   **Feeling in Summer:** We often feel thirsty and want to stay in cool places or under a fan/AC.
•   **Sun's Role in Summer:** The sun shines very brightly and for a longer time each day during summer.
•   **Summer Fun:** It's a time for holidays and outdoor play, but remember to stay hydrated and protected from the sun.
•   **Transition to Next Season:** After summer, the weather usually starts to get a little cooler, leading into the rainy season or autumn.
•   **Importance of Seasons:** Seasons are important because they bring different types of weather, which affect plants, animals, and how we live our lives.
•   **Learning About Seasons:** Understanding seasons helps us know what clothes to wear, what activities to do, and what changes to expect in nature.

---PAGE-BREAK---

•   **Season 2: Winter:** This is usually the coldest season of the year.
•   **Winter Weather:** Days are shorter, and the weather is cold, sometimes very cold. In some places, it even snows!
•   **What We Wear in Winter:** We wear warm clothes to keep ourselves cozy, like sweaters, jackets, caps, gloves, and scarves.
•   **Winter Activities:** People enjoy drinking hot chocolate, sitting by a fire, and playing in the snow (if it snows where they live).
•   **Winter Plants:** Many trees lose their leaves in winter. Some plants go dormant (sleep) to protect themselves from the cold.
•   **Winter Animals:** Some animals hibernate (sleep for a long time) during winter, while others grow thicker fur to stay warm.
•   **Feeling in Winter:** We often feel chilly and want to stay indoors or wrap ourselves in blankets.
•   **Sun's Role in Winter:** The sun shines for a shorter time each day, and its rays feel less warm.
•   **Winter Fun:** It's a time for cozy indoor activities, reading stories, and celebrating festivals like Christmas.
•   **Transition from Winter:** As winter ends, the weather slowly starts to get warmer, leading into spring.
•   **Season 3: Monsoon / Rainy Season:** This season is characterized by a lot of rain.
•   **Rainy Season Weather:** The sky is often cloudy, and it rains frequently, sometimes very heavily.
•   **What We Wear in Rainy Season:** We use raincoats and umbrellas to stay dry when we go outside. We also wear waterproof shoes.
•   **Rainy Season Activities:** Children love to splash in puddles, but it's important to stay safe and dry.
•   **Rainy Season Plants:** The rain makes everything look fresh and green. Plants grow very well during this season.
•   **Rainy Season Animals:** Earthworms come out, and frogs are often heard croaking.
•   **Feeling in Rainy Season:** The air feels cool and fresh after the rain, but it can also be humid.
•   **Importance of Rain:** Rain is very important for plants to grow and for filling up rivers and lakes. It gives us water to drink.
•   **Safety in Rain:** It's good to stay indoors during heavy rain and avoid playing in dirty water.

---PAGE-BREAK---

•   **Season 4: Spring:** This is a very pleasant season that comes after winter and before summer.
•   **Spring Weather:** The weather is mild and comfortable – not too hot and not too cold. Days start to get longer.
•   **What We Wear in Spring:** We can wear lighter clothes than in winter, but still might need a light jacket or sweater in the mornings or evenings.
•   **Spring Activities:** It's a great time to play outdoors, go for walks, and enjoy the fresh air.
•   **Spring Plants:** Spring is known as the season of new life. Trees grow new green leaves, and many beautiful flowers start to bloom everywhere.
•   **Spring Animals:** Birds start building nests and laying eggs. Animals that hibernated wake up.
•   **Feeling in Spring:** We often feel happy and energetic because of the pleasant weather and the beauty of nature.
•   **Sun's Role in Spring:** The sun starts to feel warmer, and there is more sunshine each day.
•   **Spring Fun:** It's a wonderful time to visit gardens and parks to see all the colorful flowers.
•   **Transition from Spring:** As spring progresses, the weather gets warmer and warmer, leading into summer.
•   **Season 5: Autumn / Fall:** This season comes after summer and before winter.
•   **Autumn Weather:** The weather starts to get cooler than summer, but it's not yet as cold as winter. Days get shorter.
•   **What We Wear in Autumn:** We start wearing warmer clothes like light sweaters and long-sleeved shirts as the temperature drops.
•   **Autumn Activities:** It's a good time for outdoor activities like collecting colorful leaves.
•   **Autumn Plants:** Autumn is famous for its beautiful changing leaves. Green leaves turn into shades of red, orange, and yellow before falling off the trees.
•   **Autumn Animals:** Many animals prepare for winter by gathering food or migrating to warmer places.
•   **Feeling in Autumn:** The air feels crisp and cool, and there's a special scent of falling leaves.
•   **Sun's Role in Autumn:** The sun is still visible, but its warmth is less intense than in summer.
•   **Autumn Fun:** It's a time for harvest festivals and enjoying the beautiful colors of nature.
•   **Transition from Autumn:** As autumn ends, the weather becomes colder, and we move into winter.

•   **Review of Seasons:** Each season has its own special weather, types of clothes we wear, and changes we see in plants and animals.
•   **Why Seasons Matter:** Seasons help plants grow, provide different foods at different times, and make our world interesting and varied.
•   **Connecting Concepts: Living Things and Seasons:** Living things like plants and animals are greatly affected by the changing seasons.
•   **Plants and Seasons:** Plants grow and bloom in spring and summer, shed leaves in autumn, and rest in winter.
•   **Animals and Seasons:** Animals adapt to seasons by changing their fur, hibernating, or migrating to find food and warmth.
•   **Humans and Seasons:** We also adapt by wearing different clothes, eating different foods, and doing different activities depending on the season.
•   **Connecting Concepts: Non-Living Things and Seasons:** Non-living things, like rocks or tables, do not change because of seasons, but they can be affected by the weather (e.g., a road might get wet in the rain).
•   **Our Environment:** Everything around us, both living and non-living, makes up our environment.
•   **Caring for Our Environment:** It's important to take care of all living things (plants, animals, people) and keep our surroundings clean, whether it's summer, winter, or rainy season.
•   **Observation Skills:** This chapter encourages us to observe and notice the small details in our world – how a plant grows, how a bird flies, or how the weather changes.
•   **Appreciation for Nature:** By learning about living things, plants, and seasons, we learn to appreciate the beauty and wonder of nature.
•   **Continuous Learning:** The world is full of amazing things to discover, and this chapter is just the beginning of understanding how everything fits together.
•   **Key Takeaway 1:** Living things grow, move, eat, breathe, reproduce, and feel.
•   **Key Takeaway 2:** Non-living things cannot do these actions on their own.
•   **Key Takeaway 3:** Plants are living things that make their own food and give us oxygen and food.
•   **Key Takeaway 4:** Seasons are different periods of weather that affect living things and our daily lives.

•   **Deep Dive: The Importance of Water for Living Things:** All living things, including humans, animals, and plants, need water to survive.
•   **Water for Humans and Animals:** We drink water to stay hydrated and healthy. Water helps our bodies work properly.
•   **Water for Plants:** Plants absorb water through their roots, which helps them transport nutrients and make food in their leaves.
•   **Deep Dive: The Importance of Air for Living Things:** Air is another essential non-living thing that living things need.
•   **Air for Breathing:** Humans and animals breathe in oxygen from the air to live.
•   **Air for Plants:** Plants use carbon dioxide from the air to make their food, and then release oxygen back into the air for us.
•   **Interdependence:** This shows how living things depend on non-living things (like water and air) and how living things (like plants) help other living things (like humans and animals) by providing oxygen.
•   **The Sun's Role:** The sun is a non-living thing, but it provides light and warmth, which are crucial for all living things.
•   **Sunlight for Plants:** Plants use sunlight to make their food. Without the sun, plants cannot grow.
•   **Sunlight for Warmth:** The sun keeps the Earth warm enough for living things to survive, and its warmth changes with the seasons.
•   **Natural vs. Man-Made Non-Living Things:**
    •   **Natural Non-Living Things:** Things found in nature that are not alive, like rocks, water, air, soil, and mountains.
    •   **Man-Made Non-Living Things:** Things created by humans, like toys, books, cars, houses, and clothes.
•   **Observing Changes in Nature:** Pay attention to how the trees change their leaves, how the flowers bloom, and how the animals behave as seasons change.
•   **Rhymes and Songs:** Many rhymes and songs help UKG children remember the characteristics of living and non-living things, and the names of the seasons.
