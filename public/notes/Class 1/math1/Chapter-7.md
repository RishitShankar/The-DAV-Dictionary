• **Concept of Addition**: Addition is defined as the process of calculating the total of two or more numbers or amounts.
• **The Plus Sign**: The symbol '+' is known as the plus sign, which represents the operation of addition.
• **The Equals Sign**: The symbol '=' is known as the equals sign, which denotes the result or the sum of the addition.
• **Sum**: The result obtained after adding two or more numbers together is technically referred to as the 'sum'.
• **Counting Forward**: Addition can be visualized as counting forward on a number line to find the total value.
• **Combining Objects**: Addition is fundamentally the act of putting different groups of objects together to form a larger group.
• **Increasing Value**: When we add numbers, the total value always increases compared to the individual addends.
• **Addends**: The numbers that are being added together in an addition equation are called addends.
• **Total**: The final count of all items combined is known as the total or the aggregate.
• **Visual Representation**: Using pictures of objects like apples, balls, or stars helps in understanding the basic concept of 'putting together'.
• **One-Digit Addition**: The foundational level of addition involves adding single-digit numbers ranging from 0 to 9.
• **Zero Property of Addition**: Adding zero to any number results in the number itself, as zero represents an empty set.
• **Commutative Property**: Changing the order of the addends does not change the sum (e.g., 2 + 3 is the same as 3 + 2).
• **Counting All**: A primary method for beginners is to count all the objects in two separate groups to find the total.
• **Mental Math**: As students progress, they learn to add small numbers mentally without the need for physical objects.

• **Concept of Subtraction**: Subtraction is the process of taking one number away from another or finding the difference between two numbers.
• **The Minus Sign**: The symbol '-' is known as the minus sign, which indicates the operation of subtraction.
• **Difference**: The result obtained after subtracting one number from another is called the 'difference'.
• **Taking Away**: Subtraction is often described as 'taking away' a certain number of items from a larger group.
• **Decreasing Value**: When we subtract, the total value always decreases compared to the original starting number.
• **Counting Backward**: Subtraction can be visualized as counting backward on a number line to find the remaining amount.
• **Minuend**: The starting number from which another number is subtracted is known as the minuend.
• **Subtrahend**: The number that is being taken away or subtracted from the minuend is called the subtrahend.
• **Remaining Objects**: After removing a specific quantity, the objects left behind represent the difference.
• **Comparison**: Subtraction is used to compare two groups to see which one is larger and by how much.
• **One-Digit Subtraction**: The foundational level of subtraction involves removing a single-digit number from another single-digit number.
• **Zero Property of Subtraction**: Subtracting zero from any number leaves the number unchanged, as nothing is being taken away.
• **Self-Subtraction**: Subtracting a number from itself always results in zero, as the entire group is removed.
• **Number Line Usage**: A number line is a horizontal line with numbers marked at equal intervals used to perform subtraction by jumping backward.
• **Real-World Application**: Subtraction is used in daily life to determine how many items are left after some have been used or given away.

• **Addition with Pictures**: Using illustrations of fruits or toys allows students to physically count the total number of items in two distinct sets.
• **Horizontal Addition**: Writing addition problems in a line (e.g., 4 + 2 = 6) is known as horizontal addition.
• **Vertical Addition**: Writing addition problems in a column (e.g., 4 above 2) is known as vertical addition, which helps in aligning digits.
• **Number Line Addition**: Placing a finger on the first number and jumping forward by the second number helps find the sum.

• **Grouping**: Organizing objects into groups of tens and ones makes addition easier as students advance to larger numbers.
• **Addition Table**: Learning basic addition facts (like 1+1, 2+2) helps in building speed and accuracy in calculations.
• **Pattern Recognition**: Observing that adding 1 to a number always gives the 'next' number in the counting sequence.
• **Summation**: The act of finding the sum is a cumulative process that builds on previous counting skills.
• **Totaling**: Totaling is the process of gathering all units into one single collection to determine the final count.
• **Addition Vocabulary**: Terms like 'more', 'plus', 'altogether', and 'total' are key indicators that an addition operation is required.
• **Problem Solving**: Identifying the need for addition in word problems by looking for keywords like 'how many in all'.
• **Accuracy**: Checking the sum by counting the objects again ensures that the addition was performed correctly.
• **Consistency**: Practicing addition daily helps in memorizing basic number bonds for faster mental retrieval.
• **Logical Sequence**: Addition is taught before subtraction because it is the inverse operation and builds the base for counting.

• **Horizontal Subtraction**: Writing subtraction problems in a line (e.g., 8 - 3 = 5) is known as horizontal subtraction.
• **Vertical Subtraction**: Writing subtraction problems in a column (e.g., 8 above 3) helps in organizing the digits for calculation.
• **Number Line Subtraction**: Starting at the minuend and jumping backward by the subtrahend identifies the difference.
• **Counting Backwards**: A mental strategy where one starts at the larger number and counts backward to find the result.
• **Subtraction Vocabulary**: Terms like 'less', 'minus', 'take away', 'left', and 'difference' indicate a subtraction operation.
• **Finding the Difference**: Subtraction helps in determining the gap between two different quantities.
• **Leftover Amount**: The objects remaining after the subtraction process are referred to as the 'leftovers'.
• **Inverse Relationship**: Subtraction is the inverse of addition; if 3 + 2 = 5, then 5 - 2 = 3.
• **Checking Subtraction**: One can verify a subtraction result by adding the difference to the subtrahend to see if it equals the minuend.
• **Subtraction Facts**: Memorizing basic subtraction facts (like 5 - 1 = 4) is essential for mathematical fluency.
• **Elimination**: Subtraction is essentially the elimination of a part of a set to see what remains.
• **Comparing Quantities**: Subtraction is the primary tool used to determine how many more or how many fewer items one group has compared to another.
• **Visualizing Loss**: Using physical manipulatives like blocks or beads helps students visualize the concept of 'loss' or 'removal'.
• **Precision**: Ensuring the correct number of items is removed is critical for obtaining the accurate difference.

• **Fact Families**: A group of related addition and subtraction equations that use the same three numbers (e.g., 2, 3, 5).
• **Number Bond**: A model that shows how numbers are split into parts or combined to form a whole.
• **Part-Part-Whole**: A concept where two parts are added to make a whole, and the whole is subtracted by a part to find the other part.
• **Whole**: The total amount in a number bond, which is the result of adding the parts.
• **Parts**: The individual components that, when added together, make up the whole.
• **Inverse Operations**: Addition and subtraction 'undo' each other, making them inverse operations.
• **Verification**: Using addition to check the answer of a subtraction problem is a standard mathematical verification technique.
• **Balanced Equations**: Understanding that an equation must be balanced on both sides of the equals sign.
• **Equation Structure**: Every equation consists of an expression on the left and the result on the right.
• **Mathematical Language**: Using the correct terminology (sum, difference, addend) improves mathematical communication.

• **Consistency in Logic**: Applying the same rules of addition and subtraction to any set of numbers, regardless of size.
