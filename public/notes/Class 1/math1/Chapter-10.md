• **Capacity**: The total amount of liquid a container can hold when it is completely full.
• **Liquid**: A state of matter that flows and takes the shape of the container it is poured into.
• **Container**: Any object, such as a bottle, bucket, cup, or glass, used to hold liquids.
• **Volume**: The space occupied by a liquid inside a container.
• **Full**: A state where a container holds the maximum amount of liquid possible.
• **Empty**: A state where a container holds no liquid at all.
• **Half-full**: A state where a container holds exactly half of its total capacity.
• **Comparison**: The process of determining which container holds more or less liquid than another.
• **Visual Estimation**: Looking at two containers to guess which one might hold more liquid based on size.
• **Size vs. Capacity**: Larger containers generally have a greater capacity than smaller containers.
• **Shape Influence**: Sometimes a tall, thin container may hold the same amount as a short, wide container.
• **Direct Observation**: The simplest way to understand capacity is by filling containers with water or sand.
• **Standard Units**: Units like Liters and Milliliters are used to measure capacity accurately in higher classes.
• **Non-Standard Units**: Using smaller objects like cups or spoons to measure how much a larger container holds.
• **Measurement Concept**: The number of smaller units required to fill a larger container defines its capacity.
• **Practical Application**: Understanding that a bucket holds more water than a mug.
• **Daily Life Context**: Identifying objects in the kitchen that hold liquids, such as jugs, bowls, and bottles.

• **Measurement by Pouring**: The act of transferring liquid from one container to another to compare levels.
• **Unit of Measurement**: A fixed quantity used as a standard for measuring capacity.
• **Cup as a Unit**: A common non-standard unit used to measure how many cups fill a jug.
• **Spoon as a Unit**: A smaller non-standard unit used for measuring very small amounts of liquid.
• **Bowl as a Unit**: A medium-sized non-standard unit used to measure the capacity of pots or pans.
• **Consistency**: To get an accurate measurement, the same size cup or spoon must be used throughout the process.
• **Filling Process**: Pouring liquid slowly into a container until it reaches the brim.
• **Brim**: The top edge of a container; filling to the brim means the container is at maximum capacity.
• **Overflow**: What happens when more liquid is added to a container that is already full.
• **Spillage**: The accidental loss of liquid while transferring it between containers.
• **Leveling**: Ensuring the liquid is flat and steady to get an accurate reading of the capacity.
• **Estimation Skill**: Developing the ability to predict which container will hold more before performing the measurement.
• **Relative Capacity**: Understanding that a teapot holds more than a teacup but less than a bucket.
• **Mathematical Thinking**: Using counting skills to record how many small units fill a large container.
• **Recording Data**: Writing down the number of units (e.g., "5 cups") to describe the capacity of a vessel.
• **Logical Sequence**: The order of steps: Choose a unit, fill the unit, pour into the container, and count.
• **Accuracy**: The importance of filling the measuring unit to the same level every single time.

• **Concept of 'More'**: A container has more capacity if it requires a higher number of units to be filled.
• **Concept of 'Less'**: A container has less capacity if it requires a smaller number of units to be filled.
• **Concept of 'Equal'**: Two containers have equal capacity if they require the exact same number of units to be filled.
• **Variable Containers**: Different shapes of containers can hold the same amount of liquid.
• **Width Factor**: A wide container might hold more than a narrow one, even if it is shorter.
• **Height Factor**: A tall container might hold more than a short one, even if it is thinner.
• **Water Displacement**: A method where an object is submerged to see how much water it pushes out, indicating its volume.
• **Sand vs. Water**: Both sand and water can be used to measure capacity, as both take the shape of the container.
• **Pouring Technique**: Holding the measuring unit steady to avoid spills and ensure accuracy.
• **Counting Accuracy**: Keeping track of the number of units poured without losing count.
• **Mental Math**: Predicting the capacity of a container based on its physical dimensions.
• **Comparison Logic**: If Container A holds 5 cups and Container B holds 8 cups, Container B has a larger capacity.
• **Ordering**: Arranging containers from the one with the least capacity to the one with the most capacity.
• **Sorting**: Grouping containers based on whether they hold "a lot" or "a little."
• **Observation**: Watching the liquid level rise as more units are added to the container.
• **Verification**: Re-measuring to confirm that the count of units is correct.
• **Standardization**: Recognizing that using different sized cups leads to different measurement results.
