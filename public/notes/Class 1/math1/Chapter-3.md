• **Concept of Addition**: Addition is the mathematical process of combining two or more groups of objects to find the total sum.
• **The Plus Sign (+)**: The symbol '+' is known as the plus sign, which represents the action of adding or joining quantities together.
• **The Equals Sign (=)**: The symbol '=' is known as the equals sign, which indicates that the values on both sides of the equation are the same.
• **Sum**: The result obtained after performing the operation of addition is technically referred to as the sum or total.
• **Counting Forward**: A primary method for addition is counting forward, where one starts at the first number and counts on by the value of the second number.
• **Addition with Zero**: Adding zero to any number results in the number itself, as zero represents an empty set or the absence of quantity.
• **Commutative Property**: The order of numbers in addition does not change the sum; for example, 2 + 3 is equal to 3 + 2.
• **Number Line Addition**: A number line is a visual tool where one jumps forward to the right to represent the increase in value during addition.
• **Horizontal Addition**: This involves writing the numbers side-by-side with the plus sign between them, such as 5 + 4 = 9.
• **Vertical Addition**: This involves stacking numbers on top of each other, aligning them by their place value, and placing the plus sign to the left.
• **Single-Digit Addition**: The foundation of the chapter involves adding two single-digit numbers where the sum remains between 0 and 9.
• **Double-Digit Sums**: The chapter extends to sums up to 20, requiring students to understand the transition from single-digit to two-digit numbers.
• **Concrete Objects**: Using physical items like beads, blocks, or fingers helps visualize the process of joining two distinct groups.
• **Mental Math**: Developing the ability to add small numbers mentally by visualizing the quantities without physical aids.
• **Total Count**: The ultimate goal of addition is to determine the final count of all objects combined into one single group.

• **Concept of Subtraction**: Subtraction is the mathematical process of taking away a specific quantity from a larger group to find what remains.
• **The Minus Sign (-)**: The symbol '-' is known as the minus sign, which represents the action of removing, decreasing, or finding the difference.
• **Difference**: The result obtained after performing the operation of subtraction is referred to as the difference.
• **Counting Backward**: A primary method for subtraction is counting backward, where one starts at the larger number and counts down by the value of the smaller number.
• **Subtraction with Zero**: Subtracting zero from any number leaves the number unchanged, as nothing is being removed from the original set.
• **Subtracting a Number from Itself**: When a number is subtracted from itself, the result is always zero, as the entire set is removed.
• **The Concept of 'Left'**: Subtraction is often taught using the language of 'how many are left' after some items are taken away or hidden.
• **Number Line Subtraction**: On a number line, subtraction is represented by jumping backward to the left, indicating a decrease in value.
• **Horizontal Subtraction**: This involves writing the equation in a line, such as 10 - 3 = 7, using the minus sign.
• **Vertical Subtraction**: This involves stacking the larger number on top and the smaller number below, aligning them by place value.
• **Difference vs. Sum**: While addition increases the total, subtraction decreases the total, making them inverse operations.
• **Visualizing Removal**: Using diagrams or pictures where objects are crossed out helps students understand the physical act of subtraction.
• **Comparison**: Subtraction is also used to compare two groups to see which is larger and by how much, which is known as the 'difference'.
• **Inverse Relationship**: Addition and subtraction are linked; if 5 + 3 = 8, then 8 - 3 = 5 and 8 - 5 = 3.
• **Subtrahend and Minuend**: The number being subtracted is the subtrahend, and the number being subtracted from is the minuend.
