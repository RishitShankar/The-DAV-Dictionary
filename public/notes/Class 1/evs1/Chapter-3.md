• **Food: The Basic Necessity** – Food is the most essential requirement for all living beings to survive, grow, and stay healthy.
• **Energy Source** – Food provides the energy required to perform daily activities like playing, studying, walking, and running.
• **Growth Factor** – Eating nutritious food helps children grow taller and stronger as they develop.
• **Disease Prevention** – A balanced diet helps the body fight against germs and prevents us from falling ill.
• **Repair Mechanism** – Food helps in repairing the wear and tear of the body and keeps our internal organs functioning properly.
• **Source of Food** – All food items consumed by humans come from two primary sources: plants and animals.
• **Plant-Based Foods** – Plants provide us with a variety of food items including fruits, vegetables, grains, pulses, and nuts.
• **Animal-Based Foods** – Animals provide us with essential food products such as milk, eggs, meat, fish, and honey.
• **Vegetarian Diet** – People who consume food items derived only from plants and dairy products are known as vegetarians.
• **Non-Vegetarian Diet** – People who include meat, fish, and eggs in their diet along with plant-based foods are known as non-vegetarians.
• **Cereals** – These are grains like wheat, rice, and maize that provide us with the energy to work and play.
• **Pulses** – These include lentils, beans, and peas which are rich in proteins and help our body grow.
• **Fruits** – Fruits are protective foods that keep us healthy and provide essential vitamins and minerals.
• **Vegetables** – Vegetables are vital for maintaining good health and are consumed either raw (like carrots) or cooked (like potatoes).
• **Dairy Products** – Milk is considered a complete food, and products like curd, cheese, and butter are derived from it.
• **Hydration** – Water is not food, but it is essential for digestion and keeping our body hydrated.
• **Balanced Diet** – A diet that contains all the necessary nutrients in the right amount is called a balanced diet.

• **Meal Times** – We eat different meals at different times of the day to keep our energy levels consistent.
• **Breakfast** – This is the first meal of the day, eaten in the morning, and it is considered the most important meal.
• **Lunch** – This is the meal eaten in the afternoon, which provides energy for the rest of the day's activities.
• **Dinner** – This is the final meal of the day, eaten at night, which should be light to ensure good sleep.
• **Snacks** – Small amounts of food eaten between main meals are called snacks, which help satisfy hunger.
• **Healthy Eating Habits** – Developing good habits regarding food is essential for long-term health and well-being.
• **Hand Hygiene** – Always wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water before and after every meal to remove germs.
• **Cleanliness** – Ensure that the food you eat is covered and protected from dust, flies, and insects.
• **Freshness** – Always consume fresh food; stale or uncovered food can cause stomach infections and illness.
• **Chewing Properly** – Food should be chewed slowly and thoroughly to aid in the digestion process.
• **Balanced Intake** – Do not eat too much junk food; prioritize fruits, vegetables, and home-cooked meals.
• **Water Consumption** – Drink plenty of clean, filtered water throughout the day to keep the body functioning optimally.
• **Regularity** – Try to eat your meals at the same time every day to maintain a healthy digestive rhythm.
• **Avoid Overeating** – Eating more than what the body requires can lead to lethargy and health issues.
• **Post-Meal Care** – Rinse your mouth with water after every meal to keep your teeth clean and prevent cavities.
• **Table Manners** – Sit properly while eating and avoid talking with food in your mouth.
• **Sharing** – Sharing food with friends and family promotes social bonding and kindness.

• **Energy-Giving Foods** – These are food items rich in carbohydrates and fats that give us the strength to perform physical tasks.
• **Carbohydrates** – Found in rice, wheat, potatoes, and sugar, these act as the primary fuel for our body.
• **Fats** – Found in butter, ghee, oil, and nuts, these provide concentrated energy and keep our body warm.
• **Body-Building Foods** – These are protein-rich foods that help in building muscles and repairing tissues.
• **Protein Sources** – Major sources include pulses, milk, eggs, meat, fish, and soy products.
• **Protective Foods** – These are foods rich in vitamins and minerals that protect us from various diseases.
• **Vitamins** – Essential micronutrients found in fruits and vegetables that boost our immune system.
• **Minerals** – Essential elements like calcium and iron found in green leafy vegetables and milk that strengthen bones and blood.
• **Calcium** – A vital mineral for strong bones and healthy teeth, primarily obtained from milk and dairy products.
• **Iron** – A mineral that helps in the formation of blood, found in spinach, apples, and jaggery.
• **Fiber** – Found in whole grains and raw vegetables, fiber helps in the smooth movement of food through the digestive system.
• **Raw Food** – Some vegetables like cucumbers, tomatoes, and radishes can be eaten raw as salads.
• **Cooked Food** – Most food items are cooked to make them soft, tasty, and easy to digest.
• **Cooking Methods** – Food can be cooked by boiling, steaming, frying, roasting, or baking.
• **Boiling** – A healthy method of cooking where food is submerged in hot water, such as boiling potatoes or eggs.
• **Steaming** – A method that retains nutrients, often used for items like idli or dhokla.
• **Frying** – Cooking food in hot oil; while tasty, it should be consumed in moderation.

---PAGE-BREAK---

• **Milk: The Complete Food** – Milk contains almost all the nutrients required for the growth of a young child.
• **Calcium Content** – Milk is the richest source of calcium, which is necessary for the development of strong bones.
• **Bone Health** – Regular consumption of milk prevents bone-related issues and supports skeletal growth.
• **Teeth Strength** – The nutrients in milk contribute to the health of gums and the strength of teeth.
• **Energy Boost** – Milk provides quick energy and is often consumed as a morning or bedtime drink.
• **Milk Products** – Various items are derived from milk, including curd, butter, ghee, paneer, and cheese.
• **Curd** – A fermented milk product that is excellent for digestion and gut health.
• **Butter and Ghee** – These are fats derived from milk that provide energy and are used for cooking.
• **Paneer** – A high-protein milk product often used in vegetarian cooking.
• **Cheese** – A concentrated source of protein and calcium derived from milk.
• **Importance of Water** – Water makes up a large part of our body and is essential for all life processes.
• **Digestion** – Water helps in breaking down food so that the body can absorb the nutrients effectively.
• **Waste Removal** – Water helps in flushing out toxins and waste products from the body through sweat and urine.
• **Temperature Regulation** – Water helps in maintaining a stable body temperature, especially during hot weather.
• **Clean Water** – Always drink boiled or filtered water to avoid water-borne diseases like cholera or typhoid.
• **Hydration Signs** – Feeling thirsty is the body's signal that it needs more water.
• **Daily Intake** – A child should drink at least 6 to 8 glasses of water every day.

• **Junk Food** – Food items that are high in sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats but low in nutritional value.
• **Examples of Junk Food** – Chips, cold drinks, burgers, pizzas, and candies are common examples.
• **Health Risks** – Excessive consumption of junk food can lead to obesity, tooth decay, and lack of energy.
• **Sugar Content** – High sugar in junk food causes spikes in energy followed by sudden crashes, making children feel tired.
• **Salt Content** – Excess salt can lead to high blood pressure and kidney strain even at a young age.
• **Lack of Nutrients** – Junk food does not provide the vitamins and minerals needed for growth and immunity.
• **Addictive Nature** – Junk food is often processed to taste very good, which can make it hard to stop eating.
• **Healthy Alternatives** – Replace chips with roasted nuts, and cold drinks with fresh fruit juices or coconut water.
• **Home-Cooked Food** – Food prepared at home is generally healthier because it uses fresh ingredients and controlled amounts of oil and salt.
• **Hygiene in Preparation** – Home cooking allows for better control over the cleanliness of the kitchen and the ingredients used.
• **Seasonal Foods** – It is beneficial to eat fruits and vegetables that are in season as they are fresher and more nutritious.
• **Local Produce** – Eating locally grown food supports the environment and ensures that the food has not traveled long distances.
• **Variety** – Include a variety of colors in your diet; different colored vegetables provide different types of nutrients.
• **Portion Control** – Eat only until you are satisfied, not until you are overly full.
• **Mindful Eating** – Focus on your food while eating instead of watching television or playing games.
• **Chewing Habits** – Chewing food well makes it easier for the stomach to digest it.
• **Post-Meal Hygiene** – Always wash your hands and rinse your mouth after eating to maintain oral hygiene.

• **Plants as Food Providers** – Plants are the primary producers of food on Earth, converting sunlight into energy.
• **Roots as Food** – Some plants store food in their roots, which we eat, such as carrots, radishes, and turnips.
• **Stems as Food** – We consume the stems of certain plants, such as sugarcane, ginger, and potato (which is an underground stem).
• **Leaves as Food** – Green leafy vegetables like spinach, cabbage, and coriander are rich in iron and vitamins.
• **Flowers as Food** – Some flowers are edible, such as cauliflower and broccoli.
• **Fruits as Food** – The fleshy parts of plants that contain seeds, such as apples, mangoes, and grapes.
• **Seeds as Food** – We eat the seeds of many plants, including wheat, rice, pulses, and nuts like almonds and walnuts.
• **Spices** – Plants also provide us with spices like turmeric, pepper, and cardamom, which add flavor and health benefits to our food.
• **Cooking Oil** – Oils are extracted from seeds like mustard, sunflower, and groundnut, and are used for cooking.
• **Animal Products** – Animals provide us with essential proteins and fats that are not easily found in plants.
• **Milk Sources** – Cows, buffaloes, and goats are the primary animals that provide milk for human consumption.
• **Egg Sources** – Hens and ducks are common sources of eggs, which are rich in protein.
• **Meat Sources** – Goats, chickens, and fish are common sources of meat.
• **Honey** – Produced by honeybees from flower nectar, honey is a natural sweetener with medicinal properties.
• **Food Chain Concept** – All food ultimately comes from the sun, which plants use to grow, and animals eat plants or other animals.
• **Respect for Food** – Never waste food; take only as much as you can eat on your plate.
• **Food Preservation** – Proper storage of food in refrigerators or airtight containers prevents spoilage.

• **The Importance of Breakfast** – Skipping breakfast makes you feel weak and tired during school hours.
• **Brain Function** – A healthy breakfast improves concentration and memory, helping you perform better in class.
• **Metabolism** – Eating breakfast kickstarts your metabolism, which helps the body burn energy efficiently throughout the day.
• **Lunch Essentials** – A balanced lunch should include a portion of grains, protein, and vegetables.
• **Lunchbox Habits** – Pack a variety of items in your lunchbox to ensure you get different nutrients.
• **Dinner Timing** – Try to have dinner at least two hours before going to bed to allow for proper digestion.
• **Light Dinner** – A heavy dinner can interfere with sleep quality and cause discomfort.
• **The Role of Snacks** – Healthy snacks like fruits, yogurt, or nuts can prevent overeating during main meals.
• **Avoiding Late-Night Snacking** – Eating late at night can lead to weight gain and digestive issues.
• **Food Allergies** – Some people are allergic to certain foods like peanuts, milk, or eggs; it is important to know what suits your body.
• **Cleanliness of Utensils** – The plates, spoons, and glasses used for eating must be washed and dried properly.
• **Storage of Food** – Keep raw and cooked food separately to prevent the spread of germs.
• **Pest Control** – Keep the kitchen and dining area free from flies, cockroaches, and rats to ensure food safety.
• **Washing Vegetables** – Always wash fruits and vegetables under running water to remove dirt and pesticides.
• **Cooking Safety** – Children should stay away from the stove and sharp knives to avoid accidents.
• **Dining Together** – Eating with family members creates a positive atmosphere and encourages healthy eating habits.
• **Gratitude** – Be thankful for the food you have, as many people in the world do not get enough to eat.

• **Nutrients** – These are the chemical substances in food that provide nourishment essential for the maintenance of life and for growth.
• **Carbohydrates (Energy)** – Known as 'energy-giving nutrients', they are the body's main source of fuel.
• **Proteins (Growth)** – Known as 'body-building nutrients', they are essential for the growth and repair of body cells.
• **Fats (Storage)** – These provide more energy than carbohydrates and help the body absorb certain vitamins.
• **Vitamins (Protection)** – These are required in small amounts to keep the body healthy and protect it from infections.
• **Minerals (Regulation)** – These are inorganic substances that help in various body functions like bone formation and blood clotting.
• **Water (Solvent)** – It acts as a medium for all chemical reactions in the body and helps in transporting nutrients.
• **Roughage (Fiber)** – It is the indigestible part of plant foods that helps in the regular movement of bowels.
• **Balanced Diet Composition** – A balanced diet should include a mix of cereals, pulses, dairy, fruits, and vegetables.
• **Deficiency Diseases** – Lack of specific nutrients in the diet can lead to health problems known as deficiency diseases.
• **Anemia** – A condition caused by a lack of iron in the diet, leading to weakness and fatigue.
• **Weak Bones** – A condition caused by a lack of calcium and Vitamin D, making bones brittle.
• **Immunity** – A strong immune system is built by consuming a diet rich in vitamins and minerals.
• **Energy Balance** – The energy you take in through food should match the energy you spend through physical activity.
• **Physical Activity** – Regular exercise is necessary to utilize the energy provided by food and keep the body fit.
• **Growth Spurts** – During childhood, the body requires extra protein and calcium to support rapid growth.
• **Healthy Choices** – Making the right food choices today ensures a healthy and active life in the future.

• **Food Preservation Techniques** – Methods like refrigeration, drying, and pickling help keep food fresh for longer.
• **Refrigeration** – Cold temperatures slow down the growth of bacteria, keeping food fresh.
• **Drying** – Removing moisture from food prevents the growth of molds and bacteria.
• **Pickling** – Using salt, oil, or vinegar to preserve vegetables and fruits.
• **Food Waste Reduction** – Plan your meals, store food correctly, and use leftovers creatively to reduce waste.
• **Global Food Sources** – Different regions of the world have different staple foods based on their climate and soil.

• **Cultural Diversity** – Food is an important part of our culture, and different festivals have special traditional dishes.
• **Traditional Cooking** – Traditional methods of cooking often use clay pots and slow-cooking techniques that preserve nutrients.
• **Modern Cooking** – Modern appliances like microwaves and pressure cookers make cooking faster but require careful use.
• **Food Safety Standards** – Always check for expiry dates on packaged food items before consuming them.
• **Packaging** – Avoid food in damaged or bloated packets as it may be contaminated.
• **Street Food Caution** – Street food may not always be prepared in hygienic conditions; consume with caution.
• **Water Purity** – Use water purifiers or boil water to ensure it is free from harmful microorganisms.
• **Healthy Habits Checklist** – Wash hands, eat fresh, chew well, drink water, and avoid junk food.
• **The Role of Parents** – Parents play a crucial role in providing nutritious meals and teaching children about healthy eating.
• **School Lunch** – Schools encourage healthy lunchboxes to ensure students remain energetic and focused.
