• **Chapter Title**: Chapter 1 - All About Myself and My Family from the DAV Dayanand Anglo-Vedic Public School Board Class UKG Integrated Activity Book.
• **Core Purpose**: To introduce young learners in Upper Kindergarten (UKG) to foundational concepts of self-awareness, personal identity, and family structures.
• **Educational Philosophy**: Aligns with the DAV holistic child-development framework, promoting self-worth, emotional security, and social understanding.
• **Target Audience**: UKG students (aged 4-5 years) guided by educators and parents using structured, multi-sensory learning experiences.
• **Primary Objective**: To enable children to articulate personal details, recognize their physical attributes, and understand their place within a family unit.
• **Holistic Integration**: Combines early literacy, verbal communication, social-emotional learning (SEL), and motor-skill development through guided activities.
• **Concept of Self**: **Self-Identity** is the understanding of oneself as an individual distinct from others in the classroom and community.
• **Name Recognition**: Every child learns to state their full name clearly, building foundational communication skills and confidence.
• **Age Awareness**: Children learn to state their current age numerically and use visual representations like holding up fingers.
• **Gender Identity**: Basic understanding of being a boy or a girl, fostering body awareness in a safe, age-appropriate educational environment.
• **Personal Milestone**: Recognizing oneself as a growing child transitioning from toddlerhood into early childhood learning environments.
• **Visual Self-Recognition**: Identifying oneself in mirrors and photographs to build spatial and psychological self-awareness.
• **Vocabulary Building**: Introduction of high-frequency words such as "Name," "Age," "Boy," "Girl," "Myself," and "Smile."
• **Interactive Engagement**: Encouraging students to point to themselves when saying "I" or "Me" to ground linguistic concepts in physical reality.
• **Teacher Facilitation**: Educators use circle-time introductions to give every child a platform to speak about themselves in front of peers.

• **Physical Attributes**: Introduction to the basic external parts of the human body as observed on oneself in a mirror.
• **Head and Face**: Recognizing essential facial features including eyes, nose, ears, mouth, cheeks, and hair.
• **Sensory Awareness**: Understanding the primary functions of sensory organs—seeing with eyes, hearing with ears, and smelling with the nose.
• **Limbs**: Identifying arms, hands, legs, and feet as vital body parts used for movement, holding, and playing.
• **Body Hygiene**: Basic concepts of keeping one's body clean through handwashing, brushing teeth, and bathing.
• **Clothing Identification**: Learning to recognize and name basic articles of clothing worn daily, such as shirts, skirts, shorts, and socks.
• **Gross Motor Connection**: Associating body parts with physical actions like running, jumping, clapping, and hopping.
• **Fine Motor Connection**: Using fingers and hands for drawing, coloring, buttoning, and turning book pages.
• **Height and Growth**: Observing that children grow taller and stronger over time through proper nutrition and rest.
• **Health and Nutrition**: Recognizing that eating healthy food (fruits, vegetables, milk) keeps the body energetic and strong.
• **Rest and Sleep**: Understanding the importance of adequate sleep for physical recovery and brain development.
• **Self-Care Skills**: Encouraging independence in managing small tasks like tying shoes (later stage) or washing hands unassisted.
• **Posture Awareness**: Learning to sit straight and stand tall to maintain a healthy spine and good physical carriage.
• **Emotional Connection to Body**: Expressing comfort and happiness in one's own physical appearance and abilities.
• **Activity Integration**: Pointing to body parts during rhyme recitation (e.g., "Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes") for kinesthetic reinforcement.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Emotional Self-Awareness**: Recognizing and naming basic human emotions such as happiness, sadness, anger, and surprise.
• **Facial Expressions**: Connecting internal feelings to external facial expressions, such as smiling when happy or frowning when sad.
• **Self-Expression**: Encouraging children to verbalize their feelings instead of acting out when experiencing strong emotions.
• **Empathy Foundation**: Understanding that classmates and family members also experience different feelings throughout the day.
• **Likes and Dislikes**: Expressing personal preferences regarding favorite foods, colors, toys, and games.
• **Favorite Color**: Identifying a preferred color and recognizing that peers may have entirely different favorite colors.
• **Favorite Food**: Discussing healthy foods that children enjoy eating at home and at school.
• **Favorite Play Activity**: Sharing preferred recreational activities, such as drawing, building blocks, sliding, or playing tag.
• **Uniqueness Concept**: **Individuality** is the principle that every human being is unique, special, and valuable in their own way.
• **Fingerprint Uniqueness**: Discussing how no two people have the exact same fingerprints, highlighting biological individuality.
• **Signature Sound**: Recognizing one's unique speaking and laughing voice among a group of peers.
• **Self-Esteem Building**: Affirming positive self-worth statements such as "I am special," "I am smart," and "I am loved."
• **Confidence in Expression**: Speaking clearly during show-and-tell or personal sharing sessions to build public speaking courage.
• **Acceptance of Differences**: Learning to appreciate that classmates come with different traits, likes, and backgrounds.
• **Reflective Thinking**: Simple guided moments for children to think about what makes them happy and proud of themselves.

• **Introduction to Family**: Defining a **Family** as a group of people related by blood, marriage, or adoption living together or sharing a close bond.
• **Core Family Unit**: Understanding the immediate household members who care for, protect, and love the child daily.
• **Parental Roles**: Identifying **Father (Papa)** and **Mother (Mamma/Mummy)** as the primary caregivers and guides in the household.
• **Sibling Relationships**: Recognizing brothers and sisters as companions with whom children share toys, games, and living spaces.
• **Only Child Concept**: Understanding that some children do not have siblings and live with parents only, which is equally normal and special.
• **Extended Family**: Introducing extended family members including grandparents, uncles, aunts, and cousins.
• **Grandparents' Role**: Recognizing **Grandfather (Dada/Nana)** and **Grandmother (Dadi/Nani)** as wise, loving senior members of the extended family.
• **Family Surname**: Learning the family last name (Surname) as a shared identifier connecting all members of the household.
• **Living Arrangements**: Understanding that families live together in a home, apartment, or house that provides shelter and safety.
• **Sharing and Caring**: Core DAV value emphasizing that family members share household responsibilities, love, and emotional support.
• **Mutual Respect**: Learning to respect elders and show affection and patience toward younger siblings or cousins.
• **Family Bonding**: Participating in family meals, outings, celebrations, and storytelling sessions together.
• **Vocabulary of Kinship**: Learning terms like "Brother," "Sister," "Uncle," "Aunt," and "Cousin" in conversational English.
• **Visual Representation**: Drawing or pasting a family photograph in the activity book to personalize learning.
• **Oral Narratives**: Speaking a few sentences about one's family members during classroom discussions.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Home Environment**: Defining a **Home** as a safe, warm physical dwelling where a family eats, sleeps, and spends time together.
• **Rooms in a House**: Identifying common rooms in a home, including the living room, bedroom, kitchen, and bathroom.
• **Living Room Function**: Recognizing the living room as a space where the family gathers to relax, converse, and welcome guests.
• **Kitchen Function**: Understanding the kitchen as the area where nutritious meals are prepared by parents or family elders.
• **Bedroom Function**: Recognizing the bedroom as a quiet, private space for resting, sleeping, and keeping personal belongings.
• **Bathroom Function**: Associating the bathroom with personal hygiene practices such as brushing, washing, and bathing.
• **Keeping Home Clean**: Learning the importance of keeping living spaces tidy by putting toys away and throwing trash in bins.
• **Safety at Home**: Basic awareness of avoiding dangerous items like sharp objects, hot stoves, and electrical sockets.
• **Pets in the Family**: Recognizing domesticated animals (dogs, cats, birds) as beloved family members in households with pets.
• **Shelter Concept**: Understanding that a home protects humans from rain, wind, heat, cold, and wild animals.
• **Family Hospitality**: Learning how families welcome guests into their homes with politeness, water, and snacks.
• **House vs. Home**: Differentiating between a physical house (building) and a home (filled with love, care, and family).
• **Neighborhood Connection**: Understanding that many houses together form a neighborhood or community.
• **DAV Value Integration**: Cultivating gratitude for having a safe home and a loving family to return to every day.
• **Creative Expression**: Coloring pictures of cozy houses and labeling basic rooms with teacher assistance.

• **Daily Family Routines**: Understanding the sequence of activities that families perform together from morning until night.
• **Morning Routine**: Waking up, brushing teeth, bathing, eating breakfast, and preparing for school or daily tasks.
• **Mealtime Rituals**: Gathering around the dining table or kitchen space to share meals, express gratitude, and converse.
• **Evening Routine**: Returning home, unwinding, completing homework or quiet play, and preparing for dinner.
• **Bedtime Rituals**: Storytelling, reading books, saying prayers, and wishing goodnight to family members.
• **Family Celebrations**: Recognizing festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti) and family milestones (birthdays, anniversaries).
• **Festival Sharing**: Exchanging sweets, wearing new clothes, and celebrating cultural heritage together as a family unit.
• **Weekend Activities**: Engaging in leisure activities such as park visits, grocery shopping, cleaning, and visiting relatives.
• **Helpful Hands**: Teaching children how they can help parents by keeping toys away, laying napkins, or watering plants.
• **Respectful Communication**: Using polite golden words at home: "Please," "Thank you," "Sorry," and "Excuse me."
• **Listening to Elders**: Understanding the value of listening to parental advice, instructions, and moral stories.
• **Family Decision Making**: Observing how parents discuss and make important household decisions together.
• **Caring for Sick Members**: Learning empathy through observing how family members care for someone who is unwell.
• **Shared Responsibilities**: Recognizing that keeping a home running smoothly requires cooperation from every member.
• **Reflective Sharing**: Children describing their favorite family routine during morning assembly or circle time.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Social Etiquette at Home**: Practicing good manners within the family circle as the foundation for broader social interaction.
• **Greeting Elders**: Bowing or touching feet (as per cultural tradition) and saying "Good morning" or "Namaste" to parents and grandparents.
• **Sharing Toys**: Learning to share playthings, crayons, and books with siblings and visiting cousins without fighting.
• **Taking Turns**: Practicing patience by waiting for one's turn during family games and conversations.
• **Kindness to Animals**: Showing gentle behavior toward stray animals or family pets, reflecting DAV compassionate values.
• **Table Manners**: Chewing with mouth closed, using a napkin, not talking with food in the mouth, and washing hands before meals.
• **Managing Anger**: Learning deep-breathing techniques or walking away when feeling frustrated with a sibling.
• **Apologizing and Forgiving**: Saying "I am sorry" sincerely when making a mistake and accepting apologies gracefully.
• **Expressing Gratitude**: Saying "Thank you" to parents for preparing meals, buying gifts, or helping with school projects.
• **Respecting Privacy**: Knocking on closed doors before entering bedrooms or private spaces in the house.
• **Table Conversation**: Engaging in polite dinner table talk by listening while others speak and not interrupting.
• **Cleanliness Habits**: Clearing one's plate after eating and putting dirty clothes in the laundry basket.
• **Value of Sharing**: Understanding that sharing joy multiplies it, while sharing sorrow halves it within a family.
• **Role-Playing Activities**: Acting out polite household scenarios in the classroom to reinforce positive behavioral habits.
• **Behavioral Reinforcement**: Teachers and parents praising children instantly when they display kind, polite family etiquette.

• **Connecting Self to Family**: Recognizing that an individual child is an integral extension of their family lineage and home.
• **Family Tree Concept (Introductory)**: Visualizing the family structure as a branching tree where grandparents form the roots and children form the leaves/flowers.
• **Tracing Heritage**: Learning the family's native place, mother tongue, and cultural traditions passed down through generations.
• **Mother Tongue Awareness**: Identifying the primary language spoken at home, recognizing linguistic diversity in the classroom.
• **Cultural Identity**: Celebrating traditional clothing, music, and food specific to one's family cultural background.
• **Family Resemblance**: Observing physical similarities between children and their parents (e.g., eye color, hair texture, smile).
• **Personality Traits**: Recognizing inherited behavioral traits, such as being cheerful, studious, artistic, or athletic, from family members.
• **Name and Address Memorization**: **Personal Safety Rule** - UKG students must learn their parents' full names and residential address.
• **Contact Numbers**: Beginning to memorize at least one parent's mobile phone number for emergency identification.
• **Emergency Preparedness**: Knowing who to turn to in public spaces (policemen, security guards, mothers with children) if separated from family.
• **Trust Circle**: Identifying trusted adults outside the immediate family, such as teachers and school principals.
• **Confidence in Autonomy**: Feeling secure and confident knowing one belongs to a loving, protective family network.
• **Artistic Representation**: Drawing self-portraits surrounded by miniature drawings of family members and pets.
• **Interactive Storytelling**: Listening to moral stories about ideal family relationships and filial piety from Indian and global traditions.
• **DAV Holistic Assessment**: Continuous observation of how students express love for their family and understand their personal identity.

• **Integrated Activity Book Execution**: Translating theoretical knowledge of self and family into active pencil-paper and tactile exercises.
• **Tracing and Writing**: Practicing tracing lines, curves, and letters forming personal details like name initials and family words.
• **Matching Exercises**: Connecting pictures of family members (Father, Mother, Grandfather, Grandmother) to their corresponding names or roles.
• **Coloring Tasks**: Filling colors neatly within boundary lines of family portraits, houses, and personal milestone birthday cakes.
• **Sticker Paving**: Peeling and pasting reusable stickers of body parts, family members, and household items onto designated activity book slots.
• **Counting Family Members**: Counting the total number of people living in one's home and writing the corresponding numeral.
• **Sorting Activities**: Categorizing items into groups (e.g., things we wear, things we eat at home, rooms in a house).
• **Missing Parts Puzzle**: Identifying missing facial features (eyes, nose, mouth) in self-portrait outlines and drawing them in.
• **Path Finding (Mazes)**: Guiding a child through a maze to reach their home or family members to build visual-motor coordination.
• **Handprint Art**: Pressing painted hands onto activity book pages to create permanent keepsakes marking the child's growth in UKG.
• **Oral Assessment Tasks**: Teachers asking individual students to stand and speak three sentences about themselves and their family.
• **Peer Interaction Games**: Passing a ball in a circle and stating one favorite thing about oneself or family when caught.
• **Portfolio Building**: Compiling completed activity book pages into a cumulative learning portfolio showcasing term progress.
• **Parent-Teacher Collaboration**: Encouraging parents to review completed activity pages at home and sign off on child achievements.
• **Continuous Reinforcement**: Re-visiting "All About Myself" concepts periodically throughout the academic year to track cognitive maturation.

• **Emotional Intelligence Foundation**: Basic ability to identify personal feelings, express preferences, and recognize peer emotions.
• **Family Appreciation**: Deepened respect, love, and understanding of nuclear and extended family members and their roles.
• **Domestic Responsibility**: Awareness of home cleanliness, safety rules, daily routines, and polite social etiquette.
• **DAV Values Assimilation**: Internalizing core values of compassion, respect for elders, sharing, and gratitude within the family unit.
• **Pre-Writing Skill Mastery**: Enhanced fine motor control, pencil grip, hand-eye coordination, and spatial awareness through activity book tasks.
• **Oral Communication Growth**: Increased vocabulary, improved articulation, and heightened self-confidence during public speaking opportunities.
• **Cognitive Development**: Successful transition from egocentric thinking toward basic awareness of social relationships and community structures.
• **Inclusive Perspective**: Recognizing and respecting diverse family structures and cultural backgrounds present in the classroom community.
• **Readiness for Next Chapter**: Establishing a rock-solid psychological and academic foundation to progress into Chapter 2 of the Integrated Activity Book.
• **Teacher Commendation**: Positive reinforcement and grading of the completed chapter activity book to celebrate student success.
• **Parental Pride**: Showcasing completed student work during parent-teacher meetings to foster home-school partnership.
• **Final Educational Takeaway**: Empowering every child to look in the mirror with a smile, knowing they are unique, loved, and valued members of their family and the DAV school community.
