• **Chapter Title:** Chapter 6: "Things That Move (Vehicles & Wheels)" as prescribed in the DAV Dictionary for Class LKG Environmental Studies (EVS).
• **Core Subject Focus:** Introducing young learners to the fundamental concepts of motion, transportation, vehicles, and the basic mechanics of wheels.
• **Educational Objective:** To help students observe, identify, and categorize different modes of transport seen in their immediate environment.
• **Definition of Movement:** Movement is defined as the act of changing position or location from one place to another, which is a fundamental characteristic of both living beings and non-living vehicles.
• **Introduction to Vehicles:** **Vehicles** are defined as machines or mechanical devices designed specifically to carry people and goods from one destination to another.
• **Human Dependency on Travel:** Humans naturally need to travel for various daily activities such as going to school, visiting family, purchasing groceries, and exploring new places.
• **Historical Context (Brief):** Long before modern engines were invented, humans walked everywhere or relied heavily on domesticated animals like horses, oxen, and camels for transport.
• **Invention of the Wheel:** The invention of the **wheel** is considered one of the greatest milestones in human history, fundamentally changing how objects and people are moved across distances.
• **Basic Shape of a Wheel:** A standard wheel is circular in geometry, which allows it to rotate smoothly around a central axis or axle with minimal resistance.
• **Reduction of Friction:** Wheels drastically reduce friction between a heavy load and the ground, making it significantly easier to push or pull heavy items.
• **Role of the Axle:** An **axle** is a central shaft that rotates the wheels and supports the weight of the vehicle body resting upon it.
• **Everyday Observation:** LKG students are encouraged to observe vehicles in their daily lives, such as cars parked outside, buses passing by, and bicycles ridden in the neighborhood.
• **Categorization of Transport:** Transportation is broadly categorized into three primary domains based on the medium of travel: land, water, and air.
• **Land Transport Introduction:** **Land transport** encompasses all vehicles that travel exclusively on roads, tracks, or the ground surface using wheels or treads.
• **Water Transport Introduction:** **Water transport** includes boats, ships, and rafts that float and navigate across rivers, lakes, seas, and oceans.
• **Air Transport Introduction:** **Air transport** consists of flying machines like airplanes and helicopters that travel through the sky above the ground.
• **Early Childhood Engagement:** Young learners interact with miniature toy cars and pull-toys, which serve as tactile introductions to the mechanics of motion and wheels.

• **Deep Dive into Land Transport:** Land transport is the most common form of travel experienced by LKG students on a daily basis.
• **Definition of Road Vehicles:** Road vehicles are mechanical contrivances equipped with wheels designed to move smoothly over paved or unpaved paths.
• **The Bicycle:** A **bicycle** is a human-powered, two-wheeled land vehicle driven by pedaling with the feet, making it eco-friendly and healthy.
• **Parts of a Bicycle:** Key components of a bicycle include the handlebars for steering, two circular wheels, pedals for propulsion, a chain, and a seat.
• **The Tricycle:** A **tricycle** features three wheels—one in the front and two in the back—providing extra stability for younger children learning to ride.
• **The Scooter:** A **scooter** is a narrow-platform land vehicle with two or three small wheels and a long steering handle, propelled by pushing off the ground with one foot.
• **The Car:** A **car** is a motor-powered, four-wheeled vehicle designed primarily for personal or family transport on roads and highways.
• **Engine Power:** Unlike bicycles, cars utilize fuel (petrol, diesel) or electricity stored in batteries to power their engines and turn their wheels automatically.
• **The Bus:** A **bus** is a large, heavy motor vehicle carrying many passengers simultaneously along a fixed route within or between cities.
• **The School Bus:** A school bus holds a special place for LKG students, acting as a familiar yellow vehicle that safely transports them to and from school.
• **The Auto-Rickshaw:** An **auto-rickshaw** is a three-wheeled motor vehicle commonly used for short-distance local travel in towns and cities.
• **The Motorcycle:** A **motorcycle** (or motorbike) is a fast, two-wheeled motorized vehicle requiring the rider to wear a protective helmet for safety.
• **The Scooter (Motorized):** A motorized scooter combines the platform of a child's scooter with a small engine for commuting short distances.
• **The Train:** A **train** is a long series of connected rail carriages or wagons that run strictly on fixed metal tracks called railways.
• **Locomotive Definition:** The front engine of a train is called a **locomotive**, which pulls all the passenger and cargo bogies behind it.
• **Railway Tracks:** Trains cannot wander off roads because their steel wheels are specifically grooved to fit securely onto parallel steel tracks.
• **Speed of Trains:** Modern trains can travel at very high speeds across vast distances, connecting distant towns, cities, and countries efficiently.

• **Introduction to Water Transport:** Water transport involves vehicles that float, buoyancy being the core physical principle enabling them to stay afloat on liquid surfaces.
• **Concept of Floating:** **Floating** occurs when an object stays on the surface of water instead of sinking, determined by its weight, shape, and density.
• **The Boat:** A **boat** is a relatively small watercraft designed to transport people or goods across calm rivers, small lakes, or coastal waters.
• **Human-Powered Boats:** Some small boats rely on human muscle power, utilizing oars or paddles to push against the water and create forward motion.
• **The Ship:** A **ship** is a much larger, ocean-going water vessel capable of carrying massive amounts of cargo, vehicles, and hundreds of passengers across the deep sea.
• **Engine-Powered Watercraft:** Modern ships and large motorboats are driven by powerful marine engines that turn propellers beneath the water line.
• **The Raft:** A **raft** is a very simple, flat-duty floating structure made by tying logs or planks together, representing one of the earliest forms of water transport.
• **The Canoe and Kayak:** A **canoe** and a **kayak** are narrow, lightweight human-paddled boats historically used by indigenous peoples and currently used for sport and leisure.
• **The Ferry:** A **ferry** is a specialized boat or ship used to carry passengers, cars, and trucks across a body of water on a regular, frequent schedule.
• **The Submarine:** A **submarine** is a unique watercraft that can travel both on the surface of the water and submerge to travel deep underwater.
• **Harbors and Ports:** Ships and boats park, load, and unload at designated coastal areas known as **harbors** or **ports**.
• **Life Jackets on Water:** Safety is paramount in water transport; passengers wear **life jackets**—floatation vests designed to keep individuals afloat in an emergency.
• **Lighthouses:** A **lighthouse** is a tall tower with a bright light at the top built on the coast to warn ships of dangerous rocks and guide them safely.
• **Role of Anchors:** An **anchor** is a heavy metal hook dropped from a boat or ship into the waterbed to hold the vessel securely in one place.
• **Water Transport Utility:** Water transport is vital for international trade, moving heavy and bulky goods across oceans where roads and railways cannot be built.
• **Environmental Care on Water:** Keeping rivers and oceans clean is essential; water vehicles must avoid spilling oil or littering plastic into the aquatic ecosystem.

• **Introduction to Air Transport:** Air transport represents the fastest mode of travel, lifting humans and cargo high up into the sky, overcoming gravity.
• **The Airplane:** An **airplane** (or aeroplane) is a powered, fixed-wing aircraft that flies through the air using lift generated by its wings and thrust from jet engines.
• **Wings of an Airplane:** The **wings** of an airplane are specially shaped (airfoils) to catch the air and lift the heavy metal body upward as the plane moves forward.
• **Jet Engines:** Powerful **jet engines** burn fuel to push air backward at high speed, creating forward thrust that drives the airplane through the sky.
• **The Cockpit:** The **cockpit** is the front area of the airplane where the pilot and co-pilot sit to control all instruments, navigation, and flying controls.
• **The Passenger Cabin:** The **cabin** is the interior space where passengers sit in rows of seats during their flight across countries and continents.
• **Airports:** Airplanes take off and land on long, paved pathways called runways located within large facilities called **airports**.
• **The Helicopter:** A **helicopter** is an aircraft that uses rotating overhead blades (rotor blades) to lift straight up, hover in place, and fly in any direction.
• **Versatility of Helicopters:** Unlike airplanes, helicopters do not need long runways; they can land on rooftops, small pads, or grassy fields, making them ideal for rescue missions.
• **Emergency Rescues:** Helicopters are frequently used by medical teams and disaster relief organizations to lift injured people from remote or flooded areas.
• **The Hot Air Balloon:** A **hot air balloon** is a lighter-than-air aircraft consisting of a giant fabric bag filled with heated air, lifting a wicker basket carrying passengers.
• **The Rocket:** A **rocket** is a specialized aerospace vehicle driven by rocket engines that travels beyond Earth's atmosphere into outer space.
• **Space Travel:** Rockets carry satellites, scientific equipment, and astronauts into the vacuum of space, exploring planets, the moon, and stars.
• **Parachutes:** A **parachute** is a large, umbrella-like fabric device that slows down the descent of a person or object falling from high altitudes in the air.
• **Clouds and Sky:** Air transport operates high up in the atmosphere, often flying above the **clouds** where the sky appears bright blue and clear.
• **Speed of Flight:** Traveling by air drastically reduces journey times from weeks of sea travel to mere hours of flight between distant continents.

• **Deep Dive into Wheels:** The mechanical wheel is the foundational component of almost all land vehicles, making motion smooth and continuous.
• **Anatomy of a Wheel:** A standard wheel consists of an outer circular rim, internal spokes or a solid disc, and a central hub where the axle is inserted.
• **Role of the Tire:** A **tire** (often made of rubber) wraps around the metal rim of the wheel to provide grip, traction, and cushion against bumps on the road.
• **Pneumatic Tires:** Most modern vehicle tires are **pneumatic**, meaning they are filled with compressed air to absorb shocks and provide a smooth ride.
• **The Tread Pattern:** The **tread** is the textured pattern on the outer surface of a tire designed to channel away water and prevent slipping on wet roads.
• **Rolling vs. Sliding:** **Rolling** friction is vastly lower than sliding friction, which is why objects with wheels require much less force to move across the ground.
• **Rotation Mechanics:** A wheel undergoes **rotation**, meaning it turns completely around a fixed central point or imaginary line called the axis of rotation.
• **Different Wheel Sizes:** Different vehicles use different wheel sizes; bicycles have medium wheels, trucks have large heavy-duty wheels, and roller skates have tiny wheels.
• **Materials Used for Wheels:** Historically, wheels were made entirely of solid wood, later upgraded to iron-rimmed wood, and today manufactured from steel, aluminum alloys, and rubber.
• **Casters on Furniture:** Small swiveling wheels called **casters** are attached to the bottom of office chairs, shopping carts, and hospital beds to allow movement in any direction.
• **Gears and Wheels:** In complex machines, toothed wheels called **gears** interlock to transfer motion, speed, and power from one part of a machine to another.
• **The Steering Wheel:** A **steering wheel** is a circular driver control inside a car or truck that allows the driver to direct which way the front wheels turn.
• **Wheel Alignment:** **Wheel alignment** ensures that all vehicle wheels point in the correct parallel direction, allowing for straight and safe driving.
• **Wheel Balancing:** **Wheel balancing** ensures weight is distributed evenly around the tire and rim combination, preventing vibrations at high speeds.
• **Maintenance of Wheels:** Regular checking of tire air pressure and tread depth is essential for vehicle safety, fuel efficiency, and preventing sudden flats.
• **The Legacy of the Wheel:** Beyond transport, wheels are essential in pulleys, water mills, clocks, potter's wheels, and industrial machinery across human civilization.

• **Emergency Vehicles:** Certain special vehicles are designated as emergency vehicles and are granted the right of way to rush quickly during urgent situations.
• **The Ambulance:** An **ambulance** is a specially equipped medical vehicle designed to transport sick or injured people to a hospital while receiving urgent first aid.
• **Ambulance Siren:** Ambulances feature a loud audible warning device called a **siren** and flashing red/blue lights to alert other drivers to clear the road immediately.
• **The Fire Engine:** A **fire engine** (or fire truck) is a heavy-duty emergency vehicle carrying firefighters, hoses, ladders, and water pumps to extinguish destructive fires.
• **Fire Hydrant Connection:** Fire engines connect to city water supplies through **fire hydrants** or pump water directly from their internal storage tanks to douse flames.
• **The Police Car:** A **police car** is a law enforcement vehicle used by police officers to patrol neighborhoods, maintain public safety, and respond to crimes quickly.
• **Police Patrol:** Police cars are easily recognizable by their distinctive paint schemes, flashing lights, and sirens used during high-priority law enforcement calls.
• **Traffic Police Vehicles:** Traffic police use motorcycles and cars to manage busy road junctions, direct vehicles, and enforce road safety laws.
• **Rescue Helicopters:** In mountainous or flooded disaster zones where ambulances cannot drive, rescue helicopters airlift patients directly to emergency wards.
• **Public Utility Vehicles:** Municipal corporations use specialized utility vehicles like garbage trucks, street sweepers, and water tankers to maintain community hygiene.
• **The Garbage Truck:** A garbage truck is a heavy-duty vehicle designed to collect municipal solid waste from households and transport it to recycling or disposal sites.
• **Road Rollers:** A **road roller** is a massive engineering vehicle with heavy steel drums used to compress and flatten soil and asphalt during road construction.
• **The Tractor:** A **tractor** is a powerful agricultural vehicle with large rear wheels used on farms for pulling plows, trailers, and tilling soil.
• **Harvesters:** A **harvester** is a large agricultural machine that cuts, threshes, and cleans grain crops automatically in large farm fields.
• **Cranes:** A **crane** is a tall construction tower or truck-mounted machine equipped with cables and pulleys used to lift and move heavy building materials.
• **Forklifts:** A **forklift** is a small industrial truck with two steel forks on the front used in warehouses to lift and stack heavy wooden pallets.

• **Road Safety Rules:** Understanding how to move safely around vehicles is a crucial learning outcome for LKG students studying transportation and traffic.
• **Definition of Pedestrian:** A **pedestrian** is a person walking along a road or sidewalk rather than traveling inside a vehicle or riding a bicycle.
• **The Sidewalk (Pavement):** Pedestrians must always walk on the **sidewalk** or footpath, safely separated from fast-moving motor vehicles on the roadway.
• **The Zebra Crossing:** A **zebra crossing** is a designated pedestrian crossing marked on the road with wide white stripes resembling a zebra's pattern.
• **Crossing the Road Safely:** Children must always hold an adult's hand tightly when crossing a busy road at a designated crosswalk or signal.
• **The Traffic Light System:** A **traffic light** (traffic signal) is an automated electronic signaling device that controls the flow of vehicles and pedestrians at road intersections.
• **Red Light Meaning:** The **red light** on a traffic signal means **STOP** completely before the white stop line.
• **Yellow Light Meaning:** The **yellow light** (amber) on a traffic signal means **WAIT** or prepare to stop as the light is about to change.
• **Green Light Meaning:** The **green light** on a traffic signal means **GO**, indicating it is safe for vehicles or pedestrians to proceed.
• **Looking Both Ways:** Before stepping onto a road where there are no signals, pedestrians must look to the **left, right, and left again** to ensure no cars are coming.
• **No Running on Roads:** Children should never run, play games, or chase balls on streets or roads where cars and motorcycles drive.
• **Seatbelt Safety:** When riding inside a car, all passengers must wear a **seatbelt** securely fastened across their lap and chest to prevent injury during sudden stops.
• **Helmet Safety:** When riding a bicycle, scooter, or motorcycle, wearing a properly fitted protective **helmet** protects the head from severe impact.
• **Bus Etiquette:** While riding a school bus, children must remain seated, keep their hands and heads inside the windows, and never shout or distract the driver.
• **Reflective Clothing:** Wearing bright or reflective clothing while walking outside at dusk or night helps drivers see pedestrians easily.
• **Safe Play Areas:** Children should play only in designated parks, gardens, playgrounds, and backyards, strictly away from driveways and public streets.

• **Sound and Horns of Vehicles:** Different vehicles produce distinct sounds, engine hums, and warning horns that help pedestrians and drivers identify them.
• **The Horn Sound:** A vehicle **horn** is an audible signaling device used by drivers to warn others of their presence or potential danger.
• **Bell on Bicycles:** Traditional bicycles often feature a small handlebar-mounted **bell** that rings with a cheerful "trunk-trunk" sound to alert pedestrians.
• **Car Engine Hum:** A normal car engine makes a smooth humming or purring sound when idling or moving down a paved street.
• **Heavy Truck Roar:** Large trucks and buses produce deep, loud rumbling sounds due to their powerful, heavy-duty diesel engines working under heavy loads.
• **Train Chug and Whistle:** Trains make a rhythmic **"chug-chug"** sound along the tracks accompanied by a loud, echoing **whistle** or horn when approaching stations.
• **Airplane Roar:** Jet airplanes create a massive, roaring sound wave as their powerful engines generate immense thrust during takeoff.
• **Ship Foghorn:** Large ocean ships use deep, low-pitched **foghorns** to signal their presence through thick fog or low-visibility marine weather.
• **Ambulance Siren Tone:** An ambulance siren produces a wailing, high-to-low fluctuating pitch that instantly signals motorists to yield the right of way.
• **Fire Engine Bell/Siren:** Fire engines emit urgent sirens and air horns to clear traffic rapidly while rushing to emergency fire scenes.
• **Vehicle Pollution Overview:** While vehicles are immensely useful, the smoke and exhaust gases they release can cause environmental concerns.
• **Air Pollution:** **Air pollution** occurs when harmful gases, soot, and smoke from vehicle exhaust pipes mix into the atmosphere, making the air dirty and unhealthy.
• **Noise Pollution:** Excessive honking and loud engine roars contribute to **noise pollution**, which can cause stress and hearing issues in crowded cities.
• **Eco-Friendly Transport:** **Eco-friendly transport** includes walking, cycling, and using electric vehicles (EVs) that do not release harmful exhaust smoke into the air.
• **Electric Vehicles (EVs):** Electric vehicles run on clean electrical energy stored in batteries instead of burning gasoline, making them quiet and pollution-free.
• **Planting Trees:** Growing trees alongside roads helps absorb carbon dioxide and clean the air polluted by heavy city traffic.

• **Comparison and Sorting of Vehicles:** LKG students develop critical cognitive skills by comparing, contrasting, and sorting vehicles based on size, wheels, and travel medium.
• **Comparing Sizes:** Vehicles range in size from tiny roller skates and toy cars to massive cargo ships, giant airplanes, and long freight trains.
• **Wheel Count Classification:** Vehicles can be grouped by the number of wheels they have: two wheels (bicycles, motorcycles), three wheels (tricycles, auto-rickshaws), four wheels (cars, jeeps), and many wheels (trains, large trucks).
• **Sorting by Travel Medium:** Children practice sorting picture cards of vehicles into three distinct mats or baskets: Land, Water, and Air.
• **Fast vs. Slow Transport:** Comparing speeds helps children understand that bicycles move relatively slowly, cars move faster, and airplanes are the fastest way to travel long distances.
• **Private vs. Public Transport:** **Private transport** consists of vehicles owned by individuals or families (like a family car), while **public transport** includes vehicles used by the general public (like public buses and trains).
• **Carrying Capacity:** Some vehicles carry only one or two people (bicycles, scooters), while others carry dozens (buses) or hundreds of passengers at once (large commercial airplanes and passenger liners).
• **Cargo vs. Passenger Vehicles:** Passenger vehicles are built to carry people safely, whereas cargo vehicles (trucks, freight trains, cargo ships) are designed to move goods, food, and manufactured items.
• **Animal-Powered Transport (Traditional):** In rural or historical contexts, carts pulled by oxen, horses, or donkeys represent traditional animal-powered land transport.
• **Cycle Rickshaws:** A **cycle rickshaw** is a three-wheeled passenger cart propelled by a human bicyclist pedaling from behind, common in many Asian towns.
• **Wheelbarrows:** A **wheelbarrow** is a small, single-wheeled hand cart used in gardens and construction sites to carry small loads of soil, bricks, or tools.
• **Roller Skates and Skateboards:** Roller skates and skateboards are recreational foot-mounted or board-mounted devices with small wheels used for sport and play.
• **The Skateboard Deck:** A skateboard consists of a wooden or plastic board mounted on two small wheel trucks, propelled by pushing with one foot.
• **Prams and Strollers:** A **pram** or baby stroller is a four-wheeled pushcart designed specifically to transport infants and toddlers safely.
• **Wheelchairs:** A **wheelchair** is a chair fitted with wheels, enabling people with mobility impairments or injuries to move around independently or with assistance.
• **Summary of Chapter Learning:** Through this DAV LKG chapter, students gain a comprehensive foundational understanding of how things move, the vital role of wheels, and the wonderful world of transportation.
