• Chapter Title: **रोटी** (Roti) is a thematic lesson focusing on the journey of food from the farm to the plate.
• Core Theme: The chapter emphasizes the value of hard work, agriculture, and the process of making food.
• Character Introduction: The story revolves around a young child who is curious about the origin of the roti they eat.
• Educational Objective: To teach students the importance of respecting food and understanding the labor involved in its production.
• Concept of Agriculture: The chapter introduces the basic steps of farming, starting from sowing seeds in the field.
• Role of the Farmer: The farmer is depicted as the primary provider who works tirelessly in the sun and rain.
• Soil Preparation: The process begins with plowing the field to make the soil fertile and ready for seeds.
• Sowing Seeds: Farmers sow wheat seeds in the prepared soil, which is the foundational step of the cycle.
• Irrigation: The importance of water for the growth of crops is highlighted as a vital life-giving element.
• Crop Growth: The wheat plants grow under the sunlight, eventually turning golden when they are ready for harvest.
• Harvesting: The process of cutting the ripe wheat stalks is described as a significant agricultural milestone.
• Threshing: The separation of wheat grains from the stalks is a crucial step in the processing of food.
• Milling: The grains are taken to the flour mill (chakki) to be ground into fine wheat flour (aata).
• Role of the Mother: The mother is shown as the one who transforms the flour into a nutritious roti.
• Kneading: The process of mixing water with flour to create dough (gundhna) is explained.
• Rolling: The technique of flattening the dough into a circular shape using a rolling pin (belan) and board (chakla).
• Cooking: The final step involves roasting the rolled dough on a hot griddle (tawa) to make the roti.
• Gratitude: The chapter concludes by instilling a sense of thankfulness toward those who contribute to our meals.
• Vocabulary Focus: Key terms like 'Kisan' (Farmer), 'Khet' (Field), and 'Aata' (Flour) are central to the lesson.
• Moral Lesson: Students learn that food is a result of collective effort and should never be wasted.

• **Chakla (Rolling board):** The flat surface used to support the dough while it is being shaped.
• **Belan (Rolling pin):** The cylindrical tool used to flatten the dough ball into a thin, round shape.

• **Step 2: Sowing:** Seeds are sown at the right depth to ensure they get enough nutrients from the soil.
• **Step 3: Growth:** The crop requires consistent sunlight and water to grow into tall, healthy stalks.
• **Step 4: Maturation:** The wheat turns golden, indicating the presence of starch and nutrients in the grain.
• **Step 5: Harvesting:** Using sickles or machines, the farmer cuts the stalks close to the ground.
• **Step 6: Threshing:** The grains are separated from the straw, which is often used as animal fodder.
• **Step 7: Winnowing:** The wind is used to blow away the lighter chaff, leaving behind clean grains.
• **Step 8: Milling:** The clean grains are sent to the mill to be ground into fine flour.
• **Step 9: Packaging:** Flour is packed into bags for distribution to homes and markets.
• **Step 10: Kitchen Preparation:** The mother takes the flour and prepares the dough with water.
• **Step 11: Shaping:** The dough is divided into equal parts and rolled into thin, circular shapes.
• **Step 12: Cooking:** The roti is placed on a hot tawa and flipped until both sides are cooked.
• **Step 13: Final Touch:** The roti is often brushed with a little ghee or butter for taste and nutrition.
