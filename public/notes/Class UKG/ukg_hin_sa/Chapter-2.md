• **Introduction to Chapter 2:** This chapter in The DAV Dictionary for Class UKG Shabd Abhyas introduces young learners to the foundational vowel system (**स्वर - Swar**) of the Hindi language, spanning from 'अ' to 'अः'.
• **Role of Swar:** **स्वर** (Vowels) are defined as independent sounds that can be pronounced without the help of any other letters, forming the fundamental building blocks of Hindi pronunciation and vocabulary.
• **Total Count of Swars:** The Hindi alphabet traditionally contains **13 swars** in total, starting with 'अ' and ending with the special vowel-modifier 'अः' (Visarga).
• **Visual Learning Approach:** The chapter utilizes a **sachitra parichay** (illustrated introduction) methodology, pairing each vowel with a universally recognized object, animal, or fruit starting with that specific sound.
• **Cognitive Development:** The structured layout aids Class UKG students in associating abstract phonetic sounds with concrete visual imagery, enhancing both linguistic and visual memory retention.
• **Phonetic Clarity:** Each vowel has a distinct mouth shape and duration of sound (**Matra kaal**), which forms the base for correct spelling and word formation in subsequent chapters.
• **Primary Vowel 'अ' (Chhota A):** The vowel **अ** represents the short, neutral vowel sound, serving as the inherent vowel embedded within all unmodulated Hindi consonants.
• **Illustrated Object for 'अ':** The textbook pairs 'अ' with **अमरूद** (Guava) and **अनार** (Pomegranate), teaching children the initial sounds of these common healthy fruits.
• **Primary Vowel 'आ' (Bada A):** The second vowel is **आ**, characterized by a prolonged open-mouth sound, which is represented graphically by adding a standing vertical line after the base consonant or letter structure.
• **Illustrated Object for 'आ':** The textbook pairs 'आ' with **आम** (Mango), universally hailed as the king of fruits, to help students remember the elongated vowel sound effortlessly.
• **Primary Vowel 'इ' (Chhoti I):** The third vowel is **इ**, which represents a short, quick front-vowel sound produced with minimal jaw opening and a slight smile shape of the lips.
• **Illustrated Object for 'इ':** The textbook pairs 'इ' with **इmli** (Tamarind), introducing students to its distinct tangy taste and the sharp initial phonetic sound.
• **Primary Vowel 'ई' (Badi I):** The fourth vowel is **ई**, representing a long, sustained version of the 'इ' sound, produced by drawing the corners of the lips further back.
• **Illustrated Object for 'ई':** The textbook pairs 'ई' with **ईख** (Sugarcane), familiarizing children with rural agrarian concepts and the sweet juice extracted from the plant.
• **Primary Vowel 'उ' (Chhota U):** The fifth vowel is **उ**, a short back-vowel sound produced by rounding the lips into a small circle and pushing air outward gently.
• **Illustrated Object for 'उ':** The textbook pairs 'उ' with **उल्लू** (Owl), introducing nocturnal birds and building early vocabulary around nature.
• **Primary Vowel 'ऊ' (Bada U):** The sixth vowel is **ऊ**, which is the elongated equivalent of 'उ', requiring a more pronounced forward rounding of the lips for a longer duration.
• **Illustrated Object for 'ऊ':** The textbook pairs 'ऊ' with **ऊन** (Wool), teaching students about clothing materials derived from sheep used during winter seasons.

• **Anusvara 'अं' (Am):** The twelfth linguistic unit is **अं**, featuring a base vowel 'अ' surmounted by a dot (**Anusvara**) that creates a nasalized resonant sound.
• **Illustrated Object for 'अं':** The textbook pairs 'अं' with **अंगूर** (Grapes), teaching the bunch-forming fruit and the corresponding nasal phonetic emission.
• **Visarga 'अः' (Ah):** The thirteenth and final unit in the Swar sequence is **अः**, which incorporates the **Visarga** symbol (two vertical dots on the right) representing a voiceless glottal fricative sound like a soft 'h'.
• **Absence of Direct Visual for 'अः':** Unlike other swars, 'अः' is typically explained through sound-ending words or modifiers since it rarely occurs at the absolute beginning of common children's nouns.
• **Phonetic Ordering Principle:** The sequence from 'अ' to 'अः' follows strict traditional Sanskrit-derived phonetic ordering (**Varnamala krama**), moving from throat-produced vowels to nasal and glottal modifications.
• **Visual-Acoustic Synergy:** The DAV pedagogical framework emphasizes that seeing the picture, hearing the teacher pronounce the word, and mimicking the sound creates triple-coded memory traces.
• **Foundational Vocabulary Bank:** Mastery of these initial 13 illustrations provides Class UKG students with their first core set of active Hindi vocabulary words.
• **Stroke Order Preparation:** Tracing the shapes of these swars in the companion practice sections helps develop fine motor skills necessary for clean Hindi handwriting.
• **Oral Recitation Training:** Daily rhythmic chanting of "अ, आ, इ, ई, उ, ऊ, ऋ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ, अं, अः" builds auditory processing speed and natural tongue agility.
• **Pronunciation Correction:** Teachers use this chapter to correct common regional mother-tongue influences on vowel length (differentiating short chhota swar from long bada swar).

• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'अ':** The vowel **अ** (An-aa) is phonetically the most frequently occurring sound in Hindi, acting as the neutral anchor of the entire phonological system.
• **Anatomy of 'अ':** Writing 'अ' involves drawing an upper horizontal top-line (**Shirorekha**), two intersecting semi-circular loops on the left, a middle horizontal connector, and a straight vertical trailing line.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'अ':** Beyond **अनार** and **अमरूद**, students are encouraged to recognize words like **अखबार** (Newspaper) and **अमर** (Immortal) as advanced extensions of this vowel sound.
• **Writing Discipline for 'अ':** Instructors emphasize starting below the top shirorekha, ensuring the loops do not cross below the baseline, maintaining uniform font proportions.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'आ':** The vowel **आ** (Aa) builds directly upon the skeleton of 'अ' by appending an additional parallel vertical stroke on the right side, visually doubling the mouth-open duration.
• **Anatomy of 'आ':** Composed of the base 'अ' shape followed by a second vertical spine and the top covering shirorekha that unifies the entire compound character.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'आ':** In addition to **आम**, children learn words such as **आग** (Fire), **आम्र** (Mango formal), and **आकाश** (Sky) to grasp the lengthened initial syllable.
• **Writing Discipline for 'आ':** Spacing between the two vertical lines of 'आ' must remain consistent to prevent the letter from looking distorted or merged into another glyph.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'इ':** The vowel **इ** (Ih) represents a sharp drop in mouth volume, requiring a compressed horizontal tongue placement near the palate.
• **Anatomy of 'इ':** Built with a very short initial vertical drop, a right-facing loop twisting into a left-facing loop, and a bottom hook curling inward toward the right.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'इ':** Alongside **इमली**, students explore words like **इमारत** (Building) and **इस्त्री** (Iron for clothes) to reinforce the short 'I' sound contextually.
• **Writing Discipline for 'इ':** The top hook must cleanly touch the shirorekha, and the bottom curl must not drag below the imaginary writing line provided in DAV notebooks.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ई':** The vowel **ई** (Eeh) is the long counterpart of 'इ', distinguished graphically by the addition of a curved crown (**Matra-choti**) resting atop the shirorekha.
• **Anatomy of 'ई':** Shares the exact body structure of 'इ' with the crucial addition of an upward-facing curved hook extending above the top horizontal line.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ई':** Besides **ईख**, students encounter **ईश्वर** (God) and **ईद** (Eid festival) to understand the solemn, elongated pronunciation.
• **Writing Discipline for 'ई':** The upper curve must be neatly proportioned—neither too flat nor overly arched—maintaining aesthetic balance across the writing page.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'उ':** The vowel **उ** (Oo) initiates the back-rounded vowel series, focusing oral resonance in the back of the throat and mouth.
• **Anatomy of 'उ':** Formed by two stacked upward-facing semi-circular curves resembling the mathematical numeral '3' hanging from the shirorekha.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'उ':** In addition to **उल्लू**, learners discover **उपहार** (Gift) and **उद्यान** (Garden) to expand their environment-based vocabulary.

• **Writing Discipline for 'उ':** The bottom curve of 'उ' must touch or gently approach the imaginary baseline without sagging below the line boundary, ensuring clean composition.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ऊ':** The vowel **ऊ** (Ooo) extends the acoustic property of 'उ' into a prolonged drone, requiring tighter lip constriction and steady breath support.
• **Anatomy of 'ऊ':** Constructed identically to 'उ' with the addition of a backward-curling tail sweeping downwards from the middle junction of the two loops.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ऊ':** Alongside **ऊन**, students identify words like **ऊँट** (Camel) and **ऊपर** (Above), linking the vowel to spatial and zoological concepts.
• **Writing Discipline for 'ऊ':** The tail must curve gracefully toward the left baseline without looping back onto itself to maintain legibility in early childhood writing books.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ऋ':** The vowel **ऋ** (Ri) is an ancient phonetic sound preserved from Vedic Sanskrit, functioning as a syllabic consonant in traditional word roots.
• **Anatomy of 'ऋ':** Features a central vertical pillar, intersecting left-side angular slashes, and a distinct right-side loop terminating in a downward curved tail.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ऋ':** Beyond **ऋषि**, the curriculum introduces **ऋतु** (Season) and **ऋण** (Debt), exposing students to diverse semantic applications.
• **Writing Discipline for 'ऋ':** The complex geometry of 'ऋ' requires step-by-step stroke breakdown: vertical stroke first, left angular strokes second, and the right loop last.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ए':** The vowel **ए** (E) marks the transition into front vowels that combine palate placement with lip spreading, essential for compound words.
• **Anatomy of 'ए':** Composed of a down-slanting vertical line on the left, a joining slanted stroke from the right, and a separate top shirorekha.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ए':** In addition to **एड़ी**, students learn **एक** (One) and **एल्बम** (Album), integrating basic mathematical counting with linguistic learning.
• **Writing Discipline for 'ए':** The left vertical stroke must not curve too sharply; it requires a clean diagonal slope meeting the right-side descending arm.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ऐ':** The vowel **ऐ** (Ai) is an expanded front vowel sound characterized by a broader acoustic resonance and a dual-stroke crown marker.
• **Anatomy of 'ऐ':** Built on the exact body structure of 'ए' with the addition of a slanted, hook-tipped matra stroke resting on top of the shirorekha.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ऐ':** Alongside **ऐनक**, learners explore **ऐरावत** (Mythological white elephant of Indra) and **ऐश्वर्य** (Opulence) for advanced phonetic awareness.
• **Writing Discipline for 'ऐ':** The top slanted stroke must originate from the right upper edge of the shirorekha and point sharply toward the upper left corner.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'ओ':** The vowel **ओ** (O) combines the rounded lip posture of 'उ' with the mid-palate positioning of 'ए', creating a rich open-mid back vowel.
• **Anatomy of 'ओ':** Utilizes the base structure of 'आ' (with two vertical stems) accompanied by a single slanted overhead matra hook pointing rightward.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'ओ':** Beyond **ओखली**, children practice words like **ओस** (Dew) and **ओढ़नी** (Veil/Scarf), connecting weather phenomena and traditional attire.

• **Writing Discipline for 'ओ':** The overhead matra for 'ओ' must be positioned directly above the second vertical spine of the 'आ' base structure for visual symmetry.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'औ':** The vowel **औ** (Au) is the most open back-rounded vowel sound in the core Hindi series, requiring maximum vertical jaw drop and strong lip protrusion.
• **Anatomy of 'औ':** Built on the same dual-spine 'आ' base framework as 'ओ', but distinguished by possessing **two** overhead slanted matra hooks instead of one.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'औ':** In addition to **औरत**, students encounter **औजार** (Tools) and **औषधि** (Medicine/Drug), building pragmatic, real-world utility vocabulary.
• **Writing Discipline for 'औ':** Both overhead hooks of 'औ' must be drawn parallel to each other, resting securely upon the continuous shirorekha without crowding.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'अं':** The anusvara-bearing vowel **अं** (Am) introduces nasal resonance (**Nasikya dhvani**), where air escapes simultaneously through the nose and mouth.
• **Anatomy of 'अं':** Formed by the standard base vowel **अ** with a single distinct dot (**Bindu**) centered precisely above the top shirorekha.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'अं':** Alongside **अंगूर**, children learn **अंडा** (Egg), **अंगूठी** (Ring), and **अंगार** (Ember) to solidify nasalized initial vowel recognition.
• **Writing Discipline for 'अं':** The dot must float clearly above the shirorekha without touching the horizontal line itself, preventing confusion with punctuation or smudges.
• **Detailed Deep Dive into 'अः':** The visarga-bearing unit **अः** (Ah) represents a breathy, whispered aspiration originating from the glottis, frequently found in Sanskritized loanwords.
• **Anatomy of 'अः':** Formed by the standard base vowel **अ** followed by two vertically stacked dots placed immediately to its right margin.
• **Vocabulary Expansion for 'अः':** Since words starting with 'अः' are extremely rare in primary vocabulary, teachers explain it through words like **तः** or numerical suffixes, focusing on sound production.
• **Writing Discipline for 'अः':** The two dots of the visarga must be vertically aligned, spaced equally apart, and placed at a uniform distance from the right vertical edge of 'अ'.
• **Phonetic Classification - Hrasva Swar:** DAV curriculum categorizes **अ, इ, उ, ऋ** as **ह्रस्व स्वर** (Short vowels), requiring shorter duration and minimal muscular effort during articulation.
• **Phonetic Classification - Dirgha Swar:** DAV curriculum categorizes **आ, ई, ऊ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ** as **दीर्घ स्वर** (Long vowels), requiring sustained breath and broader oral cavity shaping.
• **Phonetic Classification - Ayogavaha:** **अं** and **अः** are scientifically classified as **आयोगवाह** (Neither purely vowels nor consonants), acting as dependent modifiers attached to base sounds.
