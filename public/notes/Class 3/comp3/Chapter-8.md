• **Computer Safety**: The practice of following specific rules and guidelines to ensure that both the user and the computer hardware remain protected from harm, damage, or accidents.
• **Importance of Safety**: Computers are electronic devices that use electricity, making it essential to handle them with care to prevent electrical shocks or hardware malfunctions.
• **Ergonomics**: The study of people's efficiency in their working environment, which includes how we sit and position ourselves while using a computer to avoid physical strain.
• **Posture**: Maintaining a straight back and keeping feet flat on the floor is critical to preventing long-term back pain and spinal issues during computer use.
• **Monitor Distance**: The computer screen should be kept at an arm's length distance from the eyes to reduce strain and prevent vision-related problems.
• **Eye Level**: The top of the monitor screen should be at or slightly below eye level to ensure the neck remains in a neutral, comfortable position.
• **Lighting**: Always use the computer in a well-lit room to prevent eye fatigue caused by excessive glare or working in complete darkness.
• **Keyboard Handling**: Pressing keys gently is necessary to maintain the longevity of the keyboard and prevent mechanical wear and tear.
• **Mouse Care**: The mouse should be kept on a clean, flat surface, preferably a mouse pad, to ensure smooth movement and prevent dust from entering the sensor.
• **Cables and Wires**: Never pull or tug on computer cables, as this can cause loose connections or damage the internal wiring of the peripherals.
• **Food and Drinks**: Eating or drinking near the computer is strictly prohibited because spills can cause short circuits and permanent damage to internal components.
• **Cleanliness**: Keeping the computer area free of dust and debris helps the machine run efficiently and prevents overheating.
• **Hand Hygiene**: Always wash and dry your hands before using the computer to prevent transferring oil, dirt, or moisture onto the keyboard and screen.
• **Rest Breaks**: Taking frequent breaks is essential to give your eyes and body time to recover from the repetitive nature of computer work.
• **Adult Supervision**: Younger students should always use the computer under the guidance of a teacher or parent to ensure safety protocols are followed.

• **Electrical Safety**: Computers operate on high-voltage electricity, which requires caution when handling power cords, plugs, and sockets.
• **Power Cords**: Never touch electrical wires or power cords with wet hands, as water is a conductor of electricity and can cause a dangerous shock.
• **Socket Overloading**: Avoid plugging too many devices into a single power strip or wall socket, as this can lead to overheating and potential fire hazards.
• **Switching Off**: Always shut down the computer properly using the software interface before turning off the main power switch to prevent data corruption.
• **Power Surges**: Using a UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) helps protect the computer from sudden voltage fluctuations that could damage sensitive hardware.
• **Loose Wires**: Ensure that all cables are tucked away neatly to prevent tripping hazards, which could lead to both personal injury and equipment damage.
• **Equipment Placement**: Place the CPU, monitor, and other hardware on a stable, sturdy desk to prevent them from falling or tipping over.
• **Ventilation**: The CPU cabinet requires proper airflow; never block the vents with books or papers, as this causes the processor to overheat.
• **Overheating**: If a computer feels excessively hot to the touch, it should be turned off immediately and reported to an adult or technician.
• **Hardware Handling**: Never try to open the computer cabinet or touch internal components, as these parts are delicate and carry electrical risks.
• **Peripheral Safety**: Devices like printers and scanners should be handled with care, ensuring that paper or objects do not get jammed in the moving parts.
• **Static Electricity**: Avoid wearing clothing that generates high static electricity when working with computer hardware to prevent damage to sensitive chips.
• **Emergency Procedures**: In the event of a burning smell or smoke coming from the computer, immediately disconnect the power and alert an adult.
• **Maintenance**: Regular cleaning of the computer should be done using only approved, non-abrasive materials and specialized cleaning solutions.
• **Professional Help**: Any technical issues or hardware malfunctions should be addressed by a professional technician rather than attempting DIY repairs.

• **Cyber Safety**: This refers to the precautions taken while using the internet to protect oneself from online threats, strangers, and inappropriate content.
• **Personal Information**: Never share your full name, home address, school name, or phone number with anyone online, as this information can be misused.
• **Passwords**: A password is a secret code used to protect your account; it should be kept private and never shared with friends or strangers.
• **Strong Passwords**: Create passwords that are difficult to guess by using a combination of letters, numbers, and special characters.
• **Stranger Danger**: Treat online interactions with the same caution as meeting strangers in the real world; never talk to people you do not know.
• **Inappropriate Content**: If you encounter a website or image that makes you feel uncomfortable or scared, close the window and inform an adult immediately.
• **Cyberbullying**: This involves using digital platforms to harass, threaten, or tease others; it is a serious issue that should be reported to parents or teachers.
• **Email Safety**: Never open emails or attachments from unknown senders, as they may contain viruses or harmful software.
• **Social Media**: Avoid creating accounts on social media platforms without parental permission and supervision.
• **Privacy Settings**: Always check the privacy settings on any online platform to ensure that your information is visible only to trusted friends and family.
• **Downloading Files**: Never download games, software, or files from the internet without the permission of an adult, as these may contain malware.
• **Clicking Links**: Be wary of clicking on suspicious links or pop-up advertisements, as they often lead to malicious websites.
• **Online Gaming**: Play games only on reputable and safe websites, and avoid sharing personal details within the game chat.
• **Reporting**: If you feel threatened or see something wrong online, use the "Report" button or tell a trusted adult right away.
• **Digital Footprint**: Remember that everything you post online leaves a permanent record, so think carefully before sharing any information or photos.

• **Software Safety**: Protecting the computer from malicious programs that can damage files, slow down the system, or steal information.
• **Computer Virus**: A type of malicious software that replicates itself and spreads to other files, causing the computer to malfunction.
• **Antivirus Software**: A program designed to detect, block, and remove viruses and other malicious software from the computer system.
• **Updates**: Regularly update the operating system and antivirus software to ensure the computer has the latest protection against new threats.
• **Software Licensing**: Only use legitimate, licensed software; pirated software is often bundled with viruses and is illegal to use.
• **Pop-up Blockers**: Use browser settings to block unwanted pop-up windows, which are often used to distribute malware or deceptive advertisements.
• **Data Backup**: Regularly save copies of important files on an external drive or cloud storage to prevent data loss in case of a system crash.
• **Phishing**: A deceptive practice where attackers pretend to be a trusted source to trick users into revealing sensitive information like passwords.
• **Spam**: Unsolicited and often harmful emails that should be deleted immediately without being opened.
• **Firewall**: A security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic to prevent unauthorized access to the computer.
• **System Scans**: Run scheduled antivirus scans to ensure that the computer remains free of hidden threats.
• **Installation Safety**: Only install programs from official websites or trusted app stores to avoid downloading harmful software.
• **User Accounts**: Use separate user accounts for different people on the same computer to keep personal files and settings secure.
• **Log Off**: Always log off or lock your computer when you are finished using it to prevent unauthorized access by others.
• **Security Awareness**: Stay informed about common online scams and threats to better protect yourself and your computer.

• **Physical Health**: Using a computer for extended periods can affect physical health, making it necessary to follow specific health-related precautions.
• **Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)**: A condition caused by repeating the same movements, such as typing or clicking, for too long without breaks.
• **Stretching**: Perform simple neck, shoulder, and wrist stretches during breaks to improve blood circulation and reduce muscle tension.
• **Blinking**: Consciously blink your eyes frequently while looking at the screen to prevent dry eyes and irritation.
• **20-20-20 Rule**: A recommended practice: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds to reduce eye strain.
• **Posture Correction**: If you feel pain or discomfort, adjust your chair or monitor height immediately to maintain a healthy posture.
• **Hydration**: Drink plenty of water while working to stay hydrated, but keep the water bottle away from the computer keyboard and mouse.
• **Ergonomic Furniture**: Use chairs that provide adequate back support to maintain the natural curve of the spine.
• **Footrest**: If your feet do not reach the floor comfortably, use a footrest to maintain proper leg alignment and reduce pressure on the thighs.
• **Wrist Support**: Use a wrist rest or mouse pad with a gel cushion to keep your wrists in a neutral position while typing or using the mouse.
• **Headset Usage**: If using headphones, keep the volume at a moderate level to protect your hearing from long-term damage.
• **Lighting Balance**: Avoid working in a room where the screen is brighter than the surrounding environment, as this increases eye fatigue.
• **Physical Activity**: Balance computer time with physical outdoor activities to maintain overall fitness and health.
• **Sleep Hygiene**: Avoid using computers or screens at least one hour before bedtime, as the blue light can interfere with sleep quality.
• **Listen to Your Body**: If you feel tired, dizzy, or experience pain, stop using the computer immediately and take a rest.

• **Classroom Computer Etiquette**: Rules for using computers in a shared environment like a school lab to ensure fairness and safety for everyone.
• **Queueing**: Always wait for your turn to use the computer and do not crowd around someone else's workstation.
• **Respecting Others**: Do not touch or interfere with the work of other students, as this is considered disrespectful and disruptive.
• **Quiet Environment**: Maintain a quiet atmosphere in the computer lab to allow everyone to focus on their tasks.
• **Teacher Instructions**: Always follow the specific instructions given by the teacher regarding the use of software and hardware.
• **Reporting Issues**: If you notice a computer is not working properly, report it to the teacher instead of trying to fix it yourself.
• **Lab Cleanliness**: Leave your workstation clean and tidy for the next student, ensuring no trash is left behind.
• **Chair Management**: Push your chair back into its proper place after you finish your session to keep the lab organized.
• **No Running**: Never run or play in the computer lab, as the presence of cables and equipment makes it a dangerous environment for horseplay.
• **Device Sharing**: Share resources like printers or scanners fairly and do not monopolize equipment.
• **Login Credentials**: Never use someone else's login credentials; always use your own assigned account.
• **Saving Work**: Save your work in the designated folder or drive as instructed by your teacher.
• **Closing Programs**: Properly close all applications and shut down the computer according to the lab's standard procedure.
• **Emergency Exits**: Be aware of the location of fire extinguishers and emergency exits in the computer lab.
• **Respecting Equipment**: Treat the school's computers as if they were your own, ensuring they are handled with care and respect.

• **Data Security**: The process of protecting digital information from unauthorized access, corruption, or theft.
• **File Privacy**: Do not store personal or sensitive information on a public or shared computer, as others may be able to access it.
• **Deleting Files**: Be careful when deleting files; ensure you are not removing important system files or someone else's work.
• **External Drives**: Always scan USB flash drives or external hard drives with an antivirus program before opening them on your computer.
• **Cloud Storage**: Use secure, password-protected cloud services for storing important documents, ensuring you log out after use.
• **Encryption**: A method of protecting data by converting it into a code that can only be read by authorized users.
• **Backups**: Keep multiple copies of important school projects on different storage devices to prevent data loss.
• **Phishing Awareness**: Learn to identify fake websites that ask for your personal information, such as your school login or password.
• **Secure Websites**: Look for the "https://" prefix and a padlock icon in the browser address bar, which indicates a secure connection.
• **Public Wi-Fi**: Avoid accessing sensitive accounts or personal information while connected to public, unsecured Wi-Fi networks.
• **Updates and Patches**: Keep your software updated, as developers often release patches to fix security vulnerabilities.
• **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)**: An extra layer of security that requires a second form of identification, such as a code sent to a phone.
• **Privacy Policy**: Read the privacy policy of websites or apps to understand how your data is being collected and used.
• **Sharing Files**: Only share files with people you know and trust, and avoid sending sensitive information via email.
• **Data Disposal**: Properly delete or format storage devices before disposing of them to ensure no data can be recovered by others.

• **Environmental Safety**: The impact of computers on the environment and how to use them in an eco-friendly manner.
• **Energy Conservation**: Turn off the computer and monitor when they are not in use to save electricity and reduce your carbon footprint.
• **Sleep Mode**: Use the "Sleep" or "Hibernate" mode if you are taking a short break instead of leaving the computer fully powered on.
• **Brightness Settings**: Lower the screen brightness to a comfortable level to save energy and reduce eye strain.
• **Paper Usage**: Think before you print; only print documents when absolutely necessary to save paper and ink.
• **Recycling**: Dispose of old computer parts, batteries, and cables at designated e-waste recycling centers rather than in regular trash.
• **Energy Star**: Look for the "Energy Star" label on computer equipment, which indicates that the device meets energy-efficiency standards.
• **Sustainable Habits**: Encourage others to follow energy-saving practices to create a more sustainable computer lab environment.
• **Hardware Longevity**: Taking good care of your computer hardware helps it last longer, reducing the need for frequent replacements and waste.
• **Digital Alternatives**: Use digital documents and online platforms for assignments to minimize the need for physical printing.
• **Power Strips**: Use power strips with an "Off" switch to completely cut off power to devices, preventing "vampire energy" consumption.
• **Refurbishing**: Consider donating or refurbishing old computers that are still functional instead of throwing them away.
• **Eco-friendly Cleaning**: Use environmentally friendly cleaning products for your computer screen and keyboard.
• **Awareness**: Educate your peers about the importance of reducing electronic waste and conserving energy.
• **Green Computing**: The practice of using computing resources efficiently to minimize the environmental impact of technology.

• **Troubleshooting Basics**: Simple, safe steps to take when a computer behaves unexpectedly, emphasizing safety over complex repairs.
• **Frozen Screen**: If the computer freezes, do not bang on the keyboard; try using the "Task Manager" to close the unresponsive program.
• **Restarting**: A simple restart can often resolve minor software glitches and clear temporary memory issues.
• **Error Messages**: Read error messages carefully; they often provide instructions on how to resolve the problem or what is causing it.
• **No Power**: Check if the power cable is firmly plugged into both the wall socket and the computer before assuming a major hardware failure.
• **Peripheral Connection**: If a mouse or keyboard is not working, check the USB connection and ensure it is plugged into the correct port.
• **Sound Issues**: Check the volume settings and ensure the speakers are connected and powered on before troubleshooting audio drivers.
• **Internet Connectivity**: If the internet is down, check the router or Wi-Fi connection status before reporting a network issue.
• **Software Crashes**: If an application crashes, try reopening it; if it happens repeatedly, report it to the teacher.
• **Overheating Signs**: If the computer fan is making a loud noise, it may be struggling with heat; ensure the vents are clear.
• **Unusual Behavior**: If the computer starts acting strangely, such as opening random windows, disconnect from the internet and alert an adult.
• **Data Recovery**: If you accidentally delete a file, check the "Recycle Bin" or "Trash" folder before panicking.
• **Documentation**: Keep a record of recurring issues to help the technician identify the root cause of the problem.
• **Patience**: Troubleshooting takes time; remain calm and follow logical steps rather than rushing the process.
• **Safety First**: Never attempt to fix hardware issues that involve opening the computer case, as this is dangerous and voids warranties.
