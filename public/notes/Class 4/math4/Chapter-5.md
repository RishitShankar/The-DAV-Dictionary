• **Definition of Length**: Length is a fundamental physical quantity that measures the distance between two points or the extent of an object from end to end.
• **Standard Unit**: The standard unit of length in the International System of Units (SI) is the **Metre (m)**.
• **Historical Context**: Historically, length was measured using non-standard units like hand spans, cubits, or foot-steps, which varied from person to person.
• **Need for Standardization**: Non-standard units are unreliable for trade and science, necessitating the adoption of universal standard units like the metre.
• **The Metre Scale**: A metre scale is a rigid rod or tape used to measure length, typically marked in centimetres and millimetres.
• **Centimetre (cm)**: A smaller unit of length used for measuring objects like pencils, books, or notebooks.
• **Relationship**: 1 metre is exactly equal to 100 centimetres (1 m = 100 cm).
• **Millimetre (mm)**: The smallest unit on a standard school ruler, used for measuring very thin objects like the thickness of a coin or a wire.
• **Relationship**: 1 centimetre is equal to 10 millimetres (1 cm = 10 mm).
• **Kilometre (km)**: A larger unit of length used to measure long distances, such as the distance between two cities or schools.
• **Relationship**: 1 kilometre is equal to 1000 metres (1 km = 1000 m).
• **Measuring Tools**: Different tools are used based on the object: rulers for small items, measuring tapes for cloth or body measurements, and odometers for long road distances.
• **Precision**: Accuracy in measurement requires placing the '0' mark of the ruler exactly at the starting edge of the object.
• **Parallax Error**: When reading a measurement, the eye must be positioned directly above the mark to avoid errors caused by viewing from an angle.
• **Measurement Technique**: Always ensure the object being measured is placed perfectly parallel to the scale to avoid diagonal measurement errors.

• **Conversion Concept**: Converting units involves changing a measurement from one unit to another while maintaining the same physical length.
• **Rule for km to m**: To convert kilometres to metres, multiply the number of kilometres by 1000.
• **Example of km to m**: 5 km = 5 × 1000 = 5000 metres.
• **Rule for m to cm**: To convert metres to centimetres, multiply the number of metres by 100.
• **Example of m to cm**: 8 m = 8 × 100 = 800 centimetres.
• **Rule for cm to mm**: To convert centimetres to millimetres, multiply the number of centimetres by 10.
• **Example of cm to mm**: 12 cm = 12 × 10 = 120 millimetres.
• **Combined Units**: Often, lengths are expressed in two units, such as 'metres and centimetres' (e.g., 5 m 25 cm).
• **Conversion of Combined Units**: To convert 5 m 25 cm to centimetres, convert the metres first (500 cm) and then add the remaining centimetres (500 + 25 = 525 cm).
• **Reverse Conversion (cm to m)**: To convert centimetres to metres, divide the number of centimetres by 100.
• **Remainder Rule**: When dividing by 100, the quotient represents the metres, and the remainder represents the centimetres.
• **Example of cm to m**: 450 cm ÷ 100 = 4 m and 50 cm.
• **Reverse Conversion (m to km)**: To convert metres to kilometres, divide the number of metres by 1000.
• **Quotient and Remainder**: In m to km conversion, the quotient is the kilometre value, and the remainder is the metre value.
• **Example of m to km**: 3500 m ÷ 1000 = 3 km and 500 m.
• **Logical Sequence**: Always identify the larger unit first to determine whether to multiply or divide.

• **Addition of Lengths**: Addition is used to find the total length of two or more objects placed end-to-end.
• **Column Method**: Always write units (km, m, cm) in separate columns to avoid mixing different magnitudes.
• **Alignment**: Ensure that the digits are aligned correctly according to their place value (ones, tens, hundreds).
• **Regrouping (Carrying)**: If the sum of the smaller unit (e.g., cm) exceeds its conversion limit (e.g., 100 cm), carry the excess over to the next larger unit (e.g., m).
• **Example of Addition**: Adding 15 m 45 cm and 12 m 60 cm involves adding cm first (105 cm), which becomes 1 m and 5 cm.
• **Final Sum**: The 1 m is carried over to the metre column, resulting in 28 m 5 cm.
• **Units Consistency**: Never add metres directly to centimetres without converting them to a common unit first.
• **Real-world Application**: Addition is used to calculate the total length of wire needed for multiple segments of a circuit.
• **Verification**: Always check the sum by adding the numbers in reverse order to ensure accuracy.
• **Place Value Importance**: Keeping columns straight prevents errors in the tens and hundreds places.
• **Zero Placeholder**: If a measurement is given as 5 m 5 cm, it must be written as 5 m 05 cm to maintain column integrity.
• **Step-by-Step Addition**: Always start from the smallest unit (e.g., mm or cm) and move towards the largest unit (e.g., km).
• **Summation of km**: When adding kilometres, the process is identical to adding whole numbers, provided the metre values are handled correctly.
• **Handling Carries**: A carry from the cm column to the m column is treated as 1 unit of metre.
• **Handling Carries**: A carry from the m column to the km column is treated as 1 unit of kilometre.

• **Subtraction of Lengths**: Subtraction is used to find the difference between two lengths or the remaining length after a portion is removed.
• **Setting up Subtraction**: Place the larger value on top and the smaller value below it, maintaining strict column separation.
• **Borrowing (Regrouping)**: If the value in the top row is smaller than the bottom row, borrow 1 unit from the next higher column.
• **Borrowing Conversion**: When borrowing 1 m for the cm column, it becomes 100 cm.
• **Borrowing Conversion**: When borrowing 1 km for the m column, it becomes 1000 m.
• **Example of Subtraction**: To subtract 10 m 80 cm from 15 m 20 cm, borrow 1 m from 15 m, making it 14 m and 120 cm.
• **Execution**: Subtract 80 cm from 120 cm (40 cm) and 10 m from 14 m (4 m), resulting in 4 m 40 cm.
• **Importance of Order**: Always ensure the larger length is the minuend (the number being subtracted from).
• **Verification**: Add the difference to the smaller length (subtrahend) to check if it equals the original larger length (minuend).
• **Column Integrity**: Maintain the separation of km, m, and cm columns throughout the entire calculation.
• **Zero Handling**: If the top value has a zero (e.g., 00 cm), borrowing is mandatory from the higher unit.
• **Logical Flow**: Subtraction helps in determining how much material is left after cutting a piece from a roll.
• **Precision**: Errors in borrowing are the most common cause of incorrect results in length subtraction.
• **Mental Check**: Estimate the answer first to ensure the final result is reasonable.
• **Consistency**: Always write the units clearly next to the final answer.

• **Multiplication of Lengths**: Multiplication is used when the same length is repeated multiple times.
• **Basic Principle**: Multiply the number of units by the multiplier, just like standard multiplication.
• **Conversion during Multiplication**: If the product of the smaller unit exceeds the conversion limit, convert it to the larger unit.
• **Example**: If multiplying 5 m 30 cm by 3, first multiply cm (90 cm) and then m (15 m).
• **Handling Excess**: If the cm product was 120 cm, you would keep 20 cm and carry 1 m to the metre column.
• **Multiplication of km**: Multiply the km value by the given number, ensuring any carry from the metre column is added.
• **Step-by-Step Process**: Multiply the smallest unit first, then move to the larger unit.
• **Carry-over Rule**: Any carry-over from the cm column must be added to the result of the m column multiplication.
• **Carry-over Rule**: Any carry-over from the m column must be added to the result of the km column multiplication.
• **Multiplication by 10, 100, 1000**: Use the shortcut of adding zeros to the right of the number for quick calculations.
• **Example of Shortcut**: 15 m × 100 = 1500 m.
• **Real-world Scenario**: Calculating the total length of 10 ribbons, each 2 m 50 cm long.
• **Verification**: Use repeated addition to verify the result of the multiplication.
• **Formatting**: Always write the units clearly in the final product.
• **Accuracy**: Ensure the multiplier is placed correctly under the multiplicand.

• **Division of Lengths**: Division is used to distribute a total length into equal parts or segments.
• **Basic Principle**: Divide the larger unit first, then the smaller unit.
• **Remainder Handling**: If there is a remainder after dividing the larger unit, convert it to the smaller unit and add it to the existing smaller unit value.
• **Example**: Dividing 10 m 40 cm by 2. Divide 10 m by 2 to get 5 m. Divide 40 cm by 2 to get 20 cm. Result: 5 m 20 cm.
• **Complex Division**: Dividing 7 m 50 cm by 3. Divide 7 m by 3 (2 m with 1 m remainder).
• **Conversion**: Convert the 1 m remainder to 100 cm and add to 50 cm (150 cm).
• **Final Step**: Divide 150 cm by 3 to get 50 cm. Final result: 2 m 50 cm.
• **Division of km**: Follow the same process: divide km, convert remainder to m, add to existing m, then divide m.
• **Long Division**: Use the long division method to ensure each step is tracked accurately.
• **Check**: Multiply the quotient by the divisor to see if it returns the original total length.
• **Units**: Always ensure the quotient has the correct units attached.
• **Logical Application**: Used for cutting a long rope into equal pieces for a craft project.
• **Precision**: Double-check the conversion of the remainder, as this is where most errors occur.
• **Consistency**: Maintain the same unit throughout the division process.
• **Division by 10, 100, 1000**: Use the decimal shift or place value removal for quick division.

• **Word Problems**: Word problems require identifying the operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division) based on keywords.
• **Keyword 'Total' or 'Sum'**: Indicates that addition is required.
• **Keyword 'Difference', 'Left', 'Remaining', or 'How much more'**: Indicates that subtraction is required.
• **Keyword 'Each', 'Every', or 'Per' (given the total)**: Indicates multiplication.
• **Keyword 'Divide', 'Equal parts', or 'Each' (given the total)**: Indicates division.
• **Step 1: Identification**: Read the problem carefully to identify the given values and the units.
• **Step 2: Planning**: Decide which mathematical operation is necessary to solve the problem.
• **Step 3: Execution**: Perform the calculation using the column method for the specific units.
• **Step 4: Conclusion**: Write a concluding statement with the final answer and the correct unit.
• **Example Problem**: "A tailor has 10 m of cloth. He uses 3 m 50 cm. How much is left?"
• **Solution Strategy**: Subtract 3 m 50 cm from 10 m 00 cm.
• **Borrowing**: Borrow 1 m from 10 m, making it 9 m and 100 cm.
• **Calculation**: 100 cm - 50 cm = 50 cm; 9 m - 3 m = 6 m.
• **Final Answer**: 6 m 50 cm of cloth is left.
• **Importance of Units**: Always ensure the units in the word problem are consistent before starting the calculation.
