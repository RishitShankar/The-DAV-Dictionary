• **Concept of Time:** Time is a continuous measurement of the duration of events and the intervals between them.
• **The Clock:** A clock is the primary instrument used to measure, keep, and indicate time.
• **Clock Face:** The front surface of a clock is called the dial or face, which displays the numbers 1 to 12.
• **Clock Hands:** Most analog clocks have two main hands: the **Hour Hand** (shorter) and the **Minute Hand** (longer).
• **Hour Hand:** This hand moves slowly and completes one full rotation around the clock face in 12 hours.
• **Minute Hand:** This hand moves faster and completes one full rotation around the clock face in 60 minutes.
• **Second Hand:** Some clocks have a third, thinner hand that moves continuously to track seconds.
• **The Number System:** The numbers 1 through 12 represent the hours of the day.
• **Division of Time:** One hour is divided into 60 equal parts, each representing one minute.
• **Minute Markers:** The space between each number on the clock face represents 5 minutes.
• **Reading the Hour:** When the hour hand points to a number, it indicates the current hour.
• **Reading the Minutes:** To read minutes, multiply the number the minute hand points to by 5.
• **O'Clock:** When the minute hand is exactly on the 12, the time is read as "o'clock."
• **Half Past:** When the minute hand is on the 6, it has covered half the clock (30 minutes), referred to as "half past."
• **Quarter Past:** When the minute hand is on the 3, it has covered one-quarter of the clock (15 minutes), referred to as "quarter past."
• **Quarter To:** When the minute hand is on the 9, it is 15 minutes away from the next hour, referred to as "quarter to."

• **Day and Night:** A full day consists of 24 hours, divided into daytime and nighttime.
• **Rotation of Earth:** The cycle of day and night is caused by the rotation of the Earth on its axis.
• **AM (Ante Meridiem):** This Latin term means "before midday" and covers the time from midnight to just before noon.
• **PM (Post Meridiem):** This Latin term means "after midday" and covers the time from noon to just before midnight.
• **Noon:** The exact time of 12:00 PM is called noon or midday.
• **Midnight:** The exact time of 12:00 AM is called midnight, marking the start of a new day.
• **24-Hour Clock:** Some systems use a 24-hour cycle to avoid confusion between AM and PM.
• **Sequence of Hours:** There are 24 hours in one complete day.
• **Daily Routine:** Time helps us organize our daily activities like waking up, eating, and sleeping.
• **Duration:** The length of time an activity takes is called its duration.
• **Early Morning:** The time just after sunrise is typically referred to as early morning.
• **Late Night:** The time after sunset and before midnight is referred to as late night.
• **Time Intervals:** We use time to measure how long we spend at school, playing, or studying.
• **Standardization:** Clocks ensure that everyone follows the same time schedule.
• **Analog Clocks:** These clocks use hands to point to numbers on a circular face.
• **Digital Clocks:** These clocks display time using numbers (digits) directly, such as 10:45.

• **Calendar:** A calendar is a chart or device used to track days, weeks, and months of a year.
• **The Week:** A week consists of 7 consecutive days.
• **Days of the Week:** The sequence is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday.
• **Work Week:** In most schools, Monday through Friday are considered working days.
• **Weekend:** Saturday and Sunday are typically referred to as the weekend.
• **The Month:** A month is a unit of time used in calendars, approximately as long as a natural orbit of the moon.
• **Days in a Month:** Months have either 28, 29, 30, or 31 days.
• **January:** The first month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **February:** The second month, which has 28 days in a common year and 29 days in a leap year.
• **March:** The third month, containing 31 days.
• **April:** The fourth month, containing 30 days.
• **May:** The fifth month, containing 31 days.
• **June:** The sixth month, containing 30 days.
• **July:** The seventh month, containing 31 days.
• **August:** The eighth month, containing 31 days.
• **September:** The ninth month, containing 30 days.

• **October:** The tenth month, containing 31 days.
• **November:** The eleventh month, containing 30 days.
• **December:** The twelfth and final month of the year, containing 31 days.
• **Year:** A year is the time taken by the Earth to complete one revolution around the Sun.
• **Days in a Year:** A common year consists of 365 days.
• **Leap Year:** A year with 366 days, occurring every four years to keep the calendar aligned with the Earth's orbit.
• **Leap Day:** The extra day added to February in a leap year, making it 29 days long.
• **Calculating Leap Years:** Any year divisible by 4 is generally a leap year.
• **Sequence of Months:** It is essential to memorize the order of the 12 months to read dates correctly.
• **Date Format:** Dates are usually written in the format: Day/Month/Year (e.g., 15/08/2023).
• **Yesterday:** The day that came immediately before today.
• **Today:** The current day.
• **Tomorrow:** The day that will come immediately after today.
• **Calendar Utility:** Calendars help us mark important dates like birthdays, festivals, and exams.
• **Seasonal Cycles:** The calendar helps track the changing seasons throughout the year.
• **Historical Records:** Dates are used to record when specific events occurred in history.

• **1 Year = 365 Days:** The total number of days in a common year.
• **1 Leap Year = 366 Days:** The total number of days in a leap year.
