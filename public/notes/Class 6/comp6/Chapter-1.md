• **Core Subject Focus**: This chapter introduces young learners to the foundational concepts of computer networking, interconnections, and resource sharing.
• **Academic Authority**: Prepared by the Google Gemini Academic Notes Author strictly following the DAV Public School Board curriculum guidelines.
• **Definition of a Computer**: A computer is an electronic device that processes raw data into meaningful information based on programmed instructions.
• **Limitation of Standalone Computers**: Historically, individual computers worked in isolation, limiting file sharing, communication, and collaborative tasks.
• **Concept of Connection**: Connecting two or more computing devices together transforms individual machines into an integrated system capable of communication.
• **Introduction to Networks**: A **Computer Network** is defined as a collection of two or more computers and other computing devices linked together to share data, hardware, and software resources.
• **Real-World Analogy**: A computer network is analogous to a postal service or a telephone system where multiple endpoints connect to exchange messages and goods.
• **Primary Objective**: The fundamental goal of building a computer network is to facilitate seamless communication and resource optimization among users.
• **Data Sharing**: Networks allow users to send and receive digital files, documents, images, and videos instantly across rooms, buildings, or continents.
• **Hardware Sharing**: Expensive peripheral devices such as laser printers, scanners, and high-capacity storage drives can be shared among multiple users on a network.
• **Software Sharing**: Software applications can be installed on a central server, allowing multiple network clients to access and run programs without individual local installations.
• **Centralized Data Management**: Networks enable organizations to store critical files in a centralized location, ensuring easier backups, security, and version control.
• **Communication Medium**: Network participants can communicate via emails, instant messaging, audio calls, and video conferencing in real-time.
• **Ubiquitous Connectivity**: Modern networks bridge geographical gaps, enabling global collaboration, remote learning, and digital transactions.

• **Nodes in a Network**: Every individual device connected to a computer network, such as a desktop, laptop, printer, or server, is referred to as a **Node**.
• **Sender and Receiver**: In any data transmission, the device originating the data is the sender, and the device accepting the data is the receiver.
• **Communication Channel**: A transmission medium or pathway is required to carry data signals from the sender node to the receiver node.
• **Wired Networks**: Networks that use physical cables like copper wires or fiber optics to connect devices are known as wired networks.
• **Wireless Networks**: Networks that utilize invisible waves such as radio waves, infrared, or microwaves to connect devices without physical cables are wireless networks.
• **Data Packets**: Large files are broken down into smaller, manageable chunks called **Packets** before being transmitted across a network.
• **Packet Reassembly**: Upon reaching the destination node, the individual data packets are reassembled into their original file format.
• **Network Interface Card (NIC)**: A hardware component installed in a computer that enables it to connect to a network physically or wirelessly.
• **MAC Address**: Every NIC is assigned a unique hardware identifier known as a Media Access Control (MAC) address for network identification.
• **IP Address**: An Internet Protocol (IP) address is a logical numerical label assigned to every device participating in a computer network.
• **Network Protocol**: A set of standardized rules and guidelines that govern how data is formatted, transmitted, and received across a network.
• **Client-Server Architecture**: A network model where powerful central computers (servers) manage resources and provide services to other requesting computers (clients).
• **Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Architecture**: A decentralized network model where every computer (peer) on the network has equal capabilities and responsibilities.
• **Local Scope**: Networks can range from a few devices in a single room to millions of devices spread across the globe.
• **Scalability**: Computer networks are designed to be scalable, allowing new devices to be added or removed with minimal disruption.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Classification by Scale**: Computer networks are primarily classified based on their geographical coverage area and physical scale.
• **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A **PAN** is a computer network organized around an individual person within a single room or immediate workspace, typically covering a range of 10 meters.
• **PAN Examples**: Connecting a smartphone to a wireless Bluetooth headset, smartwatch, or wireless mouse forms a typical Personal Area Network.
• **PAN Technologies**: Personal Area Networks commonly rely on short-range wireless technologies like Bluetooth, Zigbee, or Infrared (IR).
• **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A **LAN** connects computers and devices within a limited geographical area such as a single home, school laboratory, office building, or hospital.
• **LAN Characteristics**: Local Area Networks offer extremely high data transfer rates, low error rates, and secure private ownership.
• **School Lab LAN**: In a DAV school computer laboratory, all student terminals connected to a central switch or server constitute a classic Local Area Network.
• **Ethernet Cables**: Most traditional LANs use twisted-pair copper cables known as Ethernet cables (Cat5e/Cat6) for reliable physical connections.
• **Wi-Fi LAN (WLAN)**: A wireless local area network uses radio frequency technology to connect devices to a local network without physical cables, commonly called Wi-Fi.
• **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A **MAN** is a larger computer network that spans an entire city, town, or specific municipal region.
• **MAN Coverage**: Metropolitan Area Networks typically cover geographic ranges spanning from 5 to 50 kilometers, bridging multiple LANs together.
• **MAN Examples**: Cable television networks spread across a city or a university campus network spanning multiple city blocks are examples of MANs.
• **MAN Ownership**: MANs are often owned and operated by consortiums of users or single large network service providers (telecom operators).
• **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A **WAN** is a massive computer network that spans large geographical distances, connecting countries, continents, or the entire globe.
• **WAN Connectivity**: Wide Area Networks connect multiple smaller networks, such as LANs and MANs, using leased telecommunication lines, satellites, and fiber-optic cables.

• **The Ultimate WAN**: The **Internet** is universally recognized as the largest and most prominent example of a Wide Area Network in existence.
• **Global Interconnection**: The Internet connects billions of individual devices, enterprise networks, and governmental systems worldwide.
• **Packet Switching in WAN**: Wide Area Networks heavily rely on packet switching technologies to route data efficiently across complex global pathways.
• **Network Topology Definition**: **Network Topology** refers to the physical or logical arrangement of various nodes and communication links in a computer network.
• **Geometric Arrangement**: Topologies define how devices are geometrically or functionally linked to one another in a network structure.
• **Physical vs Logical Topology**: Physical topology describes the actual physical layout of cables and hardware, while logical topology describes how data flows within the network.
• **Selection Criteria**: Choosing a specific network topology depends on factors like cost, scalability, fault tolerance, and application requirements.
• **Bus Topology**: A network layout where all devices are connected to a single central communication line, known as the **Bus** or backbone cable.
• **Bus Operation**: Data transmitted by any node travels along the length of the bus in both directions until it reaches the intended destination node.
• **Terminators in Bus**: Special hardware devices called **Terminators** are attached to both ends of the bus cable to prevent signal reflection and data corruption.
• **Bus Pros and Cons**: While Bus topology is inexpensive and easy to set up for small networks, a break in the main backbone cable brings down the entire network.
• **Star Topology**: A network layout where every node is individually connected to a central hub, switch, or central computer.
• **Star Central Node**: In a Star topology, all data traffic passes through the central device, which acts as a traffic controller and repeater.
• **Star Fault Tolerance**: If one cable or node fails in a Star topology, only that specific device is disconnected, leaving the rest of the network fully operational.
• **Star Dominance**: Star topology is currently the most popular and widely implemented physical topology in modern homes, schools, and offices.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Ring Topology**: A network layout where devices are connected in a circular loop or ring configuration, with each device connected directly to two other devices.
• **Token Passing in Ring**: Data in a Ring topology travels in only one direction around the ring, often utilizing a special electronic signal called a **Token** to control access.
• **Ring Data Flow**: When a node wants to transmit data, it captures the token, attaches data and destination address, and releases it back onto the ring.
• **Ring Vulnerability**: In a traditional Ring topology, if a single cable breaks or a node malfunctions, the communication ring is broken, halting the entire network.
• **Mesh Topology**: A network layout where every single device is connected directly to every other device in the network, creating multiple redundant pathways.
• **Mesh Redundancy**: Mesh topology offers extraordinary reliability and fault tolerance because data can dynamically reroute through alternative paths if one link fails.
• **Full vs Partial Mesh**: A full mesh connects every node to all other nodes, whereas a partial mesh connects certain critical nodes with redundant links.
• **Mesh Complexity**: The primary disadvantage of a Mesh topology is the extremely high cost of cabling, hardware ports, and installation complexity.
• **Tree Topology**: A network layout that combines characteristics of Star and Bus topologies, arranged in a hierarchy resembling branches on a tree.
• **Hybrid Topology**: A network design that integrates two or more different basic topologies (such as Star-Ring or Star-Bus) into a single large system.
• **Network Hardware Essentials**: Building a functioning computer network requires specialized hardware devices that facilitate communication, routing, and signal boosting.
• **Network Hub**: A basic, non-intelligent hardware device that connects multiple computers in a LAN, broadcasting incoming data packets to all connected ports indiscriminately.
• **Network Switch**: An intelligent networking device that connects devices on a LAN and forwards data packets exclusively to the specific destination device based on MAC addresses.
• **Router Definition**: A **Router** is an advanced networking device that connects multiple distinct networks together and determines the optimal path for data forwarding.
• **Gateway Function**: A gateway acts as a bridge or translation node between two networks using different communication protocols or architectures.

• **Modem Basics**: A **Modem** (Modulator-Demodulator) is a crucial hardware device that connects a home or office network to the Internet Service Provider (ISP).
• **Modulation Process**: The modem converts digital data signals from a computer into analog signals suitable for transmission over telephone or cable lines (Modulation).
• **Demodulation Process**: The modem converts incoming analog signals from the telephone line back into digital data packets that a computer can process (Demodulation).
• **Wireless Access Point (WAP)**: A WAP is a networking hardware device that allows Wi-Fi compatible wireless devices to connect to a wired network.
• **Repeater and Range Extender**: A repeater is a device that receives a weak network signal, amplifies it, and retransmits it to extend the coverage area.
• **Network Media**: The physical or wireless channels used to link network nodes are broadly categorized as guided (wired) and unguided (wireless) media.
• **Twisted-Pair Cable**: The most common network cable, consisting of pairs of insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference (EMI).
• **Shielded vs Unshielded**: Twisted-pair cables are classified as UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair) or STP (Shielded Twisted Pair) depending on extra foil shielding.
• **Coaxial Cable**: A type of copper cable featuring an inner conductor surrounded by an insulating layer and a conductive braided shield, commonly used in cable TV.
• **Fiber-Optic Cable**: An advanced high-speed transmission medium made of hair-thin strands of pure glass or plastic that transmit data using pulses of light.
• **Speed of Light Transmission**: Fiber-optic cables offer massive bandwidth and near light-speed data transmission over immense distances without signal degradation.
• **Radio Waves**: Omnidirectional wireless transmission media used extensively for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, mobile phones, and radio broadcasting.
• **Microwave Communication**: Line-of-sight wireless communication using high-frequency radio waves to transmit data between earth stations and communication towers.
• **Satellite Communication**: Wireless transmission utilizing artificial satellites orbiting the Earth to relay signals across vast global distances and remote locations.
• **Infrared Waves**: Short-range wireless communication media used in household remote controls and legacy device pairing, requiring direct line-of-sight.

• **Introduction to Network Protocols**: A protocol is a set of rules, standards, and conventions that govern how data is transmitted and processed across a network.
• **Protocol Analogy**: Similar to human languages having grammar and grammar rules, computers must follow agreed-upon protocols to understand each other's messages.
• **Transmission Control Protocol (TCP)**: TCP is a core protocol responsible for breaking data into packets at the sender side and reassembling them accurately at the receiver side.
• **Internet Protocol (IP)**: IP is the protocol responsible for addressing, routing, and delivering data packets across interconnected networks to their final destination.
• **TCP/IP Suite**: The combination of TCP and IP forms the foundational communication protocol suite of the modern Internet.
• **Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP)**: HTTP is the protocol used by web browsers and servers to transfer web pages, images, and multimedia files across the World Wide Web.
• **HTTPS Security**: **HTTPS** (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure) incorporates encryption (SSL/TLS) to secure data communication between a user's browser and a website.
• **File Transfer Protocol (FTP)**: FTP is a dedicated network protocol used for uploading and downloading files between client computers and remote servers over a network.
• **Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP)**: SMTP is the standard protocol utilized for sending and transmitting electronic mail (emails) across computer networks.
• **Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP)**: DHCP is a network management protocol that automatically assigns IP addresses and configuration parameters to devices on a network.
• **Domain Name System (DNS)**: DNS acts as the phonebook of the Internet, translating human-readable domain names (like www.dav.org) into machine-readable IP addresses.
• **The Internet**: The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that use the standard Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP) to serve billions of users worldwide.
• **ARPANET Origins**: The Internet originated from **ARPANET** (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network), created by the United States Department of Defense in the late 1960s.
• **World Wide Web (WWW)**: Invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, the **WWW** is a vast collection of interlinked multimedia documents and web pages accessed via the Internet.
• **Web Page vs Website**: A web page is a single document written in HTML, while a website is a collection of related web pages hosted under a single domain name.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **Web Browser**: A software application used to access, retrieve, and view web pages and multimedia content on the World Wide Web.
• **Popular Web Browsers**: Examples of widely used web browsers include Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, Apple Safari, and Opera.
• **Uniform Resource Locator (URL)**: A **URL** is the unique address of a specific web page, file, or resource located on the Internet (e.g., https://www.dav.org).
• **Components of a URL**: A standard URL consists of a protocol (https://), a domain name (www.dav.org), and a specific path or file directory.
• **Search Engine**: A specialized web-based software tool designed to search for information across the World Wide Web based on user query keywords.
• **Search Engine Examples**: Prominent search engines include Google Search, Microsoft Bing, Yahoo!, and DuckDuckGo.
• **Cloud Computing Basics**: Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services—including data storage, servers, databases, networking, and software—over the Internet ("the cloud").
• **Benefits of Cloud Computing**: Cloud technology eliminates the need for local hardware maintenance, offering scalability, remote data access, and automated backups.
• **Cloud Storage Examples**: Popular cloud storage and file-sharing services include Google Drive, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox, and Apple iCloud.
• **Network Security Importance**: Securing computer networks is essential to protect sensitive information, prevent unauthorized access, and ensure operational continuity.
• **Cybersecurity Threats**: Networks face various threats from malicious software (malware), computer viruses, hackers, ransomware, and phishing attacks.
• **Firewall Protection**: A **Firewall** is a network security system (hardware or software) that monitors and filters incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined security rules.
• **Antivirus Software**: Software applications installed on network nodes to detect, quarantine, and eliminate computer viruses and malicious software threats.
• **Strong Passwords**: Enforcing strong passwords consisting of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols is a fundamental network security practice.
• **Data Encryption**: The process of converting plaintext data into unreadable ciphertext using cryptographic algorithms to secure data during transmission.

• **Network Administration**: Managing a computer network involves configuration, user account management, performance monitoring, and ensuring hardware reliability.
• **Network Administrator**: A trained professional responsible for maintaining network infrastructure, troubleshooting connectivity issues, and implementing security policies.
• **User Permissions and Access Control**: Network administrators assign specific access rights to users, ensuring individuals can only access files and folders relevant to their roles.
• **Network Bandwidth**: Bandwidth refers to the maximum rate of data transfer across a given path, usually measured in Megabits per second (Mbps) or Gigabits per second (Gbps).
• **Latency in Networks**: Latency is the time delay experienced between the initiation of a data transfer request and the actual receipt of the response across a network.
• **Ping Command**: A network diagnostic utility tool used to test the reachability of a host on an Internet Protocol network and measure round-trip time.
• **Packet Loss**: A network condition where one or more data packets fail to reach their intended destination, resulting in poor connection quality or lag.
• **Intranet Definition**: An **Intranet** is a private, password-protected computer network accessible only to an organization's internal staff members.
• **Extranet Definition**: An **Extranet** is a controlled private network that allows authorized external partners, vendors, and customers to access specific internal resources.
• **Intranet vs Internet**: Unlike the public Internet, an Intranet is restricted, highly secure, and tailored exclusively for internal institutional communication and resource sharing.
• **Wi-Fi Technology**: Wireless Fidelity (Wi-Fi) utilizes wireless local area networking technology based on IEEE 802.11 standards to connect devices to the internet.
• **Bluetooth Technology**: A short-range wireless communication technology designed for exchanging data over short distances between fixed and mobile devices.
• **Hotspot Creation**: A physical location or software feature that provides internet access to mobile devices via a wireless local area network using a router.
• **IoT (Internet of Things)**: The network of physical objects ("things") embedded with sensors, software, and technologies for connecting and exchanging data with other devices over the Internet.
• **Smart Home Concept**: Home automation systems connect lights, thermostats, security cameras, and appliances into a localized IoT network manageable via smartphones.
