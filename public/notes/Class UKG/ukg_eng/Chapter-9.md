• **Target Audience Alignment:** Written specifically for UKG (Upper Kindergarten) learners, the language and concepts are kept concrete, highly relatable, and visually descriptive to foster early curiosity.

• **Observation Skills:** Students are encouraged to develop early scientific observation skills by looking at everyday water phenomena, such as boiling, freezing, and dissolving household items.
• **Health and Hygiene Link:** The chapter emphasizes personal hygiene and health, teaching children that clean water is necessary for washing hands, bathing, brushing teeth, and staying hydrated.

• **Parent-Teacher Collaboration:** The chapter promotes collaborative learning, encouraging parents and teachers to perform simple water experiments together in a safe environment.

• **Safety First:** All experiments outlined in the chapter and notes are designed to be performed under strict adult supervision using everyday, non-toxic household items.
• **Structured Progression:** The subsequent pages of this study note will progressively cover water sources, daily uses, physical states, properties, and step-by-step experiment analyses.

• **Natural Sources of Water:** Water exists naturally in various forms and locations across our planet, forming the hydrosphere that sustains all known life forms.
• **Rain as Primary Source:** **Rain** is identified as the primary and most important natural source of fresh water on Earth, replenishing rivers, lakes, and underground reservoirs.
• **Rivers and Streams:** **Rivers** are flowing bodies of fresh water that wind their way across the land, providing drinking water and habitats for aquatic animals and plants.
• **Lakes and Ponds:** **Lakes and ponds** are still bodies of fresh water collected in natural depressions on the Earth's surface, serving as vital water resources.
• **Oceans and Seas:** **Oceans** contain vast expanses of salty water that cover the majority of the Earth's surface, though this water is not directly fit for drinking without treatment.
• **Underground Water:** Rainwater that seeps deep into the soil becomes **groundwater**, which is accessed using wells, tube wells, and natural springs.
• **Glaciers and Ice Caps:** Frozen water stored in high mountains and polar regions forms **glaciers**, which slowly melt to feed major river systems during warmer months.
• **Man-Made Water Sources:** Humans build dams, reservoirs, canals, overhead tanks, and handpumps to store and access water for daily consumption and agricultural irrigation.
• **Cloud Formation:** Water from oceans and rivers evaporates into the air due to the sun's heat, forming **clouds** that eventually bring rain back down to the earth.
• **Water Cycle Introduction:** A simplified version of the water cycle is introduced, showing how water moves continuously from the earth to the sky and back down again.
• **Freshwater Scarcity:** The chapter notes that only a very small fraction of Earth's water is fresh and drinkable, making it precious and limited.
• **Visual Identification:** Students learn to identify water sources through colorful textbook illustrations showing rainy days, flowing rivers, and deep blue oceans.
• **Vocabulary Reinforcement:** New nouns such as *rain, river, lake, ocean, well, and cloud* are practiced through spelling exercises and picture-matching activities.
• **Interactive Discussion:** Teachers prompt students to share where they have seen water outside their homes, connecting classroom learning to personal life experiences.
• **Foundational Geography:** Understanding water sources lays the groundwork for later geographical studies in primary school regarding landforms and natural resources.

• **Daily Uses of Water by Humans:** Water is indispensable for human survival, hygiene, and domestic activities, being required from the moment we wake up until we go to sleep.
• **Drinking and Hydration:** **Drinking** clean, filtered water is vital for keeping our bodies hydrated, regulating body temperature, and flushing out bodily toxins.
• **Cooking and Food Preparation:** Water is used extensively in the kitchen for washing vegetables, boiling rice, making soups, and cooking various daily meals.
• **Bathing and Personal Cleanliness:** Taking a bath with clean water and soap keeps our skin clean, fresh, and free from harmful germs, dirt, and sweat.
• **Brushing Teeth:** Using a small mug of water to brush teeth and rinse the mouth maintains good oral hygiene and prevents tooth decay.
• **Washing Clothes and Utensils:** Water combined with detergents is used to wash dirty clothes, bedsheets, plates, spoons, and cooking pots to maintain a hygienic household.
• **Cleaning the House:** Mopping floors, wiping windows, and flushing toilets rely completely on an uninterrupted supply of water.
• **Water for Animals:** Domestic pets like dogs and cats, farm animals like cows and horses, and wild animals all need water daily to stay alive and healthy.
• **Water for Plants:** **Plants need water** to grow, make their food through photosynthesis, and keep their leaves green and upright instead of drooping.
• **Gardening Activities:** Children learn to water potted plants and school gardens using small watering cans, fostering a sense of responsibility toward nature.
• **Agricultural Importance:** Farmers use large amounts of water to irrigate crop fields, ensuring that we get wheat, rice, fruits, and vegetables to eat.
• **Industrial and Public Uses:** Water is used in factories to make products, by firefighters to put out fires, and in public swimming pools for recreation.
• **The "Thirsty" Concept:** The physical sensation of feeling **thirsty** is taught as the body's natural signal that it needs a fresh glass of water.
• **Clean vs. Dirty Water:** DAV curriculum emphasizes that drinking dirty water causes diseases, which is why we must always drink boiled or filtered water.
• **Conservation Mindset:** Students learn simple habits like turning off taps tightly after use and reporting leaking pipes to adults to save water.

• **Introduction to Water Experiments:** To make learning scientific and experiential, Chapter 9 introduces safe, hands-on classroom experiments that demonstrate the basic physical properties of water.
• **Objective of Experiments:** The primary goal of these early experiments is to cultivate curiosity, critical thinking, and observational skills among UKG students through play-way methods.
• **Safety Guidelines:** Teachers must ensure that all experiment materials are child-safe, non-toxic, break-resistant (plastic cups and bowls), and conducted under close adult supervision.
• **Property 1: Water has No Shape:** **Water takes the shape** of whatever container it is poured into, whether it is a round bowl, a tall glass, or a flat plate.
• **Container Experiment:** Pouring water from a jug into different shaped containers visually proves to children that liquid does not have a fixed shape of its own.
• **Property 2: Water is Colorless:** Pure, clean water has **no color** of its own; it is completely transparent and allows light to pass straight through it.
• **Transparency Test:** Placing a colored object or a printed letter behind a clear glass of water shows children how clearly things can be seen through water.
• **Property 3: Water is Odorless and Tasteless:** Pure water has **no smell (odorless)** and **no distinct taste (tasteless)**, distinguishing it from milk, juice, or soda.
• **Property 4: Water Flows:** Water is a fluid that always **flows downward** from higher levels to lower levels due to the pull of gravity.
• **Flow Demonstration:** Pouring water gently on a tilted plastic tray demonstrates how rapidly liquids spread and flow across surfaces.
• **Recording Observations:** Students are encouraged to use descriptive words like "wet," "clear," "runs," and "flat" to describe what they see during each experiment.
• **Teacher-Led Demonstrations:** Most experiments in UKG are performed as live teacher demonstrations with active student participation and questioning.
• **Engaging Curiosity:** Asking predictive questions like "What do you think will happen if we do this?" stimulates young brains to hypothesize before observing outcomes.
• **Connecting Science to Life:** Teachers link these experimental properties back to daily life, explaining why water fills our cups easily and flows smoothly from taps.
• **Foundational Physics:** These basic explorations lay the groundwork for understanding states of matter (solids, liquids, gases) in higher primary grades.

• **The Concept of Floating and Sinking:** One of the most exciting hands-on activities in Chapter 9 is exploring why some objects float on water while others sink to the bottom.
• **Definition of Floating:** **Floating** occurs when an object stays on the surface of the water without sinking down, supported by the water's upward force (buoyancy).
• **Definition of Sinking:** **Sinking** happens when an object is heavier or denser than water and drops straight down to the bottom of the container.
• **Experiment Setup:** A large, transparent tub filled with clean water is placed in the center of the classroom for the floating and sinking demonstration.
• **Item Selection:** Teachers gather a collection of everyday items, including plastic toys, wooden blocks, metal spoons, stones, paper clips, and empty plastic bottles.
• **Prediction Activity:** Before dropping each item into the tub, students are asked to guess whether the object will float or sink, building predictive reasoning.
• **Testing Wooden Blocks:** A small piece of wood or a cork is placed on the water, and students observe excitedly as it stays afloat on the surface.
• **Testing Metal Spoons:** A heavy metal spoon or steel coin is dropped into the water, and students observe it sink immediately to the bottom of the tub.
• **Plastic Objects Behavior:** Most light plastic items, hollow plastic balls, and empty caps are observed floating on the water's surface.
• **Stone and Pebble Behavior:** Heavy stones, marbles, and iron nails are observed sinking directly to the bottom due to their heavy weight and density.
• **The Sponge Phenomenon:** A dry sponge floats initially, but as it absorbs water and becomes heavy, it slowly sinks, teaching a secondary observation.
• **Categorization Skill:** Students learn to group objects into two distinct lists: "Things that Float" and "Things that Sink."
• **Real-World Connection:** Teachers explain why large, heavy ships made of steel float in the ocean while tiny pebbles sink, sparking early engineering wonder.
• **Vocabulary Mastery:** Children master the action words **float** and **sink** through repeated verbal reinforcement and physical gestures.
• **Cleanup Responsibility:** Students participate in tidying up the experiment station, wiping spilled water, and drying the test items, promoting hygienic habits.

• **Exploring Solubility (Mixing Things in Water):** Another fascinating experiment in Chapter 9 investigates what happens when various common household substances are mixed into water.
• **Definition of Dissolving:** **Dissolving** is the process where a solid substance mixes completely into a liquid to become invisible, forming a clear, uniform solution.
• **Soluble Substances:** Materials that dissolve completely in water, such as sugar and salt, are identified as **soluble substances**.
• **Insoluble Substances:** Materials that do not dissolve in water, such as sand, stones, and chalk powder, are identified as **insoluble substances**.
• **Sugar Experiment Steps:** A spoonful of white sugar is added to a glass of water and stirred gently with a clean spoon until the sugar crystals disappear.
• **Taste Test Observation:** Although the sugar is invisible, tasting the water reveals that it has become sweet, proving the sugar is still present in dissolved form.
• **Salt Experiment Steps:** Salt is dissolved in water in a similar manner, creating saltwater that tastes salty, demonstrating that different solids change water properties.
• **Sand Experiment Steps:** Sand is added to water and stirred vigorously, but instead of dissolving, the sand settles down at the bottom of the glass.
• **Chalk Powder Test:** Chalk powder creates a cloudy, milky liquid when mixed with water, but eventually settles at the bottom, showing incomplete mixing.
• **Oil and Water Test:** A few drops of cooking oil are added to water, revealing that oil does not mix or dissolve, but instead floats as distinct bubbles on top.
• **Concept of Solutions:** Students learn that water can hold certain dissolved substances, creating transparent mixtures called solutions.
• **Stirring Action:** The physical act of **stirring** using spoons helps speed up the dissolving process, introducing basic laboratory techniques.
• **Visual Discrimination:** Children learn to differentiate between clear solutions (sugar/salt water) and cloudy mixtures (chalk/mud water).
• **Real-Life Application:** Teachers explain how lemon juice and sugar dissolve in water to make tasty lemonade, connecting science to everyday treats.
• **Scientific Inquiry:** The experiment encourages children to ask why some things vanish in water while others remain unchanged.

• **States of Water (Ice, Liquid, Steam):** Chapter 9 introduces the fascinating concept that water can exist in three different physical forms depending on temperature.
• **Liquid State (Normal Water):** The water we drink, wash with, and see in rivers and taps is in its standard **liquid state**, flowing freely at room temperature.
• **Solid State (Ice):** When water is placed in the deep freezer of a refrigerator, it becomes very cold and turns into solid ice cubes, known as its **solid state**.
• **Freezing Process:** **Freezing** is the scientific term for the process where liquid water turns into solid ice due to extreme cold temperatures.
• **Melting Process:** **Melting** is the reverse process where solid ice cubes absorb heat from the surrounding air and turn back into liquid water.
• **Ice Cube Observation:** Teachers bring an ice cube tray to class or show melting ice, allowing children to touch the cold, hard solid and watch water droplets form.
• **Gaseous State (Steam/Vapor):** When water is heated on a stove (teacher demonstration only), it boils and changes into an invisible gas called water vapor or steam.
• **Evaporation Concept:** **Evaporation** describes how liquid water slowly turns into invisible vapor in the air when warmed by the sun's rays.
• **Condensation Hint:** Warm steam hitting a cooler surface turns back into tiny water droplets, a gentle introduction to cloud formation and rain.
• **Temperature Role:** Temperature is highlighted as the magical key that changes water from ice to liquid and from liquid to steam.
• **Weather Connection:** Freezing snow on high mountains and melting ice glaciers are linked directly to these natural states of water.
• **Sensory Exploration:** Children explore the contrast between the cold hardness of ice, the wet fluidity of water, and the warmth of steam.
• **Vocabulary Enrichment:** Key scientific terms introduced include *ice, liquid, steam, freeze, melt, and boil*, tailored for UKG phonetic reading.
• **Visual Aids:** Colorful textbook charts depict snow-capped mountains, flowing rivers, and steaming cups of tea to illustrate water's three forms.
• **Interactive Review:** Students chant rhymes and answer rapid-fire questions about what happens to water inside a freezer.
