• **Chapter Introduction:** Welcome to Chapter 4 of the Class LKG Environmental Studies curriculum for the DAV Public School Board, titled "My Friendly Animals & Pets."
• **Core Subject Focus:** This chapter introduces young learners in Kindergarten to the wonderful world of animals that live around us, specifically focusing on friendly animals and household pets.
• **DAV Academic Philosophy:** The Dayanand Anglo-Vedic framework emphasizes experiential learning, compassion for all living creatures, and the foundational understanding of our natural environment.
• **Age-Appropriate Pedagogy:** Concepts are broken down into sensory-rich, easily understandable observations suitable for four-to-five-year-old cognitive development stages.
• **Primary Learning Objectives:** Children will learn to identify common animals, recognize the sounds they make, understand basic animal needs, and develop empathy toward pets.
• **Definition of Animals:** **Animals** are living creatures that can move around, breathe, eat food, and feel sensations, just like human beings.
• **Living Beings Concept:** Animals are living parts of nature; they grow from babies to adults, require water and nutrition, and respond to their environment.
• **Introduction to Friendly Animals:** **Friendly animals** are those creatures that do not harm humans, often sharing our living spaces or environments peacefully.
• **Human-Animal Bond:** The relationship between humans and animals has existed for thousands of years, built on mutual trust, companionship, and usefulness.
• **Outdoor vs. Indoor Animals:** While some animals live in the wild, others live near human settlements, on farms, or directly inside our homes as beloved companions.
• **Introduction to Pets:** **Pets** are domestic animals kept primarily for a person's company, entertainment, and emotional support rather than utility or working purposes.
• **Role of Family in Pet Care:** Keeping a pet involves the entire family, teaching young children about sharing responsibilities, nurturing, and unconditional love.
• **Sensory Engagement:** Children learn about animals through sights, sounds, textures, and movements, making environmental studies an interactive, multi-sensory experience.
• **Vocabulary Building:** Key terms introduced in this chapter include pet, puppy, kitten, tail, bark, purr, cage, kennel, leash, and veterinary care.
• **Observation Skills:** Students are encouraged to observe animals in their neighborhood, noting their physical features, colors, and behaviors with curiosity and care.
• **Safety First:** The chapter gently introduces the golden rule of approaching unfamiliar animals: always ask the owner for permission before touching any animal.

• **The Domestic Dog:** **Dog** (Scientific name: *Canis lupus familiaris*) is widely celebrated as man's best friend due to its extreme loyalty, intelligence, and protective nature.
• **Dog Breeds and Sizes:** Dogs come in many shapes, sizes, and colors, ranging from tiny pocket-sized Chihuahuas to large, fluffy Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds.
• **Puppy Identification:** A baby dog is specifically called a **puppy**, known for its playful energy, soft fur, clumsy movements, and high curiosity.
• **Dog Communication:** Dogs communicate their feelings through vocalizations, most notably barking, whining, and howling, as well as body language like tail wagging.
• **Tail Wagging Mechanism:** A wagging tail in a dog generally indicates happiness, excitement, and a friendly disposition toward humans and other animals.
• **Sensory Superpowers:** Dogs possess an extraordinary sense of smell and hearing, allowing them to perceive their environment far more intensely than human beings.
• **Dietary Habits of Dogs:** Domestic dogs are omnivores, eating specially formulated dog food, meat, rice, and vegetables as recommended by pet care guidelines.
• **Housing for Dogs:** Pet dogs often live inside human homes, sleeping in specialized beds, crates, or outdoor shelters known as **kennels**.
• **Working Dogs:** Beyond being pets, dogs serve humanity as police dogs, guide dogs for the blind, rescue dogs, and farm herders.
• **Exercise Requirements:** Dogs require daily walks on a **leash** and active playtime to burn off energy, maintain muscle tone, and keep their minds stimulated.
• **Grooming Needs:** Dogs need regular baths, brushing of their coats to prevent tangles, and nail trimming to maintain hygiene and physical comfort.
• **Health Maintenance:** Routine visits to the veterinarian for vaccinations and deworming keep pet dogs healthy and prevent the spread of diseases.
• **Loyalty and Protection:** Dogs are renowned for guarding homes, recognizing family members, and offering profound emotional comfort to children and adults alike.
• **Socialization:** Introducing puppies to different people, sounds, and other friendly animals helps them grow into well-adjusted, confident adult dogs.
• **Playful Behaviors:** Dogs love playing games like fetching a thrown ball, chasing toys, and engaging in gentle tug-of-war with their human companions.
• **DAV Value Integration:** Respecting the dog as a loyal companion teaches children gratitude, faithfulness, and the beauty of interspecies friendship.

----PAGE-BREAK---

• **The Domestic Cat:** **Cat** (Scientific name: *Felis catus*) is a small, carnivorous mammal cherished globally as an independent, graceful, and affectionate household pet.
• **Feline Characteristics:** Cats are celebrated for their agility, soft fur, retractable claws, acute night vision, and silent, stealthy walking style.
• **Kitten Identification:** A baby cat is known as a **kitten**, characterized by boundless playfulness, tiny sharp claws, big curious eyes, and high-pitched mews.
• **Cat Communication:** Cats communicate through vocalizations including meowing, purring, chirping, and hissing, alongside subtle shifts in ear and tail positions.
• **The Purring Mechanism:** **Purring** is a rhythmic, low-pitched sound produced by cats, usually signifying contentment, relaxation, or sometimes self-soothing during stress.
• **Grooming Habits:** Cats spend a significant portion of their day meticulously cleaning their fur using their rough, sandpapery tongues to remove dirt and loose hairs.
• **Dietary Needs of Cats:** Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning their bodies require specific nutrients found almost exclusively in meat, supplemented by specialized cat food.
• **Litter Box Training:** Unlike many other pets, cats are naturally instinctual about using a designated **litter box** for their bathroom habits indoors.
• **Sleeping Patterns:** Cats are crepuscular hunters by instinct and are famous for sleeping anywhere from twelve to sixteen hours a day in warm, cozy spots.
• **Independent Nature:** While dogs seek constant interaction, cats value their independence, deciding when they want affection, lap-sitting, or quiet solitude.
• **Play and Hunting Instincts:** Domestic cats love stalking, pouncing, and batting at dangling toys, laser pointers, and toy mice, keeping their predatory instincts sharp.
• **Climbing Abilities:** Cats possess powerful hind legs and flexible spines that enable them to leap high onto furniture, windowsills, and shelves with ease.
• **Scratching Post Importance:** Cats naturally need to scratch surfaces to shed old claw sheaths, stretch their muscles, and mark territory using scent glands on their paws.
• **Historical Significance:** In ancient history, cats were valued for protecting grain stores from rodents, eventually evolving into beloved indoor family companions.
• **Health Care for Felines:** Regular brushing, flea prevention, and routine veterinary check-ups ensure that pet cats live long, healthy, and comfortable lives.
• **Calming Presence:** Having a cat in the home and listening to its rhythmic purr has been shown to reduce stress and promote emotional well-being in children.

• **Introduction to Small Mammal Pets:** Beyond dogs and cats, many families keep smaller, gentle mammals as indoor pets, offering unique learning opportunities for young children.
• **The Pet Rabbit:** **Rabbits** are small, herbivorous mammals recognized by their long ears, twitching noses, fluffy cotton-tail puffs, and powerful hind legs.
• **Rabbit Movement:** Rabbits do not walk in the traditional sense; instead, they move by **hopping** rapidly across the ground with incredible speed and agility.
• **Dietary Preferences of Rabbits:** Pet rabbits thrive on a diet of fresh hay, leafy green vegetables, and specialized pellet food, requiring constant access to clean water.
• **Rabbit Housing:** Rabbits are often housed in secure indoor enclosures or hutches lined with soft bedding materials like straw, wood shavings, or paper pulp.
• **The Cute Pet Hamster:** **Hamsters** are tiny, nocturnal rodents kept in glass tanks or wire cages equipped with running wheels, tunnels, and chewing blocks.
• **Chewing Habits:** Rodents like hamsters have continuously growing front teeth, requiring them to constantly gnaw on safe wooden toys to keep their teeth trimmed.
• **Cheek Pouches:** Hamsters possess natural **cheek pouches** that expand significantly, allowing them to stuff food inside and carry it away to secret hiding spots.
• **The Social Guinea Pig:** **Guinea pigs** (or cavies) are gentle, social rodents that communicate through happy whistling sounds called popcorning when they feel excited.
• **Cage Maintenance:** Small pets require daily cleaning of their living areas, fresh water replenishment, and removal of uneaten vegetables to maintain a healthy habitat.
• **Gentle Handling Rule:** Children must learn to handle small pets with extreme gentleness, supporting their bodies fully and avoiding squeezing or sudden loud noises.
• **Observing Small Animal Behaviors:** Watching a hamster run on a wheel or a rabbit twitch its nose teaches young students about different animal adaptations and lifestyles.
• **Responsibility in Feeding:** Allowing children to help measure out rabbit pellets or wash vegetable leaves fosters a sense of routine, duty, and care for others.
• **Allergy Awareness:** Families must consider potential animal allergies before bringing furry pets into the home, ensuring a safe and comfortable environment for everyone.
• **Bedding and Comfort:** Providing clean, comfortable bedding ensures that small pets stay warm, dry, and secure throughout both day and night cycles.
• **The Wonder of Diversity:** These small mammals showcase the incredible variety of life found within the animal kingdom, expanding children's environmental awareness.

• **Introduction to Feathered Friends:** Birds bring melody, vibrant colors, and dynamic movement into our understanding of the animal kingdom and household companions.
• **Keeping Pet Birds:** Certain bird species, such as budgerigars (budgies), canaries, cockatiels, and finches, are legally and safely kept as domestic pet birds in homes.
• **Avian Anatomy Basics:** All pet birds share common physical features: a lightweight skeleton, toothless beaks, two wings for flight, and bodies covered entirely in **feathers**.
• **The Purpose of Feathers:** Feathers provide insulation against cold weather, waterproof the bird's body, and create the aerodynamic shape necessary for soaring through the air.
• **Housing for Birds:** Pet birds live inside wire **cages** equipped with wooden perches of varying diameters, food cups, fresh water bowls, and hanging swing toys.
• **Dietary Needs of Birds:** Pet birds consume a balanced diet of seeds, grains, millet sprays, fresh fruits, vegetables, and crushed cuttlebone for essential calcium intake.
• **The Wonder of Flight:** Most pet birds have wings designed for flying, though some captive pet birds have clipped wings for indoor safety or live in spacious aviaries.
• **Mimicry and Singing:** Many pet birds are celebrated for their melodious singing voices, while species like parrots can mimic human speech and environmental sounds.
• **Beak Adaptation:** A bird's **beak** acts as its hands and tool, used for cracking seeds, preening feathers, climbing cage bars, and feeding young hatchlings.
• **Preening Behavior:** Birds spend hours meticulously grooming their feathers with their beaks, applying natural oils from a gland to keep their plumage sleek and waterproof.
• **Bath Time for Birds:** Pet birds enjoy bathing in shallow dishes of water or being gently misted with spray bottles to keep their feathers clean and dust-free.
• **Social Nature of Birds:** Many pet birds are flock animals that thrive on social interaction, requiring daily attention, talking, and mental stimulation from their human families.
• **Nighttime Rest:** Birds need consistent sleep cycles, often requiring their cages to be covered with a light cloth at night to simulate natural dusk and darkness.
• **Safety Precautions:** Keeping pet birds safe indoors requires closing windows, turning off ceiling fans, and keeping toxic household chemicals and plants completely out of reach.
• **Sensory Delight for Children:** Listening to birds chirp in the morning teaches children to appreciate natural auditory beauty and the peaceful rhythms of the waking day.
• **DAV Environmental Value:** Observing birds in cages and in the wild fosters a deep respect for freedom, flight, and the delicate balance of avian ecosystems.

• **Introduction to Farm and Dairy Animals:** Beyond household pets, many friendly animals live on farms, providing essential resources and forming a vital part of our environment.
• **The Helpful Cow:** **Cows** (Scientific name: *Bos taurus*) are large, gentle, herbivorous farm mammals revered for their immense utility, calm demeanor, and maternal nature.
• **Bovine Diet and Grazing:** Cows spend hours grazing on green grass, clover, and hay, chewing their food twice through a specialized digestive process called rumination.
• **Milk Production:** Female cows produce **milk**, a highly nutritious liquid food rich in calcium and protein consumed daily by millions of children and adults worldwide.
• **Dairy Products:** Milk from cows is processed into various delicious and essential DAV-recommended dairy products including butter, cheese, curd, paneer, and buttermilk.
• **The Working Oxen:** Male counterparts of cows, known as oxen, traditionally assist farmers by pulling heavy plows across agricultural fields and transporting goods.
• **The Patient Donkey and Horse:** Horses and donkeys are strong, hoofed mammals historically used for riding, pulling carts, and carrying heavy loads across rugged terrain.
• **The Gentle Sheep:** **Sheep** are wool-bearing farm mammals that graze peacefully in flocks on hillsides, providing humanity with natural wool for warm winter clothing.
• **Shearing Process:** The thick, fleecy wool coat of a sheep is safely sheared off during warmer months without harming the animal, providing material for sweaters and blankets.
• **The Playful Goat:** Goats are hardy, agile farm animals known for their climbing abilities, curious personalities, and production of nutritious goat's milk and leather.
• **Poultry on the Farm:** Domesticated birds raised on farms for eggs and meat are called **poultry**, including chickens, ducks, geese, and turkeys.
• **The Morning Rooster and Hen:** Hens lay nutritious eggs in nesting boxes daily, while roosters crow loudly at sunrise to announce the beginning of a new day.
• **Farm Animal Housing:** Farm animals are sheltered in specially constructed structures such as barns, stables, coops, and pens designed to protect them from weather and predators.
• **Interdependent Living:** Farm animals highlight the close relationship between humans and nature, where humans provide food and shelter while animals offer vital resources.
• **Learning Respect for Labor:** Studying farm animals teaches children where daily food items like milk and eggs originate, fostering gratitude toward farmers and animals alike.
• **DAV Educational Principle:** Understanding farm animals bridges urban lifestyles with rural realities, instilling foundational ecological literacy in early childhood education.

• **Introduction to Animal Shelters and Homes:** Just like human beings need houses to live in safely, every animal requires a specific type of shelter or home suited to its needs.
• **Dog Kennel Concept:** A **kennel** or doghouse is a small, sheltered outdoor structure designed to protect pet dogs from rain, wind, and harsh sunlight.
• **Cat Sleeping Areas:** Pet cats do not typically use traditional houses; instead, they prefer warm indoor baskets, padded cushions, sofa corners, or sunny windowsills.
• **Bird Cages and Aviaries:** Pet birds reside in secure wire **cages** with perches, while wild birds build intricate nests out of twigs, leaves, moss, and feathers in trees.
• **Rabbit Hutches:** Pet rabbits live in wooden enclosures called **hutches**, which feature elevated wire mesh floors and safe, enclosed nesting chambers for sleeping.
• **Farm Animal Barns:** Large farm animals like cows, horses, and sheep sleep in spacious, straw-lined **barns** and stables that offer warmth and social safety.
• **Poultry Coops:** Chickens and ducks are kept in enclosed structures called **coops** or hen houses featuring nesting boxes for safe egg-laying and roosting bars.
• **Natural Habitats vs. Captivity:** While pets live in human-made environments, wild animals build natural homes in forests, rivers, caves, burrows, and treetops across the globe.
• **Importance of Clean Shelter:** Keeping animal shelters clean, dry, and sanitary is critical for preventing diseases, infections, and discomfort for pets and farm animals.
• **Bedding Materials:** Soft bedding materials like clean straw, wood shavings, fleece blankets, and shredded paper provide thermal insulation and comfort for resting animals.
• **Protection from Weather:** Proper animal shelters protect creatures from extreme weather conditions, including freezing winter snows, heavy rainstorms, and scorching summer heatwaves.
• **Security from Predators:** Safe enclosures protect vulnerable domestic pets and farm animals from outside predators such as stray dogs, wild cats, foxes, and birds of prey.
• **Space to Move:** Good animal housing includes adequate space for pets to stretch their legs, play, explore, and exercise their natural instincts daily.
• **Fresh Air and Ventilation:** Animal habitats must feature proper ventilation to ensure fresh air circulates continuously, preventing the buildup of harmful dust and odors.
• **Water Access:** Every animal shelter must include clean, easily accessible drinking water sources that are replenished multiple times throughout the day.
• **DAV Empathy Lesson:** Understanding animal homes teaches children that all living creatures value safety, comfort, protection, and a place to call their own.

• **Introduction to Animal Diets and Feeding:** Proper nutrition is the cornerstone of health, growth, and happiness for all friendly animals and household pets.
• **Understanding Herbivores:** **Herbivores** are animals that eat exclusively plant-based foods such as grass, leaves, grains, fruits, hay, and vegetables.
• **Herbivore Examples:** Familiar herbivorous pets and farm animals include cows, sheep, goats, rabbits, guinea pigs, horses, and deer.
• **Understanding Carnivores:** **Carnivores** are animals that consume meat, fish, and other animal protein as the primary nutritional foundation of their diet.
• **Carnivore Examples:** Domestic cats and dogs are classic examples of meat-eating or omnivorous-leaning mammals that require high-protein diets to thrive.
• **Understanding Omnivores:** **Omnivores** are creatures that eat both plant-based foods and animal products, balancing their nutritional intake from multiple sources.
• **Specialized Pet Food:** Modern pet care utilizes scientifically formulated dry kibble and wet canned food tailored specifically for dogs, cats, birds, and small rodents.
• **The Necessity of Fresh Water:** Clean, fresh drinking water is absolutely essential for every animal, aiding digestion, temperature regulation, and overall physiological function.
• **Feeding Routine:** Feeding pets at consistent, scheduled times every day helps regulate their metabolism, prevents overeating, and establishes healthy daily habits.
• **Foods Dangerous to Pets:** Children must learn that many human foods—such as chocolate, onions, grapes, garlic, and cooked bones—are toxic and harmful to pets.
• **Treats and Rewards:** Healthy pet treats are used during training sessions to reinforce good behavior and obedience in dogs and other trainable animal companions.
• **Forage and Grazing:** Farm animals like cows and sheep spend hours naturally foraging and grazing in open pastures to gather essential fibrous nutrients.
• **Seed and Grain Mixes:** Pet birds and small hamsters require specialized mixes of seeds, grains, and nuts to satisfy their natural foraging and chewing instincts.
• **Nutritional Balance:** Just like growing children, young animals need balanced vitamins, minerals, and proteins to build strong bones, muscles, and immune systems.
• **DAV Health Value:** Learning about proper animal nutrition instills awareness of healthy dietary choices, hygiene, and the biological needs of all living beings.
• **Mindful Feeding Practice:** Children should be supervised when feeding pets, ensuring they wash their hands thoroughly before and after handling pet food.

• **Introduction to Pet Care and Responsibility:** Caring for a pet is a wonderful privilege that teaches young children vital life lessons in empathy, duty, and love.
• **The Golden Rule of Pet Care:** Pets rely entirely on their human families for food, water, shelter, safety, medical attention, and emotional affection.
• **Daily Feeding Duty:** Giving a pet fresh food and clean water at the same time every day teaches children consistency, time management, and accountability.
• **Grooming and Hygiene:** Regular brushing of fur, bathing, and cleaning pet living areas keep animals free from dirt, fleas, and uncomfortable skin irritations.
• **Veterinary Care Concept:** A **veterinarian** (or vet) is a specialized animal doctor who examines sick pets, administers life-saving vaccines, and treats injuries.
• **Vaccination Importance:** Regular visits to the veterinarian for vaccinations protect pets from dangerous viral infections and keep the entire household safe.
• **Exercise and Playtime:** Daily walks, fetch games, and interactive toy play keep pets physically fit, mentally sharp, and emotionally happy.
• **Gentle Communication:** Teaching children to speak softly, move calmly, and pet animals gently prevents startling pets and ensures safe interactions.
• **Reading Animal Moods:** Children learn to recognize when an animal wants to play, when it needs rest, and when it prefers to be left alone in peace.
• **Cleaning Up After Pets:** Responsible pet ownership includes picking up waste, cleaning litter boxes, and maintaining a clean, odor-free living environment.
• **The Importance of Identification:** Pet dogs and cats often wear collars with tags or microchips containing owner contact information in case the animal ever gets lost.
• **Unconditional Love:** In return for proper care, pets offer humans pure, unconditional love, companionship, loyalty, and emotional comfort during difficult times.
• **Preventing Animal Cruelty:** The DAV curriculum strongly emphasizes that teasing, hitting, neglecting, or abandoning animals is cruel, unacceptable, and harmful.
• **Rescue and Adoption:** Encouraging the adoption of stray or shelter animals teaches children compassion for disadvantaged creatures in our local communities.
• **Teamwork in the Family:** Pet care encourages family members to share duties, working together to ensure every animal's needs are met with joy and dedication.
• **DAV Core Takeaway:** Compassion toward animals reflects the moral health of society, teaching children that kindness to pets makes the world a better place.
