• Chapter Title: **ताँगे वाले की मूँछें** (The Tongawala’s Moustache) focuses on the linguistic concept of the **Chandrabindu (ँ)** symbol.
• **Chandrabindu (ँ)** is a diacritic mark in the Devanagari script, also known as **Anunasika**.
• The primary function of the **Chandrabindu** is to indicate nasalization of the preceding vowel.
• In this chapter, students learn how the shape of the symbol resembles a crescent moon with a dot, hence the name **Chandra (Moon)** + **Bindu (Dot)**.
• The chapter introduces the concept that when we pronounce words with **Chandrabindu**, the air escapes through both the nose and the mouth.
• **Phonetic Importance**: The sound produced by **Chandrabindu** is softer and more nasal compared to the **Anusvara (ं)**.
• **Visual Identification**: The symbol is placed above the standing line of a consonant or above a vowel matra.
• **Contextual Learning**: The chapter uses the story of a **Taangewala** (carriage driver) to provide real-world vocabulary containing this symbol.
• **Vocabulary Focus**: Words like **मूँछें** (moustaches) serve as the anchor for understanding the nasal sound.
• **Linguistic Rule**: **Chandrabindu** is typically used with vowels that have a vertical stroke or matras that do not extend above the top line.
• **Grammatical Function**: It is frequently used in Hindi to denote the plural form of feminine nouns ending in 'aa' (e.g., *chidiya* becomes *chidiyan*).
• **Pronunciation Practice**: Students are encouraged to feel the vibration in their nose while saying words like *daant* (teeth) or *paanch* (five).
• **Visualizing the Symbol**: The symbol is drawn as a small arc with a dot inside, placed on top of the letter.
• **Distinction**: It is important to distinguish between the **Anusvara** (dot only) and the **Chandrabindu** (crescent and dot).
• **Chapter Objective**: To enable Class 1 students to read, write, and identify words containing the nasal sound correctly.

• **Grammatical Nuance**: The **Chandrabindu** is a marker of the **Anunasika** (nasalized) sound.
• **Linguistic Classification**: In Hindi grammar, the **Chandrabindu** is considered a secondary vowel modifier.
• **Syllabic Structure**: When the **Chandrabindu** is added to a syllable, the duration of the vowel sound remains the same, but the resonance changes.
• **Historical Context**: The **Chandrabindu** has been used in Sanskrit and Prakrit to denote nasalization, and it persists in modern Hindi.
• **Typographical Standards**: In digital Hindi fonts, the **Chandrabindu** is rendered as a single glyph.
• **Reading Fluency**: Mastery of the **Chandrabindu** is essential for reading Hindi literature fluently.
• **Common Errors**: Students often confuse the **Chandrabindu** with the **Anusvara** (dot).
• **Correction Strategy**: The **Anusvara** is used for the 'n' or 'm' sound before consonants, while the **Chandrabindu** is for pure nasalization of the vowel.
• **Example Comparison**: *Kanth* (throat) uses **Anusvara**, while *Paanch* (five) uses **Chandrabindu**.
• **Visualizing the Arc**: The arc of the **Chandrabindu** should be wide enough to be distinct from the dot.
• **Vertical Alignment**: The dot within the crescent must be centered.
• **Stroke Order**: When writing, the vertical line is drawn first, followed by the matra, and finally the **Chandrabindu**.
• **Spatial Awareness**: The symbol should not touch the top line (shirorekha) of the word.
• **Consistency**: Maintaining a uniform size for the **Chandrabindu** across all words is a key skill for Class 1 students.
• **Teacher's Role**: The teacher acts as a model for the correct nasalized pronunciation of these words.
