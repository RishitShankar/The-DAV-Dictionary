• **Marginalisation**: Refers to the socio-economic and political process by which certain communities or groups are pushed to the margins or fringes of society, thereby denying them equal access to resources, opportunities, rights, and mainstream development.
• **Social Justice**: A foundational constitutional ideal that aims to create an egalitarian society where all citizens receive fair treatment, equitable distribution of resources, and protection against discrimination regardless of caste, creed, gender, or economic background.
• **Marginalised Groups**: Specific segments of the population—such as Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), Other Backward Classes (OBCs), religious minorities, women, and persons with disabilities—who historically and socially suffer from systemic exclusion.
• **Socio-Economic Inequality**: The unequal distribution of wealth, income, social status, and educational opportunities across different social classes and castes, perpetuating poverty and dependence among disadvantaged groups.
• **Economic Marginalisation**: Occurs when vulnerable communities are denied access to land ownership, productive capital, credit facilities, formal employment, and modern technological resources, trapping them in low-paying informal jobs.
• **Social Marginalisation**: Manifests as social exclusion, stigmatization, and enforced segregation where dominant social groups restrict marginalised sections from participating in cultural life, community gatherings, and public spaces.
• **Political Marginalisation**: The lack of adequate representation, political voice, and decision-making power in legislative bodies, policy formulation, and local governance structures for underprivileged communities.
• **Spatial Exclusion**: The geographical isolation or physical segregation of marginalised communities, such as rural dalit hamlets (tolas) or remote forest tribal settlements, away from civic amenities and infrastructure.
• **Historical Injustice**: The long-term structural oppression, exploitation, and denial of basic human rights experienced by specific communities over centuries due to traditional social hierarchies and colonial policies.
• **Equity vs. Equality**: While equality means treating everyone identically, equity involves providing differential support and targeted assistance to disadvantaged groups to bring them to a level playing field with dominant sections.
• **Stereotyping and Prejudice**: Preconceived negative opinions, fixed mental images, and discriminatory biases held by mainstream society against marginalised communities, which reinforce social barriers and systemic bias.
• **Intersectionality of Exclusion**: A phenomenon where an individual faces multiple overlapping layers of discrimination, such as being a poor, low-caste, disabled woman living in a remote rural area.
• **Role of the Welfare State**: The constitutional mandate binding the Indian Government to actively intervene through affirmative action, social welfare schemes, and protective laws to uplift vulnerable citizens.
• **Democratic Inclusivity**: The core principle of democratic governance ensuring that the benefits of national economic growth and development reach the poorest and most marginalized sections of society.
• **Targeted State Intervention**: Government policies designed specifically to address the unique vulnerabilities and socio-economic handicaps of identified disadvantaged sections rather than generic welfare policies.
• **Poverty-Marginalisation Nexus**: A vicious cycle where social exclusion leads to economic poverty, and persistent poverty further deepens social marginalisation and lack of political voice.

• **Origin of the Caste System**: A rigid social structure historically prevalent in traditional Indian society, which categorized individuals into hierarchical social groups (Varnas) based on birth rather than personal merit or choice.
• **The Four Varnas**: The classical theoretical framework dividing society into Brahmins (priests/scholars), Kshatriyas (rulers/warriors), Vaishyas (traders/agriculturists), and Shudras (laborers/service providers).
• **Avarnas / Outcastes**: Communities placed entirely outside the traditional Varna system, historically subjected to severe social ostracization, physical segregation, and systemic exploitation, later classified as Scheduled Castes.
• **Untouchability**: A dehumanizing practice associated with extreme caste discrimination, where physical contact or even the shadow of certain lower-caste individuals was considered religiously polluting by upper castes.
• **Social Disabilities**: Severe restrictions imposed on lower castes prohibiting them from drawing water from common village wells, entering public temples, using public roads, or attending village schools.
• **Economic Restrictions**: Historical enforcement of hereditary occupations on lower castes, forcing them into degrading, low-income labor such as leatherwork, sanitation, sweeping, and manual scavenging while denying them land ownership.
• **Spatial Segregation**: The forced physical location of lower-caste housing settlements outside the main boundaries of rural villages, ensuring their social distance and perpetual isolation from mainstream life.
• **Denial of Knowledge Access**: Historical prohibition preventing lower-caste individuals from studying sacred texts, acquiring formal education, or learning specialized technical trades reserved for upper castes.
• **Social Reform Movement**: 19th and 20th-century socio-religious awakening movements led by visionary Indian reformers aimed at eradicating caste discrimination, untouchability, child marriage, and gender oppression.
