• **Time:** The continuous progression of existence, events, and moments from the past, through the present, and into the future.
• **Clock:** A mechanical or digital device used to measure, keep, and indicate the current time.
• **Watch:** A small, portable clock designed to be worn on the wrist or carried in a pocket.
• **Dial:** The circular face of a clock that displays numbers and indicators for time.
• **Hands:** The moving pointers on a clock face that indicate hours, minutes, and sometimes seconds.
• **Hour Hand:** The shorter hand on a clock that moves slowly to indicate the current hour.
• **Minute Hand:** The longer hand on a clock that moves faster to indicate the current minute.
• **Second Hand:** The thinnest and fastest-moving hand on a clock, which tracks the passage of individual seconds.
• **Numbers on a Clock:** A standard analog clock features numbers from 1 to 12 arranged in a circle.
• **Full Rotation:** The minute hand completes one full circle around the clock dial in exactly 60 minutes.
• **Hour Cycle:** The hour hand completes one full circle around the clock dial in 12 hours.
• **Twelve-Hour Format:** A system of timekeeping that divides the 24-hour day into two periods of 12 hours each.
• **Analog Clock:** A clock that uses a traditional face with rotating hands to represent time.
• **Digital Clock:** A clock that displays time using numerical digits (e.g., 10:30) rather than hands.
• **Time Measurement:** The process of quantifying the duration of events using standardized units like seconds, minutes, and hours.
• **Standardization:** The use of consistent units of time globally to ensure synchronization of activities.
• **Reading Time:** The skill of interpreting the position of the clock hands to determine the exact time.

• **Day:** The period of time it takes for the Earth to complete one full rotation on its axis.
• **Duration of a Day:** A single day consists of 24 equal hours.
• **Morning:** The period of the day from sunrise until noon, characterized by the start of daily activities.
• **Noon:** The exact middle of the day, occurring at 12:00 PM, when the sun is at its highest point.
• **Afternoon:** The period of time between noon and sunset.
• **Evening:** The period of time between the end of the afternoon and the onset of darkness.
• **Night:** The period of time between sunset and sunrise, characterized by darkness and rest.
• **Sunrise:** The moment the sun appears above the horizon, marking the beginning of the day.
• **Sunset:** The moment the sun disappears below the horizon, marking the end of the day.
• **Daily Routine:** A sequence of activities performed at specific times throughout the day, such as waking up, eating, and sleeping.
• **Chronology:** The arrangement of events in the order in which they occur in time.
• **Sequence:** The logical order of events, such as morning followed by afternoon, then evening, then night.
• **AM (Ante Meridiem):** A Latin term meaning "before midday," used to denote time from midnight to noon.
• **PM (Post Meridiem):** A Latin term meaning "after midday," used to denote time from noon to midnight.
• **Midnight:** The exact middle of the night, occurring at 12:00 AM, marking the start of a new day.
• **24-Hour Day:** The total duration of one full day and night cycle.

---PAGE-BREAK---

• **Minute:** A unit of time equal to 60 seconds.
• **Hour:** A unit of time equal to 60 minutes.
• **Relationship:** There are 60 minutes in one full hour.
• **Half-Hour:** A duration of 30 minutes, representing half of a full hour.
• **Quarter-Hour:** A duration of 15 minutes, representing one-fourth of a full hour.
• **O'Clock:** A term used to indicate a full hour, such as 3:00 (three o'clock).
• **Positioning:** When the minute hand points to 12, the time is exactly on the hour.
• **Half-Past:** A term used when the minute hand points to 6, indicating 30 minutes have passed since the hour.
• **Quarter-Past:** A term used when the minute hand points to 3, indicating 15 minutes have passed since the hour.
• **Quarter-To:** A term used when the minute hand points to 9, indicating 15 minutes remain until the next hour.
• **Counting by Fives:** The minute hand moves in increments of 5 minutes for each number it passes on the clock face.
• **Minute Markers:** The small lines between the numbers on a clock face represent individual minutes.
• **Fast Movement:** The minute hand travels much faster than the hour hand because it must cover more ground to measure smaller units.
• **Slow Movement:** The hour hand moves from one number to the next over the course of 60 minutes.
• **Time Intervals:** The space between two numbers on a clock face represents a 5-minute interval.
• **Precision:** Reading the minute hand accurately requires observing exactly which minute marker it points to.

• **Week:** A period of seven consecutive days.
• **Days of the Week:** A repeating cycle of seven named days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday.
• **Monday:** The first day of the traditional work and school week.
• **Tuesday:** The second day of the week.
• **Wednesday:** The third day of the week, often considered the middle of the work week.
• **Thursday:** The fourth day of the week.
• **Friday:** The fifth day of the week, often the last day of the school or work week.
• **Saturday:** The sixth day of the week, typically part of the weekend.
• **Sunday:** The seventh day of the week, typically the final day of the weekend.
• **Weekend:** The collective term for Saturday and Sunday, usually reserved for rest and leisure.
• **Cyclical Nature:** The days of the week repeat in the same order indefinitely.
• **Yesterday:** The day immediately preceding the current day.
• **Today:** The current day.
• **Tomorrow:** The day immediately following the current day.
• **Calendar:** A chart or device used to track days, weeks, and months of the year.
• **Planning:** Using the days of the week to organize events, tasks, and appointments.

---PAGE-BREAK---

• **Month:** A unit of time used with calendars, approximately as long as a natural orbital period of the Moon.
• **Year:** The time taken by the Earth to orbit the Sun, consisting of 12 months.
• **Total Months:** There are 12 months in one calendar year.
• **January:** The first month of the year, having 31 days.
• **February:** The second month of the year, unique for having 28 or 29 days.
• **March:** The third month of the year, having 31 days.
• **April:** The fourth month of the year, having 30 days.
• **May:** The fifth month of the year, having 31 days.
• **June:** The sixth month of the year, having 30 days.
• **July:** The seventh month of the year, having 31 days.
• **August:** The eighth month of the year, having 31 days.
• **September:** The ninth month of the year, having 30 days.
• **October:** The tenth month of the year, having 31 days.
• **November:** The eleventh month of the year, having 30 days.
• **December:** The twelfth month of the year, having 31 days.
• **Sequence:** Months always follow the same chronological order from January to December.

---PAGE-BREAK---

• **Leap Year:** A year that contains an extra day to keep the calendar synchronized with the Earth's orbit.
• **Frequency:** A leap year occurs every four years.
• **February Adjustment:** In a leap year, February has 29 days instead of the usual 28.
• **Total Days in a Year:** A standard year has 365 days, while a leap year has 366 days.
• **Days in Months Mnemonic:** A common method to remember the number of days in each month is using the knuckle technique.
• **Knuckle Technique:** Using the knuckles to represent months with 31 days and the spaces between them for months with 30 days (or February).
• **Seasonality:** Months are often associated with specific weather patterns and seasons.
• **Quarterly Division:** A year can be divided into four quarters, each consisting of three months.
• **Calendar Utility:** Calendars help in tracking important dates, birthdays, and holidays.
• **Historical Origin:** The names of the months are derived from Roman history and mythology.
• **Time Span:** A year represents a complete cycle of seasons.
• **Date:** A specific day of a specific month in a specific year.
• **Month-End:** Some months end on the 30th, while others end on the 31st.
• **Consistency:** The number of days in each month remains constant every year, except for February.
• **Planning Ahead:** Understanding months allows for long-term scheduling of events.
• **Yearly Cycle:** After December, the calendar resets to January of the following year.

• **Seasons:** The natural divisions of the year based on changes in weather, ecology, and the amount of daylight.
• **Spring:** The season of renewal, where plants begin to grow and temperatures start to rise.
• **Summer:** The season characterized by the warmest temperatures and longer days.
• **Autumn (Fall):** The season where temperatures begin to drop and leaves change color and fall from trees.
• **Winter:** The season characterized by the coldest temperatures and shorter days.
• **Cyclical Change:** Seasons follow a predictable pattern throughout the year.
• **Weather Correlation:** Each season brings distinct weather patterns, such as rain, snow, or heat.
• **Adaptation:** Living beings, including humans, animals, and plants, adapt their behaviors to the changing seasons.
• **Seasonal Activities:** Certain activities are specific to seasons, such as swimming in summer or wearing heavy coats in winter.
• **Duration:** Each season typically lasts for about three months.
• **Hemispheric Difference:** Seasons are reversed in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
• **Equinox:** A time of year when day and night are of approximately equal length.
• **Solstice:** A time of year when the sun reaches its highest or lowest point in the sky at noon, marking the longest or shortest day.
• **Environmental Impact:** Seasonal changes influence agriculture and food production cycles.
• **Nature's Clock:** Seasons serve as a natural indicator of the passage of time within a year.
• **Observation:** Observing seasonal changes is a fundamental way to understand the Earth's relationship with the Sun.

• **Duration:** The length of time that something continues or exists.
• **Comparing Time:** Determining whether an event takes a short time or a long time to complete.
• **Short Duration:** Activities like blinking, snapping fingers, or taking a single step.
• **Long Duration:** Activities like growing from a child to an adult, or the changing of seasons.
• **Time Estimation:** The ability to guess how long an activity will take based on experience.
• **Efficiency:** Completing tasks within a reasonable time frame.
• **Punctuality:** The habit of being on time for scheduled events or appointments.
• **Time Management:** The process of organizing and planning how to divide time between specific activities.
• **Sequence of Events:** Understanding what happens first, second, and last in a series of actions.
• **Before and After:** Concepts used to describe the relative timing of two events.
• **Simultaneous Events:** Events that happen at the exact same time.
• **Past:** The time that has already occurred.
• **Present:** The time that is happening right now.
• **Future:** The time that is yet to come.
• **Time Awareness:** Developing a sense of how time passes during different activities.
• **Practical Application:** Using time concepts to navigate daily life and school schedules.

• **Digital Clock Display:** Shows hours and minutes separated by a colon (e.g., 04:15).
• **Analog Clock Reading:** Requires identifying the position of the hour hand first, then the minute hand.
• **Hour Hand Position:** If the hour hand is between two numbers, it has passed the smaller number but not yet reached the larger one.
• **Minute Hand Calculation:** Multiply the number the minute hand points to by 5 to find the minutes past the hour.
• **12:00 Position:** When the minute hand is at 12, it is "o'clock."
• **6:00 Position:** When the minute hand is at 6, it is "half-past."
• **3:00 Position:** When the minute hand is at 3, it is "quarter-past."
• **9:00 Position:** When the minute hand is at 9, it is "quarter-to."
• **Clock Maintenance:** Analog clocks require batteries or winding to keep the hands moving.
• **Digital Precision:** Digital clocks are often more precise as they do not rely on mechanical gears.
• **Time Zones:** Different regions of the world have different local times based on their longitude.
• **Global Synchronization:** Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks.
• **Stopwatch:** A specialized watch used to measure the amount of time elapsed from a particular time when it is activated to the time when it is deactivated.
• **Timer:** A device that counts down from a specified time interval.
• **Alarm Clock:** A clock designed to alert the user at a specific time.
• **Timekeeping History:** Evolution from sundials and water clocks to modern atomic clocks.
