• **Core Theme:** The story revolves around the negative consequences of greed and the importance of being content with what one has.
• **Protagonist:** The main character is a monkey who exhibits a strong desire for more food than he can physically manage.
• **Setting:** The story takes place in a jungle environment, providing a natural habitat for the monkey.
• **Character Trait - Greed:** The monkey is defined by his inability to share or limit his consumption, which serves as the central conflict.
• **Vocabulary Focus:** Students learn words related to jungle life, food items, and emotional states like 'greedy' and 'hungry'.
• **Narrative Structure:** The story follows a linear progression: Introduction of the monkey, the discovery of food, the act of greed, and the final consequence.
• **Lesson Objective:** To teach young learners that excessive desire often leads to loss or physical discomfort.
• **Moral Instruction:** The text emphasizes the DAV philosophy of 'Dharma' and 'Right Conduct' by highlighting the vice of greed.

• **The Monkey’s Nature:** The monkey is portrayed as restless and constantly searching for sustenance.
• **The Discovery:** The monkey finds a jar or a container with a narrow neck, which acts as the primary trap.
• **The Bait:** Inside the jar, there are nuts or fruits that the monkey finds highly desirable.
• **The Mechanism of the Trap:** The narrow neck of the jar is designed so that an empty hand can enter, but a full fist cannot exit.
• **The Monkey’s Action:** Upon seeing the food, the monkey immediately reaches inside the jar without hesitation.
• **The Grasp:** The monkey grabs as many nuts as he possibly can, filling his palm to its maximum capacity.
• **The Physical Limitation:** Because the monkey refuses to let go of any nuts, his fist becomes too large to pass through the jar's opening.
• **The Conflict:** The monkey is now stuck; he is physically attached to the jar because of his own refusal to release the food.
• **The Monkey’s Reaction:** Instead of realizing his mistake, the monkey becomes frustrated and pulls harder.
• **The Concept of 'Greed':** Greed is defined here as the desire to possess more than one can handle or use.
• **The Consequence:** The monkey’s inability to let go prevents him from escaping or moving freely.
• **The Lesson on Attachment:** The story illustrates how attachment to material objects can restrict one's freedom.
• **The Role of Wisdom:** The monkey lacks the wisdom to realize that letting go of a few nuts would save his entire situation.
• **Behavioral Analysis:** The monkey’s behavior is impulsive, driven by immediate gratification rather than long-term safety.
• **Classroom Application:** Teachers use this to explain why 'sharing' and 'moderation' are better alternatives to 'greed'.
